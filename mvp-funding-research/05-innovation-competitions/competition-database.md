## COMPETITION SELECTION FRAMEWORK

### Questions to Ask Before Applying

1. **Alignment Questions**
   - Does my company match the competition's focus/sector?
   - Is my stage (seed, Series A, MVP) right for this competition?
   - Does the prize (cash, equity, mentorship) match my needs?

2. **Timing Questions**
   - Can I meet the application deadline with quality submission?
   - If chosen, can I attend/pitch when required?
   - Does the timeline align with my fundraising goals?

3. **Effort vs. Reward**
   - How much time does the application require? (1-5 hours typical)
   - What's probability of winning? (1-10% typical)
   - What's value if I don't win? (Visibility, feedback, networking)

4. **Practical Questions**
   - Is there an application fee? (Free to $500 typical)
   - Are there any equity requirements if I win?
   - What's the time commitment if selected?

5. **Success Criteria**
   - What would success look like beyond winning? (Investor connections, media, brand)
   - Are there media/press opportunities post-competition?
   - Will I get feedback on my pitch if not selected?

## Applying to Competitions: Best Practices

### Application Preparation

1. **Create Standardized Materials**
   - Elevator pitch (30 seconds)
   - 1-minute pitch
   - 3-minute pitch
   - 5-minute pitch
   - Company overview document (1 page)
   - Pitch deck (12-15 slides)

2. **Tailor Application to Each Competition**
   - Read judging criteria carefully
   - Customize answers to specific prompts
   - Use competition language/terminology
   - Highlight attributes they value

3. **Get Feedback Before Submitting**
   - Have mentor review application
   - Get peer feedback on pitch
   - Have someone unfamiliar with company review
   - Revise based on feedback

### Post-Selection If You Win or Advance

1. **Immediate Actions**
   - Thank the organizers
   - Share news on social media (if permitted)
   - Connect with other competitors/winners
   - Prepare for next stage

2. **Pitch Preparation** (If advancing to pitch)
   - Practice 50+ times
   - Record and self-evaluate
   - Get feedback from mentors
   - Research judges
   - Prepare for likely questions

3. **Follow-up**
   - Schedule meetings with interested investors
   - Get investor contact information
   - Send thank you notes
   - Share results with your network
   - Iterate on pitch based on feedback

## Competitions NOT Recommended

### Red Flags in Competitions

**High Equity Cost**
- Demanding >10% equity for early-stage grant/prize = bad deal
- Avoid competitions with equity requirements unless extremely well-known

**Unclear Judging Criteria**
- If criteria are vague or subjective, application is likely waste of time
- Avoid if you can't understand how they'll judge

**High Application Fees**
- $500+ application fees without clear value proposition
- Avoid unless high prize probability

**Overly Niche**
- If competition requirements are too narrow (e.g., "only female immigrant founders in Portland"), may be too limiting
- Evaluate if you truly fit profile

**Predatory Business Models**
- Some competitions are marketing vehicles for venture studios wanting equity
- Some are scams (research carefully)
- Check Better Business Bureau and founder reviews
