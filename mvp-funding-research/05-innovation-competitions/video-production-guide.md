## Video Requirements Overview

### Common Competition Formats

#### Pitch Video (Most Common)
- **Duration**: 2-5 minutes (check specific requirements)
- **Format**: MP4, H.264 codec
- **Resolution**: 1920x1080 (Full HD) minimum, 4K preferred
- **Frame Rate**: 24fps or 30fps
- **File Size**: Under 500MB for uploads
- **Aspect Ratio**: 16:9 (widescreen)

#### Product Demo Video
- **Duration**: 3-7 minutes
- **Focus**: Product in action, user benefits
- **Resolution**: 1920x1080 minimum
- **Pacing**: Faster than pitch (cuts every 3-5 seconds)

#### Founder Story/Company Culture Video
- **Duration**: 1-3 minutes
- **Focus**: Founder narrative, team authenticity
- **Resolution**: 1920x1080 minimum
- **Tone**: Personal, emotional connection

#### Technical/Scientific Explainer
- **Duration**: 4-8 minutes
- **Focus**: How technology works, science/engineering
- **Resolution**: 2K or 4K preferred
- **Visuals**: Heavy use of animation, diagrams

## Filming Best Practices

### Pre-Production Planning

#### Shot List Development
Create detailed list of every shot needed:
1. Wide establishing shot (full body)
2. Medium shot (chest up)
3. Close-up (shoulder to top of head)
4. Product detail shots
5. B-roll of workspace/office
6. Graphics/slides showing data
7. Customer testimonials
8. Team members working
9. Product in use
10. Location/context shots

#### Scripting Guidelines
- **Word Count**: 150-180 words per minute of video
- **For 3-minute video**: 450-540 words
- **For 5-minute video**: 750-900 words
- **Delivery Pace**: Natural, conversational (not robotic)
- **Emphasis**: Mark key phrases for emphasis
- **Pauses**: Include pause markers for emphasis
- **Pronunciation**: Spell out difficult words phonetically

#### Location Scouting
- **Lighting**: Check natural light at recording time
- **Noise**: Identify ambient sounds (traffic, HVAC, etc.)
- **Background**: Clean, professional, branded
- **Space**: Enough room for camera movement
- **Power**: Access to electrical outlets
- **Permits**: Check requirements for public spaces
- **Backup Locations**: Have contingency indoor spots

### Recording Day Execution

#### Pre-Recording Checklist (90 minutes before)
- [ ] Charge all batteries 100%
- [ ] Format all memory cards
- [ ] Test all equipment (camera, audio, lights)
- [ ] White balance camera with actual location lighting
- [ ] Audio levels test (aim for -12dB to -6dB)
- [ ] Frame composition test (rule of thirds, headroom)
- [ ] Backup power supplies ready
- [ ] Insurance/permits verified
- [ ] Props and materials organized
- [ ] Talent (speakers) briefed and comfortable

#### Camera Settings Recommended

**Video Format**
- Codec: H.264 or ProRes (if available)
- Resolution: 1920x1080 minimum (4K if supported)
- Frame Rate: 24fps (cinematic feel) or 30fps
- Shutter Speed: 1/50 (for 24fps) or 1/60 (for 30fps)
- Aspect Ratio: 16:9

**Image Quality**
- ISO: Keep as low as possible (under 3200)
- Aperture: f/2.8-f/4 (shallow depth of field, sharp on face)
- White Balance: 5600K (daylight) or auto with custom preset
- Focus: Autofocus ON for movement, manual for static shots
- Color Profile: Rec.709 or log profile if color grading planned

**Recording Settings**
- Record format: All-I frame (independent frame, larger files)
- Zebra patterns: Enable to check overexposure
- Focus peaking: Enable if available
- Audio levels: Manual control, aim for -12dB on peaks

#### Filming Technique

**Position & Framing**
- **Medium Shot**: Top of head 1/3 from top (rule of thirds)
- **Eye Level**: Camera lens height at eye level
- **Space**: Look room (space in front of person)
- **Angle**: Slightly above eye level (2-3 inches) is flattering
- **Distance**: 4-6 feet from subject for medium shot

**Movement**
- **Slow Pans**: Only when necessary, slow and smooth (3+ seconds)
- **Zooms**: Avoid completely (reframe instead)
- **Camera Movement**: Less is more (distracting if overdone)
- **Steadiness**: Use gimbal or tripod (no shaky video)
- **Transitions**: Cut between shots (no zooms)

**Takes & Redundancy**
- **Minimum Takes**: 3 takes minimum per shot
- **Warm-Up Takes**: First take rarely best (actors need warm-up)
- **Safety Takes**: If good take, do one more backup
- **Mistakes**: Continue through (can be edited out)
- **Auto-Focus Hunting**: Do re-takes if autofocus hunting visible

**Talent Direction**
- **Look at Camera**: For direct address (eye contact with viewer)
- **Look Off-Camera**: For interview-style (look at interviewer)
- **Natural Speech**: Conversational, not robotic
- **Pauses**: Build in natural pauses for editing rhythm
- **Energy**: Maintain consistent energy throughout takes
- **Clothing**: Avoid small patterns (stripes, checks), wear solids
- **Makeup**: Light makeup for camera (not theatrical)

#### Common Recording Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Poor Audio**
   - Speaking too far from mic (loss of volume)
   - Harsh room echo (use soft furnishings)
   - Ambient noise (traffic, HVAC)
   - Audio peaking (distortion)
   - Inconsistent levels across takes

2. **Lighting Issues**
   - Harsh shadows under eyes
   - Backlighting blowing out background
   - Insufficient fill light
   - Flickering fluorescent lights
   - Inconsistent lighting between takes

3. **Video Quality**
   - Low resolution files
   - Blurry focus or soft focus
   - Rolling shutter artifacts
   - Incorrect white balance
   - Underexposed video

4. **Performance Issues**
   - Too much thinking/pausing
   - Robotic, non-natural delivery
   - Looking at cue cards too obviously
   - Inconsistent energy
   - Inappropriate facial expressions

5. **Technical Problems**
   - Wrong frame rate causing stutter
   - Corrupted files (not formatted card)
   - Camera overheating (record in segments)
   - Battery dies mid-recording
   - Audio sync problems

## Audio Professional Standards

### Audio Quality Requirements

#### Target Levels
- **Average Speaking Level**: -12dB to -6dB
- **Peak Levels**: Never exceed -3dB (headroom for peaks)
- **Noise Floor**: Below -40dB when silent
- **Dynamic Range**: At least 35-40dB
- **Frequency Response**: 80Hz - 12kHz (human speech range)

#### Microphone Selection

**Lavalier/Lapel Mic** (Most Common for Competition Videos)
- Rode Wireless GO II ($299)
- Sennheiser EW 100 G4 ($400+)
- Audio-Technica AT831cW ($300+)
- Advantages: Hands-free, natural positioning, consistent level
- Challenge: Wind noise (use windscreen), rustling sounds

**Boom Mic** (Better Sound Quality)
- Rode NT1 ($200) in boom configuration
- Audio-Technica AT4022 ($350)
- Sennheiser MKE 600 ($200)
- Advantages: Better sound quality, directional
- Challenge: Requires boom operator or pole/stand

**Shotgun Mic** (Professional)
- Rode Procaster ($200)
- Audio-Technica AT4050 ($400)
- Neumann KM137 ($500)
- Advantages: Super-directional, picks up subject only
- Challenge: Requires boom stand, may need operator

**USB/Desktop Mics** (Not Recommended)
- Blue Yeti, Audio-Technica AT2020USB
- Issues: Pick up room echo, noise, inconsistent quality
- Use only as backup

### Audio Recording Workflow

#### Setup
1. **Microphone Positioning**
   - Lapel mic: 6-8 inches below mouth, centered on chest
   - Boom mic: 12-18 inches above head, pointing down 45°
   - Positioning: Avoid rustling against clothing

2. **Level Setting**
   - Have talent speak at normal volume
   - Set input gain so peaks hit -12dB to -6dB
   - Test record 10 seconds, playback to verify
   - Leave 6dB headroom for peaks
   - Document level settings for consistency

3. **Cable Management**
   - Lavalier: Route cable under shirt (reduces rustling)
   - Boom: Secure cables to stand with gaffer tape
   - XLR cables: Use quality cables, check for noise
   - Wireless: Check frequency conflicts before recording

#### Recording Best Practices
- **Room Tone**: Record 30-60 seconds of silent room (for editing)
- **Reference Audio**: Record simultaneous camera audio (backup)
- **Test Playback**: After 3-5 minutes recording, verify quality
- **Consistency**: Use same mic, settings, position for all talent
- **Backup**: Record simultaneous backup on secondary device
- **Levels**: Check levels continuously during recording

#### Minimizing Noise Issues

**Handling Noise**
- Wind/breath: Pop filter on boom mic, foam windscreen on lavalier
- Rustling: Tape mic cord to clothing, warn talent to be still
- HVAC: Turn off if safe, schedule recording during quiet periods
- Traffic: Choose locations away from roads
- Echo: Use soft furnishings, curtains, carpeting

**Camera Audio Syncing**
- Best practice: Record audio separately on audio recorder
- Auto-sync: Use clapper board at start of each take
- Manual sync: Note timecode from both devices
- Post-production: Sync in video editing software
- Verification: Match waveforms to confirm sync

## Lighting & Production Design

### Three-Point Lighting Setup (Professional Standard)

#### Key Light (Main Light)
- **Purpose**: Illuminate subject's face, create definition
- **Position**: 45° angle from subject, slightly above eye level
- **Intensity**: Brightest light in setup
- **Modifier**: Softbox (3x3 or 4x4 feet) for flattering quality
- **Distance**: 4-6 feet from subject
- **Power**: Adjust until face is properly exposed

#### Fill Light (Secondary Light)
- **Purpose**: Reduce harsh shadows, maintain flattering look
- **Position**: Opposite key light, 45° angle
- **Intensity**: 1/2 to 1/3 brightness of key light
- **Modifier**: Larger softbox or diffusion panel
- **Distance**: 6-8 feet from subject
- **Effect**: Softens shadows without eliminating them (keeps definition)

#### Back Light (Rim Light)
- **Purpose**: Separate subject from background, add dimension
- **Position**: Behind subject, 45° angle, above head height
- **Intensity**: 1/2 to 2/3 brightness of key light
- **Modifier**: None (can be bare bulb for harsh rim)
- **Distance**: 6-8 feet from subject
- **Effect**: Creates separation, adds depth
- **Caution**: Don't let spill into camera lens

#### Background Light (Optional 4th Light)
- **Purpose**: Illuminate background evenly
- **Position**: Behind subject, pointing at background
- **Intensity**: Depends on background color/reflectance
- **Purpose**: Prevents dark background, adds depth

### Lighting for Different Settings

#### Studio Setup (Controlled Lighting)
- Full 3-point lighting setup
- Background light to ensure even background
- Overhead fill or reflector
- Total setup: 4-5 lights
- Time to setup: 30-45 minutes

#### Office/Indoor Setup (Existing Lighting)
- Add key light (existing light too harsh/dim)
- Add fill light if shadows too harsh
- Use reflector for fill if power limited
- Turn off overhead fluorescent if possible
- Total setup: 1-3 lights
- Time to setup: 15-20 minutes

#### Outdoor/Natural Light Setup
- Use sun as key light
- Position subject with sun 45° angle
- Use reflector for fill (white foam board)
- Shade subject from direct sun if too harsh
- Schedule around golden hour (early morning, late afternoon)
- Total setup: 15-30 minutes
- Advantage: Free, natural, flattering light
- Challenge: Changes throughout day, weather dependent

### Color Temperature & White Balance

#### Color Temperature Standards
- **Daylight**: 5600K (blue-ish)
- **Tungsten**: 3200K (warm, orange-ish)
- **LED**: Variable 3200K-6500K (check specs)
- **Fluorescent**: 4000-4500K (sickly green-ish)

#### White Balance Process
1. **Set Custom White Balance**
   - Film white card in actual light
   - Use camera's custom WB feature
   - Use white card for reference

2. **Verify in Recording**
   - Check skin tones in live view
   - Adjust if too blue or too orange
   - Verify in first recorded take

3. **Consistency**
   - Use same white balance for all takes
   - If lighting changes, redo white balance
   - Document settings for consistency

4. **Color Correction in Post**
   - Shoot in log/flat profile if supported
   - Correct color in editing (DaVinci Resolve, Adobe)
   - Standardize skin tones across all talent

### Production Design & Set Dressing

#### Background Considerations
- **Branded**: Include company logo, colors
- **Professional**: No clutter, clean, organized
- **Depth**: Background 4-6 feet behind subject (subject isolated)
- **Color Contrast**: Subject color contrasts with background
- **Lighting**: Background evenly lit (3-point setup)

#### Props & Set Elements
- **Minimalism**: Less is more (avoid clutter)
- **Brand Consistency**: Props aligned with company identity
- **Product Placement**: Product visible but not distracting
- **Office Elements**: Desk, computers, whiteboards (authenticity)
- **Lighting**: Props lit for visibility and interest

#### Company Branding
- **Logo Placement**: Visible but not dominant
- **Color Palette**: 2-3 brand colors maximum
- **Fonts**: Use brand fonts if clearly readable
- **Consistency**: Match branding across all videos

## Editing & Post-Production

### Video Editing Workflow

#### Software Options

**Consumer/Prosumer** (Budget: $200-400/year)
- Adobe Premiere Elements ($80)
- DaVinci Resolve FREE (professional version $295)
- Filmora ($60)
- CapCut (FREE, cloud-based)
- iMovie (FREE, Mac only)

**Professional** (Budget: $600-2400/year)
- Adobe Premiere Pro ($55/month, $660/year)
- DaVinci Resolve Studio ($295)
- Final Cut Pro ($300 one-time)
- Avid Media Composer (Industry standard)

**Recommended for Competitions**: DaVinci Resolve FREE
- Professional-grade color correction
- Excellent timeline editing
- Fusion (3D animation) included
- Fusion page for effects
- Free version is truly professional
- Learning curve steeper but worth it

### Editing Process

#### Step 1: Ingest & Organization
1. Import all video files from memory cards
2. Create folder structure:
   - Original files (backup)
   - Proxies (lower-res for editing)
   - Audio files
   - Graphics/lower-thirds
   - Color correction grade
3. Create sequences:
   - Rough cut sequence
   - Fine cut sequence
   - Final sequence
4. Back up all files (2 copies minimum)

#### Step 2: Rough Cut
1. **Assemble Shots**
   - Arrange shots in logical sequence
   - Remove obviously bad takes
   - No color correction yet
   - Audio not yet balanced

2. **Remove Dead Air**
   - Remove thinking/pausing between sentences
   - Keep natural breathing, pauses
   - Tighten pacing without feeling rushed

3. **Timing Check**
   - Total duration within competition limits?
   - Sequence feels right length?
   - Pacing too fast or too slow?

#### Step 3: Fine Cut
1. **Review Every Cut**
   - Is this cut motivated? (cut for reason)
   - Could duration be tightened?
   - Does sound match picture?

2. **Pacing & Rhythm**
   - Cut more on words (not between words)
   - Vary shot duration (3-5 seconds ideal)
   - Don't hold shots too long
   - Use B-roll to break up talking head

3. **Visual Interest**
   - Vary framing (wide, medium, close)
   - Cut to product/demo at relevant moments
   - Cut to graphics/charts when mentioned
   - Use B-roll for transitions, interest

4. **Sound Design**
   - Remove background noise during silence
   - Add subtle background music (no copyright)
   - Layer sound effects if relevant
   - Music cues for dramatic moments
   - Fade music out (don't cut abruptly)

#### Step 4: Color Correction

**Basics** (Minimal but professional)
1. **Exposure Correction**
   - Adjust if overexposed or underexposed
   - Lift shadows if crushed (too dark)
   - Crush blacks if washed out

2. **White Balance**
   - Correct color casts (blue, orange)
   - Skin tones should look natural
   - Consistency across all shots/talent

3. **Saturation & Vibrancy**
   - Slightly increase saturation (10-15%)
   - Pop colors without looking over-processed
   - Avoid oversaturated look

**Professional** (If time permits)
1. **Curves Adjustment**
   - S-curve for contrast (professional look)
   - Lift shadows, control highlights

2. **Secondary Color Correction**
   - Isolate and adjust specific colors
   - Correct green fluorescent cast on shirt
   - Adjust background separately

3. **Looks & Presets**
   - Apply subtle color grade (not extreme)
   - Hollywood look popular (warm highlights, cool shadows)
   - Match all talent to same grade

#### Step 5: Titles & Graphics

**Opening Title Card**
- Company logo + title
- 3-5 seconds duration
- Clean, professional design
- Font readable on small screens

**Lower-Thirds** (Name/Title Graphic)
- Name and title for each speaker
- Appear when speaker first introduced
- Hold for duration of speaking
- Disappear before next speaker

**Data Graphics**
- Key statistics shown visually
- Chart showing growth, metrics
- Product screenshots
- Feature callouts

**Closing Slide**
- Company name + logo
- Call to action (website, email)
- Social media handles
- 3-5 seconds duration

**Tools for Graphics**
- Adobe After Effects ($30/month)
- DaVinci Fusion (FREE, included)
- Motion (Mac, $50)
- Canva ($120/year, templates)
- Adobe Premiere built-in graphics
- Stock footage sites (Envato Elements, etc.)

### Audio Post-Production

#### Audio Levels & Mixing

1. **Normalize Audio**
   - Set all dialogue to similar level (-12dB to -6dB)
   - Consistency across multiple speakers

2. **EQ (Equalization)**
   - Remove rumble (<80Hz)
   - Reduce harshness (around 2-3kHz)
   - Add presence (5-8kHz)
   - Subtle EQ only

3. **Compression**
   - Control dynamic range
   - Prevent peaks from clipping
   - Consistent level throughout
   - Subtle compression (2-4dB reduction)

4. **Noise Reduction**
   - Remove hum (60Hz electrical noise)
   - Remove background hiss
   - Subtle reduction (not overdone, causes artifacts)

5. **Reverb Control**
   - If room echo present, add very subtle plate reverb
   - Or use noise gate to eliminate tail
   - Keep audio tight and present

#### Background Music

**Source Options**
- Royalty-free libraries:
  - Epidemic Sound ($10/month)
  - Artlist ($15/month)
  - AudioJungle ($0.50-$15 per track)
  - Free music: YouTube Audio Library, Incompetech
- Licensed: Direct from composers (permissions needed)

**Selection Criteria**
- Tempo: Matches pacing of video
- Energy: Matches tone of content
- Duration: Sufficient for your video length
- Licensing: Check commercial use allowed

**Implementation**
- Volume: Background only (-15dB to -12dB)
- Fade in: 2-3 seconds intro
- Fade out: 2-3 seconds outro
- Underscore: Beneath dialogue, not competing
- Cue points: Music swells at important moments
- Licensing: Attribute if required

#### Sound Effects

**Use Sparingly** (Not gimmicky)
- Transition whoosh (professional)
- Notification/alert sound (when product shows)
- Button clicks (product demo)
- Emphasis effects (rare moments)
- Music stings (dramatic moments)

**Source**
- Freesound.org
- Zapsplat (free)
- YouTube Audio Library
- Epidemic Sound
- Adobe Stock Audio

**Implementation**
- Subtle, professional sounds
- Level lower than dialogue
- Positioned on exact beat/action
- Don't overuse (1-2 max per minute)

### Exporting & File Specifications

#### Master Export Settings

**Video Codec**
- Format: MP4
- Codec: H.264 (widely compatible)
- Bitrate: 12-16 Mbps (high quality)
- Frame Rate: 24fps or 30fps (match source)
- Resolution: 1920x1080 or 4K
- Aspect Ratio: 16:9

**Audio Settings**
- Codec: AAC
- Sample Rate: 48kHz
- Bit Depth: 16-bit
- Channels: Stereo
- Bitrate: 320kbps

**File Size Target**
- 2-3 minute video: 50-150MB
- 5-minute video: 100-300MB
- Under 500MB if uploading

#### Multiple Export Versions

1. **Master Archive**
   - ProRes or DNxHD (preservation)
   - High quality, large file size
   - Kept for future re-editing

2. **Delivery Format**
   - MP4 H.264 (platform standard)
   - Optimized for compression
   - File size under upload limit

3. **YouTube/Vimeo Version**
   - Slight higher bitrate
   - Platform will re-compress anyway
   - 1920x1080 or 4K

4. **Social Media Version**
   - 1080x1080 (square) for Instagram
   - 1200x675 (landscape) for LinkedIn
   - 1080x1920 (vertical) for TikTok/Reels
   - Shorter clips for social (15-60 seconds)

- [ ] File format matches requirements

## Platform-Specific Formatting

### YouTube Upload Format
- **Resolution**: 1920x1080 minimum (will accept 4K)
- **Aspect Ratio**: 16:9
- **Frame Rate**: 24fps, 30fps, 60fps all acceptable
- **Codec**: H.264
- **File Format**: MP4, MOV, AVI, WMV
- **File Size**: Unlimited (but long upload time for huge files)
- **Duration**: Unlimited (for verified accounts)

**YouTube-Specific Tips**
- Use custom thumbnail (1280x720, 5MB max)
- Write compelling description with links
- Add timestamps to longer videos
- Enable subtitles/captions (auto-generated acceptable)
- Use relevant tags and keywords
- Create compelling preview (first 5 seconds critical)

### Vimeo Upload Format
- **Resolution**: 1920x1080 minimum (accept 4K)
- **Aspect Ratio**: Any (prefers 16:9)
- **Codec**: H.264, ProRes, DNxHD
- **File Format**: MP4, MOV, AVI, WMV, FLV, MXF
- **File Size**: 500MB-4GB depending on tier
- **Duration**: Unlimited

**Vimeo-Specific Tips**
- Professional platform (better for business)
- Can password-protect videos
- Clean interface (no ads if paid tier)
- Better color reproduction
- Good for sharing confidential pitches

### Competition Platform (Generic Requirements)
- Check specific upload format
- Verify maximum file size
- Confirm duration limits
- Check for required metadata
- Verify platform playback codecs
- Test upload before final deadline

## Common Video Problems & Solutions

### Audio Issues

**Problem**: Audio too quiet, inaudible in parts
**Solution**:
- Normalize audio to -6dB to -3dB
- Check microphone technique (too far away?)
- Re-record with better mic positioning
- Use noise gate to prevent dropouts

**Problem**: Audio distorted, peaks clipping
**Solution**:
- Use compression to control dynamics
- Re-record with lower input gain
- Apply limiting in post-production
- Manually reduce peak levels

**Problem**: Background noise/hum present
**Solution**:
- Use notch filter to remove 60Hz hum
- Apply subtle noise reduction
- Re-record in quieter location
- Use different microphone placement

**Problem**: Audio out of sync with video
**Solution**:
- Use clapper board and sync manually
- Use auto-sync feature in editing software
- Slip audio layer to match lips
- Check if recording at different sample rates

### Video Issues

**Problem**: Video blurry or out of focus
**Solution**:
- Re-record with proper focus (manual if needed)
- Use software sharpening (subtle, -20 to -10)
- Check camera autofocus settings
- Verify adequate lighting (autofocus needs light)

**Problem**: Color casts (too blue, orange, green)
**Solution**:
- Redo white balance during shooting
- Use color correction in post
- Apply custom white balance in editing
- Use adjustment brush for localized correction

**Problem**: Video too dark or too bright
**Solution**:
- Adjust exposure in camera settings
- Re-record with additional lighting
- Use exposure adjustment in post
- Apply curves adjustment for fine control

**Problem**: Flickering (especially fluorescent lighting)
**Solution**:
- Turn off fluorescent lights during recording
- Use anti-flicker setting if camera has
- Shoot at different frame rate (try both 24 and 30fps)
- Use shutter speed that syncs with power frequency (1/60 for 60Hz)

**Problem**: Rolling shutter distortion
**Solution**:
- Reduce camera movement (use tripod)
- Decrease panning speed
- Update camera firmware
- Avoid moving horizontally while camera pans
- If severe, re-record (some cameras have global shutters)

### Editing Issues

**Problem**: File won't import into editing software
**Solution**:
- Check file format compatibility
- Convert to intermediate codec (ProRes)
- Update video editing software
- Use transcoding tool (Handbrake, Adobe Media Encoder)
- Check file not corrupted

**Problem**: Rendering takes too long
**Solution**:
- Use proxy files (lower resolution for editing)
- Disable all effects before final render
- Reduce preview resolution during editing
- Upgrade computer specs (RAM, processor)
- Use faster storage (SSD vs HDD)

**Problem**: Video stutters during playback
**Solution**:
- Generate cache files before playback
- Lower preview resolution
- Close other applications
- Upgrade RAM
- Use proxy files instead of full resolution
