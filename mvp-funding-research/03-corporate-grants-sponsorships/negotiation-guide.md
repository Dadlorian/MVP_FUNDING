## 1. Pre-Negotiation Strategy

### Information Gathering Phase (Before First Call)

#### Understand Their Perspective
**Research questions to answer:**
1. What's their strategic reason for this program? (Growth, market entry, PR, customer acquisition, risk reduction?)
2. Who has budget authority? (Is your contact the decision-maker?)
3. What's their typical approval timeline? (30-90 days is standard)
4. What competitors have they partnered with? (Shows their preferences)
5. Have they publicly committed to this initiative? (CEO mention = higher commitment)
6. What are their success metrics? (How will they measure ROI?)
7. What's their biggest risk? (Failure, reputational, financial?)

**Research sources:**
- Earnings calls (investor.relations.com)
- Annual CSR/ESG reports
- Press releases and news articles
- LinkedIn profiles of decision-makers
- Their website (programs, case studies, testimonials)
- Former partner companies (case studies, LinkedIn recommendations)

#### Map the Decision-Making Process
**Ask directly in discovery meetings:**
- "How many approvals would this need?"
- "Who would need to sign off?"
- "What's your approval timeline?"
- "Have similar partnerships been approved before?"
- "What's the typical process for deals at this amount?"

**Document the hierarchy:**
- Budget authority (CEO, VP, Department head?)
- Legal review (General Counsel, outside counsel?)
- Procurement (If large contracts)
- Corporate Communications (For public announcements)
- Risk/Compliance review (For certain industries)

### Pre-Negotiation Checklist

**Internal Alignment (Before Negotiations Start)**
- [ ] Board/leadership approval of negotiation strategy
- [ ] Authority levels defined (Who can approve what?)
- [ ] Walk-away amount/terms identified (BATNA)
- [ ] Priority ranking of key terms (Must-have vs. nice-to-have)
- [ ] Multiple proposal tiers prepared (Plan A, B, C)
- [ ] Legal review of standard terms completed
- [ ] Relationship capital assessed (How much negotiating room do we have?)
- [ ] Escalation path defined (If negotiation stalls)

**Preparation Materials**
- [ ] 2-3 page overview of your company (one-pager)
- [ ] Case studies from previous corporate partnerships
- [ ] Social proof (other partners, metrics, press coverage)
- [ ] Financial projections (if sharing with them)
- [ ] Internal decision-making process documented
- [ ] Authority letter ready (board authorization to sign)

### BATNA Development (Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement)

**Define your BATNA:**
What will you do if this deal doesn't happen?

| Scenario | BATNA | Impact | Likelihood |
|----------|-------|--------|-----------|
| Can't reach deal on price | Apply to 3 other programs, target $250K from each | 12-month delay, diverse funding | 30% |
| They require exclusive deal | Walk away, pursue non-exclusive partnerships | Maintain optionality | 15% |
| Excessive IP demands | Seek alternative funder | Mission drift | 10% |
| Unacceptable reporting burden | Limit commitment to pilot only | Reduced revenue | 25% |

**BATNA Strength** = increases your negotiating power
- Weak BATNA = corporations can impose terms
- Strong BATNA = you can walk away with options

## 2. Key Contract Terms & Definitions

### Financial Terms

#### Amount / Consideration
**Definition**: Total value of the partnership (cash + in-kind)

**Negotiation notes:**
- Never accept less than the minimum needed to execute
- Budget for 10-20% contingency in costs
- Request: Clear payment schedule (monthly vs. lump sum)

**Sample language:**
> "Corporation agrees to provide funding in the amount of [USD $XXX] payable as follows: [Amount] upon execution, [Amount] upon completion of specified milestones, and [Amount] upon project completion."

#### Payment Terms & Schedule
**Definition**: When and how payments are made

**Common structures:**
1. **Upfront**: 100% paid at signing (rare, usually grants)
2. **Milestone-based**: Paid on achievement of defined outcomes
3. **Monthly**: Paid monthly throughout term
4. **Quarterly**: Paid quarterly in arrears
5. **In arrears**: Invoiced at end of period after delivery

**Negotiation tips:**
- Push for monthly or quarterly (improves cash flow)
- Milestone-based payments align interests (they get proof before paying more)
- Upfront payment shows corporate confidence
- Request net-15 (payment within 15 days of invoice)

**Sample language:**
> "Payment shall be made as follows: [25%] upon execution of this Agreement, [25%] upon completion of Phase 1 (due by [DATE]), [25%] upon completion of Phase 2 (due by [DATE]), and [25%] upon final project completion (due by [DATE]). All invoices shall be paid within fifteen (15) business days of receipt."

#### Currency & Foreign Exchange
**Definition**: Which currency for payments and FX risk allocation

**Negotiation notes:**
- If you're in USD and they're foreign, negotiate which currency
- Request currency locked at signing (no FX exposure to you)
- Specify their exposure to FX (usually their responsibility if their domestic currency)

**Sample language:**
> "All payments shall be made in [USD]. If Corporation is paying in foreign currency, Corporation shall be responsible for all foreign exchange gains and losses. Payments shall be valued at the exchange rate as of the date of invoice."

### Duration & Timeline Terms

#### Effective Date & Term Length
**Definition**: When agreement starts and how long it lasts

**Common structures:**
- **Fixed term**: 12 months, 24 months, etc. (most common)
- **Evergreen**: Automatic renewal unless either party terminates
- **Project-based**: Until defined milestones completed

**Negotiation tips:**
- Longer terms = more predictable revenue (prefer 24+ months)
- Evergreen with annual review = best case
- Project-based works if milestones are clear and achievable

**Sample language:**
> "This Agreement shall be effective as of the Effective Date and shall continue for a period of twelve (12) months, unless earlier terminated pursuant to Section [X]. The term shall automatically renew for successive one-year periods unless either party provides written notice of non-renewal at least ninety (90) days prior to expiration."

#### Termination Provisions
**Definition**: How either party can exit the agreement

**Three types:**
1. **For Cause**: Partner breached agreement (material breach, failure to pay, etc.)
2. **For Convenience**: Either party can terminate without cause
3. **By Mutual Agreement**: Both parties agree to end

**Negotiation tips:**
- Fight for short termination notice (30 days is good)
- Longer notice = less risk for you (gives time to adjust)
- For cause termination should follow 30-day cure period
- Request protection: if they terminate for convenience, you get reimbursement for project-specific costs

**Sample language:**
> "Either party may terminate this Agreement for material breach if the breaching party fails to cure such breach within thirty (30) days of written notice. Either party may also terminate for convenience with ninety (90) days' written notice, provided that any early termination for convenience by Corporation shall entitle [Company] to retain all payments received and shall not be subject to refund."

#### Milestones & Deliverables
**Definition**: Specific outcomes that trigger payments/contract continuation

**Negotiation tips:**
- Make milestones SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- Ensure metrics are in your control (not dependent on them)
- Request 2-week notice before deadline (if they measure)
- Build 10% buffer into timelines

**Red flag:**
- Vague milestones ("successful implementation") = disputes

**Better approach:**
- Specific metrics (e.g., "95% system uptime", "3 case studies delivered", "50 employee hours volunteered")

**Sample language:**
> "Milestones for payment release shall be as follows:
> 1. Phase 1 Completion (due by [DATE]): Onboarding completed, stakeholders trained, baseline metrics established
> 2. Phase 2 Completion (due by [DATE]): 90% of implementation tasks completed, all reporting tools operational
> 3. Final Delivery (due by [DATE]): Project completed per specifications, final report submitted, lessons learned documented
>
> Payment shall be released within fifteen (15) business days of [Company] verification that each milestone is met."

### Intellectual Property & Ownership

#### IP Ownership
**Definition**: Who owns intellectual property created during partnership

**Four models:**
1. **You own everything**: You retain all IP (ideal for you)
2. **They own everything**: They get all IP (avoid if possible)
3. **Joint ownership**: Both parties own (middle ground, risky)
4. **Licensed back**: You own, they get license

**Negotiation tips:**
- Default: You own everything you create pre-existing, they own usage rights
- "Work for hire" = they own (fight this unless well-compensated)
- Negotiate: They can use IP for case studies, testimonials (limited right)
- Preserve: Your right to reuse methodology/IP with other partners

**Red flags:**
- "Exclusive ownership rights to all work product"
- "Perpetual license" (no time limit on their usage)
- "Royalty rights" on future use

**Sample language:**
> "[Company] retains all intellectual property rights in the work product created under this Agreement. Corporation is hereby granted a non-exclusive, non-transferable license to use the work product solely for the purposes outlined in this Agreement. [Company] may use the work product (including case studies and results) for business development, marketing, and educational purposes, provided [Company] does not disclose Corporation's confidential information."

#### Attribution & Credit
**Definition**: Who gets public credit for the partnership/work

**Negotiation tips:**
- You need: Credit on your website, case studies, press releases
- They get: Logo placement, partnership mention
- Negotiate: Joint press release rights, named case study, speaking opportunities

**Sample language:**
> "Corporation agrees that [Company] may reference this partnership in marketing materials, case studies, press releases, and presentations. Both parties shall approve any public communications mentioning this partnership. The parties shall issue a joint press release announcing the partnership within [30] days of execution."

### Confidentiality & Publicity

#### Confidentiality Obligations
**Definition**: What information each party keeps private

**Negotiation tips:**
- Be restrictive about what's confidential (narrow definition)
- Carve-outs: Information that becomes public, already known, developed independently
- Request: Ability to disclose to advisors, legal counsel, auditors
- Don't accept: Blanket confidentiality about entire partnership (limits your marketing)

**Red flag:**
- "All information regarding partnership is confidential" (kills your ability to reference them)

**Better language:**
> "Confidential Information means non-public technical and business information disclosed by one party to the other. Confidential Information excludes information that: (a) is or becomes publicly available, (b) was known prior to disclosure, (c) is developed independently, or (d) is required to be disclosed by law. [Company] may disclose that Corporation is a partner and may reference project outcomes for business development purposes, provided [Company] does not disclose Corporation's confidential information or specific deal terms."

#### Publicity & Announcements
**Definition**: Whether the partnership can be publicly announced

**Negotiation options:**
1. **Mutual announcement**: Both parties issue joint press release
2. **Company announcement**: You announce, they approve messaging
3. **Confidential**: No public announcement (avoid)
4. **Controlled disclosure**: You can mention in pitch materials, but not press release

**Negotiate for:**
- Case study rights (publish results publicly)
- Logo placement (on website, materials)
- Speaking opportunity (present at conference or event)
- Testimonial (quote in your marketing)

**Sample language:**
> "Within thirty (30) days of execution, the parties shall jointly issue a press release announcing this partnership. [Company] may use Corporation's name and logo on [Company]'s website, marketing materials, case studies, and presentations. [Company] shall not disclose specific financial terms without prior written consent."

### Liability & Risk Allocation

#### Indemnification
**Definition**: One party promises to cover losses if something goes wrong

**Three scenarios:**
1. **You indemnify them**: You cover if you breach, fail to deliver, infringe IP
2. **They indemnify you**: They cover if they breach, fail to pay
3. **Mutual indemnification**: Both parties cover their respective breaches

**Negotiation tips:**
- You should only indemnify for: Your breach, IP infringement you cause, your negligence
- Don't indemnify for: Their use of your product in unapproved way, market changes, third-party actions
- Request cap: Limit indemnification to actual damages (not penalties)
- Request notice: You should be notified promptly if claim arises

**Sample language:**
> "[Company] shall indemnify Corporation against claims arising from: (a) [Company]'s breach of this Agreement, (b) [Company]'s intellectual property infringement, or (c) [Company]'s gross negligence. This indemnification shall not apply to claims arising from Corporation's use of [Company]'s services in violation of this Agreement or applicable law."

#### Limitation of Liability
**Definition**: Caps on damages in case of breach

**Negotiation tips:**
- Push for: Liability limited to fees paid under agreement
- Request: Exclusion of indirect/consequential damages
- Clarify: Direct damages (your costs to fix) vs. indirect (lost profits)

**Sample language:**
> "Except for breach of confidentiality obligations or indemnification obligations, each party's total liability under this Agreement shall be limited to the fees paid in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim. In no event shall either party be liable for indirect, incidental, consequential, special, or punitive damages, even if advised of the possibility of such damages."

### Reporting & Metrics

#### Performance Metrics
**Definition**: How you measure and report success

**Negotiation tips:**
- Define metrics in writing BEFORE execution (avoid post-agreement disputes)
- Make metrics in your control (not dependent on their actions)
- Build baselines (how will you measure improvement?)
- Request: Flexibility to adjust metrics if circumstances change

**Poor metrics (avoid):**
- "Successful implementation" (vague)
- "Positive market reception" (subjective)
- "Significant revenue growth" (undefined)

**Good metrics:**
- "95% system uptime measured monthly"
- "50+ employee participants in volunteer program"
- "15 case studies delivered by end of year"
- "$250K in cost savings measured via invoice review"

**Sample language:**
> "Success shall be measured using the following key performance indicators:
> 1. Delivery: All deliverables completed on schedule (measured by [Company])
> 2. Quality: 95% of deliverables meet quality standards (measured by Corporation feedback)
> 3. Impact: [Specific metric] improves from [baseline] to [target] (measured by [method])
>
> [Company] shall provide quarterly reports detailing performance against these metrics. If metrics fall below targets, the parties shall meet to discuss remediation options."

#### Reporting Frequency & Format
**Definition**: How often and how detailed reporting must be

**Negotiation tips:**
- Quarterly is standard (not weekly or monthly)
- 2-page summary is sufficient (not 50-page detailed report)
- Request: Flexibility in format (dashboard, email, brief deck)
- Avoid: Open-ended reporting requirements

**Red flag:**
- "Monthly detailed reporting with access to all systems/data"
- "Real-time reporting dashboard"
- "Unlimited data access"

**Better approach:**
> "Reporting: [Company] shall provide quarterly business review meetings with a summary of: (1) deliverables completed, (2) key metrics performance, (3) highlights and lessons learned. Written reports shall be 2-3 pages in length. Corporation shall have reasonable access to systems for performance verification."

#### Data Rights & Privacy
**Definition**: Who can access data created during partnership

**Negotiation tips:**
- Clarify: You own customer data, they can't reuse it
- Request: Data deletion after partnership ends (GDPR/privacy compliance)
- Specify: What data they can retain for audit/legal purposes
- Include: Data security obligations (they must protect your data)

**Sample language:**
> "[Company] retains all rights to customer data and project data. Corporation shall use such data solely for the purposes of this Agreement and shall not disclose or sell such data to third parties. All data shall be deleted within thirty (30) days of termination unless required to be retained by law. Corporation shall implement reasonable security measures to protect all data."

## 3. Negotiation Tactics by Deal Type

### Type A: Sponsorship Deals

**Typical structure**: $50K-$500K cash payment for brand recognition and event participation

#### Key Negotiation Points

**1. Deliverables Clarification**
- What exactly are they getting? (logo placement, speaking slot, booth, etc.)
- Where will their logo appear? (website, event materials, press release, etc.)
- How prominent? (size, above/below-the-fold, A/B/C tier)
- How long? (during event only, or 12 months?)

**Pre-negotiation approach:**
- Create tiered sponsorship packages:
  - **Platinum ($250K)**: All benefits, featured logo
  - **Gold ($150K)**: Most benefits, standard logo
  - **Silver ($75K)**: Basic benefits, small logo

**Negotiation tactic:**
- Start with their interest level, then reveal tiers
- Let them choose tier (they'll often choose middle tier)
- Add bonus perks if they choose higher tiers
- Keep flexibility to customize packages

**Sample conversation:**
> "We offer three sponsorship levels: Platinum at $250K includes [benefits], Gold at $150K includes [benefits], or Silver at $75K includes [benefits]. What level would work best for your goals? We can also customize benefits based on your priorities."

**2. Exclusivity Negotiation**
**Corporate ask**: "You can't work with our competitors"
**Your response**: "We can offer category exclusivity for [X] time period"

**Negotiation progression:**
1. **Your position**: No exclusivity (you need flexibility)
2. **Compromise 1**: Exclusivity within defined category only (e.g., financial services but not payments)
3. **Compromise 2**: Exclusivity for specific duration (e.g., 6 months, not full term)
4. **Compromise 3**: Partial exclusivity (they get first right of refusal, you can work with non-competing partners)

**Sample language:**
> "During the first twelve (12) months of this Agreement, [Company] agrees to non-exclusive sponsorship arrangement whereby [Company] will not enter into agreements with direct competitors of Corporation in the [specific category]. This exclusivity does not apply to [Company]'s customers, existing partnerships, or partnerships with non-competing companies."

**3. Announcement Timing**
**Corporate ask**: "We need to announce immediately"
**Your response**: "Let's time it strategically"

**Leverage:**
- If you're early-stage, their announcement is PR gold
- Request: They announce first (shows their commitment)
- Timing: Coordinate with your major funding milestones or product launch
- Multiple channels: Press release, their blog, LinkedIn, event announcement

**Sample language:**
> "Corporation shall issue announcement of partnership no earlier than [DATE] and no later than [DATE]. [Company] shall coordinate timing of announcement for maximum mutual benefit. Both parties agree to provide pre-approval of announcement messaging."

### Type B: Pilot Programs

**Typical structure**: $100K-$500K for 3-12 month pilot with defined success criteria

#### Key Negotiation Points

**1. Success Criteria & Payment Triggers**
**Risk**: Vague success metrics = payment disputes

**Your approach:**
- Propose 3-5 specific, measurable metrics
- Baseline: current state before pilot
- Target: expected state after pilot
- Measurement method: who measures and how?

**Example metrics:**
- "Reduce processing time from 2 hours to 30 minutes (measured via timestamp analysis)"
- "Achieve 95% system uptime (measured by monitoring systems)"
- "Complete 50% of anticipated use cases by month 6 (measured by implementation tracking)"

**Negotiation conversation:**
> "We want to make sure this pilot is successful and we get paid for delivering value. I propose we define success around three metrics: [Metric 1], [Metric 2], and [Metric 3]. We measure these monthly and review progress in our quarterly meetings. If we achieve targets, we both know the pilot was successful. Does that approach work for you?"

**2. Timeline Negotiation**
**Corporate ask**: "We need results in 90 days"
**Your response**: "Let's be realistic about timeline"

**Typical timeline breakdown:**
- **Week 1-2**: Onboarding, setup, baseline measurement
- **Week 3-10**: Implementation and testing
- **Week 11-12**: Results analysis, lessons learned
- **Month 4-6**: Expanded deployment decision

**Negotiation approach:**
- Propose realistic timeline (6 months is better than 3 months)
- Build in buffer: 20% contingency for delays
- Request: If they delay start, extend end date by same amount
- Clarify: Weekends and holidays don't count toward timeline

**Sample language:**
> "The pilot shall be conducted over a six (6) month period: Month 1 (onboarding), Months 2-5 (implementation and testing), Month 6 (analysis and recommendations). If Corporation delays start date beyond [DATE], the end date shall be extended by an equal period. Success shall be evaluated based on metrics achieved by end of Month 6."

**3. Scope Creep Prevention**
**Risk**: Corporation keeps adding "just one more thing"

**Prevention tactics:**
- Clearly document scope (what's in/out of pilot)
- Define: Change request process (any additions require amendment)
- Request: Additional budget for scope changes (new work = new $ or extended timeline)
- Monthly reviews: Confirm scope still aligned

**Sample language:**
> "Pilot scope is limited to: [specific deliverables]. Any requests outside this scope shall be treated as Change Requests and shall require: (a) written description of change, (b) impact analysis on timeline and budget, and (c) executed change order. Corporation may request up to [X] change orders at no additional cost; additional changes require additional compensation or timeline extension."

**4. Expansion Path**
**Your goal**: Turn pilot into larger contract

**Negotiation approach:**
- Include language: "Upon successful completion of pilot, parties may discuss expansion"
- Define: What "successful" means (metric thresholds)
- Commit: You'll propose expansion offer
- Negotiate: Pricing for expanded phase (typically 20-30% discount from first pilot)

**Sample language:**
> "If pilot achieves [success criteria], Corporation has right of first refusal to expand partnership. [Company] shall propose expanded scope and pricing within thirty (30) days of pilot completion. Expanded phase pricing shall be no more than 110% of per-unit cost from pilot phase."

### Type C: Grants

**Typical structure**: $25K-$500K one-time grant with reporting requirements

#### Key Negotiation Points

**1. Reporting Requirements**
**Corporate ask**: "Extensive quarterly reporting with impact analysis"
**Your response**: "Simple annual report with key metrics"

**Negotiation progression:**
1. **Your proposal**: Annual written report (2 pages)
2. **Their request**: Quarterly written report (5 pages) + annual meeting
3. **Compromise**: Annual written report + annual in-person meeting
4. **Their ask details**: Data access, case studies, detailed metrics
5. **Your limit**: Aggregate anonymized data only, no individual customer data

**Sample language:**
> "Grant recipient shall provide annual report within thirty (30) days of grant anniversary, including: (1) summary of grant activities, (2) quantified impact metrics, (3) beneficiaries served. Donor may request in-person meeting annually to discuss impact. All reporting shall maintain individual and customer confidentiality."

**2. Use of Funds**
**Risk**: Overly restrictive grant ("must be used for X only")

**Negotiation approach:**
- Propose: Broad category use (e.g., "staffing and operations" vs. "Marketing Manager salary only")
- Request: Flexibility to reallocate within category
- Include: If circumstances change, ability to amend use of funds with donor approval

**Sample language:**
> "Grant shall be used for [broad category] to support [stated mission]. Grant recipient may reallocate funds within this category without prior approval, provided total allocation aligns with project goals. If circumstances require allocation changes outside this category, grant recipient shall request approval from Donor, not to be unreasonably withheld."

**3. Publicity & Attribution**
**Corporate ask**: "Feature your use of our grant in marketing materials"
**Your response**: "We'll give you credit, but control how it's presented"

**Sample language:**
> "Grant recipient acknowledges grant from [Donor Foundation] in all materials funded by this grant, using language: 'This project is supported by a grant from [Donor Foundation].' Grant recipient shall not use Donor's name or logo in marketing without Donor's prior written approval. Grant recipient may reference grant in pitch materials and marketing with Donor's approval of use and context."

### Type D: In-Kind Partnerships

**Typical structure**: Cloud credits, software licenses, API access, services

#### Key Negotiation Points

**1. Credit Valuation**
**Risk**: Credits valued at list price, but you get discount anyway

**Your approach:**
- Clarify: What discount do you get if purchasing in cash?
- Calculate: True economic value to you (not list price)
- Negotiate: Credit value reflects realistic discount rate

**Example:**
- List price: $100K AWS credits
- Your typical discount: 30% (you'd pay $70K)
- Fair credit value: $70K
- Request: "Valued at your standard discount rate" in agreement

**Sample language:**
> "In-kind contribution shall be valued at [value] based on [Company]'s historical pricing with Corporation. If [Company] received the goods/services at no cost separately, valuation shall be based on [Company]'s most-favored-customer discount rate."

**2. Credit Expiration & Rollover**
**Risk**: Credits expire and you lose them

**Negotiation points:**
- Term: 2-3 year expiration is standard (not 1 year)
- Carryover: Unused credits roll to next year
- Increase: If you use credits faster than expected, request additional allocation

**Sample language:**
> "Credits shall be valid for twenty-four (24) months from date of allocation. Unused credits shall roll forward to subsequent year for twelve (12) months. If [Company] utilization exceeds allocated credits, Corporation shall provide additional credits or cash equivalent."

**3. Termination & Wind-Down**
**Risk**: Partnership ends, credits disappear mid-project

**Negotiation approach:**
- Request: If partnership terminates, credits valid for 90 days wind-down period
- Calculate: Extra credits for transition period

**Sample language:**
> "Upon termination of this Agreement, all remaining credits shall remain valid for ninety (90) days to allow [Company] to wind down services and transition to alternative providers. Corporation shall not impose changes to credit usage or restrictions during ninety-day wind-down period."

## 4. Common Clauses & How to Handle Them

### Red Flag Clauses (What to Push Back On)

#### 1. "Affiliate Restrictions"
**Corporate language**: "You can't work with any company we own or have interest in"

**Problem**: Too broad - prevents you from working with many companies

**Your response**:
> "We can agree not to directly compete with Corporation's core business. However, we must retain ability to work with [your customers/partners/industry]. We propose: No partnership with direct competitors of Corporation in [specific category]. This doesn't restrict partnerships outside [category]."

#### 2. "Most Favored Nations"
**Corporate language**: "You can't give better terms to any other partner"

**Problem**: Locks you into lowest negotiated rate forever

**Your response**:
> "We're happy to offer you competitive rates. However, rates vary by volume, contract length, and partner type. We propose: 'Pricing is market-competitive and may vary based on program scope, duration, and market conditions.'"

#### 3. "Perpetual License"
**Corporate language**: "We have rights to your work forever, even after partnership ends"

**Problem**: They use your work after you stop being paid

**Your response**:
> "We can grant license during partnership term. Upon termination, license continues for [specific limited purpose] for [specific time period]. Example: 'Corporation retains perpetual license to existing work product created during partnership term, limited to internal use and case study purposes.'"

#### 4. "Unlimited Indemnification"
**Corporate language**: "You cover any third-party claims arising from your services"

**Problem**: You're liable for anything, forever, unlimited amount

**Your response**:
> "We'll indemnify for our direct breaches and IP infringement we cause. We can't indemnify for their use of our services in unapproved ways or market changes. Propose: 'Indemnification applies to [Company] breaches only and is capped at 12 months of fees.'"

#### 5. "Exclusive Arrangement"
**Corporate language**: "You can only work with us, not other companies in [category]"

**Problem**: Kills your ability to grow with other partners

**Your response**:
> "We can offer exclusive focus in first [X months] while you evaluate partnership. After that, non-exclusive arrangement allows us to invest in partnership growth. Propose: 'Non-exclusive arrangement with [Company] having right of first refusal on [specific services] for 90 days.'"

#### 6. "Unlimited Audit Rights"
**Corporate language**: "We can audit your books, systems, everything anytime"

**Problem**: They have unlimited access to your confidential operations

**Your response**:
> "We're happy to provide financial reporting. Audits should be limited and scheduled. Propose: 'Corporation may conduct annual audit upon 30 days' notice during normal business hours. Audits limited to matters related to this Agreement.'"

#### 7. "IP Ownership of Methodology"
**Corporate language**: "We own all IP including your methodology and approach"

**Problem**: You can't reuse your own methods with other clients

**Your response**:
> "You own the outputs from this engagement. I retain ownership of my underlying methodology, processes, and general approaches. Propose: 'Corporation owns work product created for this engagement. [Company] retains ownership of pre-existing tools, methodologies, and general approaches, which Corporation is licensed to use.'"

#### 8. "Unlimited Confidentiality"
**Corporate language**: "Everything about this partnership must stay confidential"

**Problem**: You can't reference partnership in marketing or case studies

**Your response**:
> "We're happy to keep financial terms and sensitive info confidential. But we need ability to reference partnership as proof point. Propose: '[Company] may reference that Corporation is a partner and may publish case studies with Corporation's approval. Financial terms remain confidential.'"

## 5. Red Flags & Deal-Breakers

### Warning Signs During Negotiation

| Red Flag | What It Means | How to Respond |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| "Our legal team will never agree to that" | They're not empowered to negotiate | Ask to meet with legal; propose escalation |
| "We can't put this in writing" | They might not deliver | Request LOI at minimum; consider walking away |
| "This is standard in our contracts" | Probably bad for you | Propose amendments anyway; don't accept "standard" |
| "We need to move very quickly" | They're desperate (rush = worse terms for you) | Slow down; thorough negotiation = better deal |
| "We need exclusive rights" | They want to control your business | Counter with non-exclusive or time-limited exclusive |
| "Our budget is fixed, can't increase" | Budget is real constraint | Negotiate scope down or ask for in-kind additions |
| "We'll reassess after 6 months" | They're not committed | Request contract commitment; propose renewal option |
| "You're our 5th option if others fall through" | You're backup plan | Ask: Am I first choice option or backup? Adjust offer accordingly |

### Deal-Breaker Scenarios (When to Walk Away)

**Walk away if:**
1. **No written agreement**: Verbal agreements are unenforceable
2. **Exclusive = they control your business**: Non-exclusive or time-limited only
3. **No IP ownership of your methodology**: You must be able to reuse your work
4. **Unlimited indemnification**: Cap at 12 months of fees
5. **No ability to reference partnership**: You need marketing value
6. **Payment contingent on their business success**: You get paid regardless
7. **Unlimited scope changes**: Define what's in/out
8. **No termination rights**: You should be able to exit with 90-day notice

**Walk away calculation:**
> Amount × Probability of Collection × (1 - Risk Factor) = Real Value
>
> Example:
> $250K × 80% collection probability × (1 - 30% risk of non-renewal) = $140K true value
>
> If walk-away is $200K, walk away

## 6. Letter of Intent (LOI) Framework

### When to Use LOI

Use LOI when:
- Deal is complex (many terms to negotiate)
- Both parties agree in principle but need to finalize terms
- You want to build in time before full contract execution
- Amount is substantial (>$100K)

### LOI Structure

```
LETTER OF INTENT
[Partnership Name]
[Date]

1. PARTIES
   Corporation: [Name]
   Company: [Name]

2. PURPOSE
   Corporation and Company intend to establish a partnership to [brief description].

3. TERM
   The partnership shall be effective from [DATE] and continue for [12] months,
   with automatic renewal unless either party provides 90-day notice.

4. CONSIDERATION
   Corporation shall provide [type of support]:
   - Amount: [USD Amount]
   - Payment: [Monthly / Upon milestones]
   - Duration: [Timeframe]

   Company shall provide [services/deliverables]:
   - [Specific deliverable 1]
   - [Specific deliverable 2]

5. KEY TERMS (To be finalized in full agreement)
   - Payment schedule: [Details]
   - Deliverables & timeline: [Details]
   - Success metrics: [Details]
   - Confidentiality: [Parameters]
   - IP ownership: [Company owns all work product]
   - Exclusivity: [Non-exclusive partnership]

6. NEXT STEPS
   - Full contract drafted by [DATE]
   - Legal review by [DATE]
   - Execution by [DATE]

7. BINDING NATURE
   This LOI represents non-binding agreement on major terms, except:
   - Confidentiality (binding)
   - Exclusivity of negotiation (binding - neither party negotiates with competitors)
   - Good faith negotiation (binding - both parties commit to finalize deal)

   Full contract execution terminates this LOI.

[Signatures]
```

### LOI Negotiation Tips
- LOI should take 1-2 weeks to finalize (not months)
- Major financial and legal terms should be in LOI
- LOI signals serious intent (both sides invested now)
- LOI should reference that full contract will follow
- Make exclusivity of negotiation binding (they can't shop around)

## 8. Escalation & Conflict Resolution

### When Negotiations Stall

**Step 1: Pause (48 hours)**
- Don't respond immediately to unreasonable requests
- Give yourself time to assess without emotion
- Consult internally (board, advisors)

**Step 2: Diagnose the Issue**
Ask yourself:
- Is this a deal-breaker or negotiable?
- What's driving their position?
- What's my true walk-away point?
- Is there creative alternative solution?

**Step 3: Reframe the Conversation**
Move from position to interest:

**Bad**: "You need to remove the exclusivity clause"
**Better**: "We need flexibility to work with other partners. What are you actually trying to protect? Can we solve that differently?"

**Step 4: Propose Creative Alternatives**

**Scenario: They want exclusivity, you need flexibility**

| Their Request | Your Alternative | Mutual Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusive arrangement | 6-month exclusive window | You prove value, they get exclusivity proof period |
| Exclusive for category | Right of first refusal for 90 days | They have first look, you maintain optionality |
| Permanent exclusive | Exclusive for product/service only | They get what they want, you keep other products free |

**Scenario: They want unlimited access, you need privacy**

| Their Request | Your Alternative | Mutual Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Unlimited system access | Annual audit with 30-day notice | They verify compliance, you protect systems |
| Real-time data dashboard | Quarterly reporting | Easier for you, sufficient for them |
| All customer data | Aggregate anonymized data | Protects privacy, gives insights |

### Escalation Protocol

**If negotiation deadlocks:**

**Level 1: Negotiating Teams**
- Reframe positions as interests
- Propose 2-3 alternative solutions
- Set deadline for resolution (next week)

**Level 2: Relationship Manager → Sponsor**
- Escalate to their sponsorship manager's manager
- Explain impasse neutrally (not blame)
- Request higher-level negotiation

**Level 3: CEO/Executive Level**
- If >$500K deal, consider executive-to-executive call
- Frame as: "We want this partnership to work; let's solve this together"
- Often faster resolution at executive level

**Level 4: Walk Away**
- If stalled 3+ weeks, draft walk-away letter
- Sometimes walking away gets them to move
- Be prepared for walk-away to be final

### Sample Escalation Email

```
Subject: Partnership Negotiation - Request for Executive Alignment

Hi [Name],

We've made great progress on the [Partnership Name]. We're aligned on most terms
and are excited about the partnership potential.

We're currently discussing [specific issue] and want to make sure we find a
solution that works for both sides. I've proposed [alternatives], and I think
we're close.

Would it be possible for me to schedule a brief call with [Executive Name] on
your side and [Our Executive Name] to align on this? I think a 15-minute
conversation might help us get to resolution.

Are you available [Date/Time options]?

Looking forward to moving this forward.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## 9. Post-Signature Steps

### Immediate Actions (First Week)

**[ ] Contract Execution**
- Both parties have signed
- Fully executed agreement scanned and filed
- Each party has original/pdf

**[ ] Notification**
- Announce internally (all teams)
- Brief all staff on deliverables
- Establish internal point person

**[ ] Kickoff Meeting**
- Schedule within 2 weeks
- Define: Project timeline, stakeholders, communication cadence
- Confirm: Deliverables, success metrics, payment schedule

**[ ] Financial Recording**
- Finance records contract
- Payment expected date scheduled
- Invoice template prepared

### Ongoing Management (First Month)

**[ ] Project Planning**
- Create detailed project plan for deliverables
- Assign owners to each deliverable
- Weekly status meetings internally

**[ ] Stakeholder Onboarding**
- Introduce Corporation contact to relevant internal teams
- Share one-page background on Corporation
- Set communication norms (response time, escalation path)

**[ ] Documentation**
- Store contract in central location (Google Drive, Box)
- Create shared folder for partnership materials
- Log key dates and deliverables in calendar

**[ ] Early Communication**
- Schedule "30-day check-in" call
- Confirm things are on track
- Address any early concerns
- Build relationship

### Quarterly Management

**[ ] Business Review Preparation**
- Gather all metrics data
- Prepare 2-page summary (not lengthy report)
- Highlight wins and impact
- Identify any issues needing discussion

**[ ] Business Review Meeting**
- 30-60 minutes with Corporation stakeholders
- Review progress against metrics
- Discuss any challenges or needed adjustments
- Get feedback and ideas for improvement
- Schedule next QBR

**[ ] Renewal Planning (6 months before expiry)**
- Assess partnership success
- Plan renewal offer (usually with 20% expansion)
- Build renewal proposal
- Schedule renewal discussion 6 months before expiry

### End-of-Year/Contract Review

**[ ] Annual Impact Report**
- Quantify results (metrics achieved, value delivered)
- Collect quotes/testimonials from Corporation
- Document lessons learned
- Identify expansion opportunities

**[ ] Renewal Negotiation**
- If partnership successful, proactively propose renewal
- Typically propose 15-20% expansion
- Introduce additional partnerships/revenue opportunities

**[ ] Case Study Development**
- Request permission to publish case study
- Create 1-page case study with metrics
- Use for business development (reference for future partners)

## Negotiation Checklist

### Before You Start
- [ ] Have we researched their decision process thoroughly?
- [ ] Do we have internal alignment on strategy and walk-away point?
- [ ] Have we prepared multiple proposal tiers?
- [ ] Do we have legal review of our standard terms?
- [ ] Have we identified our BATNA?

### During Negotiation
- [ ] Are we asking questions to understand their needs?
- [ ] Are we listening more than talking?
- [ ] Are we finding creative solutions vs. taking positions?
- [ ] Are we documenting everything in writing?
- [ ] Are we moving forward or stalled?

### Before Signature
- [ ] Have we addressed all material terms?
- [ ] Have we minimized deal-breaker clauses?
- [ ] Is our legal team comfortable with terms?
- [ ] Do we understand all payment/deliverable obligations?
- [ ] Is everything in writing (no verbal agreements)?

### After Signature
- [ ] Have we scheduled immediate kickoff?
- [ ] Have we communicated internally?
- [ ] Have we assigned clear owners to deliverables?
- [ ] Have we recorded in finance system?
- [ ] Have we scheduled first check-in?
