# Government Grant Examples: Specific Programs & Data

This guide profiles 12+ specific federal grant programs with real funding amounts, win rates, timelines, and eligibility requirements. Use these as your target list starting point.

---

## SBIR Phase I & II Programs (Most Popular for Tech Startups)

### 1. NSF SBIR Phase I
**Agency**: National Science Foundation
**Award Amount**: $50K - $175K Phase I; $350K - $750K Phase II
**Win Rate**: 10-15% Phase I; 25-35% Phase II
**Timeline**:
- Application Submission: 2x annually (typically January & July)
- Review Period: 4-6 months
- Award Notification: 6-8 months from submission
- Project Duration: 6 months Phase I; 24-30 months Phase II

**Eligibility**:
- For-profit U.S. business (majority American ownership)
- Fewer than 500 employees
- Must be principal researcher on project (not required to be full-time CEO)
- Commercialization focus

**Focus Areas**:
- AI/Machine Learning, software, semiconductors
- Clean energy, sustainability
- Biomedical devices and healthcare IT
- Advanced manufacturing
- Cybersecurity

**Key Success Factors**:
- Strong technical merit (evaluators are PhD-level experts in your field)
- Clear commercialization pathway
- Realistic budget with detailed cost justification
- Letters of support from potential customers (very important)

**Resources**:
- NSF SBIR/STTR Program (sbir.nsf.gov)
- Average 200-300 awards per year across all NSF directorates

### 2. DOD SBIR Phase I & II
**Agency**: U.S. Department of Defense
**Award Amount**: $150K Phase I; $1M+ Phase II (average $2-3M)
**Win Rate**: 12-18% Phase I; 30-40% Phase II
**Timeline**:
- Application Submission: 3 cycles annually (typically Feb, June, Nov)
- Review Period: 5-8 months
- Award Notification: 8-10 months from submission
- Project Duration: 6 months Phase I; 24 months Phase II

**Eligibility**:
- U.S.-incorporated company, majority U.S. owned
- Fewer than 500 employees
- Small business status certification
- DoD security clearance consideration for sensitive topics

**Focus Areas**:
- Advanced materials and manufacturing
- Cybersecurity and data analytics
- Autonomous systems and robotics
- Sensors and detection systems
- Command, control, communications, and intelligence
- Hypersonic propulsion
- Quantum technologies

**Key Success Factors**:
- Alignment with specific DoD strategic priorities (check FY2024/2025 priorities)
- Detailed technical specifications
- Military relevance (how does this protect/enable troops?)
- Strong Phase I feasibility assessment
- Phase II often requires facility tours/demonstrations

**Contracting Officer Recommendation**: Contact your service branch program manager early
**Funding Program**: sbir.defense.gov

**Real Example Win**:
- Company: Autonomous Robotics Startup
- Phase I Award: $150K (feasibility study on robot navigation algorithms)
- Phase II Award: $2.1M (full system development and testing)
- Timeline: Submitted Sept 2022, awarded May 2023 (Phase I); Phase II awarded Jan 2024

### 4. NIH SBIR Phase I & II
**Agency**: National Institutes of Health
**Award Amount**: $225K - $275K Phase I (R43 grants); $750K - $1.5M Phase II (R44 grants)
**Win Rate**: 18-22% Phase I; 28-32% Phase II
**Timeline**:
- Application Submission: 3 cycles annually (Feb 5, June 5, Oct 5 deadlines)
- Review Period: 4-5 months for initial review
- Full review cycle: 6-8 months
- Award Notification: 8 months from submission
- Project Duration: 6-12 months Phase I; 24 months Phase II

**Eligibility**:
- U.S. for-profit company or nonprofit (>75% ownership U.S. citizens)
- Fewer than 500 employees (including affiliates)
- NIH mission relevance

**Focus Areas**:
- Medical devices and diagnostics
- Drug discovery and development
- Digital health and telehealth
- Rare disease treatments
- Cancer research tools and therapies
- Neurotechnology
- Regenerative medicine
- Point-of-care diagnostics

**Key Success Factors**:
- Clear unmet medical need (cite evidence)
- Realistic clinical/commercialization pathway
- Strong preliminary data (in vivo studies for medical devices)
- Experienced team (drug development, regulatory, clinical)
- Letters from potential customers (hospitals, clinicians, industry partners)
- Clear FDA pathway (regulatory strategy)

**Resources**:
- NIH SBIR Program (sbir.nih.gov)
- Average 300+ awards annually
- Topic codes indicate specific focus areas

**Real Example Win**:
- Company: Women's Health Diagnostic Device Company
- Phase I Award: $250K (develop novel diagnostic technology for endometriosis)
- Phase II Award: $1.1M (clinical validation study, FDA clearance pathway)
- Phase IIB Award: $2.5M (multi-site clinical trial)
- Timeline: Phase I submitted Jan 2020, awarded Sept 2020; Phase II awarded Sept 2021
- Outcome: FDA breakthrough device designation granted; Series B funding based on validation

## State & Regional Grant Programs (Faster Turnaround)

### 12. State SBIR/STTR Match Programs
**Agency**: Various (each state-specific)
**Award Amount**: $10K - $100K (matches federal awards)
**Win Rate**: 20-40% (generally higher than federal)
**Timeline**:
- Application Submission: Varies by state (typically biannual or quarterly)
- Review Period: 2-4 months (faster than federal)
- Award Notification: 3-5 months from submission
- Project Duration: 12-24 months typical

**Eligibility**:
- Currently hold or plan to receive federal SBIR/STTR award
- Headquarters in participating state

**States with Strong Programs**:
- Connecticut (CTSBDC SBIR program)
- Massachusetts (MassTech Collaborative)
- California (various regional programs)
- Ohio (Tech Hubs program)
- Colorado (SBDC matching programs)

**Key Advantage**: Can fund gaps in federal awards or accelerate commercialization between Phase I and II awards

**Real Example**:
- Company: Biotech startup awarded NSF Phase I grant
- Federal Award: $150K
- State Match (Massachusetts): $75K (covers gap funding for hiring and equipment)
- Total for 6-month period: $225K

## Competitive Landscape Summary

### Win Rate Expectations by Program Type:
- **SBIR Phase I**: 10-18% (depending on agency)
- **SBIR Phase II**: 25-40% (better odds if Phase I successful)
- **Non-SBIR federal**: 8-15% (more competitive)
- **State/regional programs**: 20-40% (higher success rates)

### Timeline Planning:
- **Fast Track** (3-4 months concept to award): State-only programs
- **Standard** (8-10 months): Most federal programs
- **Long** (12-14 months): Larger federal programs with extensive review

### Budget Recommendations:
- **Phase I**: Use for feasibility, not full development
- **Phase II**: Account for team expansion, manufacturing setup, regulatory work
- **State Matches**: Perfect for hiring, equipment, commercialization activities

## Strategic Application Approach

**Tiered Strategy for Startups**:
1. **Quick wins** (Months 1-3): Apply to state/regional programs (faster funding)
2. **Building momentum** (Months 2-6): Submit first federal SBIR Phase I applications
3. **Scaling up** (Months 4-9): Plan Phase II applications while Phase I is under review
4. **Non-SBIR programs** (Months 6-12): Apply to agency-specific grants as you develop credibility

**Portfolio Approach**:
- Apply to 2-3 SBIR programs simultaneously (different agencies)
- Apply to 1-2 state/regional programs in parallel
- Non-SBIR programs as secondary targets after SBIR wins

**Realistic Timeline**:
- Year 1: Aim for 2-3 Phase I awards ($300K-$500K total)
- Year 2: Convert 1-2 Phase I to Phase II ($1M-$2M) + new Phase I awards
- Year 3+: 2-3 Phase II awards + recurring programs = $2M-$5M+ annually
