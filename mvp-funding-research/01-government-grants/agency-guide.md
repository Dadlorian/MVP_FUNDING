# Federal Agency Guide: Breakdown by Sector

This guide maps federal agencies to industry sectors, helping you identify the right funding sources for your startup. Includes key contacts, focus areas, and application strategies.

---

## Technology & Software

### National Science Foundation (NSF)

**Department**: National Foundation (Independent Agency)
**Budget for Startups**: ~$250M annually in SBIR/STTR
**Contact**: sbir@nsf.gov | sbir.nsf.gov

**Program Portfolio**:
- **SBIR Phase I/II** (Core program): $50K-$175K Phase I; $350K-$750K Phase II
  - Smart systems, AI/ML, software, cybersecurity
  - Computation and data, human-centered computing
  - Advanced Manufacturing Technology

**Key Directorates for Tech**:
- Directorate for Technology, Innovation, and Partnerships (TIP)
- Directorate for Computer and Information Science and Engineering (CISE)
- Directorate for Engineering (ENG)

**Win Rate**: 10-15% Phase I; 25-35% Phase II
**Timeline**: 6-8 months submission to award

**Best For**:
- AI/ML startups
- Cybersecurity companies
- Advanced software with research component
- Hardware with software integration
- Data analytics companies

**Strategy**:
1. Review specific research topics in annual solicitation
2. Identify program officer for your topic area
3. Schedule pre-proposal meeting (highly recommended)
4. Emphasize research novelty AND commercialization
5. Include letters from potential enterprise customers

**Success Pattern**:
- NSF prefers intellectual property and publications
- Phase I is exploration; Phase II is validation
- Strong academic partnerships help
- Clear path to $100M+ market

**Program Officer Network**:
- 30+ SBIR program officers across directorates
- Responsive to pre-proposal inquiries
- Will provide feedback on concept papers

**Recent Trends**:
- Heavy focus on AI/ML and quantum computing (2024-2025)
- Climate tech and sustainable computing
- Workforce development tech
- Trusted AI and responsible AI

- **Quantum Technology**: $200K-$800K

**Focus Areas**:
- Manufacturing systems and standards
- Cybersecurity and data protection
- Quantum information science
- Biophysics and biostandards
- Physics and measurement science

**Win Rate**: 12-18% Phase I; 28-35% Phase II
**Timeline**: 8-10 months submission to award

**Best For**:
- Industrial IoT and smart manufacturing
- Quantum technology companies
- Measurement and sensing systems
- Standards development companies
- Manufacturing software

**Competitive Advantages**:
- NIST highly values standards and measurement
- Industry partnerships strengthen applications
- Manufacturing focus aligns with current administration priorities

**Strategy**:

2. Identify alignment with NIST's measurement mission

**Program Officer Strategy**:
- Contact NIST Manufacturing Division
- Request briefing on current priorities
- Ask about pilot/matching opportunities

### Department of Defense (DoD)

**Department**: Department of Defense
**Budget for Startups**: ~$400M annually in SBIR/STTR
**Contact**: sbir@dodsbt.dfars.mil | sbir.defense.gov

**SBIR Program Distribution by Service**:
- **Air Force**: ~30% of awards (~$120M)
- **Army**: ~25% of awards (~$100M)
- **Navy/Marine Corps**: ~25% of awards (~$100M)
- **OSD (Office of Secretary of Defense)**: ~20% of awards (~$80M)

**Program Portfolio**:
- **SBIR Phase I/II** (Core): $150K Phase I; $1M-$3M average Phase II
- **SBIR Phase IIB**: $2M-$5M (for Phase II winners)
- **Transition to Practice (T2P)**: $2.5M-$10M (technology acceleration)

**Key Focus Areas** (FY2024-2025):
- **AI/Machine Learning**: autonomous vehicles, decision support
- **Advanced Materials**: composites, hypersonic materials
- **Cybersecurity**: defense against emerging threats
- **Autonomy**: robotics, drones, autonomous systems
- **Hypersonic Technology**: propulsion, materials, aerodynamics
- **Quantum Technology**: sensing, computing, communications
- **Biotechnology**: biological defense, medical countermeasures
- **Space Technology**: satellite communications, on-orbit servicing

**Win Rate**: 12-18% Phase I; 30-40% Phase II
**Timeline**: 8-10 months submission to award

**Unique Requirements**:
- U.S. ownership and control essential
- Some topics may require facility tours
- Phase II often includes government facility visits
- Intellectual property rights discussions important
- Export control compliance required

**Best For**:
- Defense contractors expanding to startup operations
- Autonomous systems startups
- Materials science companies
- Cybersecurity startups with military applications
- Space technology companies

**Service Branch Focus Areas**:
- **Air Force**: AI for combat, hypersonics, space operations
- **Army**: Soldier technology, autonomous vehicles, training
- **Navy**: Fleet autonomy, cyber, communications
- **OSD**: Cross-service needs, emerging threats

**Strategy**:
1. Identify which service branch has highest need alignment
2. Review specific FY2024-2025 solicitation topics
3. Request meeting with relevant Service SBIR Manager
4. Understand military problem statement deeply
5. Demonstrate feasibility in 6-month Phase I timeline
6. Include government or military customer letters

**Program Officer Approach**:
- Each service has dedicated SBIR office
- Program officers are technical experts
- Willing to meet with applicants
- Can provide feedback on topic fit

**Red Flags to Avoid**:
- Applying without understanding military need
- Overpromising defense capabilities
- Ignoring ITAR (export control) requirements
- Proposing solutions without military validation
- Weak Phase I feasibility plan

**Advantages of DoD**:
- Largest SBIR budget of any agency
- Highest Phase II award amounts ($1M-$3M average)
- Multiple opportunities per year (3 cycles)
- Path to transition funding ($10M+)
- Strong customer demand for solutions

## Life Sciences & Healthcare

### National Institutes of Health (NIH)

**Department**: Department of Health and Human Services
**Budget for Startups**: ~$200M annually in SBIR/STTR
**Contact**: sbir@nih.gov | sbir.nih.gov

**Program Portfolio**:
- **SBIR Phase I/II** (Core): $225K-$275K Phase I; $750K-$1.5M Phase II
- **SBIR Phase IIB**: $2M-$5M (for Phase II winners)
- **Rapid Response Funding**: $150K-$300K (emergency topics)

**Key Institutes with High Startup Engagement**:
- **National Cancer Institute (NCI)**: ~$25M in SBIR
- **National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI)**: ~$20M in SBIR
- **National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS)**: ~$18M in SBIR
- **National Institute of Diabetes, Digestive, and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK)**: ~$15M in SBIR
- **National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH)**: ~$12M in SBIR

**Focus Areas** (Continuously Updated):
- **Diagnostics**: novel biomarkers, point-of-care tests
- **Therapeutics**: drug discovery, repurposing, novel mechanisms
- **Medical Devices**: minimally invasive, implantable, monitoring
- **Digital Health**: apps, wearables, clinical decision support
- **Rare Diseases**: orphan drug development, unmet needs
- **Regenerative Medicine**: cell therapy, tissue engineering
- **Infectious Disease**: vaccines, diagnostics, antivirals

**Win Rate**: 18-22% Phase I; 28-32% Phase II
**Timeline**: 6-8 months submission to award (fastest of major agencies)

**Best For**:
- Biotech startups developing new therapeutics
- Medical device companies (Class I/II focus initially)
- Digital health and health IT companies
- Diagnostics companies
- Rare disease and precision medicine startups

**FDA/Regulatory Advantage**:
- NIH understands regulatory pathway
- Awards can support FDA submissions
- Clinical validation funded under Phase II

**Strategy**:
1. Identify specific NIH institute/center best aligned with your disease/technology
2. Review recent funded projects (reporter.nih.gov)
3. Attend NIH SBIR webinars and office hours
4. Get letters from clinical collaborators (academic medical centers)
5. Include clear unmet medical need and patient impact
6. Show preliminary data for medical devices/drugs
7. Detail FDA pathway and regulatory strategy

**Program Officer Engagement**:
- Each institute has SBIR coordinator
- Highly responsive to pre-proposal inquiries
- Will provide specific feedback on topic fit
- Often willing to discuss preliminary data

**Advantage**:
- Highest win rates among federal agencies
- Fastest turnaround (6-8 months)
- Multiple submission opportunities annually (3 cycles)
- Clear pathway for follow-on funding

**Recent Hot Topics** (2024-2025):
- AI for drug discovery and diagnostics
- Neurodegeneration therapies
- Cancer immunotherapy approaches
- Cardiovascular risk prediction
- Mental health digital interventions

### FDA/CDRH (Center for Devices and Radiological Health)

**Department**: Department of Health and Human Services
**Budget for Startups**: Limited direct SBIR; focus on regulatory support
**Contact**: CDRH.Submissions@fda.hhs.gov | fda.gov/medical-devices

**Note**: FDA does not have large direct SBIR program; instead:
- Collaborate with NIH SBIR awardees
- Use CDRH programs for guidance
- Seek venture capital for device development
- Consider state health programs

**FDA Office Programs Relevant to Startups**:
- **Breakthrough Devices Program**: Expedited review for novel devices
- **De Novo Pathway**: New device classification support
- **510(k) Premarket Notification**: Substantial equivalence pathway
- **Accelerated Approval**: For critical care devices

**Strategy**:
- Develop MVPs with NIH SBIR Phase I funding
- Engage FDA early (Pre-Submission Meetings)
- Use FDA feedback to inform Phase II development
- Plan regulatory pathway into grant applications

## Energy & Environment

### Department of Energy (DOE)

**Department**: Department of Energy
**Budget for Startups**: ~$180M annually in SBIR/STTR
**Contact**: sbir@doe.gov | sbir.energy.gov

**Program Portfolio**:
- **SBIR Phase I/II**: $175K-$225K Phase I; $750K-$1.5M Phase II
- **Innovation Corps (I-Corps)**: $50K-$100K (commercialization training)
- **Scaling Programs**: $2M-$10M (post-Phase II winners)

**Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy (EERE)** - Largest SBIR Source:
- **Solar Energy**: Photovoltaics, concentrating solar, integration
- **Wind Energy**: Offshore wind, turbine technology, balance of system
- **Geothermal**: Enhanced geothermal, direct use, exploration
- **Bioenergy**: Advanced biofuels, bioeconomy
- **Energy Storage**: Battery technology, thermal storage, hydrogen
- **Grid Modernization**: Smart grid, microgrids, resilience
- **Manufacturing**: Industrial heat, advanced processes
- **Energy Efficiency**: Building systems, appliances, controls

**Office of Nuclear Energy** - Growing SBIR Focus:
- Advanced reactor designs
- Fuel cycle technology
- Nuclear materials
- Safety systems

**Office of Science** - Research-Heavy SBIR:
- Quantum computing and sensing
- Advanced materials
- High-performance computing
- Artificial intelligence for science

**Win Rate**: 8-12% Phase I; 25-30% Phase II
**Timeline**: 7-9 months submission to award

**Best For**:
- Clean energy technology startups
- Energy storage and battery companies
- Smart grid and grid-edge startups
- Industrial efficiency technology
- Building/HVAC technology
- Renewable energy integration
- Energy analytics

**Unique Advantage of DOE**:
- Customer validation from national labs
- Manufacturing partnerships available
- Technology commercialization focus
- Willing to fund later-stage (post-MVP) companies

**Strategy**:
1. Identify specific EERE program area (Solar, Wind, Geothermal, etc.)
2. Review existing funded projects (DOE project database)
3. Join relevant EERE mailing lists for RFP updates
4. Attend EERE Peer Review events to understand evaluation criteria
5. Get letters from utilities, manufacturers, or installation partners
6. Include clear market size and cost reduction roadmap
7. Address manufacturing/scale pathway

**Program Officer Approach**:
- Each EERE program has dedicated SBIR contacts
- Request informational meeting
- Program officers want to see market understanding
- Will discuss technical approach alignment

**Recent Priorities** (2024-2025):
- Grid-scale energy storage
- Heat pump technology
- Industrial decarbonization
- Carbon management and removal
- Sustainable aviation fuels
- Critical mineral processing

**Advantage Over Venture Capital**:
- Non-dilutive funding for technical validation
- Government validation boosts Series A fundraising
- Longer runway for pre-revenue companies
- Manufacturing scale support available

**Department**: National Aeronautics and Space Administration
**Budget for Startups**: ~$200M annually in SBIR/STTR

## Application Timeline Strategy

**Year 1 - Foundation Building**:
- Apply to 3 SBIR programs (primary + 2 secondary)
- Target win: 1 Phase I award ($100K-$200K)
- Timeline: Months 1-12

**Year 2 - Scaling**:
- Convert Phase I to Phase II (1 award: $500K-$1.5M)
- Apply to 3 new Phase I programs
- Target win: 1 new Phase I, 1 Phase II
- Timeline: Ongoing applications

**Year 3+**:
- Multiple Phase II awards ($1M-$3M each)
- Non-SBIR federal programs
- Follow-on transition funding ($5M-$50M+)

---

**Key Insight**: The best agency is the one where your technology solves a real problem they care about. Do your research, build relationships with program officers, and let the fit guide your application strategy.
