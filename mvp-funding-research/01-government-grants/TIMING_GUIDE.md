## SECTION 8: GRANT WRITING CALENDAR & TIMELINE

### 12-Month Grant Pursuit Calendar

**January**
- Week 1-2: Review NSF SBIR Phase I deadlines (March 5, July 2, November 5)
- Week 2-3: Activate SAM.gov and Grants.gov registrations
- Week 3-4: Begin research on DOE SBIR opportunities (LOI deadline January 14 for Phase I Release 2)

**February**
- Week 1-2: Submit DOE Phase I Release 2 full applications (Deadline Feb 26)
- Week 3-4: Plan NSF Phase I Pitch development (March 5 deadline)
- Week 4: Begin concept development for EPA SBIR (opens June)

**March**
- Week 1: Submit NSF SBIR Phase I Pitches (Deadline March 5)
- Week 2-4: Develop grant proposals while waiting for NSF pitch feedback
- Ongoing: Monitor DOD SBIR monthly windows (first Wednesday opens)

**April**
- Week 1-2: Begin USDA Value-Added Producer Grant applications (Deadline April 17)
- Week 2-3: Prepare for state-level grants (many spring deadlines)
- Week 4: Evaluate NSF pitch feedback; prepare Phase I full proposal

**May**
- Week 1-2: Monitor DOE Phase II Release 2 opening (May)
- Week 2: Continue Phase I full proposal development for NSF (July 2 deadline)
- Week 4: ARPA-E program monitoring for new focused solicitations

**June**
- Week 1-2: Final preparations for NSF SBIR Phase I (July 2 deadline)
- Week 2-3: Monitor EPA SBIR opening (typically mid-June)
- Week 3-4: Submit NSF Phase I proposals (by July 2)

**July**
- Week 1-2: Submit NSF Phase I full proposals (by July 2)
- Week 2-4: Begin EPA SBIR application development (deadline typically August)
- Ongoing: Attend grant writing workshops and webinars

**August**
- Week 1-2: Submit EPA SBIR applications (deadline typically August)
- Week 2-3: Begin Phase II grant development for agencies where Phase I awards received
- Week 4: Monitor early announcements for fall programs

**September**
- Week 1-2: Monitor agency announcements for Phase II opportunities
- Week 2-3: Begin NSF Phase I prep for November 5 deadline
- Week 3-4: Prepare for DOE Phase I Release 1 opening (typically September)

**October**
- Week 1: Monitor ARPA-E program announcements
- Week 2-3: Continue NSF Phase I development (November 5 deadline)
- Week 3-4: Prepare Phase II proposals if Phase I awards received

**November**
- Week 1: Submit NSF Phase I proposals (by November 5)
- Week 2-3: Begin planning for next year's DOE cycle
- Week 4: Year-end agency monitoring and planning

**December**
- Week 1-2: Strategic planning for next fiscal year grants
- Week 2-3: Identify emerging SBIR/STTR topics for following year
- Week 3-4: Evaluate current year results and lessons learned

## SECTION 14: TROUBLESHOOTING DEADLINES & EXTENSIONS

### Common Deadline Issues & Solutions

**Problem**: SAM.gov registration taking longer than expected
- **Solution**: Start SAM.gov registration TODAY (process takes 4-8 weeks)
- **Backup**: Many agencies allow conditional deadlines with SAM.gov in progress
- **Contact**: agency-specific program officer 2 weeks before deadline

**Problem**: System outages near deadline
- **Solution**: Submit 24-48 hours before deadline to account for delays
- **Federal Regulation**: Extensions NOT typically granted for system failures
- **Action**: Monitor agency status pages day-of-deadline

**Problem**: Grants.gov validation errors
- **Solution**: Test file uploads 1 week before deadline
- **Support**: Grants.gov has 24/7 helpline: 1-800-518-4726
- **Backup**: Email support; response time 24-48 hours

**Problem**: Resubmission after rejection
- **Solution**: No "rolling rejection periods"—can resubmit in next window
- **Timeline**: Typically 30-60 days needed for major revisions
- **Agency Feedback**: Request reviewer comments from program officer

### Deadline Extension Procedures

**Formal Extensions** (rare, granted only in exceptional circumstances):
- Contact program officer in writing at least 1 week before deadline
- Provide documented emergency reason
- Success rate: <10% unless major system failure

**What Counts as Valid Reason**:
- Major natural disaster
- Federal system failure (not caused by applicant)
- Death of key team member

**What Does NOT Count**:
- Lack of time/preparation
- Personal illness
- Internet connectivity issues on applicant side
- Company emergencies

**Action**: Plan on NO extensions; submit early.

## APPENDIX A: GLOSSARY OF TERMS

- **LOI**: Letter of Intent (required preliminary submission for some agencies)
- **Phase I**: Feasibility/proof-of-concept funding ($150K-$400K; 6-12 months)
- **Phase II**: Full development funding ($750K-$3M; 18-24 months)
- **Phase IIB**: Supplemental Phase II funding for specific activities ($500K typical)
- **SBIR**: Small Business Innovation Research (51%+ US owned, <500 employees)
- **STTR**: Small Business Technology Transfer (SBIR + research institution partnership required)
- **SAM.gov**: System for Award Management; federal business registration (replaces DUNS)
- **UEI**: Unique Entity Identifier (replaces DUNS number in SAM.gov)
- **PAMS**: DOE Portfolio Analysis and Management System (DOE applications)
- **NOFO**: Notice of Funding Opportunity (federal solicitation)
- **RFA**: Request for Applications (federal solicitation)
- **FOA**: Funding Opportunity Announcement (federal solicitation)
- **BAA**: Broad Agency Announcement (rolling solicitation model)
- **FAR**: Federal Acquisition Regulation (compliance rules)
- **ITAR/EAR**: Export control regulations for technology
- **Cost Share**: Applicant contribution (required by some agencies)
- **No-Cost Extension**: Extension of project timeline without additional funding
