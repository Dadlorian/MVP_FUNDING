# Foundation Due Diligence Checklist

## Overview
Effective foundation due diligence protects your organization and maximizes partnership value. This checklist helps you vet foundations before investing significant time in proposals, and verify foundation legitimacy, alignment, and health before accepting grants.

---

## PHASE 1: PRE-RESEARCH VERIFICATION (Before Investing 5+ Hours)

### Legal & Tax Status
- [ ] Foundation listed on IRS Tax Exempt Organizations Search (tax-exempt.org)
- [ ] EIN (Employer Identification Number) verified
- [ ] 501(c)(3) status confirmed (or equivalent for international)
- [ ] No active IRS investigations or compliance issues
- [ ] State registration current (especially for community foundations)
- [ ] Foundation website has legitimate https:// domain (not suspicious)

### Basic Legitimacy
- [ ] Foundation has published 990-PF forms (last 3 years available)
- [ ] Foundation has physical address (not just PO Box)
- [ ] Program officer contact information is current
- [ ] Foundation maintains active website with updated grant guidelines
- [ ] Phone number verification: Can you reach someone at foundation?

### Red Flags - STOP Due Diligence if:
- [ ] Foundation asks for "application fees" (legitimate foundations never charge)
- [ ] Contact person cannot verify foundation legitimacy when called
- [ ] No verifiable 990-PF forms available (scam indicator)
- [ ] Website looks outdated or unprofessional
- [ ] Promises unusually high funding rates or "guaranteed" awards
- [ ] Requires personal bank information (legitimate foundations don't)
- [ ] Communication filled with grammatical errors or poor English
- [ ] Domain name is misspelled version of well-known foundation

## PHASE 2: STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT VERIFICATION (Spend 30-45 Minutes)

### Mission Alignment Deep Dive
- [ ] Your cause area explicitly listed in foundation's current priorities
- [ ] Not just "environment" but specific focus (e.g., "climate change mitigation in agriculture")
- [ ] Foundation's stated cause aligns with your actual work (not stretched interpretation)
- [ ] No conflicting priorities in foundation's values
- [ ] Geographic focus aligns with your service area
  - [ ] If limited to certain states/regions: You operate there
  - [ ] If "national": You have demonstrated multi-state impact or ambition
  - [ ] If international: Foundation actually funds internationally

### Recent Grantee Analysis
- [ ] Download list of recent grants (990-PF Schedule I, last 3 years)
- [ ] Identify 5-10 recent grantees in your cause area
- [ ] Research each organization:
  - [ ] What was their budget when they received grant?
  - [ ] What was the grant size relative to their budget?
  - [ ] What stage were they at (startup, growth, established)?
  - [ ] How similar is their work to yours?
  - [ ] Geographic match: Are they local/regional/national?
- [ ] If no similar grantees found: Foundation may not fund your type of work

### Grant Size Alignment
- [ ] Your funding need matches foundation's typical grant range
  - Foundation's average grant: $_____ to $_____ (from 990-PF)
  - Your ask: $_____
  - Ratio: Does yours fall within typical range?
- [ ] Not asking for edge cases (smallest ever or largest ever)
- [ ] Grant size makes sense for your project scope
  - [ ] Is timeline reasonable for the ask?
  - [ ] Is team size proportional to funding?
  - [ ] Are outcomes ambitious enough to justify amount?

### Application Requirements Screening
- [ ] You meet basic eligibility criteria:
  - [ ] 501(c)(3) status (or required equivalent)
  - [ ] Years operating: _____ (does foundation require minimum?)
  - [ ] Annual budget: _____ (does foundation have minimum?)
  - [ ] Geographic location: _____ (matches foundation requirement?)
- [ ] Application format is standard (LOI + proposal, not highly unusual)
- [ ] Application timeline feasible for your team
- [ ] Required documents are standard (budget, impact metrics, board bios - not exotic)

---

## PHASE 3: FOUNDATION FINANCIAL HEALTH CHECK (30 Minutes)

### Funding Capacity Analysis
**Using Form 990-PF (available at Candid, GuideStar, or Foundation website)**

- [ ] Total assets (Line 1a): $_____ ← Foundation size indicator
- [ ] Annual qualifying distributions (Line 1c): $_____ ← Annual giving capacity
- [ ] Trend analysis - Compare last 3 years:
  - [ ] Assets stable/growing? (Shows healthy foundation)
  - [ ] Payout rate consistent? (Should be ~5% minimum)
  - [ ] No sharp drops in giving? (Red flag: recent significant drops)

### Spending Patterns
- [ ] Foundation is actively giving (not in decline)
  - Last 3 years average annual distributions: $_____
  - Trend: [ ] Stable [ ] Growing [ ] Declining
  - If declining significantly, why? (Economic downturn vs. strategic shift)
- [ ] Grants to organizations appear prudent and reasonable
- [ ] No excessive management fees (administrative expenses <20% of total)
- [ ] Investments appear diversified (not overly concentrated in one sector)

### Risk Indicators - Red Flags
- [ ] Significant decline in assets year-over-year (>10%)
- [ ] Payout rate dropped below 5% (suggests financial stress)
- [ ] Large one-time grants that seem unusual or personal
- [ ] No publicly available plan for asset distribution
- [ ] Multiple years of decreased giving
- [ ] Pending litigation or regulatory action visible in filing

## PHASE 4: RELATIONSHIP & POSITIONING ASSESSMENT (30 Minutes)

### Foundation Leadership Research
- [ ] Program officer identified: Name: _______, Title: _______
- [ ] Program officer background researched:
  - [ ] LinkedIn profile reviewed (career trajectory matches role)
  - [ ] Previous positions in relevant field?
  - [ ] Network overlap - any connections you share?
  - [ ] Any publications/speaking on their cause area?
- [ ] Executive director background reviewed
- [ ] Board composition reviewed on foundation website
- [ ] Founder/donor intent understood (informs long-term priorities)

### Strategic Positioning
- [ ] Foundation has multi-year strategic plan (demonstrates thoughtful direction)
- [ ] Foundation attended or speaking at relevant conferences
- [ ] Foundation publishes impact reports (shows commitment to transparency)
- [ ] Foundation leadership on social media (signals active engagement)
- [ ] Foundation convenes grantees (opportunity to build relationships)

### Relationship Potential Assessment
- [ ] Warm connection possible through your network (LinkedIn review)
- [ ] Foundation receptive to exploratory conversations (guidelines state this or others confirm)
- [ ] Foundation willing to provide feedback on proposals (investigate with peer grantees)
- [ ] Program officer responsive to email/phone (test with initial inquiry)
- [ ] Foundation has reasonable processing timeline
  - Typical response time to LOI: _____ weeks
  - Typical response time to proposal: _____ weeks

## PHASE 5: GRANT AGREEMENT & RESTRICTIONS REVIEW (For Shortlisted Foundations)

### Before Submitting Full Proposal, Confirm:

**Scope Flexibility**
- [ ] Grant can support core operational costs (not project-restricted unless you're fine with that)
- [ ] Foundation allows use of funds for staff salaries
- [ ] Foundation allows consultant/contractor costs
- [ ] Foundation allows overhead/administrative allocation
- [ ] Foundation allows use of funds for technology/tools
- [ ] Foundation allows travel/convening costs
- [ ] Foundation allows subgrants to partners (if relevant)

**Financial Reporting**
- [ ] Financial reports required: [ ] Quarterly [ ] Annual [ ] Upon completion
- [ ] Foundation accepts standard nonprofit accounting (not forensic audits)
- [ ] Flexibility in year-end accounting (if calendar vs fiscal year mismatch)
- [ ] Indirect cost rate: _____ % acceptable to foundation?

**Impact Reporting & Accountability**
- [ ] Impact metrics are reasonable and achievable
- [ ] Reporting format standard (narrative + data, not exotic framework)
- [ ] Reporting frequency sustainable for your team
- [ ] Foundation open to discussing metrics (not fixed, non-negotiable)
- [ ] No unusual evaluation requirements

**Restrictions & IP Issues**
- [ ] Foundation claims any intellectual property rights? [ ] Yes [ ] No
  - If yes: Can you accept those terms? ___________
- [ ] Restrictions on who you can partner with? [ ] Yes [ ] No
  - If yes: Are they acceptable? ___________
- [ ] Geographic restrictions on fund use? [ ] Yes [ ] No
- [ ] Restrictions on future funding from competing sources? [ ] Yes [ ] No
- [ ] Can you publicly acknowledge the grant? [ ] Yes [ ] No

**Compliance Burden**
- [ ] Grant agreement is reasonable length (5-10 pages standard)
- [ ] Insurance requirements are standard (general liability)
- [ ] Restrictions on program modification if context changes
- [ ] No unusual transparency/audit requirements beyond standard nonprofit

## PHASE 6: PEER VALIDATION (Talk to Actual Grantees)

### Find and Interview Recent Grantees
**Objective: Learn real experience, not what foundation says**

Identify from 990-PF: Recent grantees to contact: _________, _________, _________

**Questions for Grantee Interviews**

- How would you characterize the relationship with [Foundation]?
  - [ ] Collaborative and supportive
  - [ ] Transactional
  - [ ] Demanding/onerous
  - [ ] Hands-off

- Did the funding process match the stated timeline?
  - [ ] Faster than expected
  - [ ] As stated
  - [ ] Significantly delayed
  - Delay reason: _________________

- Was there flexibility during the grant period?
  - [ ] Very flexible (allowed strategic pivots)
  - [ ] Somewhat flexible (minor adjustments OK)
  - [ ] Rigid (must follow plan exactly)

- Reporting burden - was it reasonable?
  - Hours per month: _____
  - Was foundation responsive to reports?

- Would you apply again?
  - [ ] Yes, immediately
  - [ ] Maybe, with conditions
  - [ ] No, better options exist
  - Reason: _________________

- Biggest surprise (positive or negative): _________________

- One thing to know before applying: _________________

| **Financial Health** | ___/5 | Is foundation stable and actively giving? |
| **Flexibility** | ___/5 | How accommodating are their restrictions? |

| **Processing Speed** | ___/5 | How quickly do they make decisions? |
| **Geographic Fit** | ___/5 | Do you match their geographic focus? |

### Decision Thresholds

- **Score 38-40** (Average 4.75-5.0): Tier 1 Priority
  - Action: Fast-track this foundation, prioritize warm intro
  - When to apply: Immediately

- **Score 33-37** (Average 4.1-4.6): Tier 2 Priority
  - Action: Strong candidate for LOI
  - When to apply: After Tier 1 submissions

- **Score 28-32** (Average 3.5-4.0): Tier 3 - Selective
  - Action: Apply only if customization is easy
  - When to apply: In second/third waves

- **Score Below 28**: Reconsider Priority
  - Action: Only pursue if desperate for funding
  - Better options: Keep researching

## APPENDIX: Common Due Diligence Red Flags

### STOP - Don't Pursue These Foundations:
- No 990-PF forms available (not transparent)
- Assets declining more than 10% annually
- No grants made in past 2 years (might be defunct)
- Program officer email bounces/no response to initial contact after 2 weeks
- Grantees report "surprising restrictions" not in guidelines
- Foundation requires personal guarantees or unusual commitments
- Peer grantees report poor communication or moving goalposts
- Foundation making grants far outside stated mission area
- No way to verify legitimacy (can't reach anyone)
- Reputation concerns found on Foundation Center or peer networks

### CAUTION - Investigate Further:
- Recently established foundation (less than 5 years old) - check stability
- Very small foundation (under $1M assets) - verify active status
- No website or minimal online presence - may be dormant
- Significant leadership transitions - program may be in flux
- Major shift in grant categories recently - unclear direction
- Very few grants to similar organizations - niche could work but verify fit
- Long processing times (6+ months) - ensure timeline works
- Complex application process - requires significant staff time
- All grants to same few organizations - may favor insiders

---

## QUICK REFERENCE: What to Do If You Find Red Flags

1. **Legitimacy concerns**: Contact your state's Attorney General Charities Division or IRS before proceeding
2. **Financial health concerns**: Request clarification from foundation directly before assuming problems
3. **Restriction concerns**: Consult with a grants attorney if terms are unusual ($1-2K well spent)
4. **Peer feedback concerns**: Talk to at least 2-3 other grantees before deciding
5. **Relationship concerns**: Request an exploratory call to assess working relationship before investing

Remember: Due diligence takes 2-4 hours upfront but saves weeks of wasted effort on poor-fit foundations. The best grants are partnerships with foundations where mutual trust and shared vision exist from the start.

---

**Final Tip**: A foundation that scores well on due diligence but where you lack a warm introduction may still be worth pursuing. A foundation that scores moderately but where you have strong network access may be higher probability. Balance both factors in your strategy.
