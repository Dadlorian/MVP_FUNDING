# Foundation Grant Budget Templates

## Overview
Foundation grants require detailed, justified budgets that demonstrate fiscal responsibility and program impact per dollar. This guide provides template structures, budget narratives, and real examples for different grant sizes and project types.

**Key Principle**: Budgets should tell a story about priorities. What you fund reveals what you value.

---

## SECTION 2: BUDGET NARRATIVE TEMPLATES

### Short Budget Narrative (for LOI or small grants)

**Format: 1 paragraph or ½ page**

```
The total project budget of $[Amount] funds [Project Name] over [timeframe] and
is allocated as follows: [X]% to personnel, [Y]% to direct program costs, and
[Z]% to evaluation. Our staff team of [#] includes [specific roles] who will
[specific activities]. Direct program investments will [specific deliverable]
for [#] beneficiaries. We have secured [Amount] from [Funder] and am seeking
[Amount] from [Foundation] to complete funding. This allocation demonstrates our
commitment to [foundation's priority], with particular investment in
[specific area foundation values].
```

---

### Medium Budget Narrative (for full proposals under $250K)

**Format: 1-2 pages**

**I. Budget Overview & Priorities**

This section should:
- State total budget amount and project period
- Explain major allocation categories and percentages
- Describe how budget allocation aligns with project goals
- Note any special budget features (cost-sharing, efficiency measures)

*Example*:
```
The $[Amount] budget for [Project] prioritizes direct participant impact
(55%) while ensuring programmatic rigor through evaluation (6%) and sustainable
operations (15%). This allocation reflects our theory of change: that direct
investment in [specific intervention] yields greatest impact. We have secured
[Amount] in matching funds from [sources], leveraging [Foundation]'s [Amount]
to achieve [specific outcome].
```

**II. Personnel Justification**

This section should:
- Explain team structure and why it's needed
- Justify FTE levels for each position
- Connect staffing to project activities
- Address any gaps or limitations

*Example*:
```
The program is led by our Director of [Area] (0.5 FTE), who brings [relevant
experience/credentials]. Two Program Coordinators (1.0 FTE each) will manage
[specific activities] in our [location] sites. This team structure is right-sized
to serve [#] participants while maintaining quality [specific quality measures].
Our existing Finance Director (0.25 FTE) will manage grant reporting, minimizing
overhead costs.
```

**III. Direct Program Costs Justification**

This section should:
- Break down major cost categories
- Explain why each expense is necessary
- Provide unit costs or rationales (e.g., "$X per participant")
- Note any cost-saving measures

*Example*:
```
Direct program investments of $[Amount] include participant support ($[X] per
participant for [specific benefit]), materials ($[Amount] for [specific items]),
and space rental ($[Amount]/month for [specific facilities]). These costs
reflect market rates in [geography] and align with sector benchmarks. We have
negotiated [X]% discounts with [provider] and will leverage pro-bono [specific
contribution] from [partner].
```

**IV. Overhead & Administrative Costs**

This section should:
- Explain overhead allocation methodology
- Justify percentage as reasonable and necessary
- Show how finance/HR/IT support program quality
- Compare to nonprofit sector standards

*Example*:
```
We allocate 12% to overhead, lower than nonprofit sector average of 15-20%.
This allocation funds essential infrastructure: financial management ensuring
grant compliance, HR systems supporting staff quality, and IT systems enabling
data security and evaluation rigor. Our [specific efficiency measure] further
reduces overhead while maintaining organizational health.
```

**V. Evaluation & Learning**

This section should:
- Explain evaluation methodology and costs
- Connect evaluation to foundation's interests
- Show how learning will be shared
- Justify external evaluation (if included)

*Example*:
```
We invest $[Amount] (6% of budget) in rigorous evaluation aligned with
[Foundation]'s commitment to evidence-based practice. This includes [specific
measures] administered [frequency] to track progress toward [outcome]. An
external evaluator will conduct [specific evaluation activity], ensuring
independent rigor. Annual findings will be shared via [dissemination method]
to advance field knowledge about [specific focus area].
```

**VI. Sustainability & Long-Term Funding**

This section should:
- Explain ongoing funding beyond grant period
- Identify diversified funding sources
- Show plan to transition to scale/sustainability
- Address any sunsets or transitions

*Example*:
```
This [Foundation] grant launches [Project], which we will sustain through
[specific revenue strategy]: [Funder 1], [Funder 2], and earned revenue from
[specific model]. We project [Project] to be [fully self-sustaining / supported
by X funders] by Year [X]. Our three-year plan demonstrates clear path to
sustainability without ongoing foundation support.
```

---

### Detailed Budget Narrative (for large grants $250K+)

**Format: 3-5 pages**

Structure similar to medium narrative but with:
- More detailed justification for each cost category
- Multi-year narrative explaining year-to-year changes
- Impact per dollar analysis (cost per beneficiary, cost per outcome achieved)
- Detailed staffing plan addressing expertise, scalability, retention
- Risk analysis and contingency planning
- Detailed evaluation and learning strategy

## SECTION 4: BUDGET PRESENTATION & BEST PRACTICES

### Excel/Spreadsheet Format
Most foundations accept budgets in this format:

```
LINE ITEM                    AMOUNT          CALCULATION               %
Personnel
  Salary 1                   $[X]            [# FTE] × [Annual Salary] [X%]
  Salary 2                   $[X]
  Fringe (% of salaries)     $[X]            [%] × Salaries            [X%]
SUBTOTAL PERSONNEL           $[X]                                      [X%]

Direct Costs
  [Category 1]               $[X]            [Quantity] × [Unit Cost]  [X%]
  [Category 2]               $[X]
SUBTOTAL DIRECT COSTS        $[X]                                      [X%]

Overhead                      $[X]            [Methodology]             [X%]

Evaluation                    $[X]                                      [X%]

TOTAL PROJECT BUDGET         $[X]                                      100%

Amount Requested             $[X]
Other Funding:
  [Funder 1]                 $[X]
TOTAL FUNDING               $[X]
```

### Key Principles for Compelling Budgets

1. **Transparency**: Every line item should be explainable
2. **Alignment**: Budget priorities match project goals
3. **Justification**: Budget narrative explains the "why" not just "what"
4. **Reasonableness**: Costs align with industry standards
5. **Accountability**: Clear connection between spending and outcomes
6. **Sustainability**: Plan for continued funding/impact after grant ends
7. **Flexibility**: Note where you have some discretion vs. fixed costs

### Red Flags That Turn Off Foundations

- Excessive overhead (>25% without justification)
- Personnel costs consuming >80% of budget
- Evaluation underfunded (<3% for serious programs)
- Unexplained cost increases year-to-year
- Misalignment between budget and narrative
- Unclear allocation methodology
- No contingency or sustainability plan
- Costs that seem inflated or uncompetitive

## SECTION 5: BUDGET ANALYSIS & METRICS

### Cost Per Beneficiary Analysis
Use this framework to evaluate your budget efficiency:

```
Total Program Cost:         $[Amount]
Number of Beneficiaries:    [#]
Cost Per Beneficiary:       $[Amount ÷ #] = $[Cost/person]

Benchmark comparison:
  Sector average:           $[X]
  Similar programs:         $[X]
  Our program:              $[Cost/person]

Analysis:
[ ] Lower than benchmark (show why we're efficient)
[ ] At benchmark (show why costs are necessary)
[ ] Higher than benchmark (explain with quality/depth/geography rationale)
```

### Cost Per Outcome Analysis
```
Program Cost:               $[Amount]
Expected # Outcome 1:       [#]
Cost Per Outcome 1:         $[Amount ÷ #] = $[X]

Program Cost:               $[Amount]
Expected # Outcome 2:       [#]
Cost Per Outcome 2:         $[Amount ÷ #] = $[X]
```

**Use this to**: Show your program is cost-effective and demonstrate accountability
to foundation investments.

## SECTION 7: BUDGET TROUBLESHOOTING

### Common Foundation Concerns & Solutions

**"Your overhead is too high"**
- Solution: Analyze and justify every overhead line item
- Provide benchmark data from sector
- Consider proposing pilot with lower overhead for Year 1
- Explain how adequate operations enable program quality

**"Salaries seem inflated"**
- Solution: Provide market data for positions in your geography
- Reference nonprofit salary survey (Nonprofit HR, PayScale)
- Show salary ranges and step increases justified by experience
- Consider geographic cost-of-living adjustments

**"Where's the evaluation?"**
- Solution: Move evaluation from optional to essential line item
- Propose meaningful metrics tied to foundation's interests
- Include external evaluator if budget allows
- Show learning will be shared with field

**"The budget doesn't align with your narrative"**
- Solution: Check that stated priorities match budget allocation
- Revise either narrative or budget for consistency
- Ensure numbers in narrative match budget detail
- Have someone else review for accuracy

**"No contingency plan"**
- Solution: Include brief section on contingency planning
- Identify fixed vs. flexible costs
- Propose how you'd respond to [specific risk]
- Show financial reserves or sustainability plan

## SECTION 8: BUDGET SUBMISSION CHECKLIST

Before submitting your budget to any foundation:

- [ ] Budget total matches amount requested in proposal narrative
- [ ] All percentages add to 100%
- [ ] All calculations verified (no math errors)
- [ ] Budget period matches project period stated in proposal
- [ ] Narrative explains all major line items
- [ ] Salaries/costs reasonable for geography/sector
- [ ] Overhead percentage clearly calculated and justified
- [ ] Evaluation adequately funded (minimum 3-5%)
- [ ] Comparison/benchmarking provided
- [ ] Multi-year budgets show year-to-year growth rationale
- [ ] Sustainability plan addresses funding after grant ends
- [ ] Budget format matches foundation requirements exactly
- [ ] Totals tie to funding request
- [ ] No unexplained gaps or categories
- [ ] Professional formatting (clean, easy to read)
- [ ] Reviewed by Finance Director or external bookkeeper
- [ ] Aligned with your actual organizational capacity to execute

---

**Bottom Line**: A compelling budget does three things: (1) Demonstrates you can
execute your project, (2) Shows responsible financial stewardship, (3) Connects
spending directly to impact. Spend as much time on your budget narrative as your
program narrative—foundations read them together.
