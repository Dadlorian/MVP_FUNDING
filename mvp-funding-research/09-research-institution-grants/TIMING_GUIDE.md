## Part 1: Federal Grant Cycles and Deadlines

### National Institutes of Health (NIH)

**Website**: https://grants.nih.gov | **SBIR Portal**: https://sbir.nih.gov

#### NIH SBIR Phase I & II Deadlines (2025-2026)

**Standing Submission Deadlines**:
- **Deadline 1**: January 10, 2025 (expires April 10, 2025)
- **Deadline 2**: May 16, 2025 (expires August 16, 2025)
- **Deadline 3**: September 12, 2025 (expires December 12, 2025)
- **Deadline 1 (2026)**: January 9, 2026 (expires April 9, 2026)
- **Deadline 2 (2026)**: May 15, 2026 (expires August 15, 2026)
- **Deadline 3 (2026)**: September 11, 2026 (expires December 11, 2026)

**Application Timeline**:
- T-16 weeks: Identify relevant NIH Institute/Center (IC) and program officer
- T-12 weeks: Draft research proposal and budget narrative
- T-8 weeks: Internal review, incorporate feedback
- T-4 weeks: Scientific merit review preparation
- T-2 weeks: Final submission and e-signature
- T-0: Official submission deadline (5:00 PM ET)
- T+30 days: NIH acknowledges receipt and assigns study section
- T+60-90 days: Scientific review meeting (typically 2-3 months post-submission)
- T+180-210 days: Initial review group (IRG) summary and program officer consultation
- T+240-270 days: Program announcement of award (if successful)

**Lead Time Required**: 16-18 weeks before deadline

#### NIH STTR Phase I & II Deadlines (2025-2026)

**Standing Submission Deadlines**:
- **Deadline 1**: January 10, 2025
- **Deadline 2**: May 16, 2025
- **Deadline 3**: September 12, 2025

**Key Requirement**: Research institution partner must be committed (typically a university) with formal collaboration agreement signed minimum 60 days before submission

**Funding Bands for STTR**:
- **Phase I**: $225,000–$325,000 (1 year)
- **Phase II**: $1.5M–$2M (2 years)
- **Phase IIB** (Bridge): Up to $400K (6-12 months)

**Partner Institution Timeline**:
- T-90 days: Identify university/research partner with relevant capabilities
- T-60 days: Initiate collaboration agreement (MTA, CRADA, or sponsored research agreement)
- T-45 days: Finalize letter of support from institution
- T-30 days: Institutional approval (IRB/IACUC if needed)
- T-14 days: Final budget reconciliation with institution

**Website**: https://www.nsf.gov | **SBIR Portal**: https://www.nsf.gov/sbir

#### NSF SBIR Phase I & II Deadlines (2025-2026)

**Standing Submission Deadlines** (Program Solicitation 25-523):
- **Deadline 1**: January 15, 2025, 5:00 PM ET
- **Deadline 2**: May 15, 2025, 5:00 PM ET
- **Deadline 3**: September 15, 2025, 5:00 PM ET
- **Deadline 1 (2026)**: January 14, 2026, 5:00 PM ET
- **Deadline 2 (2026)**: May 20, 2026, 5:00 PM ET
- **Deadline 3 (2026)**: September 16, 2026, 5:00 PM ET

**Funding by Phase**:
- **Phase I**: $175,000–$225,000 (6-month project)
- **Phase II**: $750,000–$1.5M (2-year project)
- **Phase IIB** (Growth Phase): Up to $2M (1-year bridge)

**Application Timeline**:
- T-14 weeks: Register project in NSF system (FastLane/Research.gov)
- T-12 weeks: Identify NSF directorate (Engineering, BIO, CISE, etc.)
- T-10 weeks: Draft proposal with commercialization plan
- T-6 weeks: Internal PI and authorized organizational representative review
- T-4 weeks: Budget reconciliation and cumulative salary cap verification
- T-2 weeks: Submit to NSF FastLane/Research.gov
- T+30 days: NSF confirms receipt and administrative review
- T+90-120 days: Panel review (remote or in-person at NSF Arlington, VA)
- T+150-180 days: Program officer notification of decision
- T+210-240 days: Award issued (if funded)

**Lead Time Required**: 14-16 weeks before deadline

#### NSF CAREER Awards (Faculty Research)

**Website**: https://www.nsf.gov/funding/pgm_summ.jsp?pgrm_id=5734

**Typical Deadlines** (varies by directorate):
- **Engineering Directorate**: July-August (typically 1st Wednesday in August)
- **Computer & Information Science**: Late August-September
- **Biological Sciences**: September-October
- **Exact 2025 dates**: Check NSF website in Q2 2025 (https://www.nsf.gov/cgi-bin/WebObjects/nsf.woa)

**Funding**: $75,000–$200,000 per year (5 years typical)

**Eligibility**: Faculty members must be within 10 years of PhD

**Lead Time**: 12-16 weeks before deadline

#### NSF STTR Phase I & II (University-Based Research)

**Website**: https://www.nsf.gov/sbir

**Standing Submission Deadlines**:
- Same as SBIR (Jan 15, May 15, Sep 15)

**Key Requirement**: Small business (fewer than 500 employees) must partner with university or nonprofit research institution for minimum 30% research effort

**Funding**:
- **Phase I**: $225,000–$325,000 (7-month project)
- **Phase II**: $1.5M–$2M (2-year project)

## Part 5: Strategic Timing Roadmap for Applications

### 18-Month Pre-Award Application Strategy

**Timeline for Maximum Funding Success** (Example: Target Phase I award by Q2 2026)

#### Phase -6 (6 months before submission deadline): Infrastructure & Partnership Building

**Tasks**:
- Identify 3-5 target federal programs (NIH SBIR, NSF SBIR, DoE SBIR, etc.)
- Begin university partnership outreach (contact TTO or faculty directly)
- Attend relevant conferences (CES, TechCrunch, SXSW for visibility)
- Join SBIR/STTR office mailing lists
- Register SAM.gov account and obtain DUNS number

**Timing Window**:
- If targeting January 2026 deadline, start June 2025
- Conferences: September-October 2025 (position for Q1 2026 deadlines)

#### Phase -4 (4 months before deadline): Institutional Relationships

**Tasks**:
- Secure university collaboration agreement (CRADA, MTA, or sponsored research agreement)
- Identify faculty adviser or research partner
- Discuss research fit with program officers at target agencies
- Review prior Phase I awards in your tech area (available at https://sbir.nih.gov/sbirdb and https://www.sbir.gov/sbirdb)
- Develop preliminary research roadmap

**Key Contacts**:
- **NIH SBIR**: program officers at https://sbir.nih.gov/contacts
- **NSF SBIR**: https://www.nsf.gov/sbir/contacts
- **DoE SBIR**: https://www.energy.gov/sbir/contacts

#### Phase -3 (3 months before deadline): Proposal Development

**Tasks**:
- Write Phase I Specific Aims (2-3 pages maximum)
- Draft technical approach (10-12 pages)
- Develop commercialization/business plan (2-3 pages)
- Collect preliminary data (lab results, user validation, competitive analysis)
- Prepare budget and cost narrative

**Peer Review Strategy**:
- Internal: 4-week internal review cycle with business advisor or mentor
- External: 2-week review by external scientist not involved in proposal

#### Phase -6 to Phase -2 (6-2 weeks before deadline): Final Submission

**Weekly Checklist**:
- **Week 6**: All sections complete; begin institutional approvals
- **Week 5**: Budget certified by institution; faculty commit letters finalized
- **Week 4**: Proposal submitted to internal SME for final technical review
- **Week 3**: Incorporate feedback; institutional review board (IRB) clearance if human subjects involved
- **Week 2**: Final compliance review (ITAR, export controls if applicable); register in FastLane/Research.gov
- **Week 1**: Final proofreading; authorize signatures from institutional representatives
- **Final day**: Submit 24 hours before deadline to avoid server issues

**Submission Platform Readiness**:
- **NIH**: FastLane (legacy) or Research.gov (newer, preferred)
- **NSF**: Research.gov
- **DoE**: eXCHANGE system (https://eere-exchange.energy.gov)
- **DoD**: DoD SBIR portal (https://sbir.defensebusiness.org)
- **USDA**: Grants.gov (https://www.grants.gov)

#### Phase 0 (After submission): Waiting & Networking

**Expected Timeline**:
- T+30 days: Administrative review completed; assigned to study section/panel
- T+60-90 days: Scientific review meeting (for NIH/NSF; schedule shown on agency website)
- T+150-180 days: Initial review group (IRG) summary and program officer consultation
- T+210-240 days: Program announcement; funding notification (if successful)

**Actions During Waiting Period**:
- Attend industry conferences (March-May 2026 for Q1/Q2 submissions)
- Network with program officers (optional follow-up emails; allowed 60 days after panel review)
- Prepare Phase II proposal (if Phase I is being reviewed favorably)
- Identify scaling partnerships or pilot opportunities

#### Phase +6-12 (6-12 months after initial award): Phase II Preparation

**If Phase I funded** (expect award 6-9 months after submission):
- Phase I runs 6-12 months
- Phase II applications typically due 6 months into Phase I execution
- Parallel track: Apply for Phase II while completing Phase I milestones

## Part 9: Critical Success Factors & Timing Hacks

### Common Timing Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Underestimating University Approval Timelines**
   - Grant agreements require: Legal review (2-3 weeks), department sign-off (1 week), institutional approval (1 week)
   - **Action**: Start 6-8 weeks before federal deadline, not 2 weeks

2. **Missing Agency Program Officer Consultation Window**
   - Program officers review proposals only during designated windows (typically 6-8 weeks before deadline)
   - **Action**: Email program officer 12 weeks before deadline; expect 1-2 week response time

3. **Academic Calendar Blackout Periods**
   - Faculty unavailable December 15-January 15, March 15-April 15 (spring break/exam period), June-August (gaps)
   - **Action**: Plan partnership discussions for January-March and August-September

4. **Grants.gov/FastLane Server Overload**
   - System crashes common on deadline days (5:00 PM ET deadline dates)
   - **Action**: Submit 24-48 hours early; have backup submission method (hand-deliver with agency if necessary)

5. **Missing Preliminary Data Collection Windows**
   - Faculty summer research is prime time to generate preliminary data (May-August)
   - **Action**: Plan collaborative research projects 3-4 months before grant submission

### Timing Hacks for Competitive Advantage

**Hack #1: Pre-Proposal Program Officer Meeting**
- Contact program officer 14 weeks before deadline
- Request 30-minute call to discuss research fit
- Use feedback to tailor proposal to agency priorities
- Official guidance: https://sbir.nih.gov/about/pre-submission-conferences

**Hack #2: Academic Year Sponsorship Research**
- Run 2-month "academic partnership fellowship" July-August (paid) to formalize STTR research
- Generates preliminary data + formalized collaboration agreement
- Cost: $10K–$20K; ROI: $500K+ Phase I award

**Hack #3: Multi-Agency Sequential Submissions**
- **July-August**: Submit to DOE SBIR (June 15 deadline)
- **September-October**: Submit to NSF/NIH SBIR (Sept deadline)
- **January-February**: Submit to DoD SBIR
- Stagger submissions by 4-6 weeks to avoid proposal fatigue and spread workload

**Hack #4: Conference Visibility Before Applications**
- Present research at 1-2 conferences before submitting SBIR (builds credibility with reviewers)
- Apply to: SXSW (innovation track), TechCrunch Disrupt, Web Summit (startup competition)
- Cost: $2K–$5K registration; benefit: Media coverage + investor visibility + research credibility

**Hack #5: Institutional Support Letter Timing**
- Request support letters from university partner 4 weeks before deadline (not 1 week)
- Provide: Grant summary, expected deliverables, commitment amount
- This allows department budget review and formal institutional commitment

**Hack #6: Tracking Agency Panel Dates**
- NIH posts study section meeting dates 90 days in advance
- NSF posts reviewer panel dates on website
- Mark calendar: Program officer usually notifies winners 2-3 weeks after panel review
- Check weekly: https://sbir.nih.gov/recent-awards and https://www.nsf.gov/cgi-bin/WebObjects/nsf.woa
