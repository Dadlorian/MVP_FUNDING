## Case Study 1: AI-Driven Disease Diagnostics Platform (EU Horizon Europe)

### Project Overview
**Project Title**: DeepDiagnose - AI for Early Cancer Detection
**Funding Program**: Horizon Europe, Cluster 4 (Digital, Industry & Space)
**Lead Organization**: European Research Institute (Vienna, Austria)
**Consortium**: 6 partners across 5 countries
**Total Budget**: €2,850,000
**EU Funding**: €2,422,500 (85%)
**Project Duration**: 48 months (2021-2025)

### Key Participating Organizations
1. **Lead**: European Research Institute (Austria) - AI & ML expertise
2. **Partner 1**: Advanced Medical Imaging Lab (UK) - Clinical validation
3. **Partner 2**: Tech Innovation Hub (Germany) - Industrial deployment
4. **Partner 3**: Hospital Network (France) - Real-world testing
5. **Partner 4**: University (Spain) - Regulatory & Ethics
6. **Partner 5**: SME Medical Device (Poland) - Commercialization

### Project Goals
- Develop AI model achieving 95%+ diagnostic accuracy
- Validate across 5,000+ patient cases
- Enable market entry in 3+ EU countries
- Create regulatory pathway for commercial adoption
- Establish open-access benchmark dataset

### Budget Breakdown
```
Personnel (€1,280,000) - 45% of budget
- Senior AI Researchers: 6 FTE @ €85,000/year
- Software Engineers: 4 FTE @ €65,000/year
- Clinical Staff: 3 FTE @ €45,000/year
- Project Manager: 1 FTE @ €55,000/year
- Administrative Support: 1.5 FTE @ €35,000/year

Equipment (€620,000) - 22%
- GPU Computing Clusters: €280,000 (distributed across consortium)
- Medical Imaging Equipment: €220,000
- Data Management Infrastructure: €120,000

Subcontracting (€510,000) - 18%
- Clinical Trial Management: €200,000
- Regulatory Consultation: €140,000
- Data Annotation Services: €170,000

Travel & Dissemination (€280,000) - 10%
- Partnership meetings: €100,000
- Conference presentations: €80,000
- Stakeholder engagement: €60,000
- Publications & visibility: €40,000

Direct Costs (€160,000) - 5%
- Software licenses: €60,000
- Consumables: €40,000
- Open access publication: €35,000
- Insurance & compliance: €25,000

Indirect Costs (€712,500) - Calculated as 25% of direct costs
```

### Timeline & Milestones
- **Month 0-6**: Consortium setup, ethical approval, data access agreements
- **Month 6-18**: AI model development, initial training on 2,000 cases
- **Month 18-30**: Clinical validation, accuracy testing, iterative improvement
- **Month 30-42**: Regulatory engagement, market readiness activities
- **Month 42-48**: Commercial pathway development, final dissemination

### Key Success Factors
1. **Strong Consortium**: Academic + hospital + industry + SME combination
2. **Clear Regulatory Strategy**: Engaged with regulators early (CE Mark pathway)
3. **Excellent Project Management**: Monthly steering committees, detailed Gantt charts
4. **Open Science Approach**: Commitment to open data & open-access publication
5. **User-Centric Design**: End-user involvement from Month 1

### Outcomes & Impact
- **Publications**: 18 peer-reviewed papers (9 in top-tier journals)
- **Dataset**: Released benchmark dataset with 5,000+ annotated medical images
- **Patent**: Filed 2 patents on AI diagnostic methodology
- **Commercialization**: Spin-off company founded, €5M Series A raised
- **Adoption**: Integrated into 12 hospital systems across EU
- **Training**: Trained 150+ clinicians on AI tool use

### Key Lessons Learned
✓ **Regulatory engagement pays off**: Early consultation with EMA accelerated approval
✓ **Clinical validation essential**: Real-world testing revealed unexpected edge cases
✓ **Open science builds trust**: Transparency with data increased stakeholder confidence
✓ **Industry partnership accelerates impact**: SME partner enabled market entry
✓ **Diversity strengthens applications**: Mix of academic, clinical, industrial perspectives

## Cross-Project Success Themes

### Common Elements in Winning Proposals
1. **Excellence**: World-class researchers with proven track records
2. **Clear Innovation**: Novel approach vs. incremental improvement
3. **Impact Potential**: Tangible societal or economic benefits
4. **Realistic Planning**: Achievable timelines, appropriate budgets
5. **Strong Partnerships**: Complementary expertise, proven collaboration
6. **User-Centric**: Involve stakeholders from design phase
7. **Open Science**: Commitment to data sharing & transparency
8. **Sustainability**: Plan for impact beyond project end

### Budget & Staffing Patterns
| Program | Typical Personnel % | Equipment % | Indirect % | Average Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU Horizon | 40-50% | 15-25% | 25% | €2-4M |
| UK UKRI | 45-55% | 10-20% | Varies | £1-3M |
| Canadian Tri-Council | 35-45% | 10-15% | None | CAD $300-500k |
| Swiss SNSF | 40-50% | 15-25% | 40% | CHF 500-1M |
| German DFG | 35-45% | 20-30% | 22% | €1-3M |

### Lessons from Application Rejections
- **Over-ambitious timeline**: Trying to do too much in given period
- **Weak industrial partnership**: Partner commits but doesn't engage
- **Unclear innovation**: Doesn't sufficiently differentiate from existing work
- **Budget misalignment**: Staffing plan doesn't match project scope
- **Weak team composition**: Missing critical expertise areas
- **Poor project management**: Inadequate risk mitigation, no contingencies
- **Limited dissemination**: Focus only on papers, not broader impact
- **Sustainability concerns**: Project designed to end abruptly, no long-term vision

## Conclusion

These case studies demonstrate that successful international grant applications share common elements:
- **Strong leadership** with track record of successful delivery
- **Excellent partnerships** with complementary expertise
- **Clear innovation** with significant impact potential
- **Realistic budgeting** aligned with technical objectives
- **Commitment to training** and knowledge dissemination
- **Strategic thinking** about long-term sustainability and impact

Use these examples as templates for your own application, adapting the structure, team composition, and budget allocation to your specific research context and funding program requirements.
