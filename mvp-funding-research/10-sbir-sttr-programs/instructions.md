# SBIR/STTR Programs: Federal Non-Equity Funding Playbook

## Executive Summary

The Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) programs represent one of the most valuable and underutilized non-equity funding sources for tech startups. These are **federal set-asides** requiring agencies to allocate percentages of their R&D budgets to small businesses.

**Why SBIR/STTR matters:**
- Over $3.2B annually in non-dilutive funding
- Zero equity given up
- Prestigious government backing for product development
- Structured validation and commercialization support
- 70%+ of successful Phase II applicants transition to Phase III (production/commercialization)

---

## Section 1: SBIR/STTR Program Overview

### Federal Funding Phases & Target Amounts

#### SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research)
- **Phase I:** $50,000 - $250,000 (typically $150K)
  - Duration: 6 months
  - Objective: Establish feasibility of innovative concept
  - Deliverable: Phase I report + Phase II proposal outline

- **Phase II:** $750,000 - $2,000,000 (varies by agency)
  - Duration: 24 months
  - Objective: Develop prototype, conduct R&D, validate market
  - Deliverable: Working prototype/MVP + commercialization plan

- **Phase III:** Varies widely ($5M - $500M+)
  - Duration: Varies
  - Objective: Commercialize technology for government/commercial use
  - Funding: Production contracts, not SBIR budget

#### STTR (Small Business Technology Transfer)
- **Phase I:** $50,000 - $100,000 (typically $75K)
  - Must include research institution partnership (20-40% of work)
  - Duration: 6 months

- **Phase II:** $400,000 - $750,000
  - Duration: 24 months
  - Mandatory research institution collaboration

- **Phase III:** Same as SBIR

### Participating Federal Agencies (11 mandatory + others)

**Primary agencies (mandatory SBIR/STTR participation):**
- Department of Defense (DoD) - Largest budget, ~$1.5B annual
- National Institutes of Health (NIH) - Second largest, ~$700M annual
- National Science Foundation (NSF) - ~$300M annual
- Department of Energy (DOE) - ~$200M annual
- NASA - ~$150M annual
- Department of Commerce (NIST) - ~$120M annual
- Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) - ~$80M annual
- Department of Transportation (DOT) - ~$60M annual
- Department of Education (ED) - ~$50M annual
- Department of Agriculture (USDA) - ~$40M annual
- Social Security Administration (SSA) - ~$20M annual

**Secondary agencies (varying participation):**
- Department of Veterans Affairs
- Homeland Security
- Nuclear Regulatory Commission
- Department of Interior
- Others

### Key Statistics
- **Success rate (Phase I):** 10-15% nationally
- **Success rate (Phase II | Phase I awardee):** 35-50%
- **Phase II to Phase III transition:** 70% of successful Phase II companies
- **Average time to Phase I award:** 4-6 months from submission
- **Average time to Phase II award:** 4-6 months after Phase I completion
- **Average commercialization revenue post-Phase II:** $2-5M within 3-5 years

## Section 2: How Top-Tier Founders Identify Opportunities

### Strategic Agency Selection Framework

#### Step 1: Agency Alignment Assessment
Match your technology to agency missions:

**DoD Context:**
- Focuses on national security, defense innovation
- Sub-agencies: Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, DIA, NSA, etc.
- Themes: Advanced materials, autonomy, AI/ML, cybersecurity, space tech
- **Ideal for:** Hardware, defense tech, dual-use technologies

**NIH Context:**
- Focuses on life sciences, medical devices, biotech, diagnostics
- Sub-agencies: NCI, NIAID, NHLBI, NIDDK, NIA, etc.
- Themes: Drug discovery, medical devices, digital health, biomarkers
- **Ideal for:** Biotech, MedTech, life science tools

**NSF Context:**
- Focuses on broader scientific research, emerging technologies
- Programs: Seed Fund for SBIR/STTR, Computing Systems, Smart Manufacturing
- Themes: Computer science, materials, clean energy, quantum
- **Ideal for:** Deep tech, fundamental research with commercial potential

**DOE Context:**
- Focuses on energy, climate, sustainability
- Labs: Argonne, NREL, Oak Ridge, Lawrence Berkeley
- Themes: Clean energy, battery tech, grid modernization, carbon capture
- **Ideal for:** Climate tech, energy storage, nuclear innovation

**NASA Context:**
- Focuses on space exploration, earth observation, technology spinoffs
- Centers: Kennedy, JPL, Glenn, Ames, etc.
- Themes: Space tech, autonomous systems, advanced materials, robotics
- **Ideal for:** Space-adjacent tech, satellite, autonomy

#### Step 2: Topic/Solicitation Scanning

Access solicitations through:

1. **SBIR.gov** (official portal)
   - Search by agency, topic, keyword
   - Filter by release date and deadlines
   - Subscribe to email updates by topic

2. **SAM.gov** (System for Award Management)
   - Government-wide solicitation search
   - Set up saved searches by keyword
   - Get notified when new solicitations open

3. **Agency-specific sites:**
   - defense.sbir.mil (DoD specific)
   - grants.nih.gov/grants (NIH)
   - nsf.gov/sbir (NSF)

4. **Strategic monitoring approach:**
   - Create 5-10 saved searches (one per topic area)
   - Check for new solicitations weekly
   - Set calendar reminders for deadline windows
   - Build a 18-month rolling pipeline

#### Step 3: Fit Analysis Matrix

For each solicitation, assess on 5 dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | Score 1-10 | Notes |
|-----------|--------|------------|-------|
| Technical Alignment | 30% | | Does your tech directly address topic? |
| Market Opportunity | 25% | | Is there commercial runway post-Phase II? |
| Agency Need Urgency | 20% | | How critical is this to agency mission? |
| Competitive Landscape | 15% | | How differentiated is your approach? |
| Execution Capability | 10% | | Can your team deliver in timeline? |

**Target score:** 7.5+ to proceed with proposal

#### Step 4: Topic Deep-Dive Research

Before committing to proposal, research:

1. **Agency documentation:**
   - Read entire solicitation (yes, all of it)
   - Review program officer's intent sections
   - Check past winners (SBIR.gov awards database)
   - Identify 3-5 prior funded projects in same topic

2. **Market research:**
   - Identify government users/procurers
   - Estimate TAM for government + commercial use
   - Research competing solutions
   - Project commercialization path

3. **Technical landscape:**
   - Patent search in your domain
   - Literature review of recent advances
   - Identify technical risks and mitigation
   - Benchmark against existing solutions

4. **Program officer engagement** (critical):
   - Find program officer name on solicitation
   - Email: "Seeking guidance on topic [X]. Our approach focuses on [one sentence]. Does this align with agency priorities?"
   - Request 15-minute call
   - Take detailed notes on guidance
   - Build relationship for future solicitations

### Red Flags to Avoid

- Solicitations with <2 month deadline (rushed, low success)
- Topics with 50+ past winners (saturated)
- Topics requiring secret/top-secret clearance (unless applicable)
- Agencies with slow payment history (check government contractor forums)
- Topics outside your core technical competency
- Markets with 0 civilian/commercial potential

## Section 3: The 12-Step SBIR/STTR Process

### Phase I: Feasibility Validation (6 months)

#### Step 1: Topic Selection & Commitment
**Timeline:** Months -6 to -4 before submission
**Owner:** Technical co-founder + business lead

Activities:
- Complete fit analysis on 5-10 potential topics
- Engage with program officers for top 2-3
- Validate that team has requisite expertise
- Commit to specific topic by decision deadline

Deliverable: Topic selection memo (internal)

---

#### Step 2: Phase I Proposal Development
**Timeline:** Months -4 to -1 before submission
**Owner:** Proposal lead (often external consultant for first-time)

Proposal sections (standard SBIR template):

**A. Project Summary** (1 page)
- Problem statement (what government problem exists?)
- Proposed solution (what will you build?)
- Expected outcomes (what will you prove?)
- Commercial potential (market size/path)

**B. Technical Approach** (6-8 pages)
- Detailed methodology
- Work plan with milestones
- Team expertise and track record
- Risk mitigation strategies
- Literature/patent review showing novelty

**C. Commercialization Plan** (2-3 pages)
- Market analysis
- Customer identification
- Pricing strategy
- Go-to-market approach
- Revenue projection

**D. Organizational Capacity** (2 pages)
- Team bios and relevant experience
- Facilities and equipment
- Prior successful SBIR/STTR experience
- Management plan

**E. Data Management Plan** (1 page)
- How will you manage/share data?
- Compliance with open science requirements

**F. Budget & Justification** (varies)
- Personnel costs (itemized by role)
- Equipment (>$5K items)
- Supplies
- Travel
- Other direct costs
- Justification paragraph for each category

**G. Budget Narrative** (2-3 pages)
- Explanation of why budget is appropriate
- How it supports technical approach

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Generic approach (not specific to agency mission)
- Overstating technical maturity
- Unrealistic timelines
- Insufficient team experience
- Missing or vague commercialization plan
- Budget misalignment with scope

---

#### Step 3: Internal Review & Refinement
**Timeline:** Month -1 before submission
**Owner:** Proposal lead + technical leads

Process:
1. First draft written (rough version)
2. Technical review (do milestones make sense?)
3. Commercialization review (is market real?)
4. Budget review (does it align with scope?)
5. Compliance review (meets all solicitation requirements?)
6. External review (ideally by someone who reviews for agencies)
7. Final edits and formatting

Checkpoint: Does proposal pass "Would I fund this?" test?

---

#### Step 4: Submission
**Timeline:** Submission deadline
**Owner:** Grants administrator + proposal lead

Process:
1. Create SAM.gov and Grants.gov accounts (if new)
2. Register company in SAM.gov (takes 3-5 days)
3. Prepare all supporting documents:
   - Financial statements (if available)
   - Conflict of interest disclosures
   - Facility access documentation
   - Letters of commitment (if partnering)
4. Submit via Grants.gov or agency portal
5. Confirm receipt notification
6. Archive submission package and email confirmation

**Typical submission time:** 8:30 AM agency time (avoids server load)

---

#### Step 5: Agency Review & Award Decision
**Timeline:** 4-6 months post-submission
**Owner:** None (passive)

What's happening:
- Agency routes proposal to 3-5 technical reviewers
- Reviewers score on technical merit, feasibility, commercialization
- Funding recommendations compiled
- Agency selects awardees (typically top 10-20% of submissions)
- Awards announced via Grants.gov and agency website

Your role:
- Monitor Grants.gov for status updates
- Prepare financial systems for federal contracting
- Alert team to prepare for potential award notification

---

#### Step 6: Phase I Execution
**Timeline:** 6 months during Phase I
**Owner:** Technical co-founder + operations lead

Deliverables required:
1. **Quarterly technical reports** (progress, milestones)
2. **Final Phase I report** (20-30 pages)
   - Problem restatement
   - Methodology and results achieved
   - Findings and conclusions
   - Phase II plan and commercialization strategy
   - Budget for Phase II

Key success factors:
- Hit all technical milestones (critical for Phase II transition)
- Document everything (photos, data, test results)
- Engage regularly with program officer
- Start thinking about Phase II immediately
- Identify initial customers/pilots for Phase II

**Common mistakes:**
- Scope creep (staying focused)
- Delayed milestones (impacts Phase II credibility)
- Poor documentation (needed for Phase II proposal)
- Neglecting commercialization work (only 20% R&D in Phase I)
- Not planning Phase II during Phase I

---

### Phase II: Full R&D & Commercialization Planning (24 months)

#### Step 7: Phase II Proposal Development
**Timeline:** Months 4-6 of Phase I
**Owner:** Proposal lead + tech lead

Phase II proposal emphasizes:
1. **Phase I Results** (proof of concept achieved)
2. **Market Validation**
   - Customer conversations/LOIs
   - Total addressable market analysis
   - Competitive advantage
3. **Detailed Development Plan**
   - Full 24-month R&D roadmap
   - MVP to production-ready specs
   - Technical risks and mitigation
4. **Commercialization Plan** (much more detailed than Phase I)
   - Go-to-market strategy
   - Customer acquisition plan
   - Pricing and revenue model
   - Channel strategy
   - Required partnerships
   - 5-year financial projections
5. **Team Expansion Plan**
   - How will you add team members?
   - Budget for key hires
6. **Phase III Path**
   - How will government/commercial customers adopt?
   - What does Phase III commercialization look like?
   - Potential partnerships with integrators/distributors

Proposal length: 25-40 pages typical

---

#### Step 8: Phase II Award & Kick-Off
**Timeline:** Months 10-12 of Phase I (typically)
**Owner:** Operations lead

Upon award notification:
1. Review contract terms and compliance requirements
2. Establish federal accounting systems
   - Time tracking
   - Cost allocation
   - Compliance documentation
3. Set up quarterly reporting process
4. Establish relationship with contracting officer
5. Schedule Phase II kick-off meeting with agency
6. Create 24-month detailed project plan

---

#### Step 9: Phase II Execution (First 12 Months)
**Timeline:** Months 1-12 of Phase II
**Owner:** Technical co-founder + product lead

Deliverables:
- **Quarterly reports** (technical progress, budget burn)
- **Prototype/MVP development**
- **Customer validation** (pilot tests, demos, feedback)
- **Go-to-market planning**

Key activities:
1. Build MVP/prototype to specification
2. Conduct 20-30 customer discovery interviews
3. Run 2-3 pilot programs with government/commercial customers
4. Refine value proposition based on feedback
5. Develop production roadmap
6. Identify strategic partnerships
7. Patent key innovations

Success metrics:
- MVP features aligned with Phase II plan (90%+ completion)
- Customer pilots showing strong problem-solution fit
- Go-to-market plan validated with real customer conversations
- Clear path to Phase III identified

**Most critical:** This is NOT just an R&D project. You must be actively selling and validating market demand.

---

#### Step 10: Phase II Execution (Second 12 Months)
**Timeline:** Months 13-24 of Phase II
**Owner:** Technical co-founder + business lead

Deliverables:
- **Quarterly reports** (customer validation results, commercialization progress)
- **Phase III commercialization plan** (40-50 page business plan)
- **Production-ready solution**
- **Customer commitments** (LOIs, MOUs, pilot purchase orders)

Key activities:
1. Complete MVP/prototype development
2. Begin production/manufacturing planning
3. Secure strategic partnerships (resellers, integrators, channels)
4. Obtain customer commitments (LOIs, pilots, pre-orders)
5. Finalize Phase III business plan
6. Explore Phase III funding options:
   - Government contracts
   - Commercial customers
   - Follow-on venture financing
   - Strategic partnerships

Success criteria:
- Production-ready solution completed
- 2-3 customer pilots demonstrating ROI
- $500K-$2M in potential Phase III revenue identified
- Strategic partnerships signed or near-signed
- Go-to-market team and processes defined

---

### Phase III: Commercialization & Scaling

#### Step 11: Phase III Transition
**Timeline:** Months 18-30 of Phase II
**Owner:** Co-founders + finance/ops lead

Phase III is NOT an SBIR/STTR award. Instead, it's revenue from:

**Option A: Government Contracts** (Most common)
- Contracting officer issues task orders or contracts
- Based on Phase II technical development
- Can be $5M - $500M+ for successful technologies
- Examples: DoD production contracts, NIH procurement agreements

**Option B: Commercial Revenue**
- Direct customers pay for product/service
- Licensing deals with integrators/resellers
- Subscription or consumption-based models

**Option C: Blended Approach**
- Split revenue between government and commercial
- Government provides anchor customer validation
- Commercial licenses follow from government success

Typical Phase III transition:
1. Government agency identifies production need
2. Competitive procurement process (often sole-source for SBIR-developed tech)
3. Contract awarded to small business
4. 12-36 month production/delivery contract
5. Tech company grows operations to fulfill contract

---

#### Step 12: Scale & Sustain
**Timeline:** Year 2+ post-Phase II
**Owner:** Full management team

Focus areas:
1. **Revenue growth** from Phase III
2. **Team expansion** to deliver on contracts
3. **Next-generation products** via new SBIR/STTR cycles
4. **Customer diversification** beyond original agency
5. **Path to exit or sustainability** (IPO, acquisition, cash-flow positive)

Metrics:
- Phase III revenue (government + commercial)
- Team size and retention
- Product roadmap advancement
- Market penetration in target segments
- Path to profitability

## Section 4: Systems & Processes

### Opportunity Management System

#### Solicitation Tracking Spreadsheet

Create a master tracker with columns:
- Agency
- Topic number
- Topic title
- Release date
- Deadline
- Phase (I, II, or both)
- Funding (Phase I/II amounts)
- Technical fit (1-10)
- Market potential (1-10)
- Competitive fit (1-10)
- Program officer name/email
- Notes
- Status (Pipeline / Passed / In Progress / Submitted / Accepted / Rejected)

Update weekly from SBIR.gov and SAM.gov

#### Decision-Making Cadence

**Weekly:**
- Monday: Scan new solicitations
- Wednesday: Evaluate top 2 candidates
- Friday: Make go/no-go decision

**Monthly:**
- Review pipeline (18-month rolling forecast)
- Program officer outreach calls
- Proposal progress review

**Quarterly:**
- Strategic assessment: Are we targeting right agencies?
- Win/loss analysis: Why did we win/lose?
- Roadmap planning: Adjust topics for next round

### Proposal Development Workflow

#### Phase I Proposal Timeline (8 weeks)

**Week 1:** Kickoff
- Assign proposal lead
- Gather existing materials (tech overview, team bios, prior grants)
- Create shared document template
- Schedule writing sprints

**Weeks 2-3:** Research & Outline
- Deep-dive research on topic
- Competitive analysis
- Program officer interviews
- Draft outline for all sections

**Weeks 4-6:** First Draft
- Each section owner writes independently
- Weekly sync to ensure coherence
- Technical section prioritized
- Rough budget built

**Week 7:** Internal Review
- Full team reads and comments
- Compliance check against solicitation
- Budget review
- Graphics/figures added

**Week 8:** Finalization
- Address all feedback
- Executive proofread
- Format and file preparation
- Final SAM.gov/Grants.gov check
- Submit (ideally day before deadline)

#### Phase II Proposal Timeline (12 weeks)

**Weeks 1-2:** Phase I Results Compilation
- Document all Phase I achievements
- Gather customer/pilot feedback
- Compile technical data/results

**Weeks 3-4:** Commercialization Research
- Customer discovery (20-30 interviews minimum)
- Market sizing
- Competitive landscaping
- Channel research

**Weeks 5-8:** Proposal Development
- Technical approach (expanded from Phase I results)
- Detailed 24-month plan
- Comprehensive commercialization plan
- Customer letters of support

**Weeks 9-10:** Internal Review
- Technical validation
- Market assumptions check
- Financial projections review
- Phase III strategy assessment

**Weeks 11-12:** Finalization
- Incorporate feedback
- Customer/partner LOI collection
- Final formatting
- Compliance verification
- Submit

### Federal Contracting Systems

#### Pre-Award Setup

**SAM.gov Registration** (do this early - takes time)
- Register company as small business
- Obtain DUNS number (free from Dun & Bradstreet)
- Complete Entity Management Profile
- Set up authorized representatives

**Accounting Systems**
- Federal cost accounting standards compliance
- Time tracking by project (requirement for federal contracts)
- Cost allocation methodology
- Quarterly financial reporting templates

**Compliance Documentation**
- Organizational conflicts of interest disclosure
- Key personnel agreements
- Facility security clearance (if applicable)
- Insurance documentation

#### Award Management

**Upon receiving award:**
1. Sign contracts and amendments
2. Set up billing/invoicing system
3. Establish quarterly reporting process
4. Create project management structure
5. Assign program officer contact person

**Ongoing compliance:**
- Timely quarterly reports (no exceptions)
- Accurate time sheets and cost tracking
- Invoice submission within 30 days of completion
- Respond to any agency inquiries immediately
- Document all major decisions/pivots

### 5. Customer Validation Interview Script (Phase II)

**Opening (2 min):**
"Thanks for taking time today. We're developing a solution to [problem you identified]. Our product will [key capability]. We'd like to understand from you: is this something your organization would find valuable? What would need to be true for you to adopt it?"

**Problem Discovery (5 min):**
1. "Tell me about how you currently address [problem]?"
2. "What are the biggest frustrations with your current approach?"
3. "How much are you spending annually on [this problem area]?"
4. "How does [problem] impact your organization's [mission/performance]?"

**Solution Validation (8 min):**
1. "Based on what I've described, do you see how we could help?"
2. "What's missing? What else would you need to make this work?"
3. "How important is [specific feature]? Nice-to-have or must-have?"
4. "Would you prefer [option A] or [option B] approach?"
5. "If we built this, would your organization use it?"

**Commitment (3 min):**
1. "Would you be willing to run a pilot with us during development?"
2. "What would success look like in a pilot?"
3. "How many units might you adopt in Year 1 post-launch?"
4. "Would you be open to signing a Letter of Interest supporting our Phase II proposal?"

**Close (2 min):**
"This has been incredibly helpful. We'll send you [follow-up material]. Can we reconnect in [timeframe] to show you our progress?"

## Section 6: Success Criteria

### Phase I Success Metrics

**Technical Metrics:**
- [ ] All proposed milestones achieved on schedule

## Section 7: Top-Tier Founder Strategies

### Strategy 1: SBIR as Non-Dilutive R&D Funding Engine

**The Concept:**
Rather than raising venture capital for R&D, use SBIR/STTR to fund product development while raising smaller seed rounds for go-to-market and team building.

**Implementation:**
1. **Secure Phase I** ($150K) → Prove feasibility, hire first technical person
2. **Raise seed round** ($250K-$500K) → Go-to-market, first sales hire, customer pilots
3. **Secure Phase II** ($1.5M) → Full product development, customer validation
4. **Raise Series A** ($2-5M) → Sales/marketing scaling, production capacity
5. **Secure Phase III** ($5M+) → Production contracts, revenue

**Advantage:** Preserve equity, validate market risk-free, use government contracts as revenue traction for venture fundraising

**Timeline:** 3-4 years from Phase I to viable business

---

### Strategy 2: Multiple Concurrent Solicitations

**The Concept:**
Don't rely on a single SBIR topic. Build a portfolio of submissions across agencies.

**Implementation:**
- **Year 1:** Submit 3 Phase I proposals (DoD, NIH, NSF)
- **Success rate expectation:** 1 Phase I award (10-15% average)
- **Year 2:** Execute Phase I, submit 3 new Phase I proposals + 1 Phase II (from Year 1 winner)
- **Year 3+:** Maintain steady flow (1-2 new Phase II proposals annually, continue Phase I submissions)

**Advantage:** Reduces dependency on single agency, spreads risk, maximizes learning

**Execution:**
- Dedicate one person (20% time) to continuous opportunity scanning
- Build proposal templates to reduce development time/cost
- Establish relationships with 10-15 program officers across agencies

---

### Strategy 3: Dual-Track Commercialization (Government + Commercial)

**The Concept:**
Don't position solution as "government-only." Develop both government and commercial products in parallel.

**Implementation:**
- **Phase I:** Prove core technology works for both government and commercial use cases
- **Phase II:** Full product development → Version A (government specs) + Version B (commercial)
- **Go-to-market:** Simultaneously pursue government contracts (fast, less price-sensitive) and commercial customers (larger TAM, longer sales cycle)

**Advantage:** Government contracts validate technology and provide cash flow while commercial business scales

**Example:**
- Government buyer: "We'll pay $2M for [capability]"
- Commercial buyer: "We need $X capability, license your technology for $500K + 5% revenue share"
- Result: Government funds operations, commercial scales revenue

---

### Strategy 4: Strategic Agency Targeting

**The Concept:**
Different agencies have different payout structures, decision timelines, and success rates.

**DoD Strategy:**
- Largest funding pool ($1.5B)
- Longer contract cycles but larger Phase III
- Topic areas: Autonomy, advanced materials, electronic warfare, AI/ML
- Success rate: 10-12%
- Best for: Defense tech, dual-use innovation

**NIH Strategy:**
- Second largest ($700M)
- Faster decision cycles (4-5 months)
- Topic areas: Diagnostics, medical devices, digital health, biomarkers
- Success rate: 12-15%
- Best for: Life science founders, medical innovation

**NSF Strategy:**
- Smaller awards but higher success rate
- Fastest commercialization push (strong emphasis on tech transfer)
- Topic areas: Quantum, advanced manufacturing, clean energy
- Success rate: 20-25%
- Best for: First-time federal funding recipients

**Tactic:** Start with NSF (highest success rate for learning), move to DoD/NIH at scale

---

### Strategy 5: Customer-Driven Topic Selection

**The Concept:**
Before selecting a topic, identify a specific customer with a real problem. Then find SBIR topics that align.

**Process:**
1. Identify 3-5 strategic government/commercial customers (potential Phase III anchors)
2. Interview extensively about their unsolved challenges
3. Filter SBIR solicitations to find exact topic match
4. Submit proposal framed around that specific customer need
5. Submit with customer letter of support

**Advantage:**
- Dramatically increases success rate (shows real market need)
- De-risks Phase III (customer already committed)
- Stronger proposal narrative

**Success rate uplift:** 10-15% → 25-35%

---

### Strategy 6: Build IP Strategy Around SBIR

**The Concept:**
Use SBIR to fund patent development; use patents to attract venture capital and customers.

**Implementation:**
- **Phase I:** Identify patentable innovations; file provisional patents (low cost, no prosecution required)
- **Phase II:** Conduct full patent prosecution on key inventions
- **Phase III:** Leverage issued patents in licensing deals and M&A

**Advantage:**
- Government pays for R&D (low patent risk)
- Issued patents become company assets
- Creates defensible moat for venture investors

**Typical patent cost:** $3-5K per patent → Easily budgetable in Phase II

---

### Strategy 7: Build a Continuous SBIR Pipeline

**The Concept:**
Create organizational infrastructure to submit 3-4 SBIR proposals per year continuously.

**Key Infrastructure:**
1. **Dedicated SBIR PM** (0.5-1.0 FTE)
   - Manages solicitation tracking
   - Coordinates with technical teams
   - Maintains program officer relationships
   - Oversees proposal development

2. **Proposal Templates**
   - Standard sections (Summary, Technical, Commercialization, Budget)
   - Agency-specific variations (DoD vs. NIH vs. NSF)
   - Customer letter template
   - Financial projection model

3. **Program Officer Network**
   - Maintain relationships with 10-15 key officers
   - Monthly outreach for upcoming topics
   - Provide early feedback on concepts
   - Build reputation as serious applicant

4. **Phase I → Phase II Transition Process**
   - Immediate post-award: Begin Phase II customer validation
   - Month 2-4: Phase I report + Phase II proposal outline
   - Month 5-6: Full Phase II proposal developed
   - Month 6: Phase II submitted (don't wait for Phase I completion)

5. **Commercialization Tracking**
   - Customer pipeline for Phase III
   - Partnership development
   - Market validation data collection
   - Go-to-market planning

**Result:** By Year 3, running 2-3 Phase II projects simultaneously + 2-3 Phase I submissions/awards annually

### Key Takeaways: Why SBIR/STTR is a Game-Changer

1. **Non-dilutive funding:** $3.2B annually across federal agencies
2. **Zero equity given up:** Founders maintain 100% control
3. **Low competition for winners:** Top 10-20% of applicants (not top 1% like venture capital)
4. **Validation catalyst:** Government backing opens commercial doors
5. **Revenue generator:** Phase III can become 50%+ of revenue for successful startups
6. **IP protection:** Government funding provides cover for patent development
7. **Scalability:** Multiple concurrent solicitations and phases enable rapid scaling
8. **Sustainability:** Reduces dependency on venture capital, enables path to cash flow positive

### Appendix B: Common Proposal Section Mistakes

**Problem Statement:**
- ❌ Too generic ("Industry needs better solutions")
- ✓ Specific ("DoD training effectiveness is limited by lack of real-time feedback systems, reducing readiness")

**Technical Approach:**
- ❌ Too vague ("We'll use machine learning")
- ✓ Specific ("We'll use transformer-based neural networks fine-tuned on [specific dataset] to achieve [specific metrics]")

**Commercialization Plan:**
- ❌ Wishful thinking ("We'll generate $50M revenue in Year 2")
- ✓ Grounded ("We've spoken with 25 customers; 60% expressed strong interest; initial customer willing to pilot with 10-unit purchase commitment")

- ❌ Unrealistic ("Full software development + hardware manufacturing for $150K")
- ✓ Realistic ("Phase I focuses on API design and simulation framework; hardware deferred to Phase II")

**Team:**
- ❌ Missing relevant experience ("CEO has startup experience")
- ✓ Relevant ("CEO founded 2 prior companies in aerospace; CTO has 10 years AI/ML experience at DARPA contractor")

SBIR/STTR represents a unique opportunity in the tech startup ecosystem: substantial non-dilutive funding coupled with structured customer validation and commercialization support. Success requires:

1. **Strategic topic selection** → Align with customer need + agency priority

3. **Continuous submission discipline** → 3-4 proposals per year over 3-year horizon
4. **Execution excellence** → Hit Phase I milestones, validate market during Phase II
5. **Phase III focus** → Ensure commercialization path is clear from Day 1

For founders with mission-driven innovation, SBIR/STTR should be core funding strategy, not Plan B.
