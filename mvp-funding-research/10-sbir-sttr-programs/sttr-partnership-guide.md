# STTR Partnership Guide: Research Institution Collaboration Strategies

## Overview
The Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) Program requires collaboration between small businesses and research institutions (universities, federally funded research centers, non-profit research organizations). This guide provides strategic approaches to identifying, negotiating, and managing successful research partnerships.

---

## Part 1: Understanding STTR Partnership Requirements

### Eligibility Requirements
- **Small Business**: For-profit with <500 employees
- **Research Institution Partners**:
  - Universities (all accredited institutions qualify)
  - Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs)
  - Non-profit research organizations
  - State and local government research institutions (limited eligibility)

### Minimum Collaboration Requirements
- **Work Division**: At least 40% of Phase I work must be performed by research institution
- **Intellectual Property**: Must address IP ownership and licensing in proposal
- **Leadership**: Small business should be principal investigator or co-PI
- **Management**: Small business maintains control of commercialization

### Advantages Over SBIR
- Access to university equipment and facilities
- Leverage academic expertise and publications
- Create pathway for tech transfer to market
- Can achieve higher technical credibility for reviewers
- Access to student labor (lower cost in proposal budget)

## Part 2: Identifying the Right Research Partner

### Step 1: Define Your Technical Needs
**Before approaching any institution, clarify:**
- Specific technical challenges requiring academic expertise
- Equipment or facilities needed
- Desired research capabilities (materials science, bioengineering, computer science, etc.)
- Timeline alignment (university academic calendar vs. business needs)

### Step 2: Research Potential Partners

#### Tier 1 Universities (Highest Research Output)
Strong in most STEM fields, large research budgets:
- MIT, Stanford, Carnegie Mellon
- UC Berkeley, University of Pennsylvania
- Caltech, Harvard, Columbia
- University of Chicago, Northwestern

**Benefits**: World-class research, extensive facilities, strong grant pedigree
**Challenges**: Slower decision-making, complex bureaucracy, may have competing priorities

#### Tier 2 Regional Research Universities
Strong in specific fields, more agile partnerships:
- State flagship universities (University of Michigan, UT Austin, etc.)
- Carnegie Classification "Very High Research" institutions
- SUNY, University of Wisconsin system
- University of Southern California, Duke, Rice

**Benefits**: Excellent technical capacity, more accessible leadership, faster decisions
**Challenges**: Potentially fewer resources than Tier 1, but more entrepreneurial culture

#### Specialized Institutions
Focus on specific domains:
- Georgia Tech (engineering)
- MIT Lincoln Laboratory (FFRDC, defense applications)
- University of California San Diego (cleantech, biotech)
- Rochester Institute of Technology (optics, robotics)
- Clemson University (manufacturing)

**Benefits**: Deep domain expertise, aligned with mission
**Challenges**: Limited breadth, may have other commitments

#### Non-Profit Research Organizations
FFRDCs and specialized research institutes:
- Sandia National Laboratories (engineering/materials)
- Oak Ridge National Laboratory (energy)
- Lawrence Berkeley National Lab (physics/materials)
- RAND Corporation (systems analysis)
- Research Triangle Institute (interdisciplinary)

**Benefits**: High-level facilities, federal alignment, IP experience
**Challenges**: Often require significant formal agreements, may be expensive

### Step 3: Evaluate Partnership Fit

#### Technical Fit Checklist
- [ ] Institution has published research in your specific area
- [ ] Relevant faculty available and interested
- [ ] Facilities/equipment available for your work
- [ ] Track record of industry partnerships in your domain
- [ ] Research group size adequate for 40% workload

#### Institutional Fit Checklist
- [ ] University research office responsive (visit or phone test)
- [ ] Clear IP policies favoring commercialization
- [ ] Experience with STTR/SBIR programs
- [ ] Reasonable overhead rates (25-35% for universities, varies by institution)
- [ ] Supportive of entrepreneurial culture and commercialization

#### Strategic Fit Checklist
- [ ] Geographic proximity (optional but helpful for meetings)
- [ ] Complementary technology with your small business
- [ ] Potential for multi-year engagement beyond STTR
- [ ] Access to student talent pipeline
- [ ] Alignment with university research priorities

## Part 3: Making First Contact and Building Relationships

### Strategy 1: Identify the Right Faculty Champion
**Do NOT start with technology transfer office**

Instead:
1. Search university department websites and research pages
2. Review recent publications in your technical area (Google Scholar, ResearchGate)
3. Look for faculty with:
   - Recent publications (last 1-2 years)
   - Research groups with 3+ students/postdocs
   - Prior industry partnerships or licensing
   - NSF/NIH grant funding (shows grant-writing skill)
4. Cross-check conference presentations (IEEE, relevant domain conferences)

### Strategy 2: Craft Your Initial Outreach

#### Email Template for Faculty Contact
```
Subject: Research collaboration opportunity in [specific area]

Dear Dr. [Name],

I've been following your research in [specific research area], particularly your recent
publication on [specific paper]. Your work on [technical contribution] is highly relevant
to challenges we're addressing at [company name].

We're developing [brief technology description] and believe your expertise in
[faculty's specific strength] could be instrumental in [specific technical challenge].

We're exploring a Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) partnership with a university
research group for Phase I development. This program provides federal funding for
collaborative R&D between small businesses and academic institutions.

Would you be open to a brief call to discuss potential collaboration? I'm happy to work
around your schedule.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Title]
[Company]
[Contact info]
```

**Key Elements**:
- Demonstrate familiarity with their specific research
- Show how your problem aligns with their research interests
- Explain STTR benefits (funding, student involvement, publication opportunity)
- Request brief call, not large time commitment
- Professional but warm tone

### Strategy 3: Effective Initial Conversation

**What to Discuss**:
1. Your technical challenge and why their expertise is needed
2. STTR program overview (funding, timeline, partnership structure)
3. Proposed division of work (40% research institution, 60% small business)
4. Timeline and milestones
5. Potential outcomes (publications, IP, follow-on work)

**What to Ask**:
- "Is this research area you'd be excited to explore?"
- "What would be your ideal role in this project?"
- "Do you have student researchers who might be involved?"
- "What are typical overhead rates and facilities charges?"
- "What's your experience with STTR or industry-sponsored research?"
- "How would you handle IP ownership and licensing?"

**Red Flags in Response**:
- Reluctance to discuss IP early
- High overhead rates (>50% for university research)
- Inability to commit time/student researchers
- Lack of interest in commercialization pathway
- Bureaucratic resistance ("must go through tech transfer first")

## Part 4: Structuring the Partnership Agreement

### Key Elements of STTR Partnership Letter/Agreement

#### 1. Statement of Work (SOW)
- Specific research tasks (40% research institution, 60% small business)
- Milestones and deliverables
- Timeline (typically 6 months for Phase I, 24 months for Phase II)
- Personnel involved (faculty, students, research staff)
- Equipment and facilities to be used

#### 2. Budget Structure
```
Phase I Example Budget (~$225K for STTR):
- Research Institution (40% - ~$90K):
  - Faculty effort (10-15%)
  - Graduate student salary (2 students @ $20-25K each)
  - Postdoctoral researcher or senior research staff
  - Materials and supplies
  - Equipment rental/access fees

- Small Business (60% - ~$135K):
  - Key personnel (product lead, technical team)
  - Development costs
  - Equipment and prototyping
  - Testing and validation
```

#### 3. Intellectual Property Terms
**Critical to define early**:

**Patent/Invention Ownership**:
- Option 1: Small business owns all IP (most common for STTR)
- Option 2: Joint ownership with licensing agreement
- Option 3: University retains IP with exclusive license to small business

**Publication Rights**:
- Typical: 30-60 day review period before publication
- Small business gets first chance to patent before publication
- University retains right to publish for academic purposes

**Licensing Terms** (if university retains IP):
- Exclusive worldwide license for commercialization
- Royalty rates (typically 2-5% of net revenues)
- Milestone payments on commercialization
- Rights to sublicense to manufacturers

**Example Language**:
> "All inventions arising from work performed by [Company] shall be owned by [Company].
> All inventions arising from work performed by [University] research group shall be owned
> by [University], with [Company] receiving a non-exclusive, royalty-free license for
> commercialization. The parties agree to jointly own any inventions developed through
> collaborative effort, with [Company] receiving the right to sublicense."

#### 4. Conflict of Interest Management
- Faculty involvement with competitors
- Student research on behalf of other companies
- Published results and proprietary information
- IP ownership in overlapping research areas

#### 5. Project Management
- Regular meetings (monthly minimum)
- Reporting requirements and frequency
- Change order process if scope changes
- Decision-making authority
- Escalation path for disagreements

### Negotiation Tips

#### For Overhead Rates
- **University standard**: 25-50% (varies significantly)
- **Negotiation strategy**:
  - Research what institution charges for similar work
  - Negotiate fixed overhead, not percentage
  - Offer multi-year commitment for better rates
  - Don't reduce quality for cost savings

#### For IP Ownership
- **Preferred position**: Small business owns all developed IP
- **Realistic expectation**: University retains ownership of research performed there
- **Compromise**: Small business owns commercialization rights, university owns research contribution
- **Key**: Get exclusive license regardless of ownership

#### For Academic Freedom
- **University expectation**: Right to publish research
- **Small business need**: Protection of proprietary information
- **Solution**: 30-60 day review period, university can publish non-proprietary results

## Part 5: Managing the Research Partnership

### Phase I Management (6 months)

#### Month 1-2: Launch and Kickoff
- **Week 1**: Sign partnership agreement
- **Week 2**: First in-person meeting
  - Review detailed scope and deliverables
  - Introduce team members
  - Discuss communication protocol
  - Review budget allocation
  - Confirm resource allocation (students, equipment, facilities)

#### Monthly Meetings Structure
**Duration**: 90 minutes (monthly minimum)
- 15 min: Progress update from research team
- 15 min: Technical discussion and problem-solving
- 15 min: Progress update from small business team
- 15 min: Budget and resource review
- 15 min: Risk assessment and next month planning
- 10 min: Publication/IP planning

#### Deliverables Timeline
- **Month 2**: Preliminary results and experimental design refinement
- **Month 3-4**: Core research and testing
- **Month 5**: Analysis and documentation
- **Month 6**: Final report, paper drafts, IP assessment

#### Budget Tracking
- Request detailed monthly invoices with:
  - Labor (hours and rates)
  - Materials and supplies
  - Equipment usage/rental
  - Overhead allocation
- Maintain copy of timesheets and expense reports
- Flag any budget overruns immediately

#### Communication Protocol
- **Primary contact**: Faculty PI and company technical lead
- **Weekly updates**: Email summary of progress (Slack or email)
- **Monthly meetings**: Structured video calls with documented notes
- **Graduate students**: Direct technical contact for implementation questions
- **Escalation path**: Project sponsor to department chair to tech transfer office

### Phase II Management (24 months)

#### Scaled-Up Team Structure
- Research institution: 1 faculty PI + 2-3 students/postdocs
- Small business: Dedicated technical lead + 1-2 supporting engineers
- Steering committee: Quarterly meetings with broader stakeholder group

#### Quarterly Reviews
- Technical progress against milestones
- Budget tracking and forecasting
- Risk assessment (technical, budget, schedule)
- Publication/IP strategy review
- Next quarter planning and resource allocation

#### Mid-Phase Review (Month 12)
- Comprehensive assessment of progress against Phase II objectives
- Decision point: Continue as planned, modify approach, or pivot
- Preliminary commercialization pathway
- Publication and IP strategy finalization
- Student involvement plan for second year

#### Transition Planning (Months 18-24)
- Prototype validation and demonstration
- Preparation for Phase III or commercial transition
- Technical documentation and knowledge transfer
- Publications and conference presentations
- Student graduation and replacement planning

### Managing Technical Challenges

#### When Research Hits Obstacles
1. **Acknowledge the problem early** (don't hide issues)
2. **Brainstorm alternatives** with the research team
3. **Assess impact on timeline and budget**
4. **Propose solutions**:
   - Modify approach
   - Add resources
   - Extend timeline
   - Reduce scope
5. **Document decision and rationale**
6. **Communicate to SBIR program officer if needed** (for Phase II)

#### Common Issues and Solutions
| Issue | Root Cause | Solution |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| Missed milestones | Underestimated complexity | Increase researcher allocation, extend timeline |
| Budget overruns | Unexpected materials costs | Reallocate from other budget lines, seek cost-sharing |
| Student turnover | Graduation or job changes | Plan for 18-month engagement max, cross-training |
| Publication delays | Journal review times | Plan for 12+ month timeline, pre-publish on arxiv |
| IP disputes | Unclear ownership in agreement | Reference partnership agreement, involve legal counsel |

## Part 6: Maximizing Value from Research Partnership

### Knowledge Transfer and IP Development

#### Strategy 1: Structured Technology Transfer
- **Weekly lab notebook reviews** with research team
- **Monthly IP assessment** - what inventions have been created?
- **Regular patent strategy meetings** - what's patentable?
- **Maintain clear invention disclosure** process
- **File provisional patents early** - don't wait for publication

#### Strategy 2: Student Talent Pipeline
- Identify top 1-2 students in research group
- Develop relationships and mentorship
- Create internship opportunities
- Discuss full-time opportunities post-graduation
- Budget for recruiting strong talent to your company

#### Strategy 3: Publication Strategy
- Align on publications early (what, where, when)
- Use publications for marketing and recruitment
- Build company thought leadership
- Create case study for future funding rounds
- Balance secrecy (patents) with openness (hiring, credibility)

#### Strategy 4: Facility Access
- Negotiate access to facilities beyond Phase I/II period
- Build relationships with core facilities directors
- Maintain equipment access for prototyping and testing
- Develop long-term research relationship

### Building Enduring Relationships

#### Follow-On Research
- Plan for Phase III programs that continue relationship
- Consider follow-on funding (venture capital with research add-ons)
- Develop licensing agreements if university retains IP
- Create research advisory board with faculty

#### Equity and Founder Relationships
- Consider providing equity to faculty advisor (with legal/institutional approval)
- Build executive advisory board with key researchers
- Create stock option plans for faculty consultants (where allowed)
- Plan for long-term engagement beyond initial STTR

#### Joint Publications and Presentations
- Co-author papers with research group
- Present at conferences together
- Create joint case studies and white papers
- Build reputation and credibility together

## Part 7: Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

### Pitfall 1: Weak IP Ownership Language
**Problem**: Ambiguous IP agreement leads to disputes during commercialization
**Solution**:
- Use clear, specific language on what's owned by whom
- Have IP lawyer review before signing
- Address improvements and derivative works
- Plan for joint inventions

### Pitfall 2: Misaligned Expectations
**Problem**: Researcher wants basic science; company wants commercialization
**Solution**:
- Discuss commercialization goals upfront
- Align on publication timeline and restrictions
- Regular communication about business direction
- Adjust scope if misalignment emerges

### Pitfall 3: Insufficient Research Resources
**Problem**: 40% work allocation inadequate for actual needs
**Solution**:
- Be realistic in Phase I budgeting (Phase II allows larger research allocation)
- Verify researcher availability before signing
- Plan additional cost-sharing if needed
- Consider parallel small business development work

### Pitfall 4: Communication Breakdowns
**Problem**: Monthly meetings become infrequent; email updates stop
**Solution**:
- Schedule recurring meetings (calendar hold)
- Assign meeting notes responsibility
- Create shared project management tool (Asana, Trello, Monday)
- Establish escalation process for issues
- Monthly emails to all team members

### Pitfall 5: Overhead Rate Surprises
**Problem**: Budget allocated but institution charges more
**Solution**:
- Get written overhead rate quote upfront
- Understand what's included in overhead
- Negotiate fixed overhead if possible
- Clarify facilities charges, equipment rental, indirect costs
- Get commitment in writing before proposal submission

### Pitfall 6: Student Continuity Issues
**Problem**: Key graduate student graduates mid-project
**Solution**:
- Budget for 2-3 students (rotation expected)
- Provide overlap during transitions
- Cross-train on critical tasks
- Consider postdoctoral researchers for longer continuity
- Build knowledge documentation process

## Part 8: STTR Program Resources and Support

### Preparing STTR Proposals

#### Research Institution Letter of Commitment
Must include:
- Commitment to 40% research effort
- Specific personnel names and effort allocation
- Description of facilities and resources
- Budget breakdown for research institution work
- IP ownership and licensing approach
- Signature of authorized institutional official

#### Budget Justification for Research Institution
- Itemized salary costs (faculty, graduate students, postdocs, staff)
- Research materials and supplies
- Equipment purchases or rental
- Travel for research conferences
- Overhead calculation (transparent)
- Any cost-sharing commitment

### Key Government Resources
- [SBA STTR Program](https://www.sba.gov/sbir) - Official homepage
- [NSF STTR Solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/publications/pub_summ.jsp?ods_key=nsf23555) - Current solicitation with requirements
- [NIH STTR Program](https://sbir.nih.gov/) - Medical/health STTR programs
- [DOD SBIR/STTR Program](https://www.defense.gov/SBIR/) - Defense applications

### University Technology Transfer Offices
After identifying faculty partner, you'll work with:
- **Research Administration**: Budgeting and compliance
- **Technology Transfer Office**: IP, licensing, tech transfer
- **Sponsored Programs**: Grant administration and reporting
- **Legal Department**: Contract negotiation and IP agreements

## Part 9: STTR Partnership Success Checklist

Use this checklist before committing to STTR partnership:

### Pre-Partnership
- [ ] Technical need clearly defined
- [ ] 3-5 potential university partners identified
- [ ] Faculty champion identified through research literature
- [ ] Initial conversation with faculty completed positively
- [ ] IP ownership terms preliminary discussed
- [ ] Budget preliminary aligned
- [ ] Partnership letter of commitment obtained

### Pre-Proposal Submission
- [ ] Partnership agreement or LOI finalized
- [ ] Budget agreed in writing
- [ ] Research scope document completed
- [ ] 40% research allocation confirmed by faculty
- [ ] IP strategy documented
- [ ] Overhead rate confirmed
- [ ] Research institution authorized official ready to sign

### Project Launch
- [ ] Formal partnership agreement signed
- [ ] Kickoff meeting completed
- [ ] Project management tools established
- [ ] Meeting schedule set (monthly minimum)
- [ ] Escalation path documented
- [ ] Student researcher introductions completed
- [ ] Budget tracking process established

### Ongoing Management
- [ ] Monthly meetings maintained
- [ ] Budget tracking current
- [ ] Deliverables on schedule
- [ ] IP disclosures filed as needed
- [ ] Publication strategy communicated
- [ ] Risk assessment updated
- [ ] Relationship with faculty champion maintained

## Conclusion
Successful STTR partnerships require upfront investment in finding the right academic partner, clear communication of expectations, robust IP agreements, and consistent project management. The federal funding, research expertise, and potential IP developed through STTR programs can provide significant competitive advantage and pathway to commercialization.

**Next Steps**:
1. Identify technical areas requiring academic expertise
2. Research 5-10 potential university partners
3. Reach out to faculty champions in your target research areas
4. Build partnership relationships through Phase I
5. Plan for scaled investment in Phase II
