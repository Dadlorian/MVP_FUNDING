# SBIR/STTR Compliance Guide: Federal Accounting and Tracking Requirements

## Overview
SBIR/STTR is federal grant funding with specific compliance, accounting, and reporting requirements. Failure to comply can result in funding clawback, ineligibility for future awards, or legal action. This guide covers financial management, record-keeping, regulatory compliance, and reporting obligations.

---

## Part 1: Pre-Award Compliance

### Company Eligibility Verification

#### SBIR Eligibility Requirements
**Small Business Administration (SBA) defines "small business":**
- **Size**: Fewer than 500 employees (including parent company)
- **Ownership**: More than 50% owned by US citizens or permanent residents
- **For-profit entity**: Cannot be non-profit
- **Independence**: Cannot be majority-owned subsidiary of large business
- **Control**: Business must be independently managed

**Verification before applying**:
- [ ] Verify employee headcount (use SBA HUBZone list if household)
- [ ] Document ownership structure (who owns >50%?)
- [ ] Confirm no large company parent (check ownership tree)
- [ ] Verify for-profit status (LLC, C-corp, S-corp OK; non-profit not OK)
- [ ] Check if previously received 3.5 billion in SBIR funding (aggregate cap)

**Where to verify**:
- [SBA Small Business Eligibility Tool](https://www.sba.gov/sbir)
- [System for Award Management (SAM.gov)](https://sam.gov/) - Federal database
- Tax returns and business licenses

#### Employee Count Determination
**Federal definition**: "Employees" includes:
- Full-time and part-time employees on payroll
- Employees of parent company
- Employees of affiliates (companies >50% owned)

**Exclusions**: NOT counted as employees:
- Consultants (if truly independent contractors)
- Contractors and vendors
- Board members (if not employees)
- Interns (if unpaid or paid by other entity)

**Strategy**: Keep clear payroll records showing who is "employee" vs. contractor

**Caution**: Misclassification of employee status = ineligibility + potential clawback

### SAM Registration and Exclusions

**Before any federal award**:
1. Register on [SAM.gov](https://sam.gov/)
   - Free federal contractor registration
   - Takes 3-5 business days
   - Renew annually (automatic emails sent)
   - Complete all required information

2. Check OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control) list
   - Ensure company/leadership not on sanctions list
   - [Check OFAC SDN List](https://ofac.treasury.gov/specially-designated-nationals-list-data-formats-data-download)

3. Check Excluded Parties List System (EPLS)
   - Integrated into SAM.gov
   - Individuals and companies with prior federal issues
   - System automatically checks all applicants

**Consequences of non-compliance**:
- Application rejected automatically
- Cannot receive federal funding until resolved
- May require legal action to resolve

### Authorization and Signatory Authority

**Before submitting proposals**:
1. **Identify Authorized Official**: Person with power to bind company
   - Usually CEO or CFO
   - Must be documented in company bylaws/resolutions
   - Sign all grant applications and agreements

2. **Create Board Resolution** (if not existing):
   ```
   RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING SBIR/STTR GRANT APPLICATIONS

   BE IT RESOLVED, that [Name, Title] is authorized to:
   1. Apply for and accept SBIR/STTR grants
   2. Execute all grant agreements with federal agencies
   3. Commit company resources as required by grants
   4. Manage all grant compliance and reporting

   Approved by Board: [Date]
   [Board Member Signatures]
   ```

3. **Document Financial Authority Limits**:
   - Who can approve expenses?
   - Who signs checks?
   - Segregation of duties for financial controls

### IRS Tax Compliance

**Required before award**:
- [ ] Active EIN (Employer Identification Number)
- [ ] Current business tax returns filed
- [ ] No unpaid tax liens
- [ ] W-9 form completed for SAM registration
- [ ] 3 years of tax returns available (may be requested)

**If company has no prior tax returns**:
- Still eligible if filing status on SAM.gov is correct
- Will be asked to provide during grant setup
- Allow 1-2 extra weeks for processing

## Part 2: Award and Grant Setup

### Grant Award Letter and Acceptance

**When you receive award notification**:
1. **DON'T SPEND MONEY YET** - Award letter is not authorization
2. Review grant award letter for:
   - Award amount
   - Award period dates
   - Funding source (direct/indirect costs)
   - Payment method (lump sum, milestone-based, reimbursement)
   - Special conditions or restrictions

3. **Grant Agreement Setup**: Government will provide
   - Standard agreement with terms and conditions
   - Award numbering system
   - Authorized use of funds
   - Compliance requirements
   - Reporting schedule

4. **Formal Acceptance**: Sign and return agreement
   - Authorized official must sign
   - Keep signed copy in files
   - Only after this is award "activated"

### Establish Award Accounting Setup

**Create separate accounting structure** for each grant:

```
Sample Award Setup:
Award Number: NSF-2024-01234
Project Title: [Your Project]
Award Amount: $225,000 (Phase I)
Award Period: 12/1/2024 - 11/30/2025
Direct Costs: $200,000
Indirect Costs: $25,000 (F&A 12.5%)

Cost Centers:
- Labor (Personnel): 5500-SBIR-01
- Materials and Supplies: 5500-SBIR-02
- Equipment: 5500-SBIR-03
- Travel: 5500-SBIR-04
- Other Direct Costs: 5500-SBIR-05
- Indirect Costs (F&A): 5500-SBIR-06

Budget Tracking:
- Use accounting software with cost code tracking
- Monthly reconciliation against budget
- Quarterly review with project manager
- Annual audit preparation
```

**Accounting software options**:
- QuickBooks (small business standard)
- Xero (cloud-based, good for startups)
- NetSuite (enterprise option)
- Wave (free, good for startups)

**Minimum**: Spreadsheet tracking acceptable if:
- Updated monthly
- Reconciled to bank statements
- Signed off by CFO/Finance person
- Backed up and archived

## Part 3: Financial Management and Cost Tracking

### Allowable vs. Unallowable Costs

**Federal grants follow 2 CFR (Code of Federal Regulations) Part 200 (Uniform Guidance)**

#### Directly Allowable Costs

**Salaries and Wages**:
- Researcher/engineer time on project
- Administrative support (pro-rata)
- Benefits (typically 25-30% of salary)
- Payroll taxes (employer portion)
- Worker's compensation insurance

**Requirements**:
- Time tracking required (timesheets, effort reports)
- Minimum weekly timekeeping
- Charge to correct cost code/project
- Support by objective evidence (timesheets, software logs, etc.)

**Example timesheet format**:
```
Week of: 11/18/2024
Employee: John Smith
Project Code: 5500-SBIR-01

Task Description | Hours | % Effort | Comment
Algorithm Development | 40 hrs | 100% | Phase I Key Milestone
_________________________________________
Total Project Hours: 40 / 40 total = 100%

Signature: John Smith    Date: 11/22/2024
Review/Approval: CFO     Date: 11/22/2024
```

**Materials and Supplies**:
- Raw materials
- Laboratory supplies
- Software licenses (project-specific)
- Office supplies (project-specific)
- Prototype components

**Requirements**:
- Receipt showing purchase
- Description of how used on project
- Reasonable cost (not excessive)
- Less than $5,000 per item (typical threshold, varies by agency)

**Example documentation**:
```
Invoice: Amazon Business #INV-2024-456
Date: 11/15/2024
Item: Fiber Optic Cable (100m spool)
Cost: $245.00
Project Code: 5500-SBIR-03

Justification: Test harness for Phase I prototype
Approval: Project Manager - Dr. Jane Smith
Cost Center: Materials and Supplies (5500-SBIR-02)
```

**Travel**:
- Transportation (air, car, train)
- Lodging (per diem standards)
- Meals (under per diem limit)
- Conference/meeting registration

**Requirements**:
- Reasonable and necessary for project
- Conference must be related to project
- Airfare: Coach class or cheapest available
- Meals: Federal per diem limits by location
- Must include receipt/documentation

**Equipment**:
- Items >$5,000 each (varies by agency)
- Computers, lab equipment, test instruments
- Capitalized on balance sheet
- Federal government retains rights

**Requirements**:
- Justified in proposal with specific need
- Approved by agency before purchase (often)
- Documented with receipt
- Tagged/tracked by serial number
- Inventory maintained
- Government may have ownership rights

**Example equipment justification**:
```
Equipment: Oscilloscope (50 GHz, bandwidth requirement)
Cost: $8,500
Supplier Quote: [Attached]
Justification: Required for Phase I testing of RF signals
              Existing equipment insufficient (25 GHz max)
              Cannot be shared with other projects
Timeline: Purchase month 1, use months 1-12

Funding Source: Equipment line item, Phase I budget
```

**Subcontractors/Consultants**:
- Technical consultants on project
- University partners (STTR)
- Research organizations
- Specialized service providers

**Requirements**:
- Written SOW (statement of work)
- Competitive quotes (if >$25K typically)
- Fixed-price preferred over cost-plus
- Invoices and acceptance documentation
- Compliance pass-through requirements

#### Unallowable Costs (Do NOT charge to SBIR grant)

**Never allowed**:
- Political contributions or lobbying
- Fines and penalties
- Bad debts and write-offs
- Entertainment and alcohol
- Advertising (except technical publications)
- Relocation and moving expenses
- Personal use of equipment

**Questionable - Check agency guidance**:
- Employee bonuses (usually not allowed)
- Sales commissions (not allowed)
- Meals during work day (limited allowance)
- Internet/phone (if mixed business/personal)
- Office supplies (if not project-specific)
- Professional memberships (may need justification)

**Example UNALLOWABLE charge**:
❌ "Team celebration lunch" = UNALLOWABLE (entertainment)
✓ "Project review meeting lunch (within per diem)" = ALLOWABLE (necessary for project)

### Cost Tracking and Documentation System

**Required documentation for each charge**:

**For Labor**:
1. Timesheet showing hours and project allocation
2. Employee salary information
3. Benefits documentation (if challenged)
4. Approval signature from supervisor
5. Supporting notes on work performed

**For Materials**:
1. Invoice or receipt
2. Purchase order (if required)
3. Description of material
4. Justification for project use
5. Approval signature

**For Equipment**:
1. Equipment justification memo
2. Competitive quotes
3. Purchase order and receipt
4. Serial number and asset tag
5. Location tracking
6. Annual inventory certification

**For Travel**:
1. Business purpose statement
2. Airfare receipt
3. Hotel and meal receipts
4. Ground transportation receipts
5. Conference registration confirmation
6. Approved travel policy compliance

**For Subcontracts**:
1. Written SOW
2. Competitive quotes
3. Signed subcontract agreement
4. Monthly invoices with supporting documentation
5. Acceptance of deliverables
6. Compliance certification from subcontractor

### Monthly Financial Management Process

**Beginning of month**:
1. **Expense review**: Collect all receipts/invoices
2. **Code assignment**: Assign to correct cost code
3. **Documentation verification**: Confirm backup documentation complete
4. **Approval chain**: Route for appropriate signatures
5. **Data entry**: Enter into accounting system

**Mid-month**:
1. **Reconciliation**: Match receipts to accounting records
2. **Budget comparison**: Actual vs. budgeted by cost code
3. **Variance analysis**: Investigate large variances
4. **Correction requests**: Fix coding errors immediately
5. **Forecast**: Project costs for remainder of month

**End of month**:
1. **Final reconciliation**: Close out monthly records
2. **Bank statement match**: Confirm all charges posted correctly
3. **Report generation**: Budget vs. actual report
4. **Review meeting**: CFO/PM reviews with project team
5. **Variance approval**: Document approval of any overages
6. **Archiving**: File and backup all documentation

### Indirect Costs (F&A - Facilities & Administrative)

**What are indirect costs?**
- Rent/utilities for facility
- Insurance (general liability, property)
- Finance and HR staff
- Management and administration
- Depreciation on equipment
- Network and IT infrastructure

**How they're calculated**:
- Federal rate negotiated with government (Cost Accounting Standards)
- Rate applied to "modified total direct costs" (MTDC)
- Typically 20-60% depending on institution

**Example**:
```
Direct Costs Budget: $200,000
Indirect Cost Rate: 25% (negotiated rate)
Indirect Costs: $200,000 × 25% = $50,000
Total Award: $250,000
```

**Federal indirect cost rates**:
- Universities: Often 25-50% (negotiated)
- Non-profits: Often 20-40%
- For-profit companies: Often 15-25%
- Government labs: Often lower, but facility-specific

**Where to find your rate**:
- Check SAM.gov profile
- Review prior federal awards
- Contact grant management office
- NSF, NIH, DoD publish rates by institution
- If unknown, assume 15-25% for planning

**Important**: Cannot profit on indirect costs
- Must be spent on infrastructure
- Cannot be personal income
- Excess indirect costs must be returned to government

## Part 4: Time and Effort Reporting

### Time Tracking Requirements

**Federal regulations require**:
- Contemporaneous records (entered during or immediately after work)
- At least weekly documentation of effort
- Charging to correct project/cost code
- Supervisory approval
- Support for >50 hour weeks or unusual patterns

**Acceptable time tracking methods**:
- Paper timesheets signed weekly
- Electronic time tracking system (Toggl, Clockify, etc.)
- Calendar-based logs if detailed
- Project management software (if accurate)

**Unacceptable methods**:
- Retroactive estimates (too inaccurate)
- Averaged timesheets ("assume 50% on project X")
- Verbal authorization without documentation
- Monthly lump-sum allocations

**Template for time tracking**:
```
SBIR/STTR TIME TRACKING FORM
Project: [Name] | Award: [Number] | Period: Week of [Date]

Employee: [Name] | Cost Code: [5500-SBIR-XX]

Date | Task Description | Hours | %Effort | Notes
Mon 11/18 | Algorithm testing and debugging | 8 hrs | 100% |
Tue 11/19 | Project meeting + documentation | 4 hrs | 50% | (Other 50% to Project Y)
Tue 11/19 | Algorithm refinement | 4 hrs | 50% |
Wed 11/20 | Test results analysis | 8 hrs | 100% |
Thu 11/21 | Code optimization | 8 hrs | 100% |
Fri 11/22 | Week review and reporting | 2 hrs | 100% |

Total Project Hours: 34 / 40 total = 85% effort
Other Projects: 6 hours (15% to Project Y)

Employee Signature: [Signature] Date: [Date]
Manager Approval: [Signature]  Date: [Date]
```

### Effort Reporting

**Definition**: "Effort" is percentage of time spent on grant work

**Typical pattern**:
- PI (Principal Investigator): 10-20% effort on Phase I
- Key Personnel: 50-100% effort
- Support Staff: 5-10% effort (shared across projects)

**How effort is calculated**:
```
Example: Engineer spends 32 hours on SBIR project, 8 hours on other work
Total hours = 40
Effort on SBIR = 32/40 = 80% effort
```

**Reporting frequency**:
- After Phase I completion: Effort Summary Report
- During Phase II: Monthly or quarterly effort certification
- End of project: Final effort reconciliation

**Sample effort report**:
```
EFFORT CERTIFICATION - NSF SBIR-2024-01234
Project: Novel Sensor Design
Reporting Period: December 1, 2024 - February 28, 2025

Personnel | Role | Budgeted Effort | Actual Effort | Variance
Dr. Jane Smith | PI | 15% | 18% | +3% (Additional review work)
John Chen | Senior Engineer | 100% | 95% | -5% (Illness month 1)
Maria Garcia | Lab Tech | 50% | 50% | 0%

Total Project Cost: $185,000
Effort Variance Explanation: Dr. Smith additional effort due to more complex than anticipated algorithm challenges. Offset by reduced John Chen hours due to illness in December.

Certification: I certify that the above information is accurate and reflects actual effort on the project.

Authorized Official: [Signature] Date: [Date]
```

### Timekeeping System Setup

**Minimum requirements**:
- Log hours worked on project by date
- Allocate to correct cost code/project
- Track at least weekly
- Supervisor review/approval
- Monthly certification

**Low-tech option** (spreadsheet-based):
- Simple Excel template
- Weekly entry by employee
- Monthly rollup by cost code
- Supervisor sign-off
- Store in shared drive with backup

**Medium complexity** (timesheet software):
- Toggl, Clockify, Harvest
- 20-50 users = $50-200/month
- Mobile apps for ease of entry
- Automatic rollup by project/cost code
- Approval workflow

**Enterprise option** (integrated system):
- QuickBooks time tracking
- ADP/Workday integration
- Enterprise software (Deltek, UltiPro)
- Automatic sync with accounting
- Audit trail built-in

**Recommendation for startups**: Start with spreadsheet, move to Toggl/Clockify when 5+ employees.

## Part 5: Federal Audit and Compliance

### Single Audit Requirements

**What is Single Audit?**
- Annual independent audit of federal spending >$750K
- Required by federal regulation (2 CFR Part 200)
- Covers ALL federal awards combined
- Performed by external CPA firm

**When required**:
- Organization receives $750K+ in federal funds in one year
- Covers all federal awards (SBIR, contracts, other grants)
- First SBIR award usually won't trigger audit
- Phase II + Phase I (multiple awards) may trigger

**Process**:
1. Hire external CPA firm experienced in federal audits
2. Audit covers fiscal year (full 12 months)
3. Audit results submitted to Federal Audit Clearinghouse
4. Any findings must be addressed and reported
5. Results affect eligibility for future funding

**Costs**:
- Small organization single audit: $5,000-$15,000
- Larger organization: $15,000-$50,000+
- Must plan for this cost if approaching $750K threshold
- Can include in future grant proposals

**Common findings and how to avoid**:
1. **Inadequate time tracking** → Set up system early, train team
2. **Improper cost allocation** → Use cost codes consistently
3. **Unallowable costs charged** → Train staff on allowable/unallowable
4. **Missing supporting documentation** → File receipts religiously
5. **Budget overruns or unauthorized charges** → Monthly monitoring
6. **Subcontractor non-compliance** → Require subcontractor certifications

### Internal Controls Over Federal Funding

**Establish financial controls**:

1. **Segregation of Duties**:
   - Expense authorization (manager)
   - Expense processing (finance person)
   - Payment approval (CFO)
   - Reconciliation (different person)

2. **Approval Process**:
   - All charges approved before payment
   - Budget review monthly
   - Project approval required for any changes
   - Escalation for unusual items

3. **Documentation**:
   - All receipts retained for 7 years
   - Organized by cost code and date
   - Digital backup created
   - Access restricted to authorized personnel

4. **Monitoring and Review**:
   - Monthly budget vs. actual reports
   - Variance investigation and explanation
   - Quarterly compliance review
   - Annual internal audit

5. **Preventive Controls**:
   - Clear expense policy document
   - Training for all staff handling grant funds
   - Code of conduct acknowledgment
   - Regular updates on federal requirements

## Part 6: Reporting and Closeout

### Progress Reports

**Required reporting by agency**:

**NSF SBIR**:
- Phase I: One final report within 30 days of end date
- Phase II: Annual reports + final report
- Format: Detailed narrative + financial summary

**NIH SBIR**:
- Phase I: Final report within 30 days of end
- Phase II: Annual progress reports (SF 424 R&R)
- Electronic submission through eRA Commons

**DoD SBIR**:
- Phase I: Final report
- Phase II: Quarterly progress reports + final
- Varies by military department (check solicitation)

**DoE SBIR**:
- Annual reports during Phase II
- Final report at project end
- SF 425 Financial Report

**Typical report contents**:
1. **Project narrative** (2-5 pages):
   - Accomplishments vs. objectives
   - Technical progress
   - Any problems/delays
   - Upcoming milestones

2. **Financial summary**:
   - Actual vs. budgeted spending
   - Cost overruns or underruns
   - Explanation of variances

3. **Work completed**:
   - Deliverables (prototypes, software, reports)
   - Publications and patents filed
   - Personnel hired
   - Facilities/equipment procured

4. **Commercialization progress**:
   - Customer feedback
   - Market validation
   - Phase III plans
   - Licensing/partnership discussions

5. **Compliance certification**:
   - Certification that work performed as proposed
   - Compliance with all federal requirements
   - Proper use of funds
   - Authorized official signature

### Final Grant Closeout

**At end of final Phase**:

1. **Final Invoice/Reconciliation** (30 days after end):
   - All eligible expenses invoiced
   - Budget reconciliation
   - Refund unspent funds (typically required)

2. **Final Report Submission**:
   - Narrative report
   - Financial report
   - All deliverables attached
   - Compliance certification

3. **Grant Closeout Package** (agency will provide checklist):
   - Final financial status report
   - Final progress report
   - Close-out checklist signed
   - Subcontractor closeout documents
   - Equipment inventory (if any)

4. **Property/Equipment Disposition**:
   - If federal government owns equipment: Return or request retention
   - If small dollar equipment: Usually can keep
   - Document final disposition
   - Remove asset tags

5. **Records Retention**:
   - Keep all documentation 7 years after final payment
   - Includes timesheets, receipts, reports, contracts
   - Store securely (fire-proof, backup copies)
   - Organized by cost code and chronologically

## Part 7: Common Compliance Violations and Penalties

### Critical Violations (Can Result in Clawback or Debarment)

| Violation | Example | Penalty |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Fraudulent charges | Charging personal expenses to grant | Clawback of grant + penalties + possible prosecution |
| Unauthorized charges | Using grant for unallowable purposes | Clawback of funds + interest |
| Time falsification | Timesheet backdated or inflated | Clawback + ineligibility for future awards |
| Cost misallocation | Charging ineligible costs | Clawback + required corrective action |
| False compliance statement | Signing that you complied when you didn't | Clawback + penalties + fraud charges |

### Major Violations (Significant Penalties)

| Violation | Example | Penalty |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Inadequate documentation | No receipts or timesheets | Disallow costs + require repayment |
| Weak internal controls | No approval process | Audit findings + corrective action plan |
| Subcontractor non-compliance | Subcontractor not tracking time properly | Require subcontractor's correction or disallow costs |
| Equipment non-compliance | Equipment not properly tracked/inventoried | Disallow equipment purchase |
| Audit findings | Unresolved audit findings | Corrective action required |

### Minor Violations (Correctable)

| Violation | Example | Remedy |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Missing documentation | One receipt not filed properly | Provide documentation or certify its loss |
| Cost allocation error | Cost charged to wrong project (honest error) | Correct allocation via amendment |
| Minor overage | 1-2% over budget line item | Approved variance or reallocation |
| Late report | Report submitted 5 days late | File ASAP, may delay next payment |

### How to Avoid Violations

**1. Documentation**:
- Every expense must have receipt/invoice
- File by cost code and date
- Create backup copies
- 7-year retention minimum

**2. Approval Process**:
- All charges reviewed monthly
- Budget variance monitored
- Approval signatures documented
- Escalation for unusual items

**3. Training**:
- All employees handling grant funds trained
- Allowable vs. unallowable costs clearly explained
- Time tracking procedures documented
- Annual refresher training

**4. Monitoring**:
- Monthly budget vs. actual reports
- Quarterly compliance self-assessment
- Annual internal audit
- Subcontractor compliance verification

**5. Clear Policies**:
- Written expense policy
- Examples of allowable/unallowable costs
- Time tracking procedures
- Equipment policies
- Available to all staff

## Part 8: Federal Acquisition Regulations (FAR) Basics

### For Subcontractors Over $25K

**When you hire a subcontractor** ($25K+):

**Flow-down Requirements** (Subcontractor must comply with):
1. Federal funding acknowledgment in their invoice
2. Time tracking and documentation requirements
3. Allowable/unallowable cost rules
4. Property rights (if equipment involved)
5. Compliance certifications (OFAC, exclusions list, etc.)

**Sample subcontract language**:
> "Subcontractor acknowledges this work is funded by [Federal Agency] award [Number].
> Subcontractor shall comply with all applicable federal regulations including:
> - 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance)
> - [Agency-specific regulations]
> - Maintain time and expense documentation per federal standards
> - Submit compliance certification with final invoice"

### Key Federal Acquisition Rules

**When federal funding involved**:
1. **Competitive procurement** (if >$25K typically):
   - Request quotes from at least 3 vendors
   - Document rationale for vendor selection
   - Award to most advantageous (not necessarily lowest cost)

2. **Conflict of interest**:
   - Cannot award to family members
   - Cannot self-deal
   - Must disclose related-party transactions
   - Requires written approval from government

3. **Small Business Subcontracting** (for larger awards):
   - May be required to subcontract to small businesses
   - Document small business utilization

4. **Buy American** (for certain agencies):
   - Preference for US-made equipment/materials
   - Exceptions require justification
   - Some agencies very strict (DoD)

## Conclusion

Federal SBIR/STTR funding comes with rigorous compliance requirements, but these are manageable with proper planning and systems. The most successful SBIR companies:

1. **Establish controls early** - Don't wait until funding arrives
2. **Document everything** - Every receipt, approval, decision
3. **Train the team** - Everyone handling funds understands rules
4. **Monitor monthly** - Don't let problems compound quarterly
5. **Stay conservative** - When in doubt, ask the agency
6. **Plan for reporting** - Budget time and resources for compliance

**Worst-case scenario** of non-compliance is clawback of funding and ineligibility for future awards. **Best practice** is spending 5-10% of management time on compliance, preventing 100% of problems.

## Quick Reference: Cost Allowability Checklist

**Before charging ANY expense to SBIR/STTR grant, ask**:
- [ ] Is this directly related to the project?
- [ ] Is it listed as allowable in 2 CFR Part 200?
- [ ] Do I have documentation (receipt/invoice)?
- [ ] Has a supervisor approved this?
- [ ] Am I coding it to the correct cost code?
