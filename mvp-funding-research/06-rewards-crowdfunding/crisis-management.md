## Manufacturing Delays

### Common Causes & Prevention

**Cause #1: Component Supply Chain Disruptions**

**Real-World Scenario (COVID-19 Impact)**
- Many electronic component manufacturers shut down March-April 2020
- Lead times extended from 6 weeks to 6+ months
- Projects expecting Q3 delivery slipped to Q1 next year
- Backers experienced 9-12 month delays

**Prevention Strategies**
1. **Dual-Source Critical Components**
   - Never rely on single supplier for essential components
   - Negotiate contracts with 2+ manufacturers
   - Cost increase: 5-10% but risk reduction: 70-80%

2. **Buffer Stock Strategy**
   - Order 15-20% excess components in advance
   - Store in secure location with climate control
   - Cost: 3-5% of COGS but provides 3-4 month runway

3. **Long-Term Contracts**
   - Lock in pricing 6-12 months in advance
   - Include minimum/maximum delivery windows
   - Penalty clauses for supplier delays
   - Cost: Slight premium (2-3%) but predictability

4. **Alternative Component Identification**
   - Before manufacturing, identify equivalent substitutes
   - Maintain specification sheets for alternatives
   - Test compatibility with 2-3 substitute options
   - Budget: $2,000-5,000 testing cost

---

**Cause #2: Manufacturer Capacity Issues**

**Real-World Scenario (Exploding Demand)**
- Campaign raises $5M (10x goal)
- Manufacturer expected 50,000 units but received orders for 500,000
- Single production line cannot scale 10x immediately
- Manufacturer quotes 12-18 month delay vs. 4 months promised

**Prevention Strategies**
1. **Pre-Campaign Capacity Confirmation**
   - Confirm manufacturer can produce 2-3x goal amount
   - Get written commitment from manufacturer
   - Negotiate rush production terms in advance
   - Cost: Slight rate premium but guarantees capacity

2. **Multiple Manufacturer Engagement**
   - Split production across 2-3 manufacturers from start
   - Each covers 40-50% of production
   - If one delays, others continue
   - Additional management but significant risk reduction

3. **Contingency Manufacturing Plan**
   - Identify secondary manufacturer that can produce 50% volume
   - Negotiate backup agreement (costs: lower rate if unused)
   - Keep tooling/specs with secondary manufacturer
   - Activation cost: Only pay if needed

4. **Tiered Fulfillment Strategy**
   - Plan for staggered delivery in campaign messaging
   - First wave (60%): Months 4-6
   - Second wave (30%): Months 6-8
   - Final wave (10%): Months 8-10
   - Reduces pressure on single manufacturing run

---

**Cause #3: Quality Control & Rework Cycles**

**Real-World Scenario (Design-to-Manufacturing Gap)**
- Prototype looked perfect; manufacturing reveals 15% defect rate
- Root cause: Mold wear, assembly variation, component inconsistency
- Solution required: Redesign, new molds ($50K), restart production
- Timeline: 8-week delay minimum

**Prevention Strategies**
1. **First Article Inspection (FAI) Process**
   - Manufacturer produces first 100 units before full batch
   - Comprehensive inspection: Form, fit, function testing
   - Iterate if issues found (2-3 FAI cycles common)
   - Investment: $3,000-8,000 but prevents 100K-unit recall

2. **Process Capability Study**
   - Before production, test manufacturing process
   - Run 5,000-unit trial batch first
   - Measure defect rates and failure modes
   - Identify process improvements
   - Cost: 5,000 extra units (~$40,000-100,000) but worth it

3. **In-Process Quality Checkpoints**
   - Design manufacturing process with inspection gates
   - Sample testing during production (every 1,000 units)
   - Pause production if defect rate exceeds threshold
   - Cost: Labor (2-3 hours per 10,000 units)

4. **Statistical Process Control**
   - Track key metrics: dimension tolerance, defect rate, performance
   - Use SPC charts to detect process drift early
   - Adjust molds/process before full batch degradation
   - Tools: SPC software ($200-500/month)

### Recovery Actions for Manufacturing Delays

**Situation: Manufacturer informs you of 8-week delay (3 weeks into campaign)**

**Immediate Response (Within 24 Hours)**
```
1. Confirm actual delay from manufacturer
   - Get detailed timeline in writing
   - Understand root cause
   - Ask about mitigation efforts

2. Assess impact on critical path
   - How does this affect promised delivery?
   - What are downstream consequences?
   - Is delay acceptable or not?

3. Contact fulfillment/warehouse
   - Pause any storage reservations if possible
   - Request flexible terms
   - Explore contingency storage options

4. Prepare backer communication (draft only)
   - Honest acknowledgment of delay
   - Explanation of cause
   - Specific revised timeline with contingency
```

**Communications Within 48 Hours**

Create tiered messaging:

*For Your Most Engaged Backers (Email):*
```
Subject: Campaign Update - Manufacturing Timeline Adjustment

Hi [Backer Name],

We've discovered that our manufacturing partner will need 8 additional weeks
to complete production due to [specific cause]. While this is not the timeline
we originally promised, we're taking this action to ensure the product quality
you deserve.

Previous estimate: June delivery
New estimate: August delivery

Here's what we're doing:
- Our team is personally overseeing all quality inspections
- We've brought in a quality assurance specialist
- We're splitting production across two partner facilities to accelerate delivery

You have three options:
1. Keep your pledge (delivery in August)
2. Upgrade to a higher tier reward tier for August delivery
3. Request a full refund (processed immediately)

We truly appreciate your patience and support. Updates every two weeks.

[Creator Name]
```

*For General Campaign Update (Public Post):*
```
📢 Important Production Update

Due to unforeseen manufacturing challenges, we're revising our estimated
delivery from June to August. This adjustment ensures we deliver the quality
product you supported.

What we're doing:
✓ Personal oversight of all quality checks
✓ Engaging secondary manufacturer to accelerate timeline
✓ Weekly transparency updates

What this means for you:
- All rewards ship in August with quality guarantee
- Refund option available if you prefer
- Monthly updates with photos/videos of production progress

We apologize for the adjustment and appreciate your patience.
```

**Week 2-3 Recovery Actions**
```
1. Activate secondary manufacturer
   - Begin production immediately
   - Run parallel with primary manufacturer
   - Target: Split 60/40 between two facilities

2. Implement daily monitoring
   - Call manufacturing partners daily
   - Weekly video inspection of production progress
   - Share photos/videos with backers (transparency)

3. Communicate interim milestones
   - "Week 1: 30% of components received"
   - "Week 3: First molded parts completed"
   - "Week 5: Assembly begins on first batch"
   - Keeps backers engaged and informed

4. Prepare for further contingencies
   - If delay extends further, plan for:
     * Partial fulfillment (send 50% of backers early)
     * Gift cards/credit for delayed backers
     * Priority shipping for earliest backers
```

**Ongoing Communication (Every 2 Weeks)**
- Photos/videos of actual production
- Percentage complete metrics
- Revised delivery predictions with confidence level
- Transparent discussion of any new challenges
- Thank you message reinforcing value

## Shipping & Logistics Issues

### Common Shipping Crises

**Crisis #1: Carrier Strikes or Service Suspensions**

**Real-World Scenario (2023 UPS Strike)**
- UPS strike begins mid-campaign fulfillment period
- Service temporarily suspended for non-essential shipments
- Backers expecting August delivery now looking at September
- Peak fulfillment period at standstill

**Prevention/Response Strategy**

*Prevention:*
1. **Multiple Carrier Contracts** (Pre-campaign)
   - Don't rely solely on UPS, FedEx, or USPS
   - Establish accounts with 3+ carriers
   - Negotiate rate agreements with all
   - Cost: Minimal (basic account setup)

2. **Flexible Fulfillment Terms** (In Campaign)
   - State in TOU: "Delivery times are estimates, subject to carrier delays"
   - Mention potential carrier service disruptions
   - Reserve right to switch carriers

*Response (If Strike Occurs During Fulfillment):*
1. Immediately implement carrier switching plan
   - Divide shipments: 50% UPS, 40% FedEx, 10% USPS
   - Renegotiate rates with remaining carriers
   - Prioritize essential shipments

2. Prepare backer communication
   - Explain strike situation
   - Clarify revised timeline
   - Emphasize transparency and adaptability

3. Implement accelerated fulfillment for non-affected carriers
   - Fulfill USPS shipments immediately
   - Defer UPS shipments to post-strike
   - Use multiple methods to show progress

---

**Crisis #2: International Shipping Complications**

**Real-World Scenario (Customs Delays)**
- Ship 10,000 units from China to distribute internationally
- Units held in customs for 4+ weeks
- Backers in EU, UK, Australia all experience 4-week delays
- Unexpected duties/tariffs increase shipping costs 20%

**Prevention Strategy**

1. **Pre-Campaign Clearance Planning**
   - Classify product for customs purposes (HS codes)
   - Identify required certifications by country
   - Arrange customs broker agreements
   - Investigate duties/taxes by destination country
   - Cost: $1,000-3,000 initial planning

2. **Bonded Warehouse Strategy**
   - Ship inventory to bonded warehouse in regional hub
   - Warehouse handles local fulfillment and customs clearance
   - Reduces per-unit shipping cost and delays
   - Hubs: UK for Europe, Singapore for Asia, Mexico for Americas
   - Cost: 3-5% of shipping value but saves 2-4 weeks

3. **Clear Customs Documentation**
   - Prepare detailed packing lists and commercial invoices
   - Accurate value declarations (prevents suspicion)
   - Include required compliance certifications
   - Mark packages clearly with contents
   - Cost: $200-500 customs broker time

*Response (If Customs Delays Occur):*
1. Hire customs broker immediately
   - Broker can expedite clearance process
   - Cost: $500-1,500 per customs entry but worth the speed
   - Broker contacts: A.P. Moller-Maersk, DHL, Hellmann Worldwide

2. Communicate with affected backers
   - Explain customs situation (not creator's fault)
   - Provide tracking information
   - Estimate revised delivery date
   - Offer partial refund if delivery >60 days late

3. Document all issues for future campaigns
   - Track where delays occurred
   - Calculate tariff/duty impact
   - Adjust pricing for future international shipping

**Crisis #3: Lost, Damaged, or Stolen Shipments**

**Real-World Scenario (10,000-Unit Shipment Disappears)**
- TruckLoad of 10,000 units disappears from logistics partner warehouse
- Theft or misplacement (never determined)
- Affects 1,000 backers who don't receive products
- Manufacturer won't provide free replacement
- Insurance claim process takes 3+ months

**Prevention Strategy**

1. **Insurance Requirements**
   - Require fulfillment partner to carry cargo insurance
   - Verify insurance covers full value of inventory
   - Request certificates of insurance
   - Cost: Usually already included in fulfillment contract

2. **In-Transit Tracking**
   - Use GPS tracking for high-value shipments
   - Verify container seals and documentation
   - Photograph shipments before departure
   - Chain of custody documentation
   - Cost: $200-500 per major shipment

3. **Warehouse Security Audit**
   - Before sending inventory, audit partner warehouse
   - Check security systems: cameras, alarms, access control
   - Verify insurance and bonding
   - Review past customer references
   - Cost: 4 hours of your time

*Response (If Loss/Damage Occurs):*
1. Document everything immediately
   - Photos of damaged goods
   - Written reports from warehouse
   - Shipping manifests and weight tickets
   - Carrier claims forms

2. Initiate insurance claim
   - Contact carrier and file claim within 30 days
   - Provide all documentation
   - Insurance company investigates and settles
   - Timeline: 6-12 weeks typical

3. Manage backer expectations
   - Don't offer replacement until claim resolved
   - Explain insurance claim timeline
   - Offer interim solution: partial refund or credit toward next product
   - Keep backers updated weekly

4. Activate manufacturing contingency
   - If insurance won't cover, negotiate with manufacturer
   - Ask for replacement production at reduced cost
   - If manufacturer won't cooperate: pursue legal action or refunds

## Product Quality Problems

### Real-World Quality Crisis Scenarios

**Scenario #1: Widespread Defect in First Batch**

**Situation**:
- First 5,000 units reach backers
- 15% have critical defects (button doesn't work, battery drains immediately)
- Backers report issues within days
- Manufacturer admits: Mold defect found in first production run

**Root Cause Analysis Process**
1. Collect data from defective units
   - How many affected? (15% = critical, <2% = acceptable)
   - What's the specific failure mode?
   - When did failure occur? (immediate vs after use)
   - Is it manufacturing defect or design flaw?

2. Test units in lab
   - Reproduce failure mode
   - Determine root cause
   - Assess risk of future occurrence
   - Document findings

3. Determine scope
   - Does defect affect remaining inventory?
   - How many total units potentially affected?
   - Can it be fixed in field vs must be recalled?

**Response Strategy**

*Immediate (24 Hours)*
```
1. Contact manufacturer
   - Report defect and request documentation
   - Ask if they're aware of the issue
   - Request immediate investigation
   - Get timeline for response

2. Prepare temporary solution
   - Determine workaround for backers (if any)
   - Example: "Reset the device by holding button X for 10 seconds"
   - Document solution

3. Draft communication (not yet sent)
   - Acknowledge the issue
   - Explain you're investigating
   - Provide timeline for solution announcement
```

*Within 72 Hours - Decision on Resolution*

*Option A: Recall & Replacement (Best Case)*
- Manufacturer agrees to fix/replace affected units
- Cost: Manufacturer absorbs cost (avoid this negotiation)
- Backer process: Return defective unit, receive replacement
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks
- Risk: Additional delay, more logistics costs

```
Backer Communication:
"We've identified a defect in [X units]. Here's what's happening:

Option 1 (Free): Return defective unit via prepaid shipping.
We'll send replacement within 5 business days of receipt.

Option 2 (Immediate): Keep the unit and we'll send $X credit
toward your next purchase with us.

Option 3: Full refund processed immediately if you prefer."
```

*Option B: Partial Refund (If Recall Not Feasible)*
- Defect is minor and doesn't affect core functionality
- Fix is available via software update or simple mod
- Cost: Offer 20-30% refund to affected backers
- Backer satisfaction: Medium (some unhappy)

```
Backer Communication:
"We've found a defect affecting [specific feature].
While we work on a permanent fix, we're offering:

✓ $15 refund (30% of $49 pledge)
✓ Free shipping on replacement when available
✓ Lifetime warranty on the specific part that failed

We expect the fix to be available in [X weeks]."
```

*Option C: Replace with Alternative Product*
- Original product cannot be economically fixed
- Offer upgrade to newer/different product
- Cost: Likely at a loss, but maintains goodwill
- Backer satisfaction: High if upgrade is valuable

```
Backer Communication:
"We apologize for the defect in [Product A].
As a gesture of goodwill, we're offering:

✓ Upgrade to [Product B] (typically $99) for no additional cost
✓ Or full refund
✓ Or keep original and receive $30 credit"
```

**Ongoing Communication During Resolution**
- Weekly updates on fix progress
- Share photos/videos of testing
- Provide realistic timeline (don't over-promise)
- Acknowledge frustration and thank backers for patience
- Offer additional compensation if delay extends beyond 4 weeks

**Scenario #2: Design Defect Discovered Post-Launch**

**Situation**:
- Product works initially but fails after 2-4 weeks of use
- Root cause: Design flaw not caught in prototype testing
- Examples: Inadequate heat dissipation, poor solder joints, insufficient structural support
- Affects 40% of delivered units within 6 weeks

**Critical Question: Can this be fixed?**

*Option A: Software/Firmware Fix (Best Case)*
- Issue is software related
- Push update that resolves issue
- Cost: Engineering time only ($5K-20K)
- Backer effort: Download and install update
- Timeline: 1-2 weeks

```
Communication:
"We've identified a software issue affecting [feature].
Good news: We've developed a fix!

Steps to install:
1. Visit [URL]
2. Download the latest firmware
3. Connect device to computer
4. Run update (takes 5 minutes)

The fix is now live. All future units ship with the updated firmware."
```

*Option B: Hardware Fix in Field (Moderate Case)*
- Issue is hardware but can be fixed by user or service provider
- Examples: Replace cable, reseat component, tighten screw
- Cost: Shipping + labor + replacement part ($5-50 per unit)
- Backer effort: Either return unit or perform fix themselves
- Timeline: 2-4 weeks

```
Communication:
"We've identified a hardware issue and have a simple fix:

For self-repair (easy):
[Step-by-step instructions]
[Link to YouTube video guide]
Parts kit shipping free upon request

For warranty service:
Return the unit via prepaid shipping.
We'll repair and return within 5 business days."
```

*Option C: Recall & Replacement (Worst Case)*
- Hardware issue cannot be fixed in field
- Must recall all affected units
- Manufacturer manufactures replacement units
- Cost: 100% replacement cost (~$10-20 per unit × 40% of units)
- Backer effort: Return unit and wait for replacement
- Timeline: 4-8 weeks
- Backer satisfaction: Low (long process, inconvenience)

```
Communication:
"We've discovered a design flaw in units shipped before [DATE].
We're issuing a voluntary recall.

What to do:
1. Return the unit via prepaid shipping
2. We'll send a replacement within 7 days of receipt
3. All future units have the improved design

Why we're doing this:
[Explain the specific risk - don't minimize]

Questions?
[Email address and phone number for support]"
```

### Quality Control Lessons from Failed Campaigns

**Lesson #1: Prototype ≠ Production**

The Zano drone campaign ($3.25M raised, 92% refunded) reveals this fundamental truth:
- Prototype worked perfectly in controlled conditions
- Scaling to 10,000 units broke the technology
- Manufacturing tolerances too tight for mass production
- Sensor calibration issues at scale

**Lesson Application**:
- Always produce 1,000-5,000 unit pilot batch BEFORE full campaign fulfillment
- Test extensively in real-world conditions (not just lab)
- Identify manufacturing challenges early
- Budget: +$20K-50K but prevents complete campaign failure

**Lesson #2: User Behavior Differs from Testing**

Many campaigns discover that users interact with products differently than expected:
- Coolest Cooler: Blender function damaged quickly from rough use
- Smart Ring: Fitness accuracy worse than lab tests due to how users wore it
- Bike Light: Battery drain faster because users left it on constantly

**Lesson Application**:
- Create "backer testing panel" pre-campaign (100-200 real users)
- Have them use product for 2-4 weeks in actual conditions
- Collect feedback and iterate
- Cost: Free units + modest compensation (~$2,000-5,000 total)

**Lesson #3: Cost-Cutting Compromises Quality**

When manufacturing costs exceed budget, creators often cut corners:
- Cheaper plastics crack under normal use
- Thinner components fail sooner
- Reduced QA leads to higher defect rates
- One cost-cutting measure often triggers cascade failures

**Lesson Application**:
- Don't negotiate manufacturing costs below a threshold
- Better to reduce unit volume at higher quality than more units at lower quality
- Document all cost-cutting decisions and their risk impacts
- Have quality baseline: Any product <2% defect rate is acceptable

## Communication & Reputation Management

### Crisis Communication Framework

**The 5-Minute Rule**
When a major issue occurs:
1. Stop and assess (1 minute)
2. Gather key facts (2 minutes)
3. Write initial statement (2 minutes)
4. Send to core team for feedback (0 minutes - parallel)
5. Post update to campaign (ASAP)

**The key**: Post SOMETHING within 5 hours of learning about crisis.
Silence is interpreted as not caring or hiding.

---

### What NOT to Say

**Anti-Patterns that Damage Credibility**

❌ **"This is unpredictable and beyond our control"**
(Without explaining what you're doing about it)
- Backers funded YOU to manage these risks
- Saying it's beyond your control sounds like you gave up

✓ **INSTEAD**: "We've encountered [specific challenge]. Here's our action plan..."

❌ **"We're working on it"**
(Vague with no timeline)
- Backers need specificity
- "We're working on it" could mean 2 weeks or 6 months

✓ **INSTEAD**: "We expect resolution by [date]. Here's our progress so far..."

❌ **"Some backers have complained"**
(Dismissive language)
- Backers aren't complaining; they're expressing legitimate concerns
- Defensive tone makes you seem like you don't care

✓ **INSTEAD**: "We've heard concerns about [issue] and want to address them..."

❌ **"We never had this problem before"**
(Implies you don't know this market)
- Backers funded your expertise, not your inexperience
- Admitting you didn't anticipate predictable problems

✓ **INSTEAD**: "We've learned [insight] and here's how we're preventing this..."

## Financial & Refund Issues

### Refund Crisis Scenarios

**Scenario #1: Unexpected Expenses Exceed Budget**

**Situation**:
- Raised: $100,000
- Manufacturing costs: $50,000 (as expected)
- Shipping costs: $45,000 (20% higher than estimated)
- After fulfillment: Only have $5,000 left
- Campaign promises customer service support for 1 year (requires staff)

**Analysis**:
```
Budget Shortfall: $10,000-20,000
Remaining Backers To Fulfill: 500
Cost Per Unit: $10-20 extra

This is a crisis because:
- Can't fund promised customer service
- Can't cover unexpected return/replacement costs
- No contingency buffer
```

**Resolution Options**

*Option A: Reduce Scope (Not Recommended)*
- Stop customer service after 6 months (breaks promise)
- No free replacements (violates warranty)
- Backers feel cheated

*Option B: Seek Funding*
- Personal loan or investment from founders
- Ask manufacturer for extended payment terms
- Negotiate with fulfillment partner for payment plan
- Cost: Interest or dilution, but keeps promises

*Option C: Be Transparent & Offer Choice*
```
"Dear backers,

We want to be completely honest about our financial situation.

Here's what happened:
- Shipping costs exceeded our estimates by $15,000
- We're now short on funds to fully deliver promised customer service

Here are your options:

Option 1: Keep your order as-is
- We reduce customer service from 24/7 to business hours only
- We may need 2-3 weeks for replacements vs 1-week promise
- Full refund available if you'd prefer

Option 2: Receive a partial refund
- Get $15 back (15% of your pledge)
- We maintain full promised service levels
- You keep your product

Option 3: Full refund
- If you'd prefer to exit, we understand
- Full refund processed within 3-5 days

We take responsibility for underestimating costs.
Option 2 (partial refund) helps us keep our other promises to you."
```

**Scenario #2: Payment Processor Issues**

**Situation**:
- Campaign raised $200,000
- Stripe (or other processor) flags account as "high risk"
- $50,000 is held in reserve for 180 days (chargeback risk)
- You only have $150,000 available but need $200,000 for fulfillment

**Prevention**:
1. Maintain good customer service history
   - Low chargeback rate (<0.5%)
   - High approval rate for refund requests
   - Monitor fraud carefully

2. Communicate card details clearly
   - Make terms clear in campaign
   - Don't hide fees or charges
   - Send clear invoice descriptions

3. Have backup payment methods
   - Use multiple payment processors
   - Keep money in multiple accounts
   - Diversify rather than concentrating with one processor

**Response (If Held Funds)**:
1. Contact processor immediately
   - Explain situation
   - Provide campaign details
   - Request release of held funds

2. Provide documentation
   - Campaign page and details
   - Backer fulfillment plan
   - Manufacturing contracts proving legitimacy

3. Request graduated release
   - Instead of holding $50K for 180 days
   - Ask for: Release $10K after each 30 days
   - Reduces risk for processor while maintaining some protection

4. Consider factoring service
   - Company buys held funds at discount (2-5%)
   - Cost: High but get cash immediately
   - Examples: Runway, Clearco, Fundbox

### Refund Request Handling

**Philosophy**: Refunds should be easy and generous.

Each refunded backer:
- Costs you: Payment processing fee + platform fee already spent (sunk cost)
- Costs them: Hassle of returning product, disappointment
- Saves them: Having product they don't want

**Process for Handling Refunds**

1. **Make It Easy**
   - No questions asked policy
   - Single-click refund via email link
   - Full amount including shipping if returned within 30 days

2. **Respond Quickly**
   - Refund request received: Day 1
   - Refund processed: Day 2
   - Funds to backer's account: Day 5
   - Follow-up email: Day 7

3. **Optional: Recovery Offer**
   - When refund is requested, offer: "Would you prefer [partial refund] + [credit toward next product]?"
   - Example: "Refund $35 now, keep $10 credit" instead of $45 refund
   - Keeps customer in your ecosystem

4. **Document Everything**
   - Keep refund log with dates and reasons
   - If >5% refund rate: Investigate why
   - Use feedback to improve future campaigns

## Crisis Response Playbook

### Pre-Crisis Preparation

**Step 1: Prepare (Before Crisis Occurs)**

```
☐ Identify potential crises:
  ☐ Manufacturing delays (3+ weeks)
  ☐ Quality defects in >5% of units
  ☐ Shipping losses or damage
  ☐ Component supply shortages
  ☐ Team member departure
  ☐ Negative press/review
  ☐ Backer refund requests >10%
  ☐ Platform account issues

☐ For each crisis, prepare:
  ☐ Who decides response? (you, team, lawyer?)
  ☐ What's the communication plan?
  ☐ What are acceptable costs to fix? (max refunds, max credits)
  ☐ Draft template statements

☐ Set up crisis communication infrastructure:
  ☐ Email list of all backers (exported monthly)
  ☐ Draft email templates (save in secure location)
  ☐ Communication decision-making flowchart
  ☐ Escalation contacts (lawyer, insurance, etc.)
```

---

### Crisis Response Flowchart

**When Problem Occurs**:

```
1. ASSESS (15 minutes)
   ├─ What happened exactly?
   ├─ How many people affected?
   ├─ What's the financial impact?
   ├─ Is anyone hurt? (Legal implications)
   └─ How urgent is response needed?

2. INVESTIGATE (1 hour)
   ├─ Root cause analysis
   ├─ Is this preventable in future?
   ├─ Who's responsible? (Not blame, but accountability)
   └─ What's our best/worst case scenario?

3. DECIDE (1-4 hours)
   ├─ Can we fix it? (Yes/No)
   ├─ What's the fix cost? (Budget available?)
   ├─ What's our legal exposure? (Consult lawyer if >$5K impact)
   ├─ What are backer options? (Refund, replacement, credit)
   └─ What's our timeline? (When can we communicate?)

4. COMMUNICATE (Within 24 hours)
   ├─ Address to all affected backers (not public site)
   ├─ Acknowledge the problem clearly
   ├─ Explain root cause honestly
   ├─ Present solution options
   ├─ Set clear timeline
   └─ Make action easy

5. EXECUTE (Begin immediately)
   ├─ Start fix process
   ├─ Track progress daily
   ├─ Send updates every 3-7 days
   ├─ Be prepared to adjust plan
   └─ Thank backers for patience

6. FOLLOW UP (Post-crisis)
   ├─ Once resolved, send thank you message
   ├─ Offer small compensation (credit, discount on next product)
   ├─ Conduct lessons-learned analysis
   ├─ Update playbook with learnings
   └─ Share (anonymized) case study to help others
```

### Decision Matrix for Offering Compensation

**Should we offer refunds, credits, or replacement?**

```
SITUATION ANALYSIS:

If defect is minor (cosmetic, doesn't affect function):
├─ Offer: $10-20 credit toward next product
├─ Affected: 1-5%
└─ Total cost: $1,000-10,000

If defect is moderate (some function lost, workaround exists):
├─ Offer: 20-30% refund OR replacement
├─ Affected: 5-15%
└─ Total cost: $5,000-30,000

If defect is major (core function fails):
├─ Offer: Full refund OR replacement at no cost
├─ Affected: 10-40%
└─ Total cost: $10,000-80,000

If delay is minor (<4 weeks):
├─ Offer: Tracking priority + shipping upgrade
├─ Affected: All
└─ Cost: Shipping differential only

If delay is major (8+ weeks):
├─ Offer: Partial refund (10-20%) OR product upgrade
├─ Affected: All
└─ Cost: $5,000-50,000

If your mistake/misrepresentation:
├─ Always: Offer full refund (no questions)
├─ Also offer: 50% credit for replacement if they want to try again
└─ Cost: Full refund amount
```

### Legal Escalation Decision Tree

**Do you need to talk to a lawyer?**

```
Crisis cost < $5,000
└─ No lawyer needed (yet)
    - Document everything
    - Keep records

Crisis cost $5,000 - $25,000
├─ Consult lawyer if:
│  ├─ Safety issue involved?
│  ├─ Defective product that could injure?
│  ├─ False claims made in campaign?
│  └─ IP infringement concern?
├─ Otherwise: Proceed with recovery plan
└─ Cost: $500-1,500 consultation

Crisis cost > $25,000
├─ Consult lawyer immediately
├─ Have insurance involved (if applicable)
├─ Document everything per lawyer advice
└─ Cost: $2,000-5,000+ but essential

Always consult lawyer if:
├─ Safety hazard
├─ Product liability claim
├─ IP infringement accusation
├─ Regulatory agency involvement (FTC, CPSC)
└─ Major media coverage
```
