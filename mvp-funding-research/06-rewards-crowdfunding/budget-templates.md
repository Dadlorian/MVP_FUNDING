## Budget Framework & Principles

### The 40-40-20 Rule

For every dollar raised in a successful crowdfunding campaign, plan allocation as:

```
40% = Product (Manufacturing, Materials, Components, Assembly)
40% = Operations (Shipping, Fulfillment, Packaging, Returns)
20% = Overhead (Tax, Platform Fees, Legal, Team, Contingency)
```

**Important Caveat**: This assumes 40% funding efficiency. Many campaigns only retain 60-70% of gross funding after platform fees, payment processing, and failed payment chargebacks.

**Revised Realistic Model**
```
Gross Funds Raised:                    $100,000
Minus: Platform Fee (3-5%)              -$4,000
Minus: Payment Processing (2-3%)        -$2,500
Minus: Failed Payments/Chargebacks (1%) -$1,000
--------------------------------------------
Net Funds Available:                    $92,500

Allocation of $92,500:
Manufacturing/COGS (40%)                $37,000
Shipping/Fulfillment (35%)              $32,375
Overhead/Contingency (25%)              $23,125
```

---

### Budget Planning Methodology

**Step 1: Research Phase (Before Campaign Launch)**
- Manufacturing quotes from 3+ vendors (2-4 weeks)
- Shipping quotes from major carriers (1-2 weeks)
- Labor/fulfillment estimates (1-2 weeks)
- Platform fee structure confirmation (1-2 days)

**Step 2: Build Base Budget**
- Calculate per-unit COGS from best manufacturer quote
- Calculate per-unit shipping weight and dimensions
- Estimate fulfillment hours and labor costs
- Project percentage breakdown

**Step 3: Add Contingency Multipliers**
- Manufacturing delays: +10-15% to timeline, +5-10% to cost
- Shipping rate increases: +5-10% to shipping budget
- Labor overruns: +20% to fulfillment estimate
- Unexpected costs: +10-15% overall contingency

**Step 4: Model Multiple Scenarios**
- Low funding scenario (75% of goal)
- Target funding scenario (100% of goal)
- High funding scenario (200% of goal)
- Stretch scenario (500% of goal)

## Detailed Cost Breakdowns

### Manufacturing Cost Analysis (Hardware Products)

**Labor Rates by Region (2024)**
```
United States:     $18-35/hour + benefits
Mexico/Central Am: $6-12/hour + benefits
China:             $4-8/hour + benefits
Vietnam:           $4-7/hour + benefits
India:             $3-6/hour + benefits

Factor: Labor rate affects quality control ability
Higher-cost regions = easier oversight, quality guarantee
Lower-cost regions = require longer lead times, more QC
```

**Component Cost Tiers**

For 10,000-unit production run:
```
Small components (switches, sensors): $0.50-$5 per unit
Mid-range processors (ARM-based):     $5-15 per unit
High-end processors (AI/ML capable):  $15-50 per unit
RF/wireless components:               $2-10 per unit
Display/screen elements:              $5-30 per unit
Power supply/battery:                 $3-20 per unit
Case/housing (injection molded):      $2-10 per unit
```

**Batch Size Impact on Unit Cost**

```
Component ABC (mid-range):

Order Size:     Unit Cost:  Tooling Fee:  Setup Fee:
1,000 units     $8.00       $10,000       $2,000
5,000 units     $6.50       $10,000       $2,000
10,000 units    $5.00       $15,000       $2,500
50,000 units    $3.50       $30,000       $5,000
100,000 units   $2.80       $50,000       $7,500

Decision matrix: At 5K units, total COGS = (5000 × $6.50) + $12,000 = $44,500
At 10K units, total COGS = (10,000 × $5.00) + $17,500 = $67,500
Per-unit at 5K: $8.90
Per-unit at 10K: $6.75

BUT funding risk: If you only raise $75K and need 10K batch, you're underwater.
Conservative: Quote 3-5 vendor options and model at 3 different batch sizes.
```

## Financial Modeling & Scenarios

### Break-Even Analysis

**Scenario: T-Shirt Campaign**

```
Retail Price per Shirt:                 $25
Production Cost per Shirt:               $4.50
Shipping per Shirt (domestic avg):       $5.00
Fulfillment Labor per Shirt:             $0.75
Platform Fees (per shirt @ 4%):          $1.00
Payment Processing (per shirt @ 2.5%):   $0.625
Returns/Replacement (per shirt, 5%):     $0.375
------------------------------------------
TOTAL COST PER SHIRT:                    $12.25

PROFIT PER SHIRT:                        $12.75
PROFIT MARGIN:                           51%

Break-even number of backers:
If campaign goal = $10,000
Then minimum shirts to break even: $10,000 / $12.75 = 784 shirts
```

**Scenario: Smartphone Accessory Campaign**

```
Retail Price per Unit:                   $49.99
Production Cost per Unit:                $8.00
Shipping per Unit (intl avg):            $8.00
Fulfillment Labor & Packaging:           $2.00
Platform Fees (@ 5%):                    $2.50
Payment Processing (@ 2.5%):             $1.25
Returns/Warranty (@ 8%):                 $4.00
------------------------------------------
TOTAL COST PER UNIT:                     $25.75

PROFIT PER UNIT:                         $24.24
PROFIT MARGIN:                           48.5%

If you raise $100,000 and sell 2,000 units:
Gross Profit: $100,000 - (2000 × $25.75) = $48,500

BUT Subtract:
Manufacturing setup/tooling:             -$15,000
Pre-campaign development:                -$10,000
Campaign marketing:                      -$15,000
Contingency/overhead:                    -$8,000
------------------------------------------
NET PROFIT:                              $500 (barely break even!)

Lesson: Many campaigns show accounting profit but have low actual cash profit
after all pre- and post-campaign costs.
```

### Sensitivity Analysis

**Question: What happens if manufacturing costs increase 20%?**

```
Original Model ($100K campaign, 2000 units):
- Manufacturing cost: $8.00/unit
- Total COGS: $16,000
- Net profit: $500

New Scenario (Manufacturing +20%):
- Manufacturing cost: $9.60/unit
- Total COGS: $19,200
- Profit reduced: -$3,200 (now negative!)

Mitigation Options:
1. Reduce promised delivery amount (cut 400 units)
2. Increase retail pricing (+$3-5)
3. Reduce fulfillment quality (risky)
4. Negotiate manufacturing discount through larger batch
5. Use cheaper materials/components (affects quality)
```

**Question: What if you only raise 50% of goal?**

```
Original Projection:
- Campaign Goal: $100,000
- Expected Units: 2,000
- Manufacturing batch: 10,000 (not economical for 1000 units)

If You Raise $50,000 Only:
Option 1: Fulfill to all 1,000 backers (bad idea)
- Manufacturing: Batch only 1,000 (higher per-unit cost)
- Per-unit cost: $12 (vs $8 at 10K batch)
- Total COGS: $12,000
- Fulfillment: $25,000
- Net: Negative cash flow

Option 2: Raise prices / adjust tiers
- Encourage upsizing to higher-value rewards
- Or refund and restart campaign
- OR scale manufacturing down proportionally

Option 3: Find co-manufacturer for remainder
- Partner with another similar campaign
- Reduce per-unit cost through shared batch
```

### Profitability Timeline

**Typical Hardware Product Campaign Cash Flow**

```
Month 1: Campaign Running
  In:    $100,000 (gross)
  Out:   -$20,000 (marketing, overhead)
  Net:   +$80,000

Month 2: Payment Clearing & Mfg Starting
  In:    -$15,000 (payment processing, fees)
  Out:   -$45,000 (manufacturing deposit)
  Net:   +$20,000

Month 3-4: Full Manufacturing
  In:    -$0 (no new revenue)
  Out:   -$100,000 (mfg final payment, logistics)
  Net:   -$100,000

Month 5: Fulfillment
  In:    -$0 (no new revenue)
  Out:   -$40,000 (shipping, labor, packaging)
  Net:   -$40,000

Month 6: Tail Fulfillment
  In:    -$0 (no new revenue)
  Out:   -$8,000 (final shipments, returns)
  Net:   -$8,000

------------------------------------------
6-Month Total
  Gross In:      $85,000 (after fees)
  Gross Out:     $193,000
  Net Position:  -$108,000

BREAK-EVEN POINT: Month 8-10 (if selling remaining inventory)
                  OR never (if campaign was truly one-time)
```

**Key Takeaway**: Crowdfunding campaigns typically burn cash for 3-6 months
after funds are collected, with break-even point months after fulfillment.
This is why adequate working capital is critical.
