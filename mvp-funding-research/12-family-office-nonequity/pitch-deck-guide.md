# Family Office Pitch Deck Guide: Examples for Different Family Office Types

## Overview

This guide provides specific pitch deck templates and examples tailored to different types of family offices. The key insight: family offices are NOT monolithic. A pitch that works for a return-focused office will fail with a mission-focused office, and vice versa.

This document includes:
1. Pitch deck frameworks for 4 different family office types
2. Slide-by-slide examples and language
3. Design and delivery recommendations
4. Common mistakes to avoid
5. Customization checklist

---

## Part 1: Understanding Family Office Buyer Personas

Before designing your deck, understand which type of family office you're pitching:

### Type 1: Return-Focused Offices (Looking for Financial Returns)

**Profile**:
- Prioritize financial returns above all else
- Question: "Will this deliver attractive ROIC?"
- Background: Often entrepreneurs or finance-focused families
- Investment committee structure: Formal, quantitative-focused
- Timeline: 4-12 weeks decision

**What they care about**:
- Unit economics (CAC, LTV, margins)
- Revenue growth trajectory
- Path to profitability or cash flow positive
- Comparable returns (how does this vs. other investments?)
- Market size and TAM
- Founder execution track record

**What they don't care about**:
- Feel-good mission statements (unless they drive returns)
- Pretty design (substance over style)
- Industry trends (unless they validate market)

**Revenue share structure they like**:
- 3-5% revenue share with clear cap
- Realistic conservative model with 3-5 year payback
- Quarterly reporting with specific KPIs
- ROFR on future rounds

### Type 2: Mission-Focused Offices (Looking for Impact)

**Profile**:
- Prioritize social/environmental impact
- Question: "How many lives will this improve?"
- Background: Often philanthropic founders or values-driven families
- Investment committee structure: Mix of family + impact specialists
- Timeline: 6-14 weeks decision

**What they care about**:
- Measurable social/environmental impact
- Who specifically benefits (how many people? which demographics?)
- Defensibility of impact claims
- Scalability of impact model
- Alignment with family values/mission
- Sustainability of model (doesn't just depend on charity)

**What they don't care about**:
- Massive financial returns (though want sustainability)
- Venture-scale growth if it compromises mission
- Aggressive sales tactics

**Revenue share structure they like**:
- 50-100% grant component (for impact funding)
- 0-3% revenue share (secondary to mission)
- Impact measurement and reporting built in
- Long-term partnership focused on sustainability

### Type 3: Industry/Expertise-Driven Offices (Looking to Leverage Expertise)

**Profile**:
- Founders built businesses in specific industry
- Question: "Is this founder capable of winning in this space?"
- Background: Retired entrepreneurs with deep sector knowledge
- Investment committee structure: Often just founder/CIO, quick decisions
- Timeline: 2-6 weeks (fast!)

**What they care about**:
- Founder's background and credibility in sector
- Go-to-market strategy (do you know how to actually sell in this space?)
- Competitive positioning (why will you win?)
- Team capability (do they have right experience?)
- Market entry strategy and timing
- Ability to benefit from family office's network/expertise

**What they don't care about**:
- Detailed financial models (they can see through questionable assumptions)
- Polished decks (they want substance)
- TAM analysis (they know the market)

**Revenue share structure they like**:
- Advisory board seat is more important than financial terms
- Moderate check size ($150K-$500K) with strong governance
- Quarterly strategic reviews
- Clear value-add opportunities from family office expertise

### Type 4: Network/Access Offices (Looking to Deploy through Ecosystem)

**Profile**:
- Built portfolio across ecosystem
- Question: "Does this help our entire ecosystem succeed?"
- Background: Often tech or fintech founders
- Investment committee structure: Collaborative, looking for strategic fit
- Timeline: 4-10 weeks

**What they care about**:
- Ecosystem role (does this fill a gap or strengthen network?)
- Customer synergies (can other portfolio companies benefit?)
- Network effect (does this make other companies more valuable?)
- Platform potential (could this become central hub?)
- Founder's ability to build company AND collaborate

**What they don't care about**:
- Deep technical details
- Pure financial returns (ecosystem returns matter more)
- Industry-specific expertise (they have diverse portfolio)

**Revenue share structure they like**:
- Hybrid structures that benefit ecosystem
- Board access + financial terms
- Regular "ecosystem meetings"
- Mechanisms to share learnings across portfolio

## Part 2: Deck Frameworks by Family Office Type

### TEMPLATE A: Return-Focused Office Pitch Deck

**Deck Title**: "Revenue Share Investment Opportunity: [Company Name]"

**Slide 1: Title Slide**
```
PITCH DECK TITLE:
Revenue Share Investment Opportunity

COMPANY NAME: [Company]
FOUNDERS: [Founder 1], [Founder 2]
DATE: [Date]

HEADLINE (20-word summary):
[Company] is building [solution] for [market], projected to reach
$[X]M ARR by Year 3 with unit economics supporting [X]% revenue share.
```

**Slide 2: The Investment Thesis (1 slide)**
```
HEADLINE: Why [Company] Represents Strong Non-Equity Investment

KEY INVESTMENT HIGHLIGHTS:
• $[X]M TAM in [market], growing [X]% annually
• Company has achieved $[X]K revenue with [X]% gross margins
• Clear path to $[X]M ARR by Year 3 (supported by detailed model)
• Revenue share model supports [X]% annual returns on capital
• [X] founders have previous exits/relevant experience

CAPITAL USE:
$[X]M capital will be deployed:
- [X]% to product development (close feature gaps vs. competitors)
- [X]% to sales/marketing (execute go-to-market)
- [X]% to operations/team (hire [roles])

RETURN PROFILE:
If conservative scenario: $[X]M cumulative revenue share by Year 5
If realistic scenario: $[X]M cumulative revenue share by Year 5
If optimistic scenario: $[X]M cumulative revenue share by Year 5

Recommendation: Detailed financial models attached in appendix.
```

**Slide 3: Market Opportunity & Traction (1 slide)**
```
HEADLINE: Massive Market, Real Traction Today
```

**Slide 9: Non-Equity Structure Explained (1 slide)**
```
HEADLINE: Why Non-Equity Makes Sense for Both Sides

FOR INVESTORS:
✓ No equity dilution
✓ Founders fully aligned (they keep 100% ownership)
✓ Revenue share provides [X]% annual returns
✓ Predictable, capped exposure ($[X]M maximum)
✓ Clear milestones and reporting
✓ [Optional: ROFR on future equity rounds]

FOR COMPANY:
✓ Maintains 100% control and ownership
✓ No investor seats or veto rights required
✓ Faster decision timeline (4-8 weeks vs. 6+ months for equity)
✓ Lower dilution than equity round
✓ Capital can still fund aggressive growth
✓ [Optional: benefit from investor expertise via board seat]

PROPOSED STRUCTURE:
Investment: $[X]M
Components:
  - $[X]M grant (non-dilutive R&D capital)
  - $[X]M revenue share (3% of gross revenue, capped at $[X]M)
Term: [5-7] years
Reporting: Monthly revenue statements, quarterly calls, annual in-person

This structure aligns incentives: Company wins = Investor wins.
```

## Part 4: Delivery Tips for Different Family Office Types

### Return-Focused Offices

**Tone**: Data-driven, confident, specific
**Pacing**: Fast—they know what they want to hear
**Q&A approach**: Answer quickly and directly; don't over-explain
**What impresses them**: Specific metrics, realistic projections, clear unit economics
**What turns them off**: Vague language, wishful thinking, overconfident projections

**Delivery tips**:
- Lead with numbers
- Use specific examples (not hypotheticals)
- Be ready for tough financial questions
- Show you've stress-tested your model
- Demonstrate founder discipline

### Mission-Focused Offices

**Tone**: Passionate, authentic, evidence-based
**Pacing**: Slower—they want to understand impact depth
**Q&A approach**: Answer thoughtfully; tell stories; use examples
**What impresses them**: Real participant stories, measured outcomes, commitment to solving problem
**What turns them off**: Shallow impact claims, "growth for growth's sake," lack of measurement

**Delivery tips**:
- Lead with mission/problem
- Share real participant stories
- Show rigorous impact measurement
- Discuss sustainability long-term
- Acknowledge challenges and how you're addressing them

### Industry/Expertise-Focused Offices

**Tone**: Knowledgeable, specific to sector, confident
**Pacing**: Interactive—they want to dig into market dynamics
**Q&A approach**: Welcome detailed technical questions; use their expertise to validate
**What impresses them**: Evidence that you understand their industry, founder track record, market insights
**What turns them off**: Lack of industry knowledge, ignorance of key competitors, unrealistic go-to-market

**Delivery tips**:
- Reference market dynamics only industry insider would know
- Ask for their perspective/input
- Show founder's industry network/relationships
- Be comfortable diving into technical/operational details
- Ask them questions about their experience

### Network/Ecosystem-Focused Offices

**Tone**: Collaborative, strategic, thinking about broader ecosystem
**Pacing**: Medium—they want to understand both opportunity AND how it helps portfolio
**Q&A approach**: Answer by connecting to broader portfolio strategy
**What impresses them**: Understanding of network effects, clear benefit to other companies, ecosystem thinking
**What turns them off**: Siloed thinking, "just building our company," missing ecosystem opportunities

**Delivery tips**:
- Reference specific portfolio companies and benefits to them
- Talk about network effects
- Ask about opportunities to collaborate with other portfolio companies
- Show you understand ecosystem dynamics
- Propose specific ways to engage with portfolio
