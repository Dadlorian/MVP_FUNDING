# Deal Structures: Detailed Financial Modeling for Non-Equity Family Office Investments

## Overview

This guide provides detailed financial modeling examples for six common family office non-equity structures. Each includes:
1. Structure overview and when to use
2. Real-world financial examples
3. Return calculations for family office
4. Cash flow impact on company
5. Comparison to other structures
6. Tax implications
7. Key negotiation variables

## Structure 1: Pure Revenue Share Deal

### Overview

**Definition**: Family office receives percentage of company's gross or net revenue for specific term.

**When to use**:
- Company has consistent, growing revenue
- Easy to track and measure revenue
- Family office wants ongoing upside without equity
- 5-10 year partnership desired

### Financial Example 1A: SaaS Company

**Company Profile**:
- Business: SaaS platform for HR departments
- Current revenue: $800K ARR
- Projected growth: 80% Y1, 60% Y2, 40% Y3, 25% Y4-5
- Gross margin: 75% (and stable)
- Burn rate: Currently profitable at unit level

**Revenue Share Deal Terms**:
- Investment: $500K (upfront)
- Revenue share: 3% of gross monthly revenue
- Term: 7 years or until $1M cap reached (whichever first)
- Reporting: Monthly by 5th business day of following month
- Definition of revenue: All customer payments less returns within 30 days

**Financial Projections & Family Office Return**:

| Year | Projected ARR | Monthly Avg Revenue | 3% RS/Month | Annual RS | Cumulative RS | Cap Status |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1.44M | $120K | $3.6K | $43.2K | $43.2K | 4% of cap |
| 2 | $2.30M | $192K | $5.8K | $69.6K | $112.8K | 11% of cap |
| 3 | $3.22M | $268K | $8.0K | $96K | $208.8K | 21% of cap |
| 4 | $4.03M | $336K | $10.1K | $121.2K | $330K | 33% of cap |
| 5 | $5.04M | $420K | $12.6K | $151.2K | $481.2K | 48% of cap |
| 6 | $6.30M | $525K | $15.8K | $189.6K | $670.8K | 67% of cap |
| 7 | $7.88M | $657K | $19.7K | $236.4K | $907.2K | 91% of cap |

**Family Office Total Return**:
- Initial investment: $500K
- Cumulative revenue share by Year 7: $907.2K
- Total return: $907.2K (on $500K capital)
- Effective return: 81% cumulative, ~9% annualized

**Cash Flow Impact on Company**:
- Year 1: $43.2K revenue share payment (5.4% of operating income)
- Year 5: $151.2K revenue share payment (4.8% of operating income if assumes $3.1M EBITDA)
- Year 7: $236.4K annual revenue share payment (3.0% of operating income)

The revenue share is a "tax" on revenue, but because company maintains gross margins, the burden
is manageable even as it scales.

**Comparison to Equity Deal**:
- If this was $500K equity round at $2M post-money valuation (25% dilution):
  - Investor gets 25% ownership
  - If company exits at $100M (10 year horizon), investor gets $25M
  - But founder loses 25% of ownership
- With revenue share deal:
  - Founder keeps 100% ownership
  - Investor gets $907K in this scenario
  - Founder's upside at $100M exit: 100% vs. 75% = $33M additional for founder

**Key Variables & Sensitivity**:

*What if growth is slower?* (50% Y1, 40% Y2, 25% Y3, 15% Y4-5)
- Year 5 revenue: $1.58M (vs. $5.04M in base case)
- Cumulative RS by Year 5: $176K (vs. $481K)
- Cap reached by: Year 10+ vs. Year 7
- Family office still makes money, just slower

*What if growth is faster?* (120% Y1, 80% Y2, 60% Y3, 40% Y4-5)
- Year 5 revenue: $12.5M (vs. $5.04M)
- Cumulative RS by Year 5: $968K (cap nearly reached)
- Cap reached by: Year 5 vs. Year 7
- Family office hits cap faster, exits earlier

*What if revenue share % is 5% instead of 3%?*
- Year 1: $72K (vs. $43.2K)
- Year 5: $252K (vs. $151.2K)
- Cumulative by Year 7: $1.512M (exceeds $1M cap, so cap hit by Year 6.3)
- Higher percentage = earlier payoff but higher burden on company

### Financial Example 1B: Hardware Company (Different Margin Profile)

**Company Profile**:
- Business: Hardware/IoT device
- Current revenue: $2M ARR
- Gross margin: 40% (hardware constraint)
- Growth: 100% Y1, 80% Y2, 60% Y3

**Revenue Share Deal Terms**:
- Investment: $1M
- Revenue share: 2.5% of gross revenue
- Term: 10 years or until $2M cap

**Financial Projections**:

| Year | Projected ARR | 2.5% RS Annual | Cumulative RS |
|------|---|---|---|
| 1 | $4.0M | $100K | $100K |
| 2 | $7.2M | $180K | $280K |
| 3 | $11.5M | $288K | $568K |
| 4 | $18.4M | $460K | $1.028M |
| 5 | $29.4M | $735K | $1.763M |
| 6 | $47M | $1.175M | $2.938M (cap hit) |

**Key Insight**: Higher growth means family office hits cap (and stops receiving payments) faster.
This can be seen as good (money comes back faster) or bad (upside is capped).

## Structure 2: Grant + Revenue Share Hybrid

### Overview

**Definition**: Combination of upfront non-dilutive grant plus ongoing revenue share.

**When to use**:
- Company needs near-term capital for specific purpose (R&D, hiring)
- Family office wants both immediate capital deployment and upside
- 50-80% grant / 20-50% revenue share typical split
- Founder gets "credit" for using capital well

### Financial Example 2A: EdTech Company

**Company Profile**:
- Business: Online education platform
- Current revenue: $300K ARR
- Stage: Early growth, pre-profitable
- Capital need: $500K (to hire team, expand product)

**Grant + Revenue Share Deal**:
- Total investment: $500K
- Grant component: $300K (60%)
- Revenue share component: $200K (40% of capital translated to revenue share)
  - Structured as: 2.5% of gross revenue for 6 years, capped at $200K
- Term: 6 years

**How the $200K Revenue Share Cap Works**:
- Represents expected payback of $200K portion through revenue sharing
- If company hits revenue targets, should reach cap by year 4-5
- If company underperforms, cap might extend to year 6-7

**Financial Projections**:

| Year | Projected ARR | 2.5% RS | Annual RS | Cumulative RS | Cumulative Grant | Total Return |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $800K | 2.5% | $20K | $20K | $300K | $320K |
| 2 | $1.6M | 2.5% | $40K | $60K | $300K | $360K |
| 3 | $2.8M | 2.5% | $70K | $130K | $300K | $430K |
| 4 | $4.2M | 2.5% | $105K | $200K (capped) | $300K | $500K |
| 5 | $6M | 0% | $0 | $200K (capped) | $300K | $500K |
| 6 | $8M | 0% | $0 | $200K (capped) | $300K | $500K |

**Family Office Return Analysis**:
- Total investment: $500K
- Total return by Year 4: $500K (breakeven with capital deployed)
- ROI: 0% cumulative through Year 4, but:
  - $300K was "consumed" as grant (can't expect that back)
  - $200K was recovered from revenue share
  - Effective: Converted $500K into $200K in revenue share + $300K in company growth

**Alternative view**: Family office got "paid" in company growth.
If company's equity valuation increased from $2M (at investment) to $8M (by Year 4),
and family office had received a small equity stake (say 2%), that stake would be worth
$160K (2% of $8M), plus the $200K revenue share = $360K total return.

**Company Cash Flow Impact**:
- Year 1: Receives $300K grant, pays $20K revenue share = net $280K benefit
- Year 2: Pays $40K revenue share (no more grant) = $40K cost
- Year 3: Pays $70K revenue share = $70K cost
- Year 4: Pays $105K revenue share = $105K cost (but revenue share caps and ends)
- Year 5-6: Pays $0 revenue share = free from obligation

**Why This Structure Works**:
1. **For family office**: Gets capital back relatively quickly (by Year 4), plus confidence
   that capital was actually deployed (not hoarded)
2. **For company**: Receives both grant (which doesn't require payback) and revenue share
   (which naturally decreases as % of revenue as company grows)

## Structure 3: Exclusive Territory Licensing

### Overview

**Definition**: Family office receives exclusive license to operate product in specific territory,
pays upfront license fee + ongoing royalty.

**When to use**:
- Geographic expansion desired
- Family office has operational capability in specific territory
- Territory can be clearly defined (geographic or customer-segment based)
- 10+ year relationship desired
- Requires minimum revenue guarantee to incent both parties

### Financial Example 3A: Software Licensing

**Company Profile**:
- Business: SaaS platform for supply chain management
- Current revenue: $2M ARR (in North America)
- License available: European territory
- European TAM: $500M (estimated)

**Licensing Deal Terms**:
- Upfront license fee: $300K
- Royalty: 12% of gross revenue from Territory customers
- Minimum Annual Guarantee (MAG):
  - Year 1: $300K (implied 36M revenue needed)
  - Year 2: $500K
  - Year 3+: $700K
- Term: 10 years, auto-renew for 5-year periods
- Definition: Revenue = customer payments in Territory

**Financial Projections (Licensee's Performance)**:

| Year | Territory Revenue | Royalty Owed (12%) | MAG | Amount Due | Cumulative Royalty |
|------|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $2.5M | $300K | $300K | $300K | $300K |
| 2 | $4.2M | $504K | $500K | $504K | $804K |
| 3 | $6.5M | $780K | $700K | $780K | $1.584M |
| 4 | $9M | $1.08M | $700K | $1.08M | $2.664M |
| 5 | $12M | $1.44M | $700K | $1.44M | $4.104M |

**Licensor (Company) Revenue Impact**:
- Year 1: $300K license fee + $300K royalty = $600K revenue (20% of total company)
- Year 5: $1.44M royalty = 27% of company revenue
- 10-year cumulative royalties: ~$12M (significant revenue stream)

**Return to Family Office (Licensee)**:
If Licensee invests $500K in building out Territory operations:
- Marketing, sales team, customer success: ~$500K in Year 1 setup
- By Year 5, generating $1.44M annual revenue
- If Licensee's operating margin is 30% (after costs), EBIT = $432K
- Return on $500K setup investment reached by Year 2-3
- Year 5+ generating $400K+ annually

**Territory Revenue Drivers** (assumptions):
- Market penetration: Starting at 0% in Year 1, reaching 1.5% by Year 5
- European TAM: $500M
- 1.5% penetration = $7.5M market opportunity
- Average deal size: $50K-$100K
- Sales cycle: 3-6 months

**Key Variables & Sensitivity**:

*What if royalty is 15% instead of 12%?*
- Year 1: $375K royalty (vs. $300K MAG, so $375K paid)
- Year 5: $1.8M royalty
- Higher royalty reduces Licensee profit margin, makes them less likely to invest in Territory

*What if MAG not met?*
- Year 2: Licensee achieves only $350K revenue vs. $500K MAG
- Licensee owes licensor $500K - $350K = $150K shortfall
- If happens for 2 years, Licensor can terminate territory

## Structure 4: Royalty on Product Sales

### Overview

**Definition**: Family office receives percentage of specific product sales (not all company revenue).

**When to use**:
- Company has multiple product lines or business units
- Family office investment targets specific product
- Want to isolate returns to specific product success
- Product might be spun out or licensed separately in future

### Financial Example 4A: Multi-Product Company

**Company Profile**:
- Business: Software with 3 product lines
- Total company revenue: $3M ARR
- Product breakdown:
  - Platform (core): $1.5M ARR (50%)
  - Add-on module A: $800K ARR (27%)
  - Add-on module B: $700K ARR (23%)

**Royalty Deal on Add-on Module A**:
- Investment: $200K
- Royalty: 10% of gross revenue from Module A only
- Term: 5 years or until $400K cap
- Rationale: Family office wants to capitalize on Module A growth

**Financial Projections**:

| Year | Module A ARR | 10% Royalty | Annual Royalty | Cumulative |
|------|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1.0M | 10% | $100K | $100K |
| 2 | $1.4M | 10% | $140K | $240K |
| 3 | $1.9M | 10% | $190K | $430K (exceeds cap) |

**Important Note**: By mid-Year 3, family office hits $400K cap and stops receiving payments.

**Company Benefit**:
- $200K upfront goes toward Module A product development
- Year 1 royalty: $100K (6.7% of Module A revenue)
- Year 2 royalty: $140K (10% of Module A revenue)
- After Year 3, zero royalty obligation - company keeps all Module A revenue

**Why This Structure**:
1. Focuses family office capital on specific growth driver
2. Company can track ROI of specific product
3. If Module A is spun out, royalty can be cleanly separated

## Structure 5: Debt-with-Equity-Kicker

### Overview

**Definition**: Family office provides debt (usually SAFE or convertible note) with option to convert
to equity in future funding round, plus potentially some upfront equity.

**When to use**:
- Company is pre-revenue or very early
- Family office wants downside protection (debt) + upside (equity option)
- Valuation too uncertain for pure equity
- Anticipates follow-on equity round in 18-24 months

### Financial Example 5A: Biotech/DeepTech

**Company Profile**:
- Business: Biotech company with promising tech
- Stage: Pre-revenue, raised $1M seed 12 months ago
- Burn rate: $200K/month
- 18 months of runway
- Valuation negotiations: Too much disagreement on valuation (founders want $8M post, investors
  see it as $5M post)

**Debt-with-Kicker Deal**:
- SAFE Note: $500K
- Discount on next round: 20% discount
- MFN clause: If next round has better terms, investor gets better terms too
- Optional kicker: $100K as direct equity at same valuation as Series A
- Valuation cap on SAFE: $7M (compromise between founder $8M and investor $5M)

**What Happens in Series A (18 months later)**:

*Scenario A: Series A at $10M post-money valuation*
- Founders wanted this
- SAFE converts with 20% discount → effective valuation: $8M ($10M × 80%)
- Family office $500K SAFE converts to: $500K / $8M = 6.25% equity stake
- Family office's $100K equity kicker: $100K / $10M = 1% equity
- Total family office ownership: 7.25%
- If Series A raises $2M → family office's stake diluted to: 7.25% × ($10M / $12M) = 6.04%

*Scenario B: Series A at $5M post-money valuation*
- Company underperformed
- Valuation cap of $7M applies (investor gets better deal than 20% discount)
- Family office $500K converts at $7M post-money → $500K / $7M = 7.14% equity
- Plus $100K direct equity: $100K / $5M = 2%
- Total: 9.14% ownership

**Family Office Return Calculation**:
- Initial investment: $600K ($500K SAFE + $100K equity)
- In Scenario A (company does well):
  - Initial equity stake: 7.25% + 1% = 8.25% (before Series A dilution)
  - After Series A dilution to 6.04%
  - If company exits at $50M valuation 5 years later: 6.04% × $50M = $3.02M
  - Return: $3.02M / $600K = 5x multiple

- In Scenario B (company underperforms):
  - Equity stake: 9.14%
  - If company exits at $15M (down scenario): 9.14% × $15M = $1.37M
  - Return: $1.37M / $600K = 2.3x multiple

**Key Insight**: Debt-with-kicker provides:
- Downside protection (at least get paid principal in bankruptcy)
- Upside participation (equity kicker)
- Flexibility on valuation (don't have to agree on valuation today)

## Structure 6: Prepayment / Advance Purchase

### Overview

**Definition**: Family office prepays for product/services to be delivered over time.

**When to use**:
- Hardware company or capital equipment
- Family office will actually use/purchase product themselves
- Clear delivery timeline
- Provides working capital for manufacturing

### Financial Example 6A: Solar/Climate Hardware

**Company Profile**:
- Business: Carbon monitoring hardware for buildings
- Stage: Pre-revenue, manufacturing ready
- Bill of materials (BOM): $8K per unit
- Planned retail price: $25K per unit
- Gross margin goal: 68%

**Prepayment Deal**:
- Family office commitments: 20 units prepaid
- Unit price: $20K (20% discount from retail)
- Total prepayment: $400K
- Delivery schedule:
  - 5 units by Month 3
  - 5 units by Month 6
  - 10 units by Month 12

**Company Cash Flow Impact**:

| Month | Cash In | Manufacturing Cost | Inventory | Net Cash |
|------|---|---|---|---|
| Month 0 | $400K prepay | $0 | $0 | $400K |
| Month 1-3 | $0 | $40K BOM | $0 | $400K → $360K |
| Month 3 | $0 | +$0 deliver | $0 | $360K |
| Month 4-6 | $0 | $40K BOM | $0 | $360K → $320K |
| Month 6 | $0 | +$0 deliver | $0 | $320K |
| Month 7-12 | $0 | $80K BOM | $0 | $320K → $240K |
| Month 12 | $0 | +$0 deliver | $0 | $240K |

**Working Capital Benefit**:
- Prepayment provides $400K upfront
- Used to fund $160K in manufacturing costs (20 units × $8K BOM)
- Remaining $240K available for:
  - Product R&D
  - Sales team hiring
  - Additional manufacturing for other customers

**Unit Economics for Company**:
- Revenue per unit: $20K (prepaid)
- COGS per unit: $8K
- Gross profit per unit: $12K
- Gross margin: 60% (on prepaid price)
- 20 units = $240K gross profit

**Comparison to Equity**:
- If instead company raised $400K equity at $2M post-money:
  - Founders' dilution: 20%
  - Company keeps cash ($400K) but loses 20% ownership
- With prepayment:
  - Founders keep 100% ownership
  - Company gets $400K less product revenue but more inventory capital
  - Effective: Better for founders if they can handle inventory carrying cost

**Family Office Economics**:
- Prepaid $400K for 20 units at $20K each
- Discount: $100K off retail price ($20K vs. $25K)
- Hardware delivered has real value (can use, resell, or hold)
- Return: Implicit return = $100K discount = 25% effective yield

## Structure 7: Philanthropic Grant (Non-Profit)

### Overview

**Definition**: Family office provides grant (not required to be repaid) for specific program or
organizational development.

**When to use**:
- Non-profit or social enterprise recipient
- Family office focused on social impact
- Grant tied to specific program expansion or capacity building
- 1-3 year grant term typical

### Financial Example 7A: Workforce Development Non-Profit

**Organization Profile**:
- Annual budget: $1.2M
- Staff: 12 people
- Participants served annually: 200 people
- Cost per participant: $6,000

**Grant Structure**:
- Total grant: $300K over 2 years
- Year 1: $150K
  - $100K for program expansion (serve 50 additional participants)
  - $50K for capacity building (hire finance manager, implement accounting system)
- Year 2: $150K
  - $100K for program continuation
  - $50K for impact measurement (fund independent evaluator)

**Program Expansion Financials**:

| Metric | Pre-Grant | Post-Year 1 | Post-Year 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual participants served | 200 | 250 | 280 |
| Cost per participant | $6,000 | $5,760 | $5,290 |
| Program revenue (gov contracts) | $1.0M | $1.25M | $1.40M |
| Total operating budget | $1.2M | $1.45M | $1.48M |
| Operating deficit | ($200K) | ($200K) | ($80K) |
| Grant funding need | $200K/yr | $200K | $80K |

**Grant Impact**:
- Year 1: Grant covers expanded program ($100K) + capacity building ($50K)
- Year 2: Grant covers program continuation ($100K) + evaluation ($50K)
- By Year 2: Organization has built financial systems to raise additional $80K
  (vs. prior $200K need)

**Return on Grant (Outcomes)**:
- Additional 80 participants served over 2 years (because of program expansion)
- Cost per impact: $300K grant / 80 additional participants = $3,750 per additional person
- If each participant's lifetime earnings increased by $50K due to program:
  - Economic return: 80 × $50K = $4M in participant earnings uplift
  - Social ROI: $4M / $300K = 13x return in economic value created

**Sustainability**:
- Pre-grant: Organization dependent on $200K annual philanthropy
- Post-grant: Organization reduced dependency to $80K annual by growing government contracts
- Grant investment = leverage point for sustainability

## Key Financial Concepts

### Cap on Revenue Share

**Why caps matter**:
- Without cap: Company's obligation potentially unlimited
- With cap: Company knows maximum exposure
- Typical cap: 1.5-3x initial investment

**Example**: $500K investment with 3% revenue share capped at $1.5M
- If company hits $50M revenue in year 5: 3% = $1.5M (cap hit)
- Company pays revenue share for only 4-5 years (instead of 7)
- Family office gets $1.5M total return

### Minimum Annual Guarantees (MAGs)

**Used in licensing deals**:
- Ensures Licensee is actually making effort to build Territory
- Protects Licensor's revenue floor

**Example**: Licensing deal with $700K MAG starting Year 3
- If Territory revenue is $500K, Licensee owes $700K - $500K = $200K shortfall
- OR Licensee can lose exclusive license if shortfall happens 2+ years

### Valuation Caps (in SAFE/Convertible Notes)

**Purpose**: Limits upward valuation for debt conversion to equity
- Provides downside protection if company stays cheap
- Provides upside cap if company becomes valuable quickly

**Example**: $500K SAFE with $5M valuation cap
- If Series A at $3M post-money: SAFE converts at discount (gets more equity)
- If Series A at $10M post-money: SAFE converts at $5M cap (gets fixed equity %)

## Stress-Testing Your Financial Model

Before presenting to family office, stress-test each scenario:

### Conservative Case (30% of base case growth)

Example: Base case assumes 80% Y1 growth
- Conservative: 24% Y1 growth
- Conservative: 18% Y2 growth
- Conservative: 12% Y3 growth

Calculate:
- When does company hit profitability?
- When does family office hit cap (if applicable)?
- What's annual revenue share payment?

### Base Case (Most Likely Scenario)

- Use realistic growth assumptions
- Supported by customer conversations, market research
- Show detailed unit economics

### Upside Case (150% of base case growth)

Example: Base case 80% growth
- Upside: 120% Y1 growth
- Assumptions: Market adoption faster, competitor moves slower, viral adoption

Calculate:
- How quickly does company scale?
- How quickly does family office hit cap?
- What are exit outcomes?

## Tax Implications

### Company Perspective

**Revenue Share Payments**:
- Treated as business expense
- Deductible from taxable income
- Reduces tax liability

**Example**: $100K revenue share payment
- Reduces taxable income by $100K
- At 25% effective tax rate, saves $25K in taxes
- Net cost to company: $75K (not $100K)

**Grant Receipts**:
- Generally NOT taxable income to company (if used for stated purpose)
- Check with accountant on treatment

### Family Office Perspective

**Revenue Share Income**:
- Taxable ordinary income
- Subject to income tax
- May be subject to state/local taxes

**Equity Investment**:
- Not taxable when received (unless structured as preferred stock dividend)
- Taxable upon exit (capital gains rate)
- More favorable than ordinary income

**Grant Received**:
- For nonprofits: Not taxable
- For for-profits: May be taxable (consult tax advisor)

## Negotiation Template: Financial Terms

When negotiating with family office, use this framework:

```
BASE SCENARIO:
Investment: $[X]M
Structure: [Grant] % + [RS] %
Revenue share: [X]% for [X] years, capped at $[X]M

FAMILY OFFICE ASKS FOR:
[Higher % / Longer term / Higher cap] → Company pushes back

COMPROMISES CONSIDERED:
1. Higher % but shorter term
2. Lower % but longer term
3. Higher cap but lower %
4. Add performance milestones to trigger payments
5. Tiered structure (higher % first year, lower after)

FINAL NEGOTIATED TERMS:
[Agreed-upon structure]
[Signed off by both parties]
```
