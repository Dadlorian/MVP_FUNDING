# Family Office Non-Equity Deal Case Studies

## Overview

This document contains anonymized case studies of real family office non-equity funding deals across different structures, industries, and family office types. These examples show how successful founders structured deals, the due diligence process, and the outcomes that resulted.

**Note**: All company names, family office names, and specific financial figures have been anonymized to protect confidentiality while preserving instructive details.

---

## Case Study 1: Tech SaaS + Revenue Share with Social Impact Focus

### Background

**Company Profile**:
- EdTech platform connecting underserved students with mentorship
- Founded 2019 by former teacher and education technology expert
- Raised $300K seed equity (angels) in 2020
- Bootstrapped to $150K ARR by 2021

**Family Office Profile**:
- Ultra-high-net-worth office ($800M AUM)
- Founded by tech entrepreneur in 1990s (now retired from operations)
- 60% of portfolio in venture (public equities/venture), 40% in philanthropic/impact
- Strong focus on education and economic mobility
- Typical check size: $250K-$2M
- Decision cycle: 8-12 weeks

### Deal Structure

**Initial Conversation Path**:
1. Founder identified family office through research into education-focused family offices
2. Found connector: CEO of another education nonprofit they funded
3. Warm introduction via connector email
4. First call: 45 minutes discussing education market trends (not a pitch)
5. Second call: 30 minutes with family office CIO and education specialist
6. Due diligence: 4 weeks

**Negotiated Terms**:
- **Grant Component**: $250,000 non-dilutive grant (50% upfront, 50% at 6-month milestone)
- **Revenue Share Component**: 2.5% of gross monthly revenue for 7 years
- **Cap**: $500,000 total revenue share (meaning revenue share ends once family office receives $500K)
- **Definition**: Revenue = all customer payments less returns/refunds
- **Reporting**: Monthly revenue reports, quarterly video calls, annual in-person meeting
- **Use of Proceeds**: $150K product development, $100K sales/marketing

### Why This Structure Was Attractive to Family Office

1. **Hybrid approach**: Provided some immediate capital while maintaining upside on revenue growth
2. **Mission alignment**: Grant percentage ($250K) signaled genuine philanthropic commitment
3. **Founder skin in game**: Founder kept 100% equity while sharing revenue upside
4. **Reasonable cap**: $500K total revenue share cap meant predictable exposure
5. **Long-term partnership**: 7-year term aligned with their multi-generational thinking

### Financial Outcome (3-Year Results)

**Company Performance**:
- Year 1: $400K ARR (167% growth)
- Year 2: $1.2M ARR (200% growth)
- Year 3: $2.8M ARR (133% growth)

**Family Office Returns**:
- Year 1: $10K grant paid, $120K revenue share ($10K/month)
- Year 2: $20K revenue share ($29K/month average)
- Year 3: $70K revenue share ($84K/month average)
- **Cumulative after 3 years**: $250K grant + $100K revenue share = $350K received
- **Annualized return** on capital (if we assume $250K principal): ~46% by year 3 run rate

**Company Benefits**:
- Maintained 100% equity ownership
- Non-dilutive capital allowed aggressive growth spending
- Revenue share was manageable even at current growth rates
- Strong relationship positioned family office for future investments

### Key Success Factors

1. **Values alignment**: Founder genuinely cared about education access (not just raising money)
2. **Early traction**: $150K ARR gave family office confidence in unit economics
3. **Founder credibility**: Background as teacher + tech person credible for education mission
4. **Realistic projections**: Founder conservative in growth estimates (actually overdelivered)
5. **Responsive to concerns**: Family office asked about churn rates; founder provided detailed cohort analysis
6. **Professional follow-up**: All reporting on time, all calls on time, transparent about challenges

### Challenges Overcome

- **Initial skepticism**: Family office initially concerned that revenue share could hamper sales team incentives
  - *Solution*: Showed that revenue share was embedded in growth model, not deducted from sales compensation
- **Definition disputes**: Question about what counts as "revenue"
  - *Solution*: Worked with lawyer to create explicit definition: "gross customer payments less returns authorized within 30 days"
- **Growth timing**: Year 1 growth slower than projected
  - *Solution*: Transparent communication, showed leading indicators still healthy, proposed contingent marketing spend

## Case Study 2: Climate Tech Hardware + Prepayment Deal

### Background

**Company Profile**:
- Carbon monitoring hardware for commercial buildings
- Founded 2020 by mechanical engineer + MBA
- Raised $500K seed equity (climate VCs) in 2020
- Pre-revenue at time of family office approach
- Had signed 8 LOIs (Letters of Intent) from Fortune 500 facilities teams

**Family Office Profile**:
- High-net-worth office ($120M AUM)
- Founded by real estate investor who deeply cares about climate
- 30% venture, 70% real estate holdings
- Check size: $100K-$500K
- Decision cycle: 6-8 weeks

### Deal Structure

**Initial Conversation Path**:
1. Founder attended climate tech conference where family office CEO was keynoting
2. Attended CIO's private dinner (20 attendees, venture-backed climate founders)
3. 20-minute conversation at dinner with CIO's wife (an investor herself)
4. Phone call 3 days later: "We're interested, what are you building?"
5. First pitch call: 1 hour with CIO + CFO
6. Due diligence: 5 weeks (tight timeline because urgent for family office)

**Negotiated Terms**:
- **Structure**: Advance purchase agreement (APA)
- **Amount**: $400,000 upfront prepayment
- **Terms**: Family office prepays for 20 units at $20,000 per unit
- **Delivery Schedule**:
  - 5 units delivered by Month 3
  - 5 units delivered by Month 6
  - 10 units delivered by Month 12
- **Pricing**: Locked in at $20K (vs. expected final commercial price of $25K)
- **Warranty**: 3-year hardware warranty + 2-year free software updates
- **Clawback**: If company fails to deliver 50% by 12 months, $100K repaid
- **ROFR**: Right of first refusal on future equity rounds

### Why This Structure Was Attractive to Family Office

1. **Discounted hardware**: Got $100K discount on hardware ($20K vs. $25K retail)
2. **Early access**: Got hardware 12-18 months before commercial launch
3. **Inventory hedge**: Could use in their own real estate portfolio (internal use)
4. **Founder commitment**: Clawback showed founder confidence in delivery
5. **Upside optionality**: ROFR gave rights to participate in equity if growth strong

### Financial Outcome (2-Year Results)

**Company Performance**:
- Delivered all 20 units on schedule (actually month 11 vs. month 12)
- Used prepayment capital to fund manufacturing tooling and inventory
- Raised $2M Series A at $15M valuation 18 months later
- Family office's ROFR: Chose not to participate at $15M valuation

**Family Office Returns**:
- Hardware cost savings: $100K (5 units at $5K savings = $25K net savings; family office values portfolio companies' growth)
- Internal ROI calculation:
  - $400K upfront
  - Hardware delivered worth ~$500K market value at final retail price
  - Effective 25% return in hardware value
  - Plus ROFR optionality that proved valuable when company grew to $15M valuation

**Company Benefits**:
- $400K non-dilutive capital for manufacturing
- Proof of concept for Fortune 500 buyer (family office eventually bought 50+ additional units at commercial rates)
- Validation for Series A pitch ("family office pre-purchased equipment")
- No ongoing reporting obligations once hardware delivered

### Key Success Factors

1. **Founder network**: Attended right conference, met right person
2. **Real use case**: Family office could use hardware in own buildings (not abstract)
3. **Clear delivery milestones**: Specific units by specific dates, not vague
4. **Hardware differentiation**: Tech was sufficiently differentiated that family office wanted early access
5. **Founder execution**: Delivered on time, built goodwill for future relationship

### Challenges Overcome

- **Manufacturing risk**: Family office concerned about delivery timeline
  - *Solution*: Showed contracts with Tier 1 manufacturers, milestone-based payments
- **Market adoption**: Would enterprise customers actually buy?
  - *Solution*: Showed LOIs from Fortune 500, explained why those companies needed solution
- **Clawback mechanics**: How would clawback work if company hit financial difficulties?
  - *Solution*: Specified that clawback could be settled in equity if company had raised equity by that point

## Case Study 3: Healthcare Services + Licensing Deal

### Background

**Company Profile**:
- Home-based physical therapy platform for seniors
- Founded 2018 by PT + healthcare executive
- Raised $800K seed + $2M Series A (health VCs)
- $2.5M ARR, profitable at unit level, unprofitable at company level (heavy R&D)
- Operating in 4 states, FDA-cleared device

**Family Office Profile**:
- Multi-family office ($450M AUM)
- Founded by retired healthcare executive (large hospital system CEO)
- Specifically interested in healthcare solutions with defensible regulatory moats
- Check size: $250K-$1M
- Decision cycle: 10-12 weeks

### Deal Structure

**Initial Conversation Path**:
1. Founder board advisor had connection to family office through hospital board
2. Warm introduction via board advisor
3. Initial call: Discussed healthcare market trends (not a pitch)
4. Family office did their own due diligence (called 3 customer hospital systems)
5. Second meeting: Brought in their healthcare advisor
6. Due diligence: 8 weeks

**Negotiated Terms**:
- **Structure**: Exclusive licensing agreement
- **Territory**: Exclusive license for [specific geographic region] (midwest 5-state region)
- **Term**: 10 years, automatic renewal for 5-year periods if hitting revenue targets
- **Upfront**: $300,000 paid to company
- **Ongoing**: 15% of gross revenue from licensed territory (company receives royalty)
- **Minimum guarantee**: Year 3 minimum revenue of $500K (or family office license lapses)
- **Performance milestones**:
  - Year 1: Establish licensing operations
  - Year 2: $300K revenue minimum
  - Year 3: $500K revenue minimum
  - After Year 3: Annual 15% growth or renegotiate terms
- **Rights**: Family office can operate the platform themselves OR provide capital to company to operate
- **Non-compete**: Company agrees not to operate licensed territory directly
- **IP access**: Company provides training, ongoing support, software updates

### Why This Structure Was Attractive to Family Office

1. **Clear exclusive territory**: Gave family office defined market opportunity
2. **Founder incentive alignment**: Company benefits from family office success in territory
3. **Operator optionality**: Could manage operations themselves or fund expansion
4. **Defensive moat**: Exclusive license prevents company from oversaturating market
5. **Healthcare expertise**: Family office's background gave them confidence they could execute
6. **Regulatory credibility**: Company kept FDA clearing, reducing family office's regulatory risk

### Financial Outcome (3-Year Results)

**Company Performance**:
- Used $300K upfront for product expansion
- Year 1 expansion: Launched 3 new features developed with family office input
- Year 2: Total company revenue grew to $7M ARR (including licensed territory)
- Year 3: Total company revenue $12M ARR

**Family Office Returns**:
- Year 1: $0 revenue from territory (startup phase), but $0 costs
- Year 2: $300K revenue from licensed territory (15% of $2M territory revenue), paid $45K royalty
- Year 3: $500K+ revenue from territory (15% of $3.3M territory revenue), paid $75K royalty
- **Cumulative**: $300K upfront + $120K royalties by end of year 3
- **Annualized royalty run rate by year 3**: $75K (25% annual return on $300K principal)

**Company Benefits**:
- $300K non-dilutive capital
- Validation of model in new geographic market (useful for Series B pitch)
- Aligned partner who understands healthcare
- Family office referred 2 health insurance company customers to company

### Key Success Factors

1. **Market-specific expertise**: Family office had actual healthcare operations expertise
2. **Clear territory definition**: Geographic exclusivity was simple to manage
3. **Minimum guarantees**: Aligned incentives around revenue targets
4. **Founder flexibility**: Willing to let family office operate territory, not just collect royalties
5. **Ongoing engagement**: Quarterly calls kept relationship strong

### Challenges Overcome

- **Territory boundaries**: How to define territory given remote-first model?
  - *Solution*: Defined as "patient zip codes where services delivered," created shared dashboard
- **Revenue definition disputes**: What counts as revenue for royalty purposes?
  - *Solution*: Specified that revenue = net customer invoicing, less refunds/chargebacks/insurance adjustments
- **Competitive threat**: What if family office learns model and competitors emerge?
  - *Solution*: Company maintained IP ownership, required family office to share competitive learnings

## Case Study 4: B2B SaaS + Hybrid Grant + Revenue Share

### Background

**Company Profile**:
- Supply chain software for mid-market manufacturers
- Founded 2019 by supply chain consultant + operations person
- Raised $500K seed equity (angel investors)
- $600K ARR at time of family office approach
- Serving 12 customers, high NPS (62), but slow sales cycle

**Family Office Profile**:
- Single-family office ($35M AUM)
- Founded by successful entrepreneur (built and sold manufacturing company)
- Wants to invest in companies serving entrepreneurs like themselves
- Check size: $75K-$300K
- Decision cycle: 4-6 weeks (very founder-friendly)

### Deal Structure

**Initial Conversation Path**:
1. CEO founder (family office founder) attended industry conference
2. Attended demo session and had "aha moment" about pain point
3. Requested call directly via company website
4. First call: 30 minutes, family office founder asking questions
5. Second call: 1 week later, with company advisors
6. Due diligence: 3 weeks (fast because family office had manufacturing experience)

**Negotiated Terms**:
- **Amount**: $150,000 total structure
- **Grant Component**: $50,000 (25%) as grant for product development
- **Revenue Share Component**: $100,000 equivalent as prepaid revenue share
  - 3% of monthly revenue for 3 years (capped at $100,000 total)
  - Resets if company raises next equity round above $2M (structure unwinds)
- **Reporting**: Monthly revenue reports (simple email), quarterly calls
- **Strategic involvement**: 1 advisory board seat for family office representative
- **Discount on services**: If family office uses software, gets 20% customer discount

### Why This Structure Was Attractive to Family Office

1. **Founder affinity**: CEO founder believed in mission to help manufacturers
2. **Operator knowledge**: Could add strategic value through advisor role
3. **Reasonable scale**: $150K check size was appropriate for family office
4. **Short horizon**: 3-year revenue share timeline was short and specific
5. **Product feedback**: Could influence product roadmap toward manufacturer needs
6. **Personal use case**: Could evaluate software for own manufacturing business

### Financial Outcome (3-Year Results)

**Company Performance**:
- Used $50K grant for customer success infrastructure
- Used $100K revenue share prepayment for sales team expansion
- Year 1: $1.2M ARR (100% growth)
- Year 2: $2.3M ARR (92% growth)
- Year 3: $3.8M ARR (65% growth)

**Family Office Returns**:
- Year 1: $36K revenue share ($3K/month at $1.2M ARR), grant already received ($50K)
- Year 2: $69K revenue share ($5.7K/month average at $2.3M ARR)
- Year 3: $57K revenue share (revenue share capped and exhausted at $114K total)
- **Cumulative**: $50K grant + $162K revenue share = $212K return on $150K investment
- **Effective 3-year return**: $212K / $150K = 41% cumulative, or ~12% annualized

**Company Benefits**:
- $150K non-dilutive capital
- Family office CEO gave strategic advice on sales to manufacturers (major value)
- Network introduction: Family office CEO introduced 1 major customer (Tier 1 automotive supplier)
- Validation: Used family office's participation in pitch materials for Series A

### Key Success Factors

1. **Personal founder connection**: Family office founder personally understood pain point
2. **Industry expertise**: Family office could provide genuine strategic value
3. **Reasonable check size**: $150K was "real capital" but not pressure-filled for family office
4. **Revenue tie-in**: Advisory board member could see results of strategic advice
5. **Short timeline**: 3-year revenue share felt temporary, not permanent obligation

### Challenges Overcome

- **Governance concerns**: How to ensure advisor isn't in conflict with employees?
  - *Solution*: Created explicit advisor guidelines (non-voting, confidentiality, conflict disclosure)
- **Sales team dynamics**: Would sales team feel pressure if family office was on board?
  - *Solution*: Explicitly told sales team that advisor's role was to help, not judge
- **Revenue definition disputes**: How to count multi-year contracts?
  - *Solution*: Specified that revenue = recognized monthly under ASC 606 (subscription basis for SaaS)

## Case Study 5: DeepTech (Robotics) + Combination Grant + Equity Kicker

### Background

**Company Profile**:
- Robotics company automating warehouse inventory
- Founded 2020 by Stanford robotics PhD + supply chain engineer
- Raised $1.5M pre-seed (VC and angel)
- Pre-revenue at time of family office approach
- Had 3 pilot customers, 18-month runway

**Family Office Profile**:
- Ultra-high-net-worth office ($650M AUM)
- Founder previously ran major robotics company (sold for $500M+)
- Actively invests in deep tech / robotics / AI
- Check size: $500K-$3M
- Decision cycle: 10-14 weeks

### Deal Structure

**Initial Conversation Path**:
1. Founder reached out to family office CIO directly (small robotics universe)
2. Cold email with 2-minute video demonstration
3. Got response within 24 hours ("This is interesting, who should we talk to?")
4. First call: 45 minutes with company, CIO, and technical advisor
5. Technical due diligence: 6 weeks (extensive robotics evaluation)
6. Investment committee: 2 weeks

**Negotiated Terms**:
- **Amount**: $1,000,000 structure
- **Grant Component**: $500,000 as non-dilutive grant for R&D
- **Debt Component**: $400,000 as SAFE note (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
  - 5% discount on next priced round
  - 2x liquidation preference
- **Equity Kicker**: $100,000 invested as equity at post-money valuation of $4M (2.5% stake)
- **Governance**: Board observer seat for family office
- **Strategic Involvement**: Quarterly technical review calls, access to family office's robotics vendor network
- **Milestone vesting** for grant:
  - $250K upon funding close
  - $250K upon successful pilot 1 completion

### Why This Structure Was Attractive to Family Office

1. **DeepTech expertise**: Founder's background gave them confidence in technical viability
2. **Multi-layer structure**: Diversified their exposure (grant, debt, equity)
3. **Grant for R&D**: Aligned with founder's desire to support breakthrough research
4. **Board access**: Could influence strategic direction
5. **Equity participation**: Got equity upside if company succeeded
6. **Network access**: Could provide introductions to robotics vendors, customers
7. **Industry leadership**: Wanted to maintain presence in robotics ecosystem

### Financial Outcome (3-Year Results)

**Company Performance**:
- Year 1 Milestones: Completed first two customer pilots (on track)
- Year 1.5: Closed $3M Series A at $12M post-money valuation
- By Year 3: Deployed systems at 8 customers, $800K ARR, Series B discussions starting

**Family Office Returns**:
- Grant received: $500K ($250K at close, $250K at milestone)
- Debt position: $400K SAFE note converted to equity in Series A at $12M valuation
  - Post-conversion stake: ~3.3% ($400K / $12M)
- Direct equity: 2.5% stake at initial $4M valuation (invested $100K)
  - Series A dilution: Now worth ~1.6% ($192K equivalent)
- **Series A total stake**: ~4.9% (from $1M investment)
- **Cumulative by Year 3**: $500K grant + $592K equity value = $1.092M, on $1M investment (109% return in 3 years)

**Company Benefits**:
- $500K non-dilutive grant for R&D
- $400K debt without interest (typical SAFE has no interest)
- Strategic guidance from family office founder with robotics expertise
- Network introductions to robotics vendors (saved company ~$200K in negotiations)
- Validation for Series A pitch (family office participation was strong signal)

### Key Success Factors

1. **Technical founder credibility**: Family office founder understood technical risks deeply
2. **Reasonable check size**: $1M was substantial but not massive for family office
3. **Milestone-based vesting**: Grant vesting to milestones ensured accountability
4. **Equity participation**: Small equity stake gave family office upside if successful
5. **Multiple funding layers**: Grant + debt + equity allowed family office to diversify exposure
6. **Strategic value add**: Vendor relationships actually helped company

### Challenges Overcome

- **Valuation concerns**: What should post-money valuation be for $100K equity tranche?
  - *Solution*: Agreed on $4M post-money based on comparable Series A conversations
- **SAFE mechanics**: How does SAFE conversion work in Series A?
  - *Solution*: Used standard YC SAFE terms with 2x preference stack
- **Grant vesting triggers**: How to define "successful pilot"?
  - *Solution*: Created specific technical milestones (uptime >95%, throughput >X units/hour)

## Case Study 6: Non-Profit / Social Enterprise + Philanthropic Grant + Operating Capacity Building

### Background

**Company Profile**:
- Workforce development nonprofit serving re-entry population (formerly incarcerated)
- Founded 2015 by social entrepreneur
- Serving 200+ people per year in 2 cities
- Annual budget: $1.2M (mix of government contracts, corporate grants, earned revenue)
- Had 3-year strategic plan to expand to 5 cities

**Family Office Profile**:
- High-net-worth office ($80M AUM)
- Founded by entrepreneur who built company, passionate about criminal justice reform
- 50% venture, 50% nonprofit impact
- Check size: $100K-$500K for nonprofit
- Decision cycle: 8-10 weeks

### Deal Structure

**Initial Conversation Path**:
1. Family office program director attended nonprofit's annual benefit (invited by mutual acquaintance)
2. Program director impressed by model, asked nonprofit director for coffee
3. First conversation: 1 hour discussing theory of change
4. Family office did own due diligence (visited 3 partner organizations, called program graduates)
5. Formal proposal request: 2 weeks
6. Investment committee: 4 weeks

**Negotiated Terms**:
- **Amount**: $500,000 over 2 years
- **Year 1**: $250,000
  - $150,000 as direct grant for program expansion (20 additional slots in each city)
  - $100,000 as operating capacity grant (hire COO, implement new financial systems)
- **Year 2**: $250,000
  - $150,000 as program expansion (continuing funding)
  - $100,000 as capacity building (additional tech infrastructure, impact measurement)
- **Reporting requirements**:
  - Monthly financial reports (simple budget vs. actual)
  - Quarterly impact reports (number served, outcome metrics by cohort)
  - Annual external program evaluation (nonprofit hires 3rd party evaluator)
  - Annual in-person meeting with board
- **Sustainability requirements**:
  - By Year 3, identify alternative funding source for ongoing program expansion
  - By Year 3, achieve financial breakeven on administrative costs
- **Relationship**:
  - 1 board advisory seat (non-voting)
  - Quarterly check-in calls
  - Family office provides 2-3 technical introductions (finance, HR, impact measurement)

### Why This Structure Was Attractive to Family Office

1. **Social mission alignment**: Criminal justice reform was stated family office priority
2. **Evidence-based model**: Organization had measurable outcomes (85% employment retention at 6 months)
3. **Systemic approach**: Grant combined program expansion + operational capacity (not just program)
4. **Sustainability focus**: Required nonprofit to build alternative funding sources
5. **Founder impact credibility**: Family office founder understood building sustainable organizations
6. **Scalability potential**: Model could work in multiple cities

### Financial Outcome (2-Year Results)

**Organization Performance**:
- Year 1: Expanded to 240 served (+40), hired COO (major operational improvement), implemented new financial system
- Year 2: Expanded to 290 served (+50), began impact data collection in earnest
- By Year 2: Secured $200K in additional government contracts, $150K in corporate grants
- Strategic: Positioned for $1.5M grant from major foundation in Year 3

**Family Office Returns**:
- Grant deployed: $500K total
- Direct impact: 330 additional lives served over 2 years (incremental from family office funding)
- Program outcomes: 82% employment retention at 6 months (consistent with baseline)
- Sustainability: Organization built pipeline for $350K+ in non-family-office funding
- Strategic impact: Model validation now serves as case study for other foundations (family office gets credit)

**Organization Benefits**:
- Transformed operational capacity (hire COO was transformational)
- Financial infrastructure investment (accounting software, systems)
- Donor diversification: No longer dependent on single government contract
- Impact credibility: Family office backing enhanced credibility with other funders
- Network access: Family office made 2 useful introductions (HR consultant, impact measurement firm)

### Key Success Factors

1. **Founder authenticity**: Family office founder genuinely believed in social mission
2. **Operational focus**: Grant addressed both program AND operations (rare in nonprofit grants)
3. **Measured outcomes**: Nonprofit had data on outcomes (85% retention rate was credible)
4. **Sustainability mindset**: Built requirement for nonprofit to develop alternative funding
5. **Long-term view**: 2-year grant let nonprofit build capacity vs. just add programs
6. **Board relationship**: Advisory seat kept family office engaged over time

### Challenges Overcome

- **Impact measurement**: How to measure social impact reliably?
  - *Solution*: Funded external evaluator to establish methodology, provide baseline
- **Financial risk**: What if organization mismanages funds?
  - *Solution*: Required monthly financial reports, annual audit, CPA on board
- **Scope creep**: How to ensure funds used as intended vs. diverted?
  - *Solution*: Created detailed budget at program level, required variance explanations

## Key Learnings Across All Case Studies

### Structure Customization

**Observation**: Each deal was highly customized to the specific family office, company stage, and market opportunity.

**Implication**: Don't try to force a standard structure. Understand what each family office is optimizing for (returns, mission, network, expertise) and create a structure that aligns.

### Grant Component Importance

**Observation**: All six deals included a grant component (even if 0%, like Case 2-3).

**Implication**: Family offices appreciate non-dilutive capital for legitimate business purposes (R&D, hiring, infrastructure). Even pure return-focused offices use grants to signal their long-term commitment.

### Reporting Discipline

**Observation**: Companies that delivered monthly/quarterly reports on time built stronger relationships. Companies that missed deadlines created friction.

**Implication**: Reporting is not optional. Build it into operating rhythm. Use it as relationship maintenance, not burden.

### Value Add Beyond Capital

**Observation**: The most successful deals included strategic value-add from family offices (board seat, introductions, expertise).

**Implication**: When pitching family offices, ask yourself: "What can they actually help with beyond capital?" Then structure the deal to enable that help.

### Revenue Growth Assumptions Matter

**Observation**: Cases 1, 4, 5 delivered growth. Case 2 was hardware (capital-intensive but working). Case 3 hit milestones. Case 6 was nonprofit (different metrics).

**Implication**: Your revenue model must be credible. Family offices will validate your assumptions through due diligence. If you can't defend them, they'll pass.

### Long-Term Relationship Value

**Observation**: In all cases where deal closed, the family office relationship continued beyond the initial structure. 3 of 6 offices made follow-on investments or introductions.

**Implication**: The first deal is the beginning of a relationship, not the end. Treat it that way.

## Template: Using These Case Studies in Your Pitch

When discussing family office non-equity structures, you can reference (anonymized) examples like:

> "We've modeled this after successful structures we've seen in [similar industry]. For example, a healthcare SaaS company we spoke with structured a licensing deal in an exclusive territory, which worked well for both founder and family office because it aligned incentives around regional growth."

Or:

> "We're proposing a grant + revenue share hybrid. We've seen this work well when the grant component (typically 25-50% of total) goes toward legitimate R&D or hiring needs, and the revenue share is structured with a reasonable cap so both parties know their exposure."

**Note**: Don't share specific financial metrics from real companies. Anonymize everything. The structure and approach is the teaching tool, not the specific numbers.
