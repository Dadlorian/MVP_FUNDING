## Part 3: Choosing Your Path

### Path A: Continue Bootstrapping

**Best for:**
- Profitable business ($5K+ MRR, positive unit economics)
- Niche market (low competition)
- Founder values control above all
- Business growing 20-50% annually (sustainable)

**Timeline:** 3-5 years to $1M+ ARR

**Pros:**
- Keep 100% equity
- No board pressure
- Sustainable lifestyle
- Build loyal customer base
- Can raise later from strength

**Cons:**
- Slower growth (12-24 months to major milestones)
- Harder to attract top talent
- Risk of competition outpacing
- Limited resources for marketing
- May hit growth ceiling at $1-5M ARR

**Decision:**
- [ ] Recommend: Continue bootstrapping

**Next steps:**
1. Set 3-year sustainability plan
2. Focus on profitability (gross margin >70%)
3. Explore non-dilutive funding (grants, RBF)
4. Build advisory board for guidance
5. Review every 6 months

**Best for:**
- Strong traction ($3-5K+ MRR)
- Qualified for grants/credits (tech, R&D)
- Want to grow faster without equity dilution
- 18-24 month runway to profitability

**Timeline:** 18-24 months to $50K+ MRR

**Funding cocktail:**

- [PLACEHOLDER]: R&D tax credits ($10K-$50K/year)
- [PLACEHOLDER]: Revenue-based financing ($100K-$300K)
- [PLACEHOLDER]: Competitions ($5K-$50K)

**Pros:**
- Raise $100K-$500K with NO equity loss
- Accelerate growth while maintaining control

- De-risks equity financing later
- Tests market before raising VC

**Cons:**
- Complex to manage multiple funding sources
- Time-intensive application process

### Term Sheet Red Flags

**Be cautious if:**

1. **Excessive dilution** (30%+ for Series A)
2. **Liquidation preference** (2x non-participating preferred)
3. **Restrictive covenants** (can't raise more capital)
4. **Full ratchet anti-dilution** (punishes successful fundraising)
5. **Investor gets super-pro-rata rights** (can participate in future rounds disproportionately)
6. **Board control** (investor gets 2+ of 3 board seats)
7. **Multiple founders dividing focus** (investor wants one founder only)

### Key Terms to Negotiate

| Term | Default | Negotiate To | Priority |
|------|---------|--------------|----------|
| **Valuation (pre-money)** | [PLACEHOLDER] | Higher ✓ | High |
| **Dilution** | 25% | 15-20% | High |
| **Board seats** | 2 (investor + you) | 3 (investor + 2 founders) | Medium |
| **Liquidation preference** | 1x non-participating | 1x non-participating | High |
| **Pro-rata rights** | Full | Limited | Medium |

## Part 6: Valuation Guidance

### How Valuation Is Determined

**For bootstrap companies (Series A):**

```
Valuation = Revenue × Multiple

Typical multiples:
- 2-3x ARR (if < $100K MRR, strong growth >50%)
- 5-8x ARR (if $100K+ MRR, 30-50% growth)
- 10x+ ARR (if $200K+ MRR, <10% churn)

Example:
- Current MRR: $10,000 ($120K ARR)
- Growth rate: 30% (strong but not hypergrowth)
- Multiple: 5x
- Valuation: $600,000

Another example:
- Current MRR: $20,000 ($240K ARR)
- Growth rate: 50% (hypergrowth)
- Customer churn: 2% (excellent retention)
- Multiple: 7x
- Valuation: $1,680,000
```

### Valuation Determination Methods

**Method 1: Comparable Companies**
- Look at recent raises in your space
- Similar stage, revenue, growth
- Use their valuation as benchmark

**Method 2: Discounted Cash Flow**
- Project 5-year revenue
- Apply industry margin (70% SaaS margin)
- Discount back to present value at 30-40% discount rate
- Rarely used pre-IPO

**Method 3: VC Formula Method**
- Target exit value: $100M (example)
- Investor wants 25% of company
- Amount raised: $2M
- Implies valuation: $8M pre-money
- Work backward from target exit

**Method 4: Benchmark to Prior Funding**
- SAFE had valuation cap: $2M
- Series A at 1.5-2x SAFE cap
- Therefore: $3-4M pre-money

### Cap Table Modeling Tool

**Example:**
```
PRE-SERIES A

Founder A (vested):           400,000 shares = 50.0%
Founder B (vested):           240,000 shares = 30.0%
Early employees:               80,000 shares = 10.0%
Friends & family SAFE:             - = 5.0% (converts into Series A)
Employee option pool:          80,000 shares = 10.0% (post-Series A)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL PRE-SERIES A:           800,000 shares = 100.0%

SERIES A ROUND

Valuation (pre-money):        $2,000,000
Investment:                     $500,000
Valuation (post-money):       $2,500,000
Investor dilution:            20% ($500K ÷ $2.5M)

Calculation:
New shares issued:            200,000 shares ($500K @ $2.50/share)
Total shares post-Series A:  1,000,000 shares
Investor ownership:           200,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = 20%

Founder A ownership after:    400,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = 40% (was 50%)
Founder B ownership after:    240,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = 24% (was 30%)
```

## Part 7: Common Fundraising Mistakes

### Mistakes Bootstrap Founders Make

| Mistake | Impact | How to Avoid |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| **Raising too early** | Wastes capital on wrong priorities | Wait until product-market fit ($3K+ MRR) |
| **Undervaluation** | Give away too much equity | Use comp analysis + VC formula |
| **No clear use of funds** | Investors confused about plan | Create detailed spending breakdown |
| **Weak financial model** | Investor skepticism | Use realistic, conservative projections |
| **No customer traction** | Hard to raise | Ship MVP, get early revenue first |
| **Weak founding team** | Investor concern | Hire CTO/co-founder before fundraising |
| **No cap table** | Confusion later | Maintain accurate cap_table.xlsx |
| **Messy legal** | Legal delays in funding | Clean up IP, agreements, structure |
| **Too many conversations** | Exhausting, slow process | Focus on top 10 most likely investors |
| **Negotiating alone** | Miss market terms | Hire lawyer for Series A ($5-15K) |

## Part 9: Post-Funding Execution

### Month 1: After Capital Closes

**Priorities:**
1. **Announce professionally** (press release, social media)
2. **Onboard investors** (regular updates, board meeting)
3. **Lock in team** (convert key people to full-time, give them equity)
4. **Plan 18-month roadmap** (what needs to happen before next raise or exit)
5. **Set aggressive but achievable metrics** (revenue, hiring, product milestones)

### Quarterly Cadence (Post-Funding)

**Every quarter:**
- [ ] Board meeting (or investor update call)
- [ ] Financial review (runway check, burn rate)
- [ ] Metric review (MRR, retention, CAC, LTV)
- [ ] Hiring progress (team expansion)
- [ ] Product milestones (features shipped)
- [ ] Risks and opportunities (what's changed?)

**Board materials template:**
- Highlights (wins this quarter)
- Metrics (MRR, growth rate, churn, runway)
- Challenges (what's not going well)
- Asking (what do we need from investors?)

### Common Post-Funding Pitfalls

- **Spending money too fast:** Keep 12+ months runway
- **Losing focus:** Avoid new product ideas, focus on growth
- **Team scaling too fast:** Hire slowly, culturally fit
- **Losing product focus:** Don't chase shiny competitor moves
- **Ignoring unit economics:** Just because you have money doesn't mean burn is OK
