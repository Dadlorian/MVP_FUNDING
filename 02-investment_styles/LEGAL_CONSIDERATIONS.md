## WHEN TO HIRE A LAWYER

### By Funding Stage and Vehicle Type

| Stage | Vehicle | Legal Required? | Best Time to Engage | Cost Impact |
|-------|---------|-----------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Pre-Seed** | Friends & Family | Optional | After verbal commitment | Low |
| **Pre-Seed** | SAFE | Recommended | Once investor interest solid | Medium |
| **Seed** | Convertible Note | Required | Before investor pitches | Medium-High |
| **Seed/Series A** | Priced Round | Required | At term sheet stage | High |
| **Series A+** | Complex Deals | Required | Initial investor meeting | Very High |

### Detailed Guidance by Situation

#### 1. You MUST Hire a Lawyer

**Series A and Later Rounds:**
- Complex legal structures required
- Multiple investor classes
- Board seats and governance
- Liquidation preferences
- Anti-dilution provisions
- Information and inspection rights

**When Multiple SAFEs/Convertibles Exist:**
- 5+ SAFEs or convertible notes
- Different terms from different investors
- Conversion complexity at Series A
- Cap table management issues

**After Investor Insists on Legal:**
- Professional investors always require legal review
- Your lawyers vs. investor's lawyers (they'll compare)
- Your lawyer should negotiate terms, not just execute

**Special Circumstances:**
- Foreign investors (visa/tax issues)
- Employee stock option plan (ESOP) needed
- Previous fundraising with complex terms
- Liquidation scenario likely

#### 2. You Should Hire a Lawyer

**Early Seed Rounds ($250K+):**
- Investment large enough to matter legally
- Investor likely institutional (angels, micro-VCs)
- Term sheet likely (not just SAFE)
- Multiple investors with different terms

**First Convertible Note:**
- Even at lower amounts ($100K+)
- Complexity of debt instrument
- Conversion mechanics matter
- Terms vary significantly by investor

**Your First Institutional Money:**
- Sets precedent for future rounds
- Terms may become standard for subsequent investors
- Negotiating power highest at beginning

**Employee Equity Planning:**
- Stock option plan creation
- Equity grant structures
- 409A valuation

#### 3. You Can Skip a Lawyer (But Risks Exist)

**Small Friends & Family Rounds:**
- Under $50K total
- 2-3 close family members/friends
- Using simple SAFE template
- Everyone agrees on basic terms
- Risk: Misunderstandings later with money at stake

**Subsequent SAFEs with Identical Terms:**
- Once first SAFE negotiated with lawyer
- All follow-up SAFEs use same template
- No new legal negotiation needed
- Still review each one carefully yourself

**Very Early Stage (Pre-Seed):**
- Under $25K per investor
- Pure friends/family (true relationships)
- No board seats or governance involved
- Risk: Informal terms become problematic

**Time is Critical Factor:**
- If raising in 1-2 weeks, pro se might be forced choice
- But better to delay raise 2-3 weeks than make legal mistakes
- Mistakes take months/years to fix

## TYPICAL LEGAL COSTS BY VEHICLE

### Cost Breakdown by Funding Type

| Vehicle Type | Typical Cost Range | Includes | Timeline | Notes |
|--------------|-------------------|----------|----------|-------|
| **SAFE** | $1-5K | Document review, light negotiation | 3-5 days | Lower end: template review only; Higher end: negotiation |
| **Convertible Note** | $5-15K | Document prep, conversion mechanics, board resolutions | 1-2 weeks | Depends on note complexity and investor changes |
| **Seed Round (Priced)** | $15-40K | Stock docs, conversion calcs, cap table work, corporate setup | 2-4 weeks | Covers 1-3 investor closings |
| **Series A** | $40-100K+ | Full documentation, multiple investors, governance setup | 4-8 weeks | Can exceed $100K with complex structures |
| **Series B+** | $100K-250K+ | Complex cap tables, multiple classes, international investors | 6-12 weeks | Often 50/50 split with investor counsel |

### What's NOT Included in Standard Costs

**Often Billed Separately:**
- State incorporation/certificate of formation: $500-2K
- 409A valuation: $1-3K (required before option grants)
- Accounting/tax: $3-10K+ annually
- Intellectual property review: $3-8K
- Employment law review: $2-5K
- International legal (if needed): $5K-30K+

**What You Can Control:**
- Do preliminary corporate setup yourself (incorporate, bylaws)
- Use standardized templates (SAFE, YC note terms)
- Consolidate legal work (batch conversions together)
- Use cost-saving strategies (see Section 9)

### Cost as Percentage of Raised Capital

| Amount Raised | Legal Cost % | Example |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| $50K SAFE | 2-10% | $2-5K legal from $50K raised |
| $200K SAFE | 1-5% | $5-10K legal from $200K raised |
| $500K Series A | 2-8% | $10-40K legal from $500K raised |
| $2M Series A | 2-5% | $40-100K legal from $2M raised |
| $5M Series A | 2-4% | $100-200K legal from $5M raised |

**Key Insight:** As round size increases, legal cost as percentage decreases. Plan accordingly.

### Why Costs Vary So Much

**Factors Pushing Costs Up:**
- Investor requires significant customizations
- Multiple investor closing events (separate legal work each)
- Complex cap table at start of round
- Foreign investors (visa sponsorship, tax issues)
- Previous investor litigation or disputes
- Need for warrant coverage
- Convertible note conversion requiring heavy lifting

**Factors Keeping Costs Down:**
- Using standardized templates (SAFE from Y Combinator)
- Single investor closing (vs. multiple investors)
- Simple cap table to start
- Investor comfortable with founder-friendly terms
- Bundle work (close multiple SAFEs at once)
- Regional law firms vs. top-tier Silicon Valley firms

## STANDARD LEGAL DOCUMENTS

### For Each Investment Vehicle

#### SAFEs (Simple Agreements for Future Equity)

**Core Documents:**
1. **The SAFE itself** (4-5 pages)
   - Most important document
   - All key terms in one place
   - Side letter for non-standard terms (if any)

2. **Board Resolutions** (1 page)
   - Authorizing SAFE issuance
   - Setting conversion mechanics
   - May be waived for earliest SAFEs

3. **Cap Table Amendment** (tracking only)
   - Records SAFE holder
   - Tracking shares/caps/discounts

**Optional but Common:**
- **Side Letter**: For investor-specific terms (board observation, information rights)
- **Investor Rights Letter**: If investor gets special information/participation rights

**Sample SAFE Checklist:**
```
Essential Terms to Review:
□ Investment amount
□ Post-money valuation cap
□ Discount percentage (if any)
□ Valuation cap vs. post-money cap (which applies?)
□ Pro rata rights in future rounds
□ Information rights (what gets disclosed?)
□ Most-favored-nation clause (you match better terms for others)
□ Termination date (does it expire?)
□ Conversion triggers (what events convert SAFE?)
□ Series seed carve-out (if doing Series Seed later)
```

#### Convertible Notes

**Core Documents:**
1. **Convertible Note** (8-15 pages)
   - Principal amount
   - Interest rate
   - Maturity date
   - Conversion terms
   - Optional provisions

2. **Board Resolutions** (1-2 pages)
   - Authorizing note issuance
   - Setting conversion mechanics
   - Debt holder voting

3. **Investor Note Purchase Agreement** (varies)
   - May be combined into note itself

4. **Cap Table Tracking**
   - Recorded as convertible debt
   - Separate from equity

**Sample Convertible Note Checklist:**
```
Essential Terms to Review:
□ Principal amount
□ Interest rate (6-10% typical)
□ Maturity date (12-24 months typical)
□ Conversion discount (15-30% typical)
□ Conversion cap (sets maximum valuation)
□ Pro rata rights after conversion
□ Investor governance rights
□ Early repayment terms
□ What happens if no qualifying financing by maturity?
□ Board observation rights (if any)
□ Information/inspection rights
```

#### Priced Equity Rounds (SEED / Series A)

**Core Documents:**
1. **Stock Purchase Agreement** (20-40 pages)
   - Parties and shares
   - Purchase price per share
   - Investment amount
   - Representations and warranties
   - Conditions to closing

2. **Investor Rights Agreement** (15-25 pages)
   - Information rights
   - Inspection rights
   - Registration rights
   - Anti-dilution provisions
   - Drag-along/tag-along rights

3. **Amended & Restated Certificate of Incorporation** (5-10 pages)
   - New preferred share class
   - Liquidation preferences
   - Anti-dilution mechanics
   - Conversion rights
   - Voting rights

4. **Stock Option Plan** (if not already in place) (5-15 pages)
   - Authorizes equity grants to employees
   - Exercise price mechanisms
   - Vesting schedules

5. **Stock Option Grant Agreement** (2-3 pages per employee)
   - Specific employee grants
   - Vesting schedule
   - Exercise terms

6. **Cap Table** (working document)
   - Pre-money ownership
   - Post-money ownership
   - Dilution percentages
   - Option pool impact

7. **Board Resolutions** (2-3 pages)
   - Authorizing the financing
   - Setting terms
   - Allocating shares

8. **Closing Documents** (1-2 pages each)
   - Officer certificate
   - Secretary certificate
   - Legal opinion letter

**Sample Priced Round Checklist:**
```
Essential Terms to Review:
□ Valuation and price per share
□ Investment amount
□ New preferred share rights
□ Liquidation preferences (1x non-participating typical)
□ Anti-dilution (weighted average typical, broad-based conversion)
□ Conversion rights
□ Dividend rights
□ Voting rights (including on major decisions)
□ Board seat for investor
□ Registration rights (when can investor sell?)
□ Drag-along rights (investor can force all holders to accept acquisition)
□ Tag-along rights (minority holders can sell with majority)
□ Pro rata rights (investor can buy in future rounds)
□ Information rights (what gets reported?)
□ Inspection rights (accessing company books)
□ Participation rights (can investor double-dip on liquidation preference?)
```

#### Convertible Securities at Conversion

**Key Documents Needed:**
1. **Conversion Mechanics Memo** (internal)
   - Shows SAFE/note conversion
   - Which terms apply
   - New share count
   - Investor dilution/ownership

2. **Amendment to Original SAFE/Note**
   - If terms being adjusted at conversion
   - Not always needed (automatic conversion)

3. **Board Resolutions** (1 page)
   - Confirming conversion
   - Accepting conversion price
   - Amending cap table

## KEY LEGAL TERMS TO UNDERSTAND

### Representations and Warranties

**What They Are:**
Statements by each party about facts that are true. If false, the other party can sue.

**Founder/Company Representations (Investor Wants to Know):**
- "Company is properly organized and in good standing"
- "There are no material lawsuits pending against company"
- "Company owns/controls all intellectual property needed for business"
- "No prior company agreements would be violated by this investment"
- "Financial information provided is accurate and complete"
- "No material adverse change has occurred"
- "Company has proper licenses and permits"

**Investor Representations (Company Wants to Know):**
- "Investor has authority to make this investment"
- "Investor is accredited (if Series A)"
- "Investment funds are legal funds (not stolen/laundered)"
- "Investor has read documents and understands terms"

**Founder Risk:**
- Reps are often broad and survive closing for 12-24 months
- If reps are false, investor can sue for damages or rescission
- Can buy reps insurance
- Reps are often biggest negotiation point

**What Founders Should Negotiate:**
- "To the company's knowledge" qualifiers
- Time limits (representations expire after 12-18 months)
- Caps on liability
- Baskets (claims under $X don't count)
- Carve-outs for known issues
- Not disclosing every small problem

**Example Problematic Rep:**
```
PROBLEMATIC:
"Company has obtained all necessary intellectual property licenses
and none of the company's products infringe any third-party IP."

BETTER:
"To the company's knowledge, company has obtained all necessary
intellectual property licenses and none of the company's products
infringe any third-party IP, except as disclosed in Schedule 1.2(b)."

Even better:
"To the company's knowledge (without duty to investigate), company
owns or has valid licenses for all IP necessary for its business,
and no product functionality currently offered infringes known
third-party rights, provided that general design and functionality
cannot be guaranteed free from third-party claims."
```

### Indemnification

**What It Is:**
Agreement to pay for losses the other party suffers due to breach of representations/warranties or other wrongdoing.

**Structure:**
- Party A (Indemnitee) gets protected
- Party B (Indemnifier) pays losses
- Usually mutual (both sides indemnify each other)

**Investor Indemnification of Company:**
- Investor will cover losses if investor misrepresented itself
- Usually very limited
- Company rarely gets meaningful protection

**Company Indemnification of Investor:**
- Company must cover losses if company breached reps/warranties
- Can be significant
- What triggers indemnification:
  - Third-party lawsuit against investor related to company
  - Investor losses from bad company information
  - IP infringement claims

**Key Terms to Negotiate:**
- Basket: Claims under $X,000 don't trigger indemnification
- Cap: Liability capped at (often) investment amount or 50% of round
- Time limits: Reps survive for 12-24 months only
- Escrow: Part of proceeds held to cover claims
- Control: Who controls defense of third-party claims?

**Example Indemnification Issue:**
```
SCENARIO:
Investor invests $2M in Series A. Company claimed:
"Company owns all IP in its product."

Reality: A year later, competitor claims company's algorithm
infringes their patent. Lawsuit costs $500K to defend.

PROBLEMS:
- Company must indemnify investor for lawsuit costs
- Plus any settlement/damages
- No cap means unlimited exposure
- Investor profit from investment ($3M gain) protected by company's insurance

HOW TO LIMIT:
- Cap indemnification at investment amount ($2M)
- Require baskets ($25K minimum claim size)
- Time limits (don't survive beyond 18 months)
- Separate for IP vs. other claims
- Investor insurance should cover these
```

### Drag-Along Rights

**What They Are:**
Right for majority/investor to force minority shareholders to sell shares in acquisition.

**How They Work:**
```
Company has acquisition offer at $10/share.

Ownership:
- Founders: 40% ($4M value at $10/share)
- Series A Investor: 35% ($3.5M value)
- Other investors: 25% ($2.5M value)

Series A Investor has drag-along rights.

If investor agrees to acquire at $10/share, investor can force:
- Founders to sell at $10/share
- Other investors to sell at $10/share
- Everyone sells, deal closes

WITHOUT drag-along:
- Any shareholder can block acquisition
- Founders could demand higher price
- Deal could fall apart

WITH drag-along:
- Investor can close deal
- Founders forced to accept $10/share
- Can't negotiate better terms
```

**Founder Impact:**
- Positive: Provides exit (can't be stuck forever)
- Negative: Can't block bad acquisitions or demand higher prices
- Risk: Investor might agree to low acquisition price

**Standard Terms:**
- Typically held by Series A and later investors
- Usually triggered by majority investor agreement
- Often carve-outs: Won't drag minority below liquidation preference

**What Founders Should Negotiate:**
- Minimum price threshold (can't drag if acquisition below $X)
- Carve-out: Won't drag below founders' liquidation preference
- Right to negotiate separately if acquisition includes cash earnout
- Definition of "sale" (asset sales vs. stock sales treated differently)

### Tag-Along Rights

**What They Are:**
Right for minority shareholders to sell shares at same terms as majority (piggyback on majority's sale).

**How They Work:**
```
Company has acquisition offer.

Ownership (as before):
- Founders: 40%
- Series A Investor: 35%
- Other investors: 25%

Series A investor agrees to sell to acquirer at $10/share.

WITH tag-along rights:
- Founders can sell at $10/share (same terms as investor)
- Other investors can sell at $10/share
- Everyone gets same price

WITHOUT tag-along:
- Founders might be forced out by drag-along at $10/share
- But Series A investor negotiates side deal: gets $12/share
- Founders get $10, investor gets $12
- Founders disadvantaged

Tag-along prevents this unfair outcome.
```

**Founder Impact:**
- Positive: Ensures fair treatment (same price as investors)
- Negative: Limited negotiating power
- Usually present in Series A+ deals

**What Founders Should Negotiate:**
- Definition of when tag-along applies
- Whether earnout payments count toward tag-along price
- Can founders demand their own sale process separately?
- Do all founders have tag-along or only certain ones?

### Anti-Dilution Provisions

**What They Are:**
Rights that protect investor's ownership percentage if company raises new shares at lower valuation.

**Types of Anti-Dilution:**

#### Full Ratchet (Worst for Founders)
```
Investor bought 1M shares at $1.00 = invested $1M
Investor owns 10% at $10M pre-money

Down round happens: New round at $0.50/share

With full ratchet:
- Investor's shares converted to $0.50 basis
- Investor now owns as if they invested at $0.50
- Investor gets MORE shares for same $1M investment
- Founder dilution: Massive
- Example: Investor might go from 10% to 15% ownership

FORMULA:
New conversion price = Lower price
Investor gets: Original investment ÷ New price (vs. original price)

This is devastating for founders in downrounds.
```

#### Weighted Average (Standard, Founder-Friendly Option)
```
Same scenario:

With weighted average (broad-based):
- Investor's ownership diluted proportionally to round impact
- Investor protected but not overprotected
- Founder dilution: Proportional to economic dilution

FORMULA:
New conversion price = Old price ×
  (pre-money valuation + new investment) /
  (pre-money valuation + new investment at old price)

More complex but fairer to founders.

Example math:
- Pre-round: 10M shares at $1 (assuming) = $10M valuation
- New round: $2M at $0.50 (down round)
- Using broad-based formula:
  New price = $1 × ($10M + $2M) / ($10M + $2M/$0.50)
  = $1 × $12M / $14M
  = $0.857/share

Investor gets some protection (~15% on math) but not 100%.
```

#### No Anti-Dilution (Rarest, Most Founder-Friendly)
```
Investor gets no protection in down round.

Founder perspective: Best
Investor perspective: Terrible

Rarely accepted by institutional investors.
```

**Founder Perspective:**
- Broad-based weighted average = acceptable
- Narrow-based weighted average = negotiable
- Full ratchet = bad, avoid if possible
- Pay-to-play provisions = very bad (old investors must participate in down round or lose rights)

**What to Negotiate:**
- Insist on broad-based weighted average
- If investor demands full ratchet, request cap (protection only up to X%)
- Carve-outs: Anti-dilution doesn't apply if down round above certain threshold
- Exclude certain rounds (ESOP issuance, employee stock purchases)

### Liquidation Preferences

**What They Are:**
Order in which shareholders get paid in sale/liquidation. Often investors get paid before founders.

**Types:**

#### 1x Non-Participating Preference (Most Founder-Friendly Among Investors)
```
Series A Investor invested $2M for 20% at 1x non-participating.

Scenario 1: Company sells for $15M
- Investor gets: $2M (1x preference)
- Remaining $13M distributed pro-rata: Investor gets $13M × 20% = $2.6M
- Investor takes MAXIMUM of: $2M (preference) or $2.6M (pro-rata) = $2.6M

Founders get remaining: $15M - $2.6M = $12.4M

Investor preference doesn't hurt here because sale price so high.

Scenario 2: Company sells for $5M
- Investor gets: $2M (preference first)
- Remaining $3M distributed pro-rata: Investor would get $3M × 20% = $600K
- Investor takes MAXIMUM = $2.6M TOTAL

But wait, only $5M total:
- Investor gets $2M (preference)
- Remaining $3M: Investor's pro-rata = 20% of $3M = $600K
- Total investor gets: $2M + $600K = $2.6M

Founders get: $5M - $2.6M = $2.4M

Investor preference hurts here.
```

**1x Non-Participating is Good Because:**
- Investor only gets preference if liquidation value exceeds preferred shares
- Once preference satisfied, investor participates equally
- Doesn't mean investor gets 2x return

#### Preferred Return (Multiple, Participating)
```
Series A Investor got 2x participating preference.

Scenario: Company sells for $5M
- Investor gets: $4M (2x preference, not $2M)
- Remaining $1M split pro-rata
- This is bad for founders (investor gets 80% of proceeds)

2x participating means:
- Investor's preference is $2M × 2 = $4M
- AND investor participates in remaining distribution
- This is called "double-dipping"
- Very rare in modern deals (investors usually accept non-participating)
```

**Key Negotiation Points:**
- Insist on non-participating (or at least cap on participation)
- Multiple should be 1x, not 1.5x or 2x
- Clarify: Does preference apply in "sale of substantially all assets"?
- Clarify: What about acquisitions vs. liquidations?

### Pro-Rata Rights

**What They Are:**
Investor's right to participate in future funding rounds to maintain ownership percentage.

**How They Work:**
```
Series A Investor owns 20% after $2M investment.

Series B comes around: $5M round.

Series A Investor's pro-rata right:
- Can invest 20% × $5M = $1M
- Maintains 20% ownership
- If doesn't invest, ownership dilutes to ~16.7%

Without pro-rata:
- Investor dilutes every round
- Can lose significant ownership
```

**Founder Impact:**
- Pro-rata rights are standard, usually acceptable
- Problem: Investor can dominate future rounds
- Solution: Anti-dilution does the same thing mostly

**What to Negotiate:**
- Time limit: Pro-rata rights expire after X years or Y rounds
- Minimum investment: Investor can't participate in tiny rounds
- Exclusion: Pro-rata doesn't apply if investor has board seat (avoids conflicts)

## FOUNDER PROTECTIONS TO NEGOTIATE

### Essential Founder Protections

#### 1. Board Representation/Observation Rights

**What to Negotiate:**
- **Board Seat:** Likely only available if $1M+ round and Series A+
- **Board Observer:** Gets to attend board meetings, limited voting rights
- **Minimum Quorum:** Ensure founder voice even if they don't have majority

**Founder Perspective:**
- Critical: Ensures founders aren't surprised by investor decisions
- Provides advance notice of major decisions
- Allows input on hiring, spending, strategy

**What to Push For:**
```
CRITICAL CLAUSES:
□ Board observer rights mandatory if investor has board seat
□ Founder CEO has board seat (for Series A and later)
□ Observer can attend all meetings (no exclusions)
□ Observer can review materials 3 days before meeting
□ Observer signs confidentiality but can access information
□ Board meetings at least monthly (not quarterly)
```

#### 2. Information and Inspection Rights (Founder-Favorable Version)

**Standard Investor Version (Too Broad):**
```
"Company must provide investor with:
- Monthly financial statements
- Annual audited financials
- Quarterly reports on business metrics
- Right to inspect company records anytime"
```

**Better Founder Version:**
```
"Company must provide investor with:
- Quarterly financial statements (not monthly, saves accounting time)
- Annual budget and business plan
- Right to inspect company records during business hours with 5 days notice
- Information rights terminate if investor no longer owns shares
- Company can redact confidential information not related to investment"
```

**Founder Perspective:**
- Monthly financials are expensive and time-consuming
- Quarterly is standard and sufficient
- Inspection rights should have reasonable limitations
- Information rights should terminate if investor leaves

#### 3. Anti-Dilution Cap/Carve-Outs

**What Matters:**
Company should carve out from anti-dilution:
- ESOP issuances (employee stock option plan)
- Employee equity grants
- Strategic equity partnerships
- Stock splits/recapitalizations

**Why Important:**
Without carve-outs, giving employees equity triggers investor anti-dilution.
With carve-outs, can hire and compensate team without investor overreacting.

**What to Push For:**
```
Standard language:
"Anti-dilution rights do not apply to:
- Issuances under employee stock option plan (up to 15% of fully diluted cap table)
- Stock splits, recapitalizations, or stock dividends
- Strategic partnerships authorized by board
- Equity compensation authorized by board not exceeding [X]% of fully diluted shares"
```

#### 4. Governance Protections

**Decisions Requiring Founder Approval (Not Just Investor):**

| Decision | Protect? | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| Hiring/firing CEO | Yes | Founders should approve CEO changes |
| Acquisitions over $X | Yes | Founders may not want exit |
| Dividends or distributions | Yes | Preserves capital |
| Liquidation | Yes | Don't want surprise exit |
| Major debt financing | Yes | Affects balance sheet |
| Change of business | Yes | Affects founder equity value |
| Related-party transactions | Yes | Prevents self-dealing |
| Hiring investor's firm members | Maybe | Conflicts of interest |

**What to Negotiate:**
- **Stockholder Approval:** Certain decisions require all shareholders, not just board
- **Founder Approval:** Certain decisions require founder consent specifically
- **Class Voting:** Decisions affecting preferred stock require preferred shareholder approval
- **Protective Provisions:** Cannot amend terms unfavorable to founder without consent

**Example Language:**
```
"The Company shall not, without the written consent of the Founders:
- Sell substantially all assets
- Liquidate or dissolve
- Issue preferred stock senior to the Series A
- Issue over [X]% new equity in capital raise
- Change the number of board seats
- Amend certificate or bylaws adversely affecting founder shares
- Related-party transactions over $500K"
```

#### 5. Founder-Friendly Vesting and Continuation

**Protection Against:**
- Being fired without equity vesting
- Losing equity in company acquisition
- Investor replacing founder

**What to Negotiate:**

**Single-Trigger Acceleration (Good for Founder):**
```
Scenario: Company gets acquired, founder fired

Single-trigger acceleration means:
- Founder's unvested shares fully vest upon acquisition
- Prevents acquirer from stripping founder's equity
- Founder gets full benefit of equity

Example:
- Founder has 100k shares, 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff
- After 1.5 years: 50k vested, 50k unvested
- Company acquired for $100M
- With single-trigger: Founder's full 100k shares vested
- Founder gets full liquidation preference on their shares
- Very valuable protection
```

**Double-Trigger Acceleration (Compromise):**
```
Acceleration requires TWO triggers:
1. Acquisition of company
2. Termination of founder

Scenario:
- Company acquired, founder fired
- Both triggers met, equity vests
- If founder stays, equity still vests over time
- More investor-friendly but still protects founder

More common in modern deals.
```

**What to Push For:**
- If Series A and later: Double-trigger acceleration on equity
- If Series A: 1-year cliff minimum
- If Series A: Acceleration for "without cause" termination
- If Series A: Acceleration for "constructive termination" (forced to resign)

#### 6. Side Letters and Special Terms

**Use Side Letters For:**
- Investor-specific terms (not in main agreement)
- Most-favored-nation clause
- Founder-favorable modifications
- Information access modifications
- Board seat/observer terms

**Important:** Side letters supplement but don't contradict main agreement. Use for accommodations.

**Founder-Friendly Example:**
```
[Company] will grant [Investor] information and inspection rights as follows:
- Quarterly (not monthly) financial statements within 45 days of quarter end
- Annual budget 30 days before fiscal year
- Right to inspect books during normal business hours with 5 business days notice
- Information rights terminate if investor transfers all shares or drops below 1% ownership
- Company may redact confidential information not related to this investment
```

## INVESTOR PROTECTIONS (WHAT TO EXPECT)

### What Investors Always Get (Accept This)

| Protection | Standard Terms | Founder Concern | Negotiation Points |
|------------|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Representations** | Company reps + founder reps | Company liable for false statements | Get "knowledge qualifier" and time limits |
| **Board Seat/Observer** | Seat for lead, observer for participating investors | Investor influence | Ensure board access is reciprocal |
| **Liquidation Preference** | 1x non-participating for Seed, 1x participating for Series A+ | Investor gets priority in exit | Negotiate to non-participating |
| **Anti-Dilution** | Weighted average broad-based | Ownership protection | Accept this, negotiate carve-outs |
| **Pro-Rata Rights** | Can participate in future rounds up to ownership % | Investor controls future decisions | Accept, push for minimum participation |
| **Drag-Along Rights** | Can force exit if majority agrees | Can't block bad acquisitions | Get minimum price threshold |
| **Registration Rights** | Can require company to register shares for sale | Investor can forced IPO timeline | Negotiate to demand rights not S-3 |

### What Investors Often Push For (Negotiate These)

#### 1. Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) Clause

**What It Is:**
Investor automatically gets terms as good as any other investor.

**How It Works:**
```
Series A: You get two investors
- Investor A: $2M at $10/share
- Investor B: $3M at $10/share

If you later give Investor C a better deal:
- Investor C: Better terms, lower price, higher cap, etc.

Both A and B can invoke MFN:
- "Give us same terms as C or we get automatic adjustment"
- You can't differentiate investor terms

This locks in earliest investor terms for all subsequent investors.
```

**Founder Perspective:**
- Negative: Can't negotiate flexibly with different investors
- Positive: Doesn't matter if all investors get same terms anyway
- Risk: Locks you in to worst terms from first investor

**What to Negotiate:**
- MFN applies to new investors same round (not future rounds)
- Excludes side letters (special terms don't trigger MFN)
- Excludes timing/price differences (SAFE timing differences okay)
- Time limit: MFN only applies if new investor within 30/60 days

#### 2. Participation Rights

**What They Are:**
Right to participate in liquidation preferences AND pro-rata share.

**How It Works (Bad for Founders):**
```
Investor has 2x participating preference on $2M investment.

Company sells for $10M.

Investor gets:
- $4M (2x preference)
- Plus pro-rata share of remaining $6M
- = $4M + (2x% × $6M) = ~$5.2M+

This is "double-dipping": Investor benefits twice.

Without participation:
- Investor gets max of: $4M (pref) or pro-rata ($2M+)
- = $4M
- Founder gets $6M instead of $4.8M
```

**What to Negotiate:**
- Insist on non-participating preference (very standard now)
- If investor insists on participating: Cap it at 1.5x or 2x maximum
- Exclude from participation: Founder shares, common stock

#### 3. Registration Rights

**What They Are:**
Investor can force company to register shares for public sale (IPO).

**Types:**

**Demand Rights (Investor Can Demand):**
- Investor can force IPO even if founders don't want it
- Usually limited to 2-3 demands after valuation threshold
- Very founder-unfavorable

**Piggyback Rights (Investor Piggybacking):**
- If company doing IPO, investor can sell shares too
- Standard, acceptable
- Founders usually want this too

**S-3 Rights (Easy Public Sale):**
- Company must allow investor to sell shares via S-3 registration
- Only available after company reaches $75M+ public float
- Far in future, lower impact

**What to Negotiate:**
```
PUSH FOR:
□ No demand rights (or limit to 1 demand after $1B+ valuation)
□ Investor must notify 30 days before demanding IPO
□ Piggyback rights only (standard)
□ S-3 rights optional (later stage)
□ Exclude from registration: Information rights, investor can't force disclosure
```

#### 4. Cumulative Preferences (If Multiple Rounds)

**What They Are:**
Investor preference stacks with previous investor preferences.

**Example Problem:**
```
Seed Round: $1M at 1x preference (Seed investor)
Series A: $5M at 1x preference (Series A investor)

Company sells for $10M.

Cumulative preferences:
- Seed investor gets: $1M
- Series A investor gets: $5M
- Total preferences: $6M
- Remaining: $4M for founders and common holders

This "piles up" quickly with multiple rounds.

Without cumulative, only latest round investors get preference.

SAFER FOR FOUNDER:
- Only latest investor get preferences (Series A)
- Seed investors converted to common-equivalent
```

**What to Negotiate:**
- Convertible securities (SAFE, note) should convert, not keep preferences
- Each round investor gets preference, but earlier rounds become common
- Clear waterfall: Which preferences apply first?

## RED FLAGS IN TERM SHEETS

### Massive Red Flags (Walk Away)

| Red Flag | Why It's Bad | Example | What to Do |
|----------|------------|---------|-----------|
| **Full Ratchet Anti-Dilution** | Destroys founder equity in any down round | "Investor's price adjusts to any lower future price" | Reject, walk away |
| **No Board Seat for Founder** | Company taken over by investor | "Investor gets board seat, CEO doesn't" | Reject, negotiate |
| **Cumulative Preferences** | Multiple investor preferences eliminate founder value | "$5M seed + $20M Series A = $25M preferences from $30M sale" | Require each round investor preference only |
| **Liquidation Preference > 2x** | Investor gets everything if exit under 2x multiple | "3x participating preference" | Reject, offer 1x non-participating |
| **Approval Required for Minor Decisions** | Investor has veto over everything | "Investor approval required for any hire over $50K" | Reject, limit to major decisions |
| **Personal Guarantees on Company Debt** | Founder personally liable if company fails | "Founder personally guarantees investor loan" | Reject, negotiate to company liability only |
| **20+ Year Rights** | Investor rights hang over company forever | "Registration rights, information rights never expire" | Add sunset: 7 years or IPO, whichever first |
| **Indemnification Cap > Investment Amount** | Liability unlimited for misrepresentations | "Company indemnifies investor for unlimited IP claims" | Cap at 50% of investment or time limit |

### Serious Red Flags (Negotiate Hard)

| Red Flag | Why It's Concerning | What to Negotiate |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Dividend Preference** | Investor gets paid money instead of equity | Limit to residual after preference, or exclude |
| **Conversion Multiple** | Investor's convertible note converts at unfavorable multiple | Limit to 1x conversion price maximum |
| **Call Rights** | Investor can force founder to buy out shares | Require "double trigger" + founder approval |
| **Forced Redemption** | Investor can force company to repay investment | Limit to liquidity events, require board approval |
| **Onerous Conditions to Close** | Too many requirements prevent closing | List conditions, negotiate limits |
| **Founder Vesting Cliffs > 1 Year** | Founder loses equity if leaves before cliff | Push down to 6 months, get double-trigger acceleration |
| **Series A Price Too Low** | Valuation doesn't reflect progress | Challenge with data, get multiple investor bids |
| **No Founder Protections** | Agreement only protects investor | Add founder vesting, board observation, acceleration |
| **Extensive Reps & Warranties** | Too many representations founder can breach | Get "knowledge qualifiers," time limits, carve-outs |
| **No Time Limits on Indemnification** | Investor can sue forever | Cap at 18-24 months |

### Yellow Flags (Usual But Challenge Them)

| Flag | Investor Expects | Counter-Offer |
|------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Monthly Financial Reporting** | "We want monthly financials immediately" | Quarterly is sufficient, give grace period |
| **Unlimited Information Rights** | "We can inspect books anytime" | Business hours, 5 days notice, reasonable scope |
| **Very Broad Reps** | "Company owns all IP worldwide" | Add "to company's knowledge," exclude known issues |
| **Long Representation Survival** | "Reps survive 3 years" | Cap at 18 months, with carve-outs |
| **Automatic Board Seat** | "Investor gets board seat automatically" | Board seat + founder CEO seat (not either/or) |
| **Pro-Rata Rights No Time Limit** | "Pro-rata rights forever" | Expire after 7 years or 3 years post-exit |
| **Broad Definitions** | "Sale of substantially all assets" | Define "substantially" = > 50% assets + > 50% revenue |

### Green Flags (What to Look For)

| Good Signal | What It Means |
|------------|--------------|
| **1x Non-Participating Preference** | Investor not trying to double-dip |
| **Weighted Average Anti-Dilution** | Fair to founder in downturns |
| **Founder-Friendly Information Rights** | Quarterly not monthly, reasonable scope |
| **Time-Limited Reps (18 months)** | Not suing founder forever |
| **Non-Participating, No MFN** | Flexible for future rounds |
| **Clear Board Structure** | Founder seat protected |
| **Simple One-Page Side Letter** | No hidden surprises |
| **Quick Closings (3-5 days)** | Investor prepared, not last-minute |
| **Investor Reps Equal to Company Reps** | Fair deal, mutual obligations |
| **Multiple Investor Bids** | Competition drives better terms |

## HOW TO REVIEW LEGAL DOCUMENTS

### Step-by-Step Founder Checklist

#### Phase 1: Initial Document Receipt (Day 1)

```
□ Get full document package:
  - SAFE / Convertible / Stock Purchase Agreement
  - All exhibits and schedules
  - Investor side letter (if any)
  - Term sheet (if priced round)

□ Check dates and versions:
  - Document dated today (not old draft)
  - Version number (get latest)
  - Exhibits current and complete

□ Request redlines (if not included):
  - "Do you have tracked changes showing what changed from template?"
  - Compare to standard (Y Combinator SAFE, TechStars, etc.)

□ Request timeline:
  - When must we respond?
  - When is closing?
  - Any conditions precedent?
```

#### Phase 2: High-Level Review (Days 1-2)

```
□ Read introductory sections:
  - Who are the parties?
  - What's the investment amount?
  - What's the instrument type (SAFE, note, stock)?

□ Find and review key terms:
  - Investment amount
  - Valuation cap or price per share
  - Discount (if convertible)
  - Liquidation preference type and multiple

□ Identify deal killers:
  - Full ratchet anti-dilution?
  - Cumulative preferences?
  - Founder not on board?
  - Terms you definitely won't accept?

□ Note sections to discuss with lawyer:
  - Mark concerning provisions
  - Create questions list
  - Compare to other investor terms
```

#### Phase 3: Section-by-Section Deep Dive (Days 2-3)

```
REPRESENTATIONS & WARRANTIES SECTION:
□ Company reps reasonable?
□ Survival period? (18-24 months = good)
□ Baskets/caps on liability?
□ Knowledge qualifiers?
□ Carve-outs for known issues?

PURCHASE TERMS:
□ Price per share or SAFE cap clear?
□ Calculation of conversion fair?
□ Valuation cap how calculated?
□ Any weird contingencies?

INVESTOR RIGHTS:
□ Board seat / observer rights clear?
□ Information rights reasonable scope?
□ Inspection rights with notice period?
□ Pro-rata rights reasonable?

ANTI-DILUTION:
□ Type specified (weighted average)?
□ Carve-outs for ESOP, hires, etc.?
□ Any caps on anti-dilution effect?
□ Clear trigger for anti-dilution?

LIQUIDATION / PREFERENCES:
□ Preference type clear?
□ Multiple correct (1x or higher)?
□ Participating or non-participating specified?
□ Clear waterfall of who gets paid in what order?

GOVERNANCE & MAJOR DECISION APPROVALS:
□ Any investor veto rights?
□ Board approval sufficient or more needed?
□ List of decisions requiring approval?
□ Founder approval needed for any?

INDEMNIFICATION:
□ Cap on indemnification?
□ Basket (minimum claim size)?
□ Time limit on survival?
□ Any investor indemnification of company?

VESTING & ACCELERATION:
□ Founder equity vesting schedule?
□ Any single or double trigger acceleration?
□ What happens if founder fired?
```

#### Phase 4: Compare to Market Standards (Day 3)

```
Create comparison table:

TERM | YOUR AGREEMENT | STANDARD | GAP
-----|----------------|----------|-----
Liquidation Preference | 1.5x participating | 1x non-participating | WORSE
Anti-Dilution | Full ratchet | Weighted average | WORSE
Information Rights | Monthly | Quarterly | WORSE
Founder Board Seat | Not mentioned | Automatic | MISSING
Information Survival | 3 years | 18 months | WORSE
Drag-along | Unrestricted | >$10M threshold | WORSE
Registration Rights | Demand after 1 investor | Piggyback only | WORSE

Where you have gaps or worse terms, these are negotiation priorities.
```

#### Phase 5: Question List for Lawyer (Day 4)

```
CREATE DOCUMENT FOR LAWYER:
1. Deal summary (amount, type, investor)
2. List of specific concerns
3. Comparison to standards
4. What's non-negotiable for you
5. What you're willing to compromise on

EXAMPLES:
Q: "Liquidation preference is 1.5x participating. Market is 1x non-participating.
    Can we negotiate to non-participating?"

Q: "Full ratchet anti-dilution will devastate founders in down round.
    Can we change to weighted average?"

Q: "Representation survival is 3 years. Can we limit to 18 months?"

Q: "Where is founder board seat? This needs to be added."

Q: "Information rights require monthly financials. Our CFO says quarterly
    is all we can do. Can we negotiate to quarterly?"
```

#### Phase 6: Lawyer Review & Redlines (Days 4-7)

```
What to Expect:
□ Lawyer reviews (2-3 business days typically)
□ Lawyer provides redlines (tracked changes)
□ Lawyer provides memo with key issues
□ Lawyer recommends negotiation priorities

What to Ask Your Lawyer:
□ "What are the 3-5 biggest issues to negotiate?"
□ "What's industry standard?"
□ "What would you recommend we accept vs. reject?"
□ "What's a reasonable timeline for negotiations?"
□ "Are there any deal-killer issues?"

Review Together:
□ Lawyer walks through redlines
□ You understand each change
□ You prioritize which terms to fight for
□ You identify what to compromise on
```

#### Phase 7: Negotiation (Days 7-14)

```
NEGOTIATION STRATEGY:
□ Prioritize: What MUST change vs. nice-to-have?
□ Group changes: Related changes negotiated together
□ Propose package: "We'll accept X if you accept Y"
□ Use comps: "Our other Series A investor has Y term"
□ Get in writing: All agreements documented in redlines

PACE:
- First round: Submit all changes at once
- Second round: Investor responds with counter-offers
- Third round: Final negotiation on remaining issues
- Closing: All parties sign final version
```

#### Phase 8: Final Review Before Signing (Day of Closing)

```
□ Read entire final document word-for-word
□ Compare to last version you agreed to
□ Verify all negotiated changes included
□ Verify no new terms snuck in
□ Check signature blocks:
  - All parties signing?
  - Correct company name?
  - Founder names spelled right?

□ Check exhibits:
  - Are all referenced exhibits included?
  - Are exhibits correct/current?
  - Do exhibits match document references?

□ Check supporting docs:
  - Board resolutions prepared?
  - Cap table updated?
  - 409A valuation done (if needed)?

□ Verify closing timeline:
  - When are documents signed?
  - When is money wired?
  - When do funds appear in account?
```

### Red Flag Sections to Review Carefully

#### Most Important: Liquidation Preferences
```
Why: Determines what you get in exit
Review carefully: The entire waterfall
Ask: "What do I own if company worth $X in exit?"

Create scenarios:
- $5M exit (down round)
- $20M exit (modest success)
- $100M+ exit (success)
- Calculate your proceeds in each scenario
```

#### Most Negotiable: Anti-Dilution
```
Why: Biggest value loss in down round
Standard: Weighted average broad-based
Problem: Full ratchet or narrow-based
Fix: Propose weighted average with carve-outs
```

#### Most Vague: Representations
```
Why: Endless liability if too broad
Standard: 18-month survival with basket/cap
Problem: 3+ year survival, unlimited liability
Fix: Add knowledge qualifier, survival limits
```

## COST-SAVING STRATEGIES

### Legitimate Ways to Save 50%+ on Legal Costs

#### 1. Use Standardized Templates (Saves $10-20K)

**Free or Cheap Templates:**
- **Y Combinator SAFE:** Free, standard market terms
  - Use post-money version (most founder-friendly)
  - Minimal customization needed
  - Available at ycombinator.com

- **TechStars Model Docs:** Free/cheap for TechStars companies
  - Pre-negotiated terms
  - Used by many angels

- **Series Seed:** Flat-fee legal service
  - Fixed price: ~$3-5K
  - Full Series A documents
  - Good for $500K+ rounds

- **Online Services:** LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer, etc.
  - $500-2K for document prep
  - Okay for simple cases
  - NOT okay for complex negotiations

**Strategy:**
- Start with free template (SAFE)
- Have lawyer customize, not draft from scratch
- Edit time = much less expensive than drafting time
- Can save $15-25K in legal fees

#### 2. Batch Work to Minimize Legal Hours

**What This Means:**
Lawyer charged by hour, so batch similar work.

**Example:**
```
Bad: Close 3 SAFE investors over 3 weeks
- Lawyer time: 5 hours per closing = 15 hours
- Cost: 15 × $300/hour = $4,500

Good: Close 3 SAFEs in one week with same terms
- Lawyer time: 5 hours for first, 2 hours for 2nd, 1 hour for 3rd
  (because similar work) = 8 hours
- Cost: 8 × $300/hour = $2,400
- Savings: $2,100 (47% reduction)

Key: Identical or very similar terms = much faster
```

**How to Batch:**
- Negotiate main terms with first investor
- Use exact same terms for similar investors
- Use side letter for investor-specific modifications (faster)
- Close all similar investors in one week
- All lawyer work at once = efficiencies

#### 3. Negotiate with Investor on Legal Terms

**What This Means:**
Ask investor to use standardized language and reasonable terms.

**Script:**
```
"We're raising from multiple investors with similar checks.
To keep costs down for company and make this efficient:

Can we use [Y Combinator SAFE / Series Seed] terms?
Standard terms investors are comfortable with?

For any investor-specific requests, we can use a side letter
rather than redlining the base agreement.

This saves legal time (and costs) for both of us."
```

**Why Investors Accept This:**
- Professional investors want standard terms anyway
- Reduces THEIR legal review time too
- Shows founders are thoughtful about business

**Savings:**
- Using SAFE vs. custom note: $15-20K savings
- 2-3 investors on same SAFE terms: $5-10K savings
- Side letters instead of redlines: $3-5K savings
- **Total: $25-35K potential savings**

#### 4. Do Your Own Work Before Legal

**What You Can Do (Free or $100-200 in tools):**

| Task | What to Do | Savings |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Document assembly** | Download template, fill in investor amounts | $1-2K |
| **Cap table management** | Use spreadsheet or Carta free version | $1-2K |
| **Signature collection** | Use DocuSign or Hellosign | Included in cost |
| **Entity formation** | Use LegalZoom or Stripe Atlas | $200-500 |
| **First draft side letters** | Copy prior investor side letter, modify | $1-2K |
| **Board minutes template** | Use precedent from first round | $500-1K |
| **Investment timeline** | Create project timeline yourself | Included |
| **Investor packet assembly** | Compile docs yourself, lawyer reviews | $1-2K |

**What NOT to Do Yourself:**
- DON'T negotiate major terms (waste time, get it wrong)
- DON'T redline legal docs without lawyer review
- DON'T draft representations/warranties
- DON'T structure anti-dilution mechanics
- DON'T do indemnification language

**Strategy:**
- You do heavy lifting on assembly/organization
- Lawyer does actual legal work
- Saves lawyer time = lower bills

#### 5. Use Less Expensive Firms

**Cost by Firm Type:**

| Firm Type | Hourly Rate | Quality | When to Use |
|-----------|------------|---------|------------|
| **Top-Tier Venture Counsel** | $400-700/hr | Top quality | Series B+, complex |
| **Boutique Venture Firm** | $250-400/hr | Very good | Series A, $1M+ round |
| **Regional Startup Attorney** | $150-250/hr | Good | Seed/Series A |
| **Contract Counsel Platform** | $100-200/hr | Okay | Simple SAFE/note |
| **Law School Clinics** | $0-100/hr | Variable | If available, pre-seed only |

**Strategy:**
- For SAFE (under $500K): Regional firm fine
- For Convertible ($100K-1M): Regional firm or boutique
- For Series A ($1M+): Boutique or top-tier
- For Series B+: Top-tier recommended
- Mix approach: Use cheaper for drafting, expensive for negotiation

**Savings:** $50-100K potential depending on firm choice

#### 6. Defer Legal Work Strategically

**What You Can Defer (Not Skip):**

| Work | Must Do Before | Can Defer Until |
|------|---|---|
| SAFE/Note | Investment | N/A (done before) |
| Conversions at Series A | Series A close | 30 days after series A (sometimes) |
| Option grant documents | First employee | Can batch later employees |
| 409A valuation | Option grants | Can do for first batch |
| IP assignment agreements | Employee hire | Can batch every 6 months |
| Updated cap table | Investor request | Before Series A (not every update) |

**What NOT to Defer:**
- Legal documents before investment
- Incorporation before fundraising
- Board resolutions before closing
- Representations signing at closing

**Savings:** $5-10K by consolidating later work

#### 7. Get Investor to Pay Legal (Sometimes)

**When This Happens:**
- Series A and later rounds
- Multiple investors
- Company might be able to use "company counsel" for both

**How to Ask:**
```
"We're raising from multiple investors. Can we share legal counsel
between investors to reduce redundancy? This reduces costs for
everyone - company and investors both benefit."

Or:

"Our lead investor and we using same counsel to reduce costs.
Other investors welcome to have counsel review, but we'll batch
legal work to minimize additional expense."
```

**Don't Expect:**
- Investor to pay company's legal
- Investor to allow you to use investor's counsel
- Investor to waive counsel review entirely

**What Sometimes Works:**
- Company law firm drafts documents
- Investor counsel does light review instead of full redline
- Negotiation work shared between counsel
- Investor counsel doesn't re-litigate already settled terms

**Savings:** $5-15K if investor shares legal costs

#### 8. Flat-Fee Legal Services

**Emerging Options:**

| Service | Cost | What's Included | Pros | Cons |
|---------|------|----------------|------|------|
| **Series Seed** | $3-5K flat | Series A documents, board docs | Fixed cost, known price | Limited customization |
| **Stripe Atlas** | Free (w/ incorporation) | LLC formation | Very cheap | Simple only |
| **Airtake** | ~$3K flat | Series A documents | Fixed price, templates | Limited negotiation support |
| **Carta legal** | Varies | Cap table + docs | Integrated with cap table | Expensive overall |

**Strategy:**
- For first raise: Flat-fee might be good if simple
- For follow-up: Flat-fee probably too limiting
- Use flat-fee for baseline documents, layer in attorney for customization

**Savings:** $5-15K if simple structure

### What Not to Cut (Spend Money Here)

**DO NOT Try to Save Money On:**

1. **Experienced Lawyer for Series A+**
   - Risk: Misunderstand anti-dilution
   - Risk: Liquidation preference errors
   - Cost of error: $100K-1M+
   - Save: $0 here, spend $50K on good counsel

2. **Representations Review**
   - Risk: Broad reps create future liability
   - Risk: Get sued 2 years later
   - Cost of lawsuit: $50K-500K
   - Save: $0 here, spend proper time on reps

3. **Indemnification Language**
   - Risk: Unlimited indemnification liability
   - Risk: Lawsuit costs exceed investment amount
   - Cost of error: Capped at investment or more
   - Save: $0 here, spend proper time

4. **Equity Math/Anti-Dilution**
   - Risk: Don't understand dilution
   - Risk: Assume dilution X%, actually Y%
   - Cost of error: Ownership loss worth $100K-1M+
   - Save: $0 here, spend $5K to understand properly

5. **Board/Governance Terms**
   - Risk: Investor takes over company
   - Risk: Can't make decisions without investor approval
   - Cost of error: Loss of company control
   - Save: $0 here, negotiate properly

```

□ Compare 3 attorney quotes (free)

AFTER HIRING ATTORNEY:
```

## WHEN TO WALK AWAY FROM A DEAL

### Deal-Killer Red Flags (Walk Away Immediately)

#### 1. Founder Completely Off Board (No Governance)

**The Situation:**
- Investor gets board seat
- Co-founder not on board
- Founder has no governance involvement
- Major decisions made without founder input

**Why This Is Unacceptable:**
- Company could be sold without your agreement
- Major strategic decisions made against your vision
- Company could hire incompetent CEO
- You could be fired and have no say

**Example:**
```
Term sheet says: "Series A investor gets 1 board seat.
Company will have 3 board seats total."

If only investor, you (as co-founder), and someone else:
- Investor could vote with CEO/other to outvote you
- Investor could replace you with new CEO
- You're minority with no governance rights

WALK AWAY unless:
- You get board seat as CEO
- Or you get observer rights with veto on major decisions
- Or board size ensures founder cannot be minority
```

**Non-Negotiable Fix:**
```
"Series A investor gets one board seat. CEO [Founder name] has
second board seat. Any future board seat requires mutual
agreement of investor and founder."
```

#### 2. Liquidation Preference That Gives Investor Everything

**The Situation:**
```
Series A investor: 2x participating preference on $2M investment

Company sells for $10M

Investor gets: $4M (2x preference) + pro-rata share = ~$5-6M
Founder gets: $4-5M total (after investor)

You just built a $10M company but only get $4-5M
because investor set onerous preference.
```

**When to Walk:**
- 2x+ participating preference in Series A (unacceptable)
- Investor preference leaves founder with <30% of upside in modest exits
- Cumulative preferences from multiple rounds exceed 1.5-2x total investment

**Example Calculation:**
```
Your situation:
- Seed: $500K at 1x non-participating
- Series A: $2M at 2x participating (investor asking)

Company sells for $10M:
- Seed investor: $500K
- Series A investor: $4M (2x on $2M) + extra from pro-rata

You get: < $5M from $10M company

WALK AWAY: Liquidation preference worse than 1.5x non-participating in Series A
```

**What to Demand Instead:**
- 1x non-participating for Series A (industry standard)
- If investor insists on more: Walk away

#### 3. Full Ratchet Anti-Dilution

**The Situation:**
Any down round and investor's ownership adjusts downward automatically.

**Why This Kills Deals:**
- Down round happens (inevitable for some companies)
- Investor ownership increases (you diluted massively)
- Down round financing becomes impossible
- Can't hire with equity (can't dilute further)

**Example:**
```
Series A: $2M at $10/share, investor owns 20%

Down round: New investor at $5/share

Full ratchet:
- Investor's shares convert to $5 basis
- Investor now owns 30%+ (massive dilution to founders)
- Series B investors see massive down round = won't invest
- Company can't raise further = dies

Happens to: Many startups that had down round
Result: Founder dilution makes hiring impossible, company fails
```

**When to Walk:**
- Investor insists on full ratchet anti-dilution
- Won't agree to weighted average broad-based
- This is common in first-time VCs, worth walking away

**What to Demand:**
- Weighted average broad-based anti-dilution (standard)
- With carve-outs for ESOP and hires
- Walk away if investor won't agree

#### 4. No Founder Equity Acceleration or Cliffs > 1 Year

**The Situation:**
```
You as founder have 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff.

After 2 years, investor wants to replace you.

You lose all unvested equity (~50%).
Acquirer doesn't want to wait for your vesting.
You get fired and lose half your ownership stake.
```

**Why This Is Unacceptable:**
- You're building the company, your equity at-risk
- But investor can force you out anytime
- You lose everything you haven't vested

**When to Walk:**
- Investor wants you to vest 4 years with 1-year cliff
- Won't agree to single-trigger acceleration
- Won't reduce cliff to 6 months or less
- For Series A, this is often negotiable, worth fighting for

**What to Demand:**
```
Founder equity vesting:
- 4-year vesting with 6-month cliff (or 1 year maximum)
- Single-trigger acceleration (100% of remaining vests if company acquired)
- Double-trigger acceleration acceptable (if good severance)
- Severance: 6-month salary if fired without cause

This is standard for Series A.
```

#### 5. Excessive Personal Guarantees or Founder Liability

**The Situation:**
```
Investor: "We need your personal guarantee on company debt."

You: "If company fails, I'm personally liable for $2M investment."

This means: If company fails, investor can sue you personally
and seize your home, other assets, etc.
```

**Why This Is Unacceptable:**
- Company failure = you're bankrupted personally
- Contradicts equity investment model
- Highly unusual and dangerous

**When to Walk:**
- Investor demands personal guarantee
- Investor demands security interest in founder's home
- Investor demands personal liability for company debt
- Walk away immediately

**What to Accept Instead:**
- Equity investment with no personal guarantees
- If investor requires guarantee: This is more like a loan, not equity
- Explore loan options instead

#### 6. Complete Loss of Control (No Voting Rights)

**The Situation:**
```
Term sheet: "Founder common stock has no voting rights.
Preferred investor shares have exclusive voting rights."

You can't:
- Approve major decisions
- Hire/fire CEO
- Sell company
- Change business direction
- Issue new equity
- Approve budgets

This is not a job, it's a loss of control.
```

**When to Walk:**
- Investor tries to strip voting rights from founder
- Tries to make founder fully non-participating
- This is highly unusual but worth walking away if attempted

**What to Accept:**
- Preferred investors have extra rights (normal)
- But founder common shares still have equal voting to other common
- Investor super-majority approval on major decisions (normal)
- But not complete voting stripping

#### 7. Terms That Become Impossible to Raise Again

**The Situation:**
```
First investor terms:
- Full ratchet anti-dilution
- Cumulative 1.5x participating preferences
- Onerous quarterly reporting
- Broad indemnification
- Investor approval required for any hire

Series A comes around:
- New Series A investor reviews terms
- Sees full ratchet = investor can't raise Series B easily
- Sees cumulative preferences = investor gets squeezed
- New investor demands:
  - New terms as good or better (MFN kicks in)
  - Or discount for complexity

Result:
- Series A terms become worse
- Or Series A doesn't happen
- Or future rounds become impossible
```

**When to Walk:**
- Investor terms are so bad they preclude future fundraising
- Investor doesn't understand they're setting precedent for all future investors
- Terms prevent future investors from getting reasonable deal

**What to Accept:**
- Reasonable investor protections
- Standard market terms for round size
- Terms that future investors find acceptable

### Walkaway Decision Matrix

| Situation | Should You Walk? | Why? |
|-----------|-----------------|------|
| Investor won't negotiate full ratchet anti-dilution | **YES** | Full ratchet is deal-killer |
| Investor won't give founder board seat | **YES** | Loss of control |
| Liquidation preference leaves you <30% upside | **MAYBE** | Depends on alternative options |
| Investor wants 3x participating preference | **YES** | Investor taking everything |
| Investor demands personal guarantee | **YES** | Not equity investment |
| Investor won't agree to board observation | **MAYBE** | Can work with side letter |
| Representations survive 3 years | **NO** | Standard, negotiate to 18 months |
| Information rights require monthly reporting | **NO** | Negotiate to quarterly |
| Investor wants veto on every hire | **YES** | Loss of operational control |
| Investor cap table shows 40% founder post-round | **MAYBE** | Depends if funds sufficient |
| No acceleration on acquisition | **MAYBE** | Can negotiate double-trigger |
| Series A price lower than expected | **MAYBE** | Do your diligence, possibly walk |
| Only one investor interested (no alternatives) | **NO** | Keep negotiating but accept some terms |

### Negotiation Walkaway (Different from Deal Walkaway)

**Know When Negotiations Themselves Aren't Working:**

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Investor won't respond to redlines for 5+ days | Investor not serious | Move on to next investor |
| Investor demands new concession for every concession you make | Negotiation in bad faith | Walk away from investor |
| Investor threatens to pull deal if you push back | Investor is bully | Walk away, better investors exist |
| Investor asks for new major term after agreeing to terms | Investor changing goalposts | Walk away, likely scam |
| Investor legal counsel more aggressive than investor | Lawyer being unreasonable | Escalate to investor directly |
| Timeline keeps extending with no clear close date | Investor stalling | Walk away, fundraise elsewhere |
| Investor's references say deal takes 6+ months to close | This is true indicator | Accept long timeline or walk |

### How to Walk Away Professionally

**If You Decide to Walk:**

1. **Send professional email:**
```
"Dear [Investor],

After careful consideration of the proposed terms, we've decided
to pursue other opportunities at this time. We particularly had
concerns about [specific terms], which are outside our parameters
for this round.

We appreciate your interest in [Company] and would welcome
re-engaging if terms align in future.

Best regards,
[Founder]"
```

2. **Don't Burn Bridges:**
   - Investor landscape is small
   - You might work together later
   - Keep it professional and brief
   - Don't send 10-page criticism letter

3. **Close the Loop:**
   - Return any documents
   - Confirm you're ending negotiations
   - Move forward to other investors
   - Stop responding to investor follow-ups

4. **Reframe in Your Head:**
   - You didn't fail, you maintained standards
   - Bad deal worse than no deal
   - Better investors exist
   - You'll appreciate the standards later

## CONCLUSION: LEGAL IS YOUR RESPONSIBILITY

### Key Takeaways

**1. Hire an Experienced Lawyer for Priced Rounds**
- Seed round ($250K+): Regional lawyer, $15-40K
- Series A+: Experienced venture counsel, $50K+
- Error costs 10-100x legal fees, worth the investment

**2. Use Standard Terms for SAFEs**
- Y Combinator SAFE template: Free
- Minimal customization: Save $20K+
- Professional investors expect standard terms

**3. Understand the Key Terms Yourself**
- Don't just sign what lawyer says is fine
- Learn liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, etc.
- You live with these terms for years
- Lawyer guides, but you decide

**4. Negotiate Hard on Deal-Killers**
- Liquidation preferences: Non-negotiable, must be reasonable
- Board seats: Non-negotiable, founder must have voice
- Anti-dilution: Must be weighted average, not full ratchet
- Everything else: Negotiable within reason

**5. Create Walkaway Criteria Before Negotiations**
- What terms are unacceptable?
- What level of dilution can you live with?
- What does success look like?
- Only negotiate within bounds you set

**6. Build Relationships with Good Counsel**
- Find lawyer early (before you need them)
- Build relationship for future rounds
- Good counsel becomes business advisor
- Worth premium for experience

**7. Legal is Not Just About Closing**
- Legal terms affect future fundraising
- Legal terms affect your final ownership
- Legal terms affect control of company
- Get this right, then build company
