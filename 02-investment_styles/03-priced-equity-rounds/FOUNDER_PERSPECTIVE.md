# Founder Perspective on Priced Equity Rounds

## Overview: What Founders Need to Know

Priced equity rounds are milestone moments for founders. You've reached product-market fit, proven metrics, and now investors want to bet on your company at a specific valuation. This is exciting—but also the moment when you lose absolute control of your company.

This guide covers what founders should focus on, watch out for, and negotiate during priced rounds.

---

## The Harsh Reality: What Equity Rounds Actually Mean

### Your Ownership Dilution Path

**Before you raise any capital**, understand the dilution path:

- **After Seed ($2M at $8M pre)**: You own ~83% (you had 100%)
- **After Series A ($10M at $20M pre)**: You own ~65% (diluted from seed pool)
- **After Series B ($30M at $60M pre)**: You own ~45-50%
- **After Series C ($50M at $100M pre)**: You own ~30-35%
- **After Series D+**: You own <25%

**By the time you reach Series D**, you own less than 1/4 of your company.

### Loss of Control: The Real Cost

**With each round, you lose decision-making power**:

1. **Seed**: You still control strategy. Investor has observer rights.
2. **Series A**: You lose majority control (1-2 investor directors on 5-person board). Major decisions require investor approval via protective provisions.
3. **Series B**: You're outnumbered on board (2 investors, maybe 1-2 founders). Investor veto on material decisions.
4. **Series C**: You're a minority stakeholder in decision-making. Investors control company direction.

**By Series C, investors control your company. You're an employee.**

## Key Terms That Matter Most to Founders

### 1. Pre-Money Valuation (Your Biggest Negotiation)

**Why it matters**: Pre-money valuation determines how much dilution you take.

**Simple math**:
- $10M pre-money + $2M investment = $12M post-money
- Your dilution = $2M / $12M = 16.67%
- You go from 100% to 83.33%

**If pre-money was $8M instead**:
- $8M pre-money + $2M investment = $10M post-money
- Your dilution = $2M / $10M = 20%
- You go from 100% to 80%

**Higher pre-money = less dilution. This is THE negotiation.**

**How to benchmark pre-money**:
- Look at comps: similar-stage companies, same metric level
- Post-revenue multiple: 2-5x annual revenue for Seed, 5-10x for Series A
- Traction multiples: $1M ARR valued at $10-20M pre-money (Series A)
- Growth multiple: 10x growth rate (100% growth = $100M pre-money)
- Rule of 40: Company growing at X% + Y% profit margin
- Comparable companies: raised at similar stage with similar metrics

**Reality**: Investors will offer lower than you want. This is the negotiation.

### 2. Type of Liquidation Preference (Participating vs. Non-Participating)

**Non-Participating Preferred** (You want this):
- Investor gets their $2M back **OR** their pro-rata share of proceeds
- Whichever is greater
- In good exits, investor converts and gets pro-rata (good for you)
- In modest exits, investor takes their preference and leaves more for you

**Participating Preferred** (Investor wants this):
- Investor gets their $2M back **PLUS** their pro-rata of remaining proceeds
- "Double-dipping" - get back investment AND extra return
- In all exit scenarios, investor gets their preference plus additional share

**Example Impact at $50M exit with $10M Series A investment**:

**Non-Participating**:
- Investor gets $10M back (preference is $10M, proceeds are $50M)
- Since proceeds > preference, investor converts and gets 20% of $50M = $10M
- (Same result, but investor didn't "stack" preference)
- Founder gets remaining $40M

**Participating**:
- Investor gets $10M back (preference satisfied)
- Investor gets additional share of remaining $40M (say 20%)
- Investor gets $10M + $8M = $18M total
- Founder gets remaining $32M

**Impact**: Founder loses $8M in participating scenario.

**What to negotiate**:
- Non-participating preferred for Seed (can usually achieve this)
- Non-participating for Series A (harder but achievable)
- Participating is market standard for Series B+ (harder to negotiate away)

### 3. Anti-Dilution Protection (Broad-Based Weighted Average)

**Why it matters**: In down rounds, anti-dilution protects investors by adjusting their conversion price downward, which dilutes founders.

**Example down round impact**:
- Seed at $5/share
- Series A at $10/share (up round)
- Series B at $8/share (down round - triggers anti-dilution)
- Series A's conversion price adjusts downward
- Founders' dilution increases

**Broad-Based Weighted Average** (Best for founders):
- Weighted average formula minimizes repricing impact
- Shares anti-dilution burden across all investors
- Most balanced approach

**Narrow-Based Weighted Average** (Worse for founders):
- Only counts preferred shares (larger denominator)
- More aggressive repricing
- Greater founder dilution in down rounds

**Full Ratchet** (Worst for founders - rare):
- Investor's price fully adjusts to new down round price
- Maximum founder dilution
- Rarely used (too harsh)

**What to negotiate**:
- Broad-based weighted average (market standard, should demand this)
- Anti-dilution exemptions: employee options, strategic grants
- Cap on anti-dilution triggers (e.g., only if >20% down)

### 4. Board Composition (Your Decision-Making Power)

**Seed (3-person board)**:
- 1 founder (you as CEO)
- 1 investor
- 1 independent director (hopefully favorable)
- **You have decision-making power** with board consent

**Series A (5-person board)**:
- 1 founder (you as CEO)
- 1 founder or neutral board member
- 1 Series A investor
- 1 Series Seed investor (if still holding %)
- 1 independent director
- **You might have board control if founders + neutral vote together**

**Series B (5-7 person board)**:
- 1 founder (you as CEO)
- 1 Series B investor
- 1 Series A investor
- 2-3 independent directors (hopefully favorable)
- **You're likely outnumbered. Investors control decisions.**

**What to negotiate**:
- Keep CEO seat at ALL COSTS (this is non-negotiable)
- Try for second founder seat or strong founder ally
- Select independent directors favorable to founder vision
- Ensure independent directors have experience in your space

### 5. Protective Provisions (Investor Veto Rights)

**What are they**: These are items investors can veto, blocking company from taking action.

**Common protective provisions**:
- Sell company (biggest one - investors can block exit)
- Hire/fire CEO
- Change business materially
- Create new preferred stock
- Budget changes >20%
- Related-party transactions

**Impact on founders**:
- Investor can block pivots (even if metrics declining)
- Investor can block hires you want
- Investor can block sale that you want (worst case)
- Investors can deadlock company (rare but happens)

**What to negotiate**:
- Limit protective provisions to truly material items (5-8 max)
- Exclude operational matters (hiring, budgets at lower thresholds)
- Raise thresholds for investor approval (not every hire, not every budget)
- Higher ownership threshold for veto (25% instead of 15%)
- Mutual consent (not unilateral investor veto)
- Sunset provisions (after hitting milestones, fewer protections)

## Scenarios Where Founders Get Hurt

### Scenario 1: Down Round & Liquidation Preferences Stack

**Setup**:
- Seed: $2M investment, 1x preference
- Series A: $10M investment, 1x preference
- Series B: $20M investment, 1x preference
- Founders: own 40% on paper

**Company exit at $35M** (down from $100M expected Series C):

**Liquidation Waterfall**:
1. Series B gets $20M (preference satisfied)
2. Series A gets $10M (preference satisfied)
3. Series Seed gets $5M (only $5M remaining)
4. Founders get $0

**Result**: Founders own 40% of company, get nothing in exit.

**Founder learning**: Understand preference stacking. Three rounds of 1x preferences can stack to >100% of typical exit value.

### Scenario 2: Protective Provisions Block Your Exit

**Setup**:
- Series A investor has protective provision: must approve any exit
- Acquirer offers $100M for company (excellent outcome)
- Investor wants to block sale for strategic reason

**Your situation**:
- You want to exit and take your $30M
- Investor blocks and holds company for next round
- Company misses next series, valuation drops
- Next exit at $60M (Series A protected, you get nothing)

**Founder learning**: Protective provisions should NOT include unilateral veto over exit (use Board approval instead).

### Scenario 3: Investor Recruits CEO Above You

**Setup**:
- You founded company, raised Seed as CEO
- Series A investor insists on "experienced CEO"
- Investor recruits VP from Google as new CEO
- You become "Chief Scientist" or pushed out

**Your situation**:
- Investor+Board votes in new CEO
- You lose operational control
- New CEO manages the company (not you)
- You're founder-in-name-only
- If company exits, new CEO gets credit + bonus

**Founder learning**: Negotiate for Board seat and protective provision: "Cannot hire/fire CEO without founder consent."

### Scenario 4: Down Round Vesting Reset

**Setup**:
- You have 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff
- After 3 years, you've vested 75% of shares
- Series C is down round, company hits trouble
- Investor demands: "Reset vesting, founders have new 4-year clock"

**Your situation**:
- You've worked 3 years, 75% vested
- Investor forces new 4-year vesting from Day 0
- Now you have 1 year of service toward new 4-year cliff
- If you leave, you lose all vested equity (new cliff resets)

**Founder learning**: Negotiation milestone: vesting cannot be reset post-funding. Once vested, stays vested.

### Scenario 5: Valuation Too High, Series A Impossible

**Setup**:
- Seed at $10M pre-money valuation
- 18 months later, Series A investors won't pay >$15M pre
- You're worth $10M for 18 months? Market disagrees.
- This is a "down round" (valuation didn't increase)

**Your situation**:
- Wanted $30M Series A at $20M pre
- Market only supports $15M pre
- You're forced to take down round
- Anti-dilution triggers, founders diluted
- Investors lose confidence (why no growth?)

**Founder learning**: Don't oversell valuation in Seed. Taking $2M at $8M pre is better than $2M at $10M pre that leads to forced down round.

## The Negotiation: What Matters Most

### Priority 1: Pre-Money Valuation (Highest Priority)

**This determines your dilution.** Everything else is secondary.

**Strategy**:
1. Research comps (angel list, pitchbook, crunchbase)
2. Model different valuations and dilution impact
3. Understand what "fair" is for your metrics
4. Have a walk-away number (lowest pre-money you'll accept)
5. Don't lead with number - let investor offer first
6. Always counter-offer, don't accept first offer

**Negotiation tactics**:
- "We've modeled different scenarios. Here's where we land."
- "Comparable companies at our stage are at $X. We're slightly better."
- "Take a look at our metrics. Here's the market rate."
- "We're willing to negotiate, but we need $Y minimum valuation."

### Priority 2: Type of Preference (Next Priority)

**Non-participating preferred is worth $500K-$1M+ difference** in founder returns.

**Strategy**:
- For Seed: "Market standard is non-participating. Let's use that."
- For Series A: Harder to negotiate, but try non-participating or capped participation (1x participating capped at 2x multiple)
- For Series B: Participating is standard, but push for "pro-rata participation" (not double-dipping)

### Priority 3: Anti-Dilution (Important)

**Broad-based weighted average is market standard.** If investor wants narrow-based or full ratchet, push back.

**Strategy**:
- "Broad-based is market standard for Seed/Series A. That's what we're doing."
- "Narrow-based is investor-friendly. Let's stick with broad-based for fairness."
- "Full ratchet is rarely used. That's off the table."

### Priority 4: Board Control (Important)

**Keep CEO seat. Try for second founder or founder ally.**

**Strategy**:
- "I'm staying CEO. That's non-negotiable."
- "We should have [2] founder directors given our stage."
- "Let's jointly select [X] as independent director. They have relevant experience."
- "Board should be [founder, investor, founder, independent, independent]."

### Priority 5: Protective Provisions (Moderate)

**Limit to 5-8 material items. Exclude operational items.**

**Strategy**:
- "Let's limit protective provisions to material items only. Here's our list: [sale, hire/fire CEO, major pivot, create new preferred stock, annual budget >$X.]"
- "We want to operate the company. Investors should have oversight, not micromanagement."
- "Protective provisions for major items, but not for hiring or quarterly budget variances."

## The Offer Stage: What to Expect

### Term Sheet Negotiation (1-2 weeks)

1. **Investor sends term sheet**
   - Non-binding summary of terms
   - Pre-money valuation, investment amount, terms
   - Board composition, protective provisions
   - Some terms binding (confidentiality, exclusivity)

2. **You review with lawyer**
   - Startup attorney reviews (cost: $3K-$5K)
   - Lawyer explains implications
   - Lawyer provides feedback on terms

3. **You counter-propose key terms**
   - Respond to valuation
   - Counter on protective provisions
   - Propose board structure
   - Respond to anti-dilution

4. **1-2 rounds of negotiation**
   - Some movement on both sides
   - Reach agreed term sheet
   - Sign (binding on confidentiality/exclusivity usually)

### Due Diligence (2-4 weeks)

1. **Investor diligence on you**
   - Reference calls with customers
   - Reference calls with team members
   - Background check on founders (yes, they do this)
   - Review of IP/contracts/legal
   - Review of financials

2. **You review investor**
   - Ask: What's your follow-on strategy? (Will they fund future rounds?)
   - Ask: How much do you have for future rounds?
   - Ask: Are you the lead investor or part of syndicate?
   - Ask: What are your expectations for exits?
   - Talk to founders they've invested in (are they happy?)

### Legal Documentation (2-4 weeks)

1. **Lawyer drafts Stock Purchase Agreement**
   - Legal binding contract for stock purchase
   - Includes all representations & warranties
   - Includes all closing conditions

2. **Lawyer drafts Rights Agreements**
   - Investors' Rights Agreement (information, registration, pro-rata)
   - Voting Agreement (board elections)
   - ROFR/Co-Sale Agreement (stock transfer restrictions)

3. **Negotiation of documents**
   - Back and forth on details
   - Some pushing back and forth
   - Usually reaches final form within 2 weeks

### Closing (1 week)

1. **Everyone signs documents**
2. **Investor wires funds** ($X million to your bank account)
3. **Shares issued** to investor
4. **Cap table updated** with new investor and fully diluted numbers
5. **Announcement** (usually - optional)

## Founder Checklist for Priced Equity Round

### Pre-Round
- [ ] Have PMF evidence (strong retention, unit economics)
- [ ] Have team in place (especially CTO if you're CEO)
- [ ] Have financial model projections
- [ ] Have business plan and use of proceeds
- [ ] Have cap table and fully diluted numbers
- [ ] Have updated financial statements (last 12 months)
- [ ] Have list of potential lead investors (who's invested in comps?)
- [ ] Have investor intro strategy (who do you know?)

### During Fundraising
- [ ] Engage startup attorney early ($3K-$5K for seed, $10K-$20K for Series A)
- [ ] Research comparable companies and their valuations
- [ ] Understand your walk-away valuation minimum
- [ ] Know what control/governance matters most to you
- [ ] Build relationships with multiple investors (don't rely on one)
- [ ] Don't share term sheet with team (until confidential)
- [ ] Plan legal/diligence timeline (typically 6-10 weeks total)

### During Negotiation
- [ ] Prioritize: valuation > preference type > anti-dilution > board > protective provisions
- [ ] Have lawyer explain implications of each term
- [ ] Understand fully diluted cap table post-investment
- [ ] Understand your dilution in future rounds (model Seed → Series A → Series B)
- [ ] Negotiate protective provisions down to 5-8 items
- [ ] Ensure you stay CEO
- [ ] Get second board seat if possible
- [ ] Ensure non-compete/non-solicitation agreements with team

### At Closing
- [ ] Verify stock certificates issued
- [ ] Verify cap table updated
- [ ] Verify investor receives board materials
- [ ] Plan board meeting schedule
- [ ] Plan monthly update cadence with investor
- [ ] Understand investor's expectations for next milestone
- [ ] Understand investor's follow-on investment plans

## Post-Round Expectations & Responsibilities

### Investor Reporting (Monthly)

**Every month, send investor**:
- Key metrics (DAU/MAU, churn, revenue, CAC, LTV)
- Progress vs. plan
- Major developments
- Risks/challenges
- Next month's plans
- Burn rate & runway

**Time investment**: ~2 hours/month

### Board Meetings (Monthly)

**Host monthly board meetings** (once you have Series A):
- Presentation of metrics/progress
- Q&A from board
- Discussion of major decisions
- Approval of material matters
- Questions from observer investors

**Time investment**: ~3-4 hours/month (prep + meeting)

### Governance & Compliance

**Post-investment, you have new compliance**:
- Board resolutions for material decisions (hiring, budgets, contracts >$X)
- Option grants need Board approval
- Customer/partner contracts >$[__] may need Board approval
- Related-party transactions need Board approval
- Investor approval of changes to business plan

## Key Negotiations by Round

### Seed Round Priorities

1. **Valuation**: $8-15M pre-money typical (depends on traction)
2. **Amount**: $500K-$2M typical
3. **Preference**: Non-participating preferred (should demand this)
4. **Board**: 3-person (you + 1 investor + 1 independent)
5. **Protective Provisions**: 3-5 items (keep to minimum)
6. **Investor relationship**: Should feel collaborative, not adversarial

### Series A Priorities

1. **Valuation**: $20-60M pre-money (depends on growth)
2. **Amount**: $2-15M typical
3. **Preference**: Non-participating if possible, 1x participating if not
4. **Board**: 5-person (2 founders if possible, 2 investors, 1 independent)
5. **Protective Provisions**: 8-10 items (keep to material only)
6. **Pro-rata rights**: Essential for investor participation in Series B

### Series B Priorities

1. **Valuation**: $60-300M pre-money (depends on growth)
2. **Amount**: $10-50M typical
3. **Preference**: 1x participating (market standard)
4. **Board**: 5-7 person (1 founder maybe, 2-3 investors, 1-2 independent)
5. **Protective Provisions**: 10-15 items (investor-heavy by this stage)
6. **Drag-along rights**: Investor likely gets this, founder gets drag-along protection

## Red Flags: When to Walk Away

### Red Flag 1: Investor Demands Too Much Control

**Warning signs**:
- Investor demands you hire their preferred CEO
- Investor wants 3+ board seats
- Investor wants veto on all hiring/budget
- Investor wants to reduce your equity further (forced dilution)

**Response**: Walk away. You need an investor partner, not a dictator.

### Red Flag 2: Valuation Makes No Sense

**Warning signs**:
- Valuation is <$5M lower than you're confident in
- Valuation is >2x comparable companies (red flag for down round)
- Investor won't budge despite good metrics
- Investor bases valuation only on "what we want to own %"

**Response**: Walk away or renegotiate. Get second opinion from broker.

### Red Flag 3: Investor Has Bad Track Record

**Warning signs**:
- Investor has reputation for removing founders
- Investor portfolio companies underperform
- Investor portfolio founders are unhappy (talk to them)
- Investor has pattern of down rounds

**Response**: Walk away. Investor relationship lasts 7-10 years.

### Red Flag 4: Unreasonable Timeline or Terms

**Warning signs**:
- Investor wants closing in 48 hours (insufficient due diligence)
- Investor adds new terms after term sheet (bad faith)
- Investor demands founder personal guarantees
- Investor demands key person insurance forfeit to investor

**Response**: Walk away or slow down. Pressure is a tactic.

### Red Flag 5: Terms Trap You in Future Rounds

**Warning signs**:
- Anti-dilution formula is narrow-based or full ratchet
- Participating preference is uncapped
- Protective provisions are extensive and onerous
- Board composition leaves you with zero control

**Response**: Renegotiate heavily or walk away. These terms compound in future rounds.

## The Long Game: Thinking Beyond Series A

### Series A is Not an Exit

**Remember**: Series A is a checkpoint, not the goal.

**Path typically looks like**:
1. **Seed** ($2M) at $8M pre
2. **Series A** ($10M) at $30M pre (growth story)
3. **Series B** ($30M) at $100M pre (product expansion)
4. **Series C** ($50M+) at $300M+ pre (scaling)
5. **Exit** ($1B+ valuation) or IPO (7-10 years)

**Your ownership trajectory**:
- After Seed: 80%
- After Series A: 65%
- After Series B: 50%
- After Series C: 35%
- At IPO/Exit: 25-30% (if all goes well)

### Ownership vs. Control

**Common founder mistake**: Focusing on maintaining 50% ownership instead of focus on control.

**Reality**: In Series B+, even with 50% ownership, you're outnumbered on the Board (2-3 investor directors vs. 1 you).

**Better strategy**: Focus on **key control mechanics**:
- Keep CEO seat (requires no board vote, you have power to hire/manage team)
- Ensure your vision is shared by at least one board member
- Have veto on major pivots via protective provisions
- Ensure Board is 5+ people so investors can't control alone

## Final Advice to Founders

### 1. Optimize for Control, Not Valuation

Higher valuation feels good, but control is what matters. A $50M Series A at $30M pre with bad governance is worse than $40M Series A at $20M pre with strong founder control.

### 2. Pick Your Investor, Not Your Valuation

Investor quality matters more than valuation. A supportive investor at lower valuation beats an adversarial investor at higher valuation.

### 3. Understand the Full Picture

Don't just read the term sheet. Understand:
- Liquidation preferences and down-round scenarios
- Board composition and decision-making power
- Anti-dilution and dilution in future rounds
- Protective provisions and your flexibility
- Investor follow-on expectations

### 4. Build Advisor Board

Get advisors who've been through this:
- Founder advisor who raised successful rounds
- Startup lawyer specializing in venture
- Mentor investor or CEO from your space
- They will save you from costly mistakes

### 5. Document Everything

Post-investment, keep careful records:
- Board meeting minutes
- All investor communications
- Major decisions and approvals
- Option grants and equity details
- Anything that might matter in dispute

## Conclusion

Priced equity rounds are a pivotal moment. You're trading equity for capital and growth acceleration. The terms you negotiate will determine your financial outcome, your control, and your ability to execute your vision.

Spend time on negotiation. Get good advice. Understand the implications. Then make an informed decision.

**The best priced equity round is one you're comfortable with for 7-10 years, because that's how long you'll be living with the investor.**
