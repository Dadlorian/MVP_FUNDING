## 4. LIQUIDATION PREFERENCE

**Type**: [PARTICIPATING/NON-PARTICIPATING] Preferred Stock

### Standard Series A Structure: 1x Participating Preferred
(More investor-friendly than Seed, but becoming market standard)

**Liquidation Preference Formula**:
- Series A investors receive (in order of priority):
  1. Their invested amount ($[INVESTMENT_AMOUNT])
  2. Then their pro-rata share of remaining proceeds

**Example Waterfall at $50M Exit**:
- Seed (assumed $2M investment) → gets $2M
- Series A ($10M investment) → gets $10M, then [10/(10+common)] × remaining
- Common (founders) → gets remaining

**Alternative: Non-Participating (More Founder-Friendly)**:
- Series A gets either their $[INVESTMENT_AMOUNT] back **OR** [X]% of proceeds (whichever is greater)
- This is more common in early-stage/Seed but rare for Series A

### Preference Stacking Risk

**Important Note**: Multiple layers of preferences can compress founder returns:

**Scenario**: $20M Exit with Seed + Series A preferences:
- Seed: 1x participating on $2M
- Series A: 1x participating on $10M
- Common Stock (Founders): ~60% ownership

**Waterfall**:
- Seed gets $2M (preference)
- Series A gets $10M (preference)
- Common/Founders get $8M (only $8M left)
- Result: Founders with 60% ownership get only 16% of proceeds

**Series A investors should understand**: If Series B happens at lower valuation (down round), preferences from Series A and Seed may stack heavily against Series A returns.

## 5. ANTI-DILUTION PROTECTION

**Type**: [BROAD-BASED WEIGHTED AVERAGE/NARROW-BASED WEIGHTED AVERAGE]

### Broad-Based Weighted Average (Market Standard for Series A)
- Formula: New CP = Old CP × √[(Old Price × New Price) / (Current Price²)]
- Triggered by any future financing below Series A price per share
- Applies to "down rounds" only (financings at lower price)
- Most balanced approach

### Narrow-Based Weighted Average (More Aggressive)
- Formula: New CP = Old CP × [(Old Price × New Shares) / (Current Price × Current Shares)]
- Only counts preferred shares outstanding (not common)
- More aggressive repricing in down rounds
- Can cause severe founder dilution

**Anti-Dilution Exceptions** (don't trigger repricing):
- Exercises of options, warrants, or convertible securities
- Stock splits, stock dividends, recapitalizations
- Issuance from employee option pool (if within reserved amount)
- Strategic partnerships involving issuance (sometimes negotiated exclusion)
- Debt financing conversions at fixed terms

**Down Round Trigger Threshold**:
- Many term sheets include threshold: if price falls below [___]% of Series A price, anti-dilution triggers

## 6. PROTECTIVE PROVISIONS (INVESTOR VETO RIGHTS)

2. **Business Fundamentals**
   - Sale of company, merger, consolidation, or liquidation
   - Any fundamental change to business operations
   - Acquisition of another company (if material)
   - Change size of Board of Directors

3. **Compensation & Benefits**
   - Hire, fire, or change compensation of CEO (>$[__] annually)
   - Establish compensation policies affecting [__]% or more of employees
   - Approve bonuses in excess of [__]% of salary for any executive
   - Related party transactions exceeding $[__]
   - Loans to any officer or director

4. **Budget & Capital**
   - Annual operating budget and business plan
   - Capital expenditures exceeding $[__] in any fiscal year
   - Lease or purchase real property exceeding $[__]
   - Any material deviation from approved budget

5. **Debt & Obligations**
   - Incur indebtedness exceeding $[__] (other than trade payables)
   - Assume any guarantees or contingent liabilities exceeding $[__]
   - Create any liens or security interests in company assets

6. **Equity & Governance**
   - Issuance of options/RSUs except within approved pool
   - Amendment of Bylaws, Certificate of Incorporation affecting preferred holders
   - Change voting rights or preferences of any class of stock
   - Any action that adversely affects preferred stockholders

7. **Financing & Governance**
   - Any increase or decrease to Board size
   - Fill Board seats designated for preferred shareholders
   - Authorize new Series of stock without preferred consent

8. **Corporate Actions**
   - Related party contracts over $[__]
   - Material contracts (vendor, customer, partnership)
   - Affiliate transactions
   - Register or sell company shares

### Scope of Protective Provisions:
- **Single Investor**: If one investor holds [___]% (typical: 15-20% threshold)
- **Investor Consortium**: If holders of [___]% aggregate (typical: majority of preferred)
- **Board Action**: Often delegated to board in practice, but preferred holders can override

### Negotiation Points (Founder Perspective):
- Request lower ownership threshold (20% instead of 15%)
- Limit to truly material items only
- Exclude operational matters (hiring, budgets at lower thresholds)
- Carve out strategic initiatives
- Sunset certain provisions if company reaches milestones

## 8. PARTICIPATION & PRO-RATA RIGHTS

### Pro-Rata Participation Right

**Each holder of [___]% or more of Series A Preferred Stock shall have the right to participate in future preferred stock financings to maintain their ownership percentage (pro-rata right).**

**How it works**:
- If Series B is $20M, investor with 15% ownership can invest $3M to maintain 15%
- If investor doesn't participate, their ownership dilutes
- Pro-rata right is often most valuable right to Series A investors
- Allows "follow-on" investments in Series B, C, D

**Exercise Mechanics**:
- Company must offer new financing to pro-rata right holders
- Holders have [__] days (typically 10-20) to accept or decline
- Must commit within [__] days of acceptance
- Proceeds at same price and terms as other Series B investors

### Co-Investment Right
- Holders may elect to invest additional capital beyond pro-rata
- Not required, but option available in Series B and beyond

### Carve-Outs (pro-rata rights typically don't apply to):
- Secondary sales of existing shares
- Equity grants to employees
- Conversions of convertible instruments
- Debt with conversion features

---

## 9. DRAG-ALONG & TAG-ALONG RIGHTS

### Drag-Along Rights
**Holders of [___]% of Series A Preferred Stock can force minority shareholders to sell in company sale.**

- Majority holders (typically [___]%) can approve a sale/merger
- ALL shareholders must sell on same terms and timeline
- Minority (including founders) cannot refuse or negotiate separate terms
- All shares receive same price per share (pro-rata recovery)

**Protection for Founders**:
- Drag-along rights typically require majority consent of preferred holders
- And approval of [___]% of common stock holders
- This prevents minority preferred from forcing bad sale

### Tag-Along Rights
**If majority shareholders sell company, minority shareholders can force inclusion at same terms.**

- Founder wants to sell 5% stake to third party buyer
- Other 95% holders can "tag along" requiring buyer to purchase their stakes too
- Prevents insider deals or founder enrichment without other shareholders
- All shareholders get same price per share

### Example Drag-Along / Tag-Along Scenario:
- Company valued at $100M
- Acquirer offers $100M for company
- Preferred holders have drag-along rights
- They vote to accept acquisition
- All shareholders forced to sell on pro-rata basis:
  - Preferred holders get pro-rata of proceeds
  - Common holders get their pro-rata
  - Founders cannot refuse or negotiate different terms

## 10. VOTING, CONVERSION & MISCELLANEOUS RIGHTS

### Voting Rights
- Each share of Series A Preferred Stock has [__] vote per share
- Series A votes with Common on all matters except:
  - Those requiring protective provision consent (see Section 6)
  - Election of Preferred director
  - Matters affecting preferred terms only

### Conversion Rights

**Automatic Conversion Upon IPO**:
- All Series A Preferred automatically converts to Common Stock
- Conversion ratio: 1:1 (one preferred share → one common share)
- Adjusted for stock splits, dividends, recapitalizations
- Triggers at IPO or other "qualifying event"

**Optional/Voluntary Conversion**:
- At any time, holder can request conversion
- Conversion price = adjusted issue price based on anti-dilution events
- Typically irrelevant for Series A (prefers to stay as preferred)

**Forced Conversion**:
- Upon request of holders of majority of Series A
- Upon IPO registration statement effectiveness

### Redemption Rights (Rare for Venture Equity)
- Some term sheets include redemption right after [__] years
- Example: If no IPO by year 5, preferred shareholders can force company to redeem shares
- Very rare in modern VC (creates liability)
- If included: redemption at original purchase price, or FMV, or formula-based

## 14. ANTI-DILUTION EXAMPLES & CALCULATIONS

### Example 1: Broad-Based Weighted Average (Down Round)

**Series A Facts**:
- Price per share: $5.00
- Series A investors own 20% (fully diluted)

**Series B Down Round Facts**:
- Price per share: $2.50 (50% down)
- New shares issued: [AMOUNT]

**Calculation**:
- New CP = Old CP × √[(Old Price × New Price) / (Current Price²)]
- New CP = $5.00 × √[($5.00 × $2.50) / ($5.00²)]
- New CP = $5.00 × √[0.5]
- New CP = $5.00 × 0.707
- **New CP = $3.54**

**Impact**:
- Series A investor's conversion price drops from $5.00 to $3.54
- Fewer new Series B shares needed to maintain ownership
- But all common shareholders (founders) diluted

### Example 2: Preference Stacking (Exit at Below Preference)

**Cap Table Pre-Exit**:
- Seed: $2M investment, $2M liquidation preference
- Series A: $10M investment, $10M liquidation preference
- Founders: 60% common stock

**Exit Scenario: $15M Acquisition**

**Waterfall**:
1. Seed gets $2M (liquidation preference)
2. Series A gets $10M (liquidation preference)
3. Remaining: $15M - $2M - $10M = $3M
4. Founders get $3M (despite owning 60%)

**Result**: Founders get only 20% despite owning 60%

## 16. MISCELLANEOUS TERMS

### Expenses
- [ ] Company reimburses investor legal fees upon closing (capped at $[__])
- [ ] Investor reimburses company legal fees upon closing (capped at $[__])
- [ ] Each party bears own advisory fees (bankers, consultants, etc.)
- [ ] All parties split filing/administrative costs

### Governing Law & Venue
- Governed by laws of [STATE], without regard to conflicts of law
- Exclusive venue: Courts of [COUNTY], [STATE]

### Entire Agreement
- Term sheet + Stock Purchase Agreement + Rights Agreements = entire agreement
- Supersedes all previous discussions, emails, LOIs

### Confidentiality & Exclusivity

**Confidentiality**:
- Both parties maintain confidentiality of terms
- Exceptions:
  - Necessary disclosures to advisors, lawyers
  - Required by law/court order
  - Disclosures to future investors (in confidence)
  - Disclosures to lenders/banks

**Exclusivity**:
- For [__] days (typically 60-90 days), company shall not:
  - Solicit other investments
  - Negotiate with other investors
  - Accept other investment proposals
- Exceptions:
  - Unsolicited inquiries (can respond but not solicit)
  - Named excluded parties: [LIST]

### Binding vs Non-Binding Provisions

**Binding** (legally enforceable):
- Confidentiality
- Exclusivity
- Expense provisions
- Representations & warranties (if in Stock Purchase Agreement)

**Non-Binding** (until Stock Purchase Agreement executed):
- All economic terms
- Liquidation preferences
- Anti-dilution mechanics
- Board composition
- Protective provisions
- Rights and agreements
