## ARTICLE III: BOARD OBSERVATION RIGHTS

### 3.1 Board Observation

**Holders of [___]% of Series [SEED/A/B] Preferred Stock shall have the right to attend all meetings of the Board of Directors as non-voting observers.**

**Specifically**:
- Right to attend all regular board meetings
- Right to attend all special board meetings
- Receive board materials same as board members
- May ask questions (at discretion of Board chair)
- Must maintain confidentiality of board discussions

### 3.2 Executive Sessions

**Observer shall not attend executive sessions**:
- Sessions without Company management
- Sessions discussing executive compensation
- Sessions discussing litigation or sensitive matters
- Exclusion must be determined by Board for good cause

### 3.3 Removal of Observation Rights

**Company may revoke observation rights if**:
- Holder transfers shares to third party (unless affiliate)
- Holder becomes competitor of Company (as determined by Board)
- Holder breaches confidentiality obligations
- Holder engages in misconduct at board meetings

## ARTICLE IV: PRO-RATA PARTICIPATION RIGHTS

### 4.1 Right to Participate in Future Rounds

**Holders of [___]% of Series [SEED/A/B] Preferred Stock shall have the right to participate in any future preferred stock financing on a pro-rata basis.**

**Pro-Rata Right Mechanics**:

**Example**:
- Investor A holds 20% of Company
- Series C financing announced: $20M
- Investor A's pro-rata = 20% × $20M = $4M
- Investor A can invest up to $4M to maintain 20% ownership

### 4.2 Exercise of Pro-Rata Right

**Procedure for exercising pro-rata**:

**Step 1: Offer Letter**
- Company sends offer letter to all pro-rata rights holders
- Describes new financing round
- Specifies: amount, price per share, terms, timing
- Specifies pro-rata amount available to each holder
- [__]-day deadline to accept (typically 10-15 days)

**Step 2: Acceptance**
- Holder delivers signed acceptance letter
- Commits to invest pro-rata amount
- Acceptance within [__] days of offer

**Step 3: Funding**
- Holder funds investment by closing date
- Closing date [__] days after acceptance
- Funds received in escrow or directly to Company

**Step 4: Issuance**
- Holder receives shares at same price and terms as Series C investors
- Shares issued at closing

### 4.3 Failure to Participate

**If holder declines pro-rata**, consequences:
- Holder's ownership percentage dilutes in Series C
- Holder no longer has pro-rata right for future rounds (unless restated)
- Other holders' pro-rata rights increase

**Waiver of Pro-Rata**:
- Holder can voluntarily waive pro-rata right
- Waiver must be in writing
- Can waive for specific round or all future rounds

### 4.4 Stacking of Pro-Rata

**If multiple holders have pro-rata rights**:
- Company allocates new financing among all pro-rata holders
- Each gets their proportionate share
- If insufficient capital for all, pro-rata amounts scale down

### 4.5 Pro-Rata Right Termination

**Pro-Rata rights terminate upon**:
- IPO of Company (registration on national exchange)
- Holder's ownership falls below [___]%
- Transfer of shares to non-affiliate (unless consent given)
- Acquisition of Company
- [__] years post-issuance (if specified)

### 4.6 Anti-Dilution and Pro-Rata

**Pro-Rata right and anti-dilution are separate**:
- Anti-dilution adjusts conversion price in down rounds
- Pro-rata allows participation in future rounds
- Both typically apply simultaneously

- Company must hire underwriters and legal counsel
- Company must make all necessary disclosures
- Holders can participate in IPO if [___]% included

**Timing**:
- Company must file registration statement within [__] days
- Use reasonable efforts to achieve effectiveness within [__] days

**Frequency**:
- Holders can make [__] demand registrations during life of Company
- Or [__] demand registrations after IPO eligibility reached

**Limitations**:
- Company can defer registration if material non-public information
- Deferral limited to [__] days per year
- Company cannot defer more than [__] times per year

### 6.2 Piggyback Registration Rights

**If Company registers shares** (including IPO), holders can include their shares in registration.

**Piggyback Right**:
- Right to include shares in any Company-initiated registration
- Applies to IPO, secondary offering, or other registration
- Notice required: [__] days before offering

**Company Cut-Back Right**:
- If underwriter limits offering size, Company can cut back shares
- Reduction order: typically Company shares > investor shares
- Underwriter cannot eliminate investor piggyback entirely

**Once Company qualifies for Form S-3 registration** (typically $75M+ public float):
- Holders can demand registration on Form S-3

### 6.4 Registration Obligations & Expenses

**Company shall**:
- Prepare all registration documents
- Pay all registration fees and expenses
- Pay Company legal, accounting, and underwriting fees
- Pay investor counsel fees (capped at $[__])

**Investors shall**:

### 6.5 Lock-Up Agreements

## ARTICLE VII: DRAG-ALONG RIGHTS

### 7.1 Drag-Along Provision

**Upon approval by holders of [___]% of Series [SEED/A/B] Preferred Stock** to sell the Company, all other stockholders shall be obligated to sell their shares on the same terms and conditions.

### 7.2 Drag-Along Mechanics

**Trigger for Drag-Along**:
- Sale or merger of Company
- Requires approval of [___]% of preferred holders (or Board approval)
- Or approval of [___]% of common stockholders

**Founder/Common Stockholder Obligations**:
- Must sell shares at same price per share as preferred holders
- Cannot negotiate separate terms or side agreements
- Must sign all transaction documents
- Must deliver shares by closing date
- Cannot block or refuse transaction

### 7.3 Pricing Mechanics

**All stockholders receive same price per share**:
- Preferred holders receive per their liquidation preference
- Common holders receive pro-rata share of proceeds
- Creates fairness in transaction pricing

**Example**:
- Company sold for $50M
- 50M shares outstanding total
- Preferred gets $30M (liquidation preference)
- $20M remaining for common shareholders
- $20M / common shares = price per share for common holders

### 7.4 Conditions & Protections

**Drag-Along Can Be Used For**:
- Sale to strategic buyer
- Acquisition by larger company
- Merger for cash or stock consideration
- Liquidation transaction

**Protections for Founders**:
- Drag-along requires majority consent (not single investor veto)
- Drag-along must be approved by Board
- Drag-along price must be deemed fair (typically Board-approved)
- Tax gross-ups may be negotiated

## ARTICLE VIII: TAG-ALONG RIGHTS

### 8.1 Tag-Along Provision

**If majority stockholders (holders of [___]% or Board-approved) approve sale of Company, all minority stockholders have the right to sell their shares on the same terms.**

### 8.2 Tag-Along Mechanics

**Minority Founder Protection**:
- If majority decides to sell, founder can force inclusion
- Prevents insiders from selling to buyer while founders are left behind
- Ensures founder gets paid at same price per share

**Exercise of Tag-Along**:
- Founder notifies Company of intent to tag along
- Founder agrees to sell on same terms as majority
- Founder's shares included in transaction at same price per share
- Founder signs transaction documents

### 8.3 Tag-Along Example

**Scenario**:
- Founder owns 40% common stock
- Series B investor owns 30% preferred stock
- Series B investor has opportunity to sell stake to acquirer
- Investor wants to monetize position
- Founder would be left behind without tag-along

**With Tag-Along Rights**:
- Founder exercises tag-along right
- Founder's 40% must be purchased at same price/terms as Series B investor's 30%
- Founder receives payment for shares at transaction closing
- No side deal or separate negotiations for founder
