# Priced Equity Rounds: Comprehensive Guide

## Overview

Priced equity rounds are financing transactions where investors purchase preferred stock at a specific valuation. Unlike convertible instruments (SAFEs/convertible notes), priced rounds establish immediate ownership and governance rights.

## Types of Priced Equity Rounds

### 1. **Seed Round**
- **Funding**: $500K - $2M typically
- **Valuation**: $2M - $10M pre-money
- **Investors**: Angel investors, early-stage VCs, syndicates
- **Timeline**: Usually happens 12-18 months after founding
- **Investor Type**: Often less institutional, more founder-friendly

### 2. **Series A**
- **Funding**: $2M - $15M typically
- **Valuation**: $10M - $100M pre-money
- **Investors**: Institutional VCs (Tier 1-3), strategic investors
- **Timeline**: 18-24 months after seed
- **Requirements**: Product-market fit evidence, strong metrics

### 3. **Series B**
- **Funding**: $10M - $50M typically
- **Valuation**: $50M - $500M pre-money
- **Investors**: Larger institutional VCs, growth investors
- **Timeline**: 24-36 months after Series A
- **Requirements**: Proven business model, clear path to profitability

### 4. **Series C & Beyond**
- **Funding**: $50M+ typically
- **Valuation**: Varies significantly ($500M+)
- **Investors**: Growth equity, late-stage institutional investors
- **Focus**: Scaling, international expansion, profitability path

## Key Terminology & Concepts

### **Pre-Money vs. Post-Money Valuation**

**Pre-Money Valuation**: The company's valuation before the investment
- Example: $10M pre-money valuation

**Post-Money Valuation**: Pre-money + investment amount
- Example: $10M (pre) + $2M (investment) = $12M (post)

**Investor Ownership %** = Investment / Post-Money Valuation
- Example: $2M / $12M = 16.67%

**Impact on Founders**: Lower pre-money = higher founder dilution

### **Preferred Stock Structure**

**Preferred Stock** (what investors receive):
- Liquidation preferences
- Anti-dilution protection
- Board seat representation
- Protective provisions (veto rights)
- Conversion rights to common stock

**Common Stock** (founder/employee stock):
- Subordinate to preferred in liquidation
- One vote per share (often)
- No special rights
- Subject to protective provisions

### **Liquidation Preferences**

**Non-Participating Preferred** (founder-friendly):
- Investor receives investment back OR converts to common
- Investor chooses whichever is more valuable
- Example: 1x non-participating = get $2M back or 16.67% of company

**Participating Preferred** (investor-friendly):
- Investor gets investment back PLUS percent of proceeds
- Example: 1x participating = get $2M back plus 16.67% of remaining proceeds
- Often called "double-dipping"

**Multi-Tiered Liquidation** (multiple rounds):
- Each round has separate preference bucket
- Earlier rounds have senior preferences
- Can be "stacked" creating significant founder risk

**Impact**: In down exits or acquisitions under valuation, founders may get nothing

### **Anti-Dilution Protection**

**Broad-Based Weighted Average** (founder-friendly):
- Reduces conversion ratio based on all outstanding shares
- Formula: New Conversion = Old Conversion × √(Old Price × New Price / Current Stock Price)
- Minimizes founder dilution

**Narrow-Based Weighted Average** (investor-friendly):
- Reduces conversion ratio based only on preferred shares
- More aggressive anti-dilution
- Example: Down round at lower price triggers automatic repricing

**Full Ratchet** (very investor-friendly):
- Investor's conversion price adjusts to new lower price
- Massive dilution for founders
- Rarely used (very harsh)

**No Anti-Dilution** (founder-friendly):
- Investor price stays fixed
- Founder risk if down round happens

**Impact**: Down rounds trigger re-pricing that dilutes founders

### **Board Composition**

**Common structure in Seed**:
- 3 board seats: 1 founder/CEO, 1 seed investor, 1 neutral/mutual

**Common structure in Series A**:
- 5 board seats: 1 founder/CEO, 1 seed investor, 1 Series A investor, 1 independent director, 1 mutual

**Impact**:
- Investor gains operational oversight
- Founders lose unilateral decision-making
- Major decisions require approval

### **Protective Provisions (Veto Rights)**

Investors can block major decisions:

**Common protective provisions**:
- Hiring/firing CEO
- Materially changing business
- Related party transactions
- Additional fundraising (debt/equity)
- Sale of company (exit event)
- Merger or consolidation
- Liquidation of company
- Changes to preferences of preferred stock
- Changes to board composition
- Issuance of senior/equal preferred stock

**Impact**:
- Founders lose control over strategy
- Creates deadlock potential
- Investor has veto over major business changes

### **Investment Rights Documents**

**Term Sheet** (non-binding usually, binds many provisions):
- Summary of key terms
- Valuation, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, board seats
- Conditions precedent
- Representations & warranties
- Exclusivity & expense provisions

**Stock Purchase Agreement** (binding):
- Legal documentation of stock sale
- Representations & warranties (detailed)
- Covenants and conditions
- Closing conditions
- Indemnification provisions

**Investors' Rights Agreement** (binding):
- Information & inspection rights
- Board observation rights
- Participation rights (pro-rata in future rounds)
- Registration rights (IPO access)
- Drag-along rights (force minority to sell)
- Tag-along rights (sell with majority)

**Voting Agreement** (binding):
- Election of board directors
- Voting provisions
- Stockholder consents
- Cumulative voting elimination
- Often called "Stockholders Agreement"

**Right of First Refusal & Co-Sale Agreement** (binding):
- Company ROFR on founder stock sales
- Investor ROFR on founder stock sales
- Co-sale rights (investor sells with founder)
- Drag-along rights (force minority stockholders to sell)

## Founder Risks in Priced Equity Rounds

### **1. Loss of Control**

**Dilution from Ownership**:
- Founder starts with 100%
- Series Seed: Founder dilutes to ~83% (17% to seed)
- Series A: Founder dilutes to ~67% (16% to Series A from post-seed pool)
- Series B: Founder dilutes to ~50-55%
- By Series C+: Founder might own <30%

**Dilution from Preferences**:
- Liquidation preferences compound with down rounds
- Non-participating preferred is better for founders
- Each round can have different terms

**Board Control**:
- Lose sole decision-making authority
- Investor has veto over major decisions
- CEO can be fired with investor votes
- Creates potential for founder ouster

### **2. Liquidation Preferences Create Scenarios**

**Scenario 1: Modest Exit ($15M from $10M post-money Series A)**
- 1x non-participating: Investor converts, gets ~15% of proceeds
- Founder gets remaining ~85%
- **Outcome: Founder does well**

**Scenario 2: Failed Exit ($8M from $10M post-money Series A)**
- 1x non-participating: Investor gets $5M back, founder gets $3M
- Founder dramatically harmed despite owning majority
- **Outcome: Founder gets disproportionately small share**

**Scenario 3: Layered Preferences (Seed + Series A)**
- Seed has 1x preference ($2M)
- Series A has 1x preference ($5M)
- Exit at $10M total:
  - Seed gets $2M
  - Series A gets $5M
  - Founder gets $3M (despite owning ~60%)
- **Outcome: Founder gets almost nothing**

### **3. Protective Provisions Can Trap Company**

**Example blocking scenarios**:
- Investor blocks pivot even if metrics decline
- Investor blocks hiring that founders want
- Investor blocks sale of company founders want
- Creates deadlock if investor & founder disagree
- Company can become unfundable (no investor wants investor veto)

### **4. Anti-Dilution Protection Harms Founders**

**Down Round Scenario**:
- Seed at $5M pre-money valuation
- Series A at $10M pre-money valuation
- Series B attempted at $8M pre-money (down round)
- Broad-based weighted average trigger:
  - Series A investors' shares adjust downward
  - Founder dilution increases (fewer shares left for founders)
- **Outcome: Founder is punished by down round along with investors**

### **5. Future Round Dilution**

**Compounding effect**:
- Each round dilutes existing shareholders
- Founders see greatest cumulative dilution
- If company raises many rounds before exit, founder ownership can drop significantly
- After Series C, D, E: Founder might own <10%

### **6. Tax Consequences**

**Preferred Stock Structure**:
- Preferred stock triggers 409A valuation impact
- Founders holding common stock see their shares valued lower (on paper)
- Impacts future option grants to employees
- Can create tax complications

### **7. Acceleration & Vesting Implications**

**Standard vesting**: 4-year vest with 1-year cliff
**Change of control** (due to preferred investment):
- Some investors negotiate acceleration of vesting for themselves
- Founders might lose acceleration benefits
- Creates equity incentive misalignment

## Investor Risks in Priced Equity Rounds

### **1. Valuation Risk**

**Overpaying for equity**:
- Seed at $10M pre-money that should have been $5M
- Immediate underwater position
- Diluted in Series A at lower valuation
- Creates "valuation risk" - overpayment at entry

**Market conditions**:
- Macro downturn can make valuations unjustifiable
- Series A investors may refuse to follow-on at higher valuation
- Creates extended Series A delays

### **2. Illiquidity & Long Hold Periods**

**Illiquid equity**:
- 7-10 year typical hold period to exit
- No secondary market (usually)
- Returns depend on eventual IPO/acquisition
- Capital locked up for extended period

**Opportunity cost**:
- Could invest in 10+ companies in that time
- Concentration risk with single bet
- Cash flow challenges for later rounds

### **3. Dilution in Future Rounds**

**Follow-on round dilution**:
- Seed investor invests $2M at $10M pre
- Gets 16.67% post-money
- Series A at $20M pre: Seed investor diluted (no pro-rata participation)
- Investor ownership drops from 16.67% to ~8% (rough estimate)
- Each subsequent round dilutes further

**Failed pro-rata reserves**:
- VCs typically reserve for follow-on investments
- May not have capital available
- Dilutes along with everyone else
- Reduces expected returns

### **4. Preference Stacking Creates Paradox**

**Scenario: Three rounds of 1x preferences**
- Seed: $2M at $8M pre
- Series A: $5M at $20M pre
- Series B: $10M at $60M pre
- Exit at $35M:
  - Seed gets $2M
  - Series A gets $5M
  - Series B gets $10M (only partial, needs remaining $8M)
  - Founder gets remaining scraps
  - Investor returns are poor despite owning significant %

**Outcome**: Investor capital structure works against later-stage investor returns

### **5. Founder Attrition Risk**

**Vesting cliffs hit founders**:
- Founder loses control via board/protective provisions
- Considers leaving company
- 1-year cliff means founder has no acceleration
- Company loses key person (founder)
- Valuation collapses without founder

**Investor outcome**: Preferred stock worth fraction of face value

### **6. Down Round & Preference Adjustment Risk**

**Down round triggers**:
- Company misses milestones
- Market conditions deteriorate
- Series B doesn't materialize
- Investor must re-price previous investment
- Anti-dilution mechanisms trigger
- Complex waterfall calculations needed

**Investor outcome**:
- Returns compressed
- May need to invest more capital in down round
- Or take write-down on previous investment

### **7. Information Asymmetry & Bad Actor Founders**

**Founder-run risks**:
- Founders have incomplete information on company finances
- Founders might hide bad news until too late
- Related party transactions
- Self-dealing (paying themselves excessively)
- Lack of professional management early on

**Investor outcome**: Downside not discovered until later rounds

## Pain Points in Priced Equity Rounds

### **1. Valuation Negotiation Paralysis**

**Challenge**:
- No objective valuation method for early-stage
- Founder wants high valuation (less dilution)
- Investor wants low valuation (more ownership)
- Wide gap between expectations
- Negotiations can stall entire process

**Example**:
- Founder wants $20M pre-money
- Investor offers $8M pre-money
- Gap of 150% - hard to bridge

**Resolution**:
- Market comparables (similar stage/metrics)
- VCs use benchmarks from cohorts
- Use independent valuation input
- Sometimes use formula-based approaches

### **2. Term Sheet Complexity**

**Too many moving parts**:
- Liquidation preference type (1x participating vs non-participating)
- Anti-dilution mechanism (broad vs narrow weighted average)
- Board composition
- Protective provisions (10+ items)
- Information rights
- Participation rights
- Drag/tag along rights
- ROFR terms
- Voting provisions

**Outcome**:
- Parties don't fully understand implications
- Sign terms they later regret
- Disputes emerge later
- Costly legal fees to unwind or litigate

### **3. Preference Stacking Creates Mathematic Illusion**

**Problem**:
- Seed investors see 1x participation preference
- They think they're protected in down scenarios
- Series A investors also get 1x preference
- Combined, preferences can stack > company value
- In any but best case outcome, math doesn't work out

**Founder impact**: Feels completely diluted

**Investor impact**: Returns compressed because preferences stack

**Example waterfall at $50M exit**:
- Seed: $2M invested, 1x preference = $2M
- Series A: $10M invested, 1x preference = $10M
- Series B: $20M invested, 1x preference = $20M
- Total: $32M in preferences, but exit is $50M
- $18M left for founders/common

### **4. Board Composition Deadlock**

**Problem**:
- Investor wants seat, founder wants seat
- Company not big enough for 5-seat board (3 is typical)
- Investor + founder = 2 seats, 1 neutral
- Investor wants favorable neutral
- Founder wants favorable neutral
- Deadlock on board selection

**Outcome**:
- Board ineffectual (deadlocked on decisions)
- Board too involved in operations (bad for scaling)
- Neutral director becomes kingmaker

### **5. Protective Provisions Can Paralyze**

**Problem**:
- Investor wants veto on major decisions
- Founder wants operational autonomy
- Terms like "material change to business" are vague
- What counts as material?
- Does pivot require approval?
- Does hiring key VP require approval?
- Ambiguity creates conflict

**Outcome**:
- Founder fears investor veto
- Minor decisions escalated to investor approval
- Company moves slowly
- Investor frustrated with operational questions

### **6. Anti-Dilution Disputes**

**Problem**:
- What constitutes a "down round"?
- Does restructuring trigger anti-dilution?
- Does grant of employee options trigger anti-dilution?
- Broad-based weighted average calculations are complex
- Disagreements on fairness

**Outcome**:
- Legal disputes
- Expensive remediation
- Relationship breakdown between founder & investor

### **7. Information Rights Burden**

**Problem**:
- Investors want monthly financials, board updates
- Founders need to spend time on reporting
- Multiple investors demand different formats
- Creates administrative overhead
- Small companies struggle with accounting

**Outcome**:
- Founder time diverted from business
- Investors get bad/late information
- Creates mutual frustration

### **8. Secondary Sales & Co-Sale Complexity**

**Problem**:
- Founder wants to sell some shares (liquidity event)
- Investor has co-sale rights (sells with founder)
- Investor has ROFR (can prevent any external sale)
- Creates impossible choice for founder
- Early founder liquidity blocks investor participation

**Outcome**:
- Founder locked in (can't sell shares)
- Creates contentious renegotiation

## Document Checklist

When raising a priced equity round, you'll typically encounter:

- [ ] **Term Sheet** - 4-5 pages, covers major economic terms
- [ ] **Stock Purchase Agreement** - 20-40 pages, legal closing docs
- [ ] **Investors' Rights Agreement** - 10-20 pages, governance & information
- [ ] **Voting Agreement** - 10-15 pages, board voting & elections
- [ ] **ROFR & Co-Sale Agreement** - 5-10 pages, stock sale restrictions
- [ ] **Board Consent/Resolutions** - Resolution authorizing preferred stock issuance
- [ ] **Cap Table Updates** - Showing fully diluted ownership post-close
- [ ] **Certificate of Incorporation Amendment** - If preferred stock class being created
- [ ] **Disclosure Schedules** - Lists of exceptions to reps & warranties
- [ ] **Legal Opinion** - Law firm opinion on formation, authority, validity
- [ ] **Financial Statements** - Last 3 years or since founding

## Next Steps

1. Review term sheet thoroughly - understand all terms
2. Consult startup lawyer - don't skip this step
3. Benchmark against comps - validate valuation
4. Negotiate key pain points early:
   - Liquidation preference type (1x non-participating preferred)
   - Anti-dilution mechanism (broad-based weighted average)
   - Board composition (minority protection for founder)
   - Protective provisions (minimize veto items)
5. Get legal review of SPAs and rights agreements
6. Plan 4-6 week negotiation timeline
7. Close financing (allow 2-4 weeks for due diligence)
