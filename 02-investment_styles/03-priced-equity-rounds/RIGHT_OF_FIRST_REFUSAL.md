## ARTICLE II: RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL

### 2.1 Company Right of First Refusal (ROFR)

**Whenever a stockholder (other than Company) proposes to transfer any shares**, the Company shall have the right to repurchase those shares before they may be sold to any third party.

**ROFR applies to**:
- Founder/employee stock sales
- Investor stock sales
- Any transfer for value

**ROFR does NOT apply to**:
- Transfers to spouse or lineal descendants
- Transfers to trusts for benefit of holder or family
- Transfers by will or operation of law (inheritance)
- Pledges as collateral (if not actual sale)

### 2.2 Exercise of Company ROFR

**Procedure**:

**Step 1: Proposed Sale Offer**
- Stockholder proposes sale to third party
- Stockholder must obtain written offer from third party
- Offer must include: price, terms, conditions
- Stockholder notifies Company in writing
- Notice includes copy of third-party offer

**Step 2: Notice Period**
- Company has [__] days to exercise ROFR
- Notice period begins upon receipt of stockholder notice
- Company can exercise by written notice to stockholder

**Step 3: Exercise of ROFR**
- Company notifies stockholder: "Company exercises ROFR to purchase shares at offered price and terms"
- If no written notice within [__] days, ROFR deemed waived
- Stockholder can then proceed with third-party sale

**Step 4: Closing**
- Closing of Company purchase within [__] days
- Purchase price: same as third-party offer
- Terms: same as third-party offer
- Stockholder cannot negotiate different terms with Company

### 2.3 Valuation of ROFR

**Purchase Price**: ROFR price must equal third-party offer price
- If third-party offer is $10/share, Company purchases at $10/share
- No right to renegotiate price in Company's favor
- Company must match third-party offer exactly

### 2.4 Company Funding of ROFR

**Company must have capital to fund purchase**:
- Company responsible for obtaining funds
- If Company cannot fund, ROFR fails and stockholder can sell to third party
- Company may borrow funds or use existing cash
- Company may require third-party financing

### 2.5 Restrictions on Resale by Company

**If Company exercises ROFR and repurchases shares**:
- Shares become treasury stock (company-owned)
- Company can use shares for:
  - Employee stock option plan
  - Future equity financings
  - Key hire equity grants
  - Founder buybacks/vesting modifications

### 2.6 Investor ROFR (Co-Investment Right)

**In addition to Company ROFR**, holders of [___]% of Preferred Stock shall have their own ROFR:

**Investor ROFR**:
- If Company does not exercise ROFR (or ROFR expires)
- Investors have [__] days to exercise right
- Investors can purchase shares at third-party offer price
- If multiple investors have ROFR, allocation pro-rata to their holdings

**Investor ROFR Process**:
1. Stockholder notifies investors of proposed sale
2. Investors have [__] days to respond
3. If investors exercise, they purchase at third-party price
4. Closing [__] days after exercise
5. If investors don't exercise, stockholder can sell to third party

### 2.7 Information in ROFR Notice

**ROFR Notice must include**:
- Name of proposed third-party buyer
- Number of shares proposed to sell
- Price per share
- Total purchase price
- Payment terms (cash, stock, earn-out)
- Any conditions on purchase
- Proposed closing date
- Copy of written offer or purchase agreement
- Material terms of the transaction

## ARTICLE III: CO-SALE AGREEMENT

### 3.1 Co-Sale Right (Tag-Along Right)

**If a Founder/major stockholder (holding [___]% or more) proposes to sell shares**:
- Other stockholders have the right to sell their shares at the same time
- On the same terms and conditions
- To the same buyer
- At the same price per share

**Purpose**: Prevents founder from having exclusive sale while other shareholders are locked in.

### 3.2 Co-Sale Mechanics

**Trigger for Co-Sale**:
- Founder proposes to sell [__]% or more of their stake
- Or founder proposes to sell [___]+ shares
- Or founder proposes transaction valued at $[__]+ million

**Co-Sale Notice**:
- Founder notifies other stockholders of proposed sale
- Notice includes:
  - Buyer identity
  - Number of shares founder selling
  - Price per share
  - Other material terms
  - Proposed closing date
- Notice given [__] days before closing

**Exercise of Co-Sale**:
- Other stockholders notify founder: "We exercise co-sale rights"
- Stockholders can sell pro-rata to founder's sale
- Example: Founder selling 50% of their stake
  - Other stockholders can sell 50% of their stakes at same price

### 3.3 Co-Sale vs. ROFR

**ROFR** (Company/Investor right to match):
- Company can block sale by repurchasing
- Investor can block sale by investing same capital

**Co-Sale** (Minority protection):
- Doesn't block sale
- Allows other shareholders to participate in buyer
- Ensures fair price per share across all sellers

### 3.4 Co-Sale Example

**Scenario**:
- Founder A owns 40% of company
- Investor B owns 30%
- Other shareholders own 30%
- Buyer offers to purchase Founder A's 40% at $10/share

**Co-Sale Exercise**:
- Investor B and other shareholders notified
- They can exercise co-sale to sell their shares too
- Investor B can sell up to 30% of their holdings at $10/share
- Other shareholders can sell up to 30% of their holdings at $10/share
- Buyer now purchasing: 40% (founder) + 30% (investor) + 30% (others) = 100% at $10/share
- Or buyer can decline to buy if all shares not available

### 3.5 Allocation of Purchase

**If multiple shareholders want to co-sell**:
- Shares allocated pro-rata to their co-sale request
- If insufficient buyer interest, allocation method:
  - Founder gets priority (they initiated)
  - Then other shareholders pro-rata to their holdings

### 3.6 No Co-Sale in Employee Sales

**Co-sale does NOT apply to**:
- Employee equity sales (option exercises)
- Founder/employee vesting purchases
- Awards of restricted stock to employees
- Pledges as collateral

## ARTICLE IV: DRAG-ALONG RIGHTS

### 4.1 Drag-Along Provision

**Upon approval by holders of [___]% of Preferred Stock** to sell the Company:
- ALL minority shareholders (including founders) shall be obligated to sell
- At the same price per share
- On the same terms and conditions
- Cannot refuse or block transaction

### 4.2 Drag-Along Trigger

**Drag-along can be used when**:
- Holders of [___]% of Preferred approve sale
- OR Board of Directors approves transaction
- AND sale is approved by holders of [___]% of Common Stock
- For acquisition valuing company at $[__]+ million

**Restrictions on Drag-Along Abuse**:
- Should be used only for legitimate company sales
- Cannot be used for minority squeezeouts at unfair prices
- Board should recommend transaction
- Price should be reasonable and fair

### 4.3 Drag-Along Mechanics

**Process**:

1. **Vote**: Preferred stockholders vote to approve sale (majority or required threshold)
2. **Notification**: Company notifies all shareholders
   - Sale approved at $X per share
   - Mandatory participation for all shareholders
   - Closing date: [DATE]
3. **Stockholder Response**: All shareholders must:
   - Sign purchase agreement
   - Deliver shares at closing
   - Accept same price per share
   - No separate negotiations
4. **Closing**: All shares purchased at same price per share pro-rata
5. **Payment**: Proceeds distributed per liquidation preference waterfall

### 4.4 Drag-Along Pricing

**All stockholders receive same price per share**:

**Example**:
- Company acquired for $100M
- 10M shares outstanding (50% preferred, 50% common)
- Price per share: $100M / 10M = $10/share

**Waterfall on Proceeds**:
1. Preferred stock holders get liquidation preferences first
2. Common stock holders get remaining proceeds

**Example with Liquidation Preference**:
- Preferred: 1x participation on $50M investment
- Common: Founders with 50% ownership
- Acquisition: $100M total

**Waterfall**:
1. Preferred gets $50M back (liquidation preference)
2. Remaining $50M split pro-rata to all stockholders:
   - Preferred holders get $25M (half)
   - Common holders get $25M (half)

**Net for Founder**: $25M (not equal to preferred return)

### 4.5 Dissenter's Rights

**Stockholders forced into drag-along have limited recourse**:
- Can challenge if sale is unfair (fraudulent) or coercive
- Cannot challenge just because they disagree with decision
- Can appraise shares if Delaware law applies (rarely used)
- Delaware appraisal rights only available if publicly traded (in merger)

### 4.6 Founder Protections in Drag-Along

**To mitigate drag-along risk**, founders should negotiate:
- Require Board recommendation (not just investor vote)
- Require [___]% of common stock approval (founder veto)
- Minimum price threshold (cannot drag below certain valuation)
- Representative on Board for founder voice
- Key person retention bonuses

## ARTICLE V: TRANSFERS TO AFFILIATES & PERMITTED TRANSFEREES

### 5.1 Permitted Transfers (No ROFR/Co-Sale)

**The following transfers do NOT trigger ROFR or co-sale**:

**Founder Transfers**:
- To spouse or lineal descendants (children, grandchildren)
- To trust for benefit of founder or family
- By will or inheritance (upon death)
- To foundation or charity (if estate planning)
- To retirement accounts/IRAs (if permitted)

**Investor Transfers**:
- To affiliate entity of investor (same PE fund)
- To successor fund of investor
- To investment partners/LPs
- To insurance company in connection with policy
- Pledges as collateral (if not actual sale)

**Conditions on Permitted Transfers**:
- Transferee must sign this Agreement
- Transferee assumes all rights and obligations
- Company must approve transferee (not to be unreasonably withheld)
- Proper documentation of transfer

### 5.2 Notification of Transfers

**Even for permitted transfers**, stockholder should notify Company:
- For cap table accuracy
- For transfer agent records
- For consent if required
- To document affiliate relationship

## ARTICLE VI: MECHANICS OF SALES & CLOSINGS

### 6.1 Stock Transfer Mechanics

**Upon completion of sale**:
- Stockholder delivers shares to buyer
- Delivery by: physical stock certificate, book entry transfer, or electronic transfer
- Buyer delivers payment per terms of purchase agreement
- Company updates cap table and transfer agent

### 6.2 Purchase Agreement Terms

**When ROFR/co-sale exercised**, parties execute Stock Purchase Agreement including:
- Price per share
- Number of shares
- Payment terms (timing and method)
- Representations and warranties
- Closing conditions
- Indemnification (if any)
- Escrow or holdback (if any)

### 6.3 Closing Documentation

**At closing of any sale**:
- Stock certificates/book entries delivered
- Purchase price paid (or evidence of payment)
- Stock transfer forms completed
- Non-compete/non-solicitation agreements (if applicable)
- Confidentiality agreements (if applicable)
- Resignation letters (if selling founder/employee)

### 7.1 Time Periods

**ROFR/Co-Sale Exercise Periods**:
- Company ROFR: [__] days from notice
- Investor ROFR: [__] days after Company waives/expires
- Co-Sale: [__] days from founder sale notice
- All notices must be in writing to trigger periods

### 7.2 ROFR Exceptions & Carve-Outs

**ROFR does NOT apply to**:
- Founder to spouse/family transfers
- Transfers by will or intestacy
- Pledges as collateral (if not actual sale)
- Sales under $[__] value
- Sales to affiliates (if pre-approved)
- Court-ordered sales (divorce, creditor levy)

### 7.3 Prohibited Transfers

**Stockholder cannot transfer shares to**:
- Direct competitors of Company
- Without Company consent and approval
- Company can reasonably withhold consent
- Competitor defined as: company in [INDUSTRY/MARKET] with revenue >$[__]

### 7.4 Forfeiture of ROFR/Co-Sale by Failure to Exercise

**If Company/Investor fails to exercise within notice period**:
- ROFR/co-sale deemed waived
- Stockholder can proceed with third-party sale
- No waiver of future ROFR/co-sale rights
