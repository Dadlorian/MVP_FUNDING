# Venture Debt Founder Perspective & Checklist

## Should You Take Venture Debt? Self-Assessment

### Quick YES Indicators

You should **seriously consider** venture debt if:

- [ ] **Have revenue**: Minimum $[REVENUE_THRESHOLD]/month in recurring revenue
- [ ] **Have runway**: 12-18+ months of cash remaining (not needing immediate funding)
- [ ] **Have growth plan**: Clear, quantified path to next funding round (12-18 months away)
- [ ] **Have unit economics**: Understand CAC, LTV, payback period (revenue-based models)
- [ ] **Have funding path**: Planning Series B or later funding (not seed stage)
- [ ] **Have multiple lenders**: 3+ term sheets to negotiate from
- [ ] **Have board support**: Your board agrees that debt won't constrain strategy
- [ ] **Have strong metrics**: Revenue growing month-over-month consistently

### Quick NO Indicators

You should **avoid** venture debt if:

- [ ] **Pre-revenue**: No proof of market fit or paying customers
- [ ] **Negative revenue**: Customer churn exceeding new customer acquisition
- [ ] **Short runway**: Less than 6 months of cash; need immediate funding
- [ ] **Seed stage**: Planning Series A (not Series B); insufficient scale
- [ ] **Unpredictable revenue**: Highly variable month-to-month (can't forecast)
- [ ] **Single lender only**: No negotiating power if only option
- [ ] **Board disagreement**: Your investors don't want debt (common among VCs)
- [ ] **Strategy conflict**: Planned pivots or experiments incompatible with covenants

## Founder Decision Matrix

| Situation | Best Financing | Why |
|-----------|---|---|
| **Pre-product, seeking funding** | Equity (SAFE/Seed) | Too risky; no revenue for repayment |
| **Product-market fit, pre-revenue** | Equity (Series A) | Prove model first before debt service |
| **Series A company, $100K revenue** | Mix: 60% Equity / 40% Debt | Revenue enough for debt; equity for growth |
| **Series B ready, $500K revenue** | Mix: 50% Equity / 50% Debt | Strong revenue; debt is efficient |
| **Series B company, $1M+ revenue** | Mix: 40% Equity / 60% Debt | Debt more efficient; proven model |
| **Late-stage, $5M+ revenue** | Debt: 70%+ + little equity | Debt is cheapest; equity reserved for acquisitions |
| **Declining revenue, negative unit economics** | Don't raise | Fix the business first |

| **Interest (Year 1)** | $120,000 | 12% on $1M |
| **Warrant dilution** | ~$200,000 value | 20% coverage; dilutes cap table ~2% |
| **Origination fee** | $20,000 | Deducted upfront |
| **Covenant restrictions** | $[UNKNOWN] | Opportunity cost; can't use cash freely, pivot restricted |
| **Default risk** | Personal guarantee | Could lose personal assets if fails |
| **Prepayment penalties** | $30,000 | If repay in Year 2 at 3% |

## Covenant Impact Analysis

### Revenue Covenant Example

**Scenario:** $500K/month revenue, 25% month-over-month growth target

**Lender's proposed covenant:**
- Q1 minimum: $500K
- Q2 minimum: $600K
- Q3 minimum: $700K
- Q4 minimum: $750K

**Your actual projection:**
- Q1: $500K ✓
- Q2: $550K ✗ (below $600K covenant, breach!)
- Q3: $675K ✗ (below $700K covenant, breach!)
- Q4: $850K ✓ (exceeds covenant)

**Problem:** Even though total Q4 revenue is strong, covenant breaches in Q2-Q3 trigger default risk.

**Negotiation:**
- Request covenant holiday (first 2 quarters)
- Request more conservative targets (15% growth vs. 25%)
- Request cure period (30 days after quarter end to discuss)

### Burn Rate Covenant Example

**Scenario:** $500K/month burn rate, planning product expansion

**Lender's proposed covenant:**
- Maximum monthly burn: $500K
- If exceed, default triggered

**Your issue:**
- Want to hire 3 engineers at $300K/month total → Burn increases to $800K
- This breaches covenant immediately
- Can't make hiring decision without lender approval

**Negotiation:**
- Request tiered burn rates (increase allowed after each funding round)
- Request exemptions for planned, approved hires
- Request temporary increase if compensated by revenue growth

### Cash Balance Covenant

**Scenario:** $2M cash balance required at all times

**Problem:**
- Have $2.5M cash
- Major enterprise customer needs monthly prepayment ($1M/month)
- Sales team identifies huge opportunity, want to hire 2 VPs @ $400K total
- Can't do both; cash drops below $2M threshold → Default

**Negotiation:**
- Request lower minimum ($1.5M) to give more flexibility
- Request exception for customer prepayments (should be inflows, not breaches)
- Request temporary waiver when raising new funding

## Personal Guarantee Implications

### What Personal Guarantees Mean

**The guarantee is PERSONAL and UNLIMITED:**

1. **Unlimited liability**: Liable for full amount, not just your equity stake
   - Example: $1M loan, you own 40%, but personally liable for $1M (not $400K)

2. **Personal assets at risk**:
   - Your house (if mortgaged with low equity)
   - Your bank accounts
   - Your car
   - Your retirement accounts (in some cases)
   - Future earnings

3. **Survives bankruptcy**:
   - Even if company declares bankruptcy, personal guarantee remains
   - Lender can pursue personal assets despite company bankruptcy
   - Credit score damaged

4. **Joint and several liability**:
   - Multiple founders: Each liable for full amount
   - Lender can pursue just one founder for 100% (not split 50/50)
   - You could end up liable for co-founder's share

### Negotiating Personal Guarantees

**Founder positions:**

1. **Eliminate entirely**
   - Push: "Series B company; don't need personal guarantee"
   - Lender response: Will increase interest rate 2-3%
   - **Workable for strong revenue companies**

2. **Cap the guarantee**
   - Push: "Cap personal guarantee at [PERCENTAGE]% of loan"
   - Example: Liable for only 50% of $1M = $500K max
   - **Often accepted with higher interest rate**

3. **Joint instead of several**
   - Push: "Split guarantee among founders; each responsible for their share"
   - Example: With 2 founders, each liable for $500K (not $1M)
   - **Lender may reject or charge more**

4. **Release provision**
   - Push: "Release guarantee when [MILESTONE] achieved or [TIME PERIOD] passes"
   - Example: "Release if we raise Series B"
   - **Common negotiation point**

5. **Insurance**
   - Get "guarantee repayment insurance" policy (expensive but available)
   - Covers personal guarantee if company defaults
   - **Rare; may not exist for your company**

## Covenant Negotiation Tactics

### 1. Start with Conservative Numbers

**Mistake:** Pitching overly optimistic covenants
- Lender sees $1M/month projection, sets covenant at $900K/month
- You miss target by 10%, you're in default

**Better approach:**
- Present 80% confidence projections to lender
- Request covenant at 70% of 80% confidence level
- Gives buffer for execution risk

**Example:**
- Your projection: $100K/month revenue (80% confidence)
- Safe covenant: $70K/month (provides 30% margin)
- Even if miss by 20%, covenant at $80K still safe

### 2. Negotiate Holiday Periods

**Request covenant holiday** for first 12 months:
- Rationale: Need time to hit stride, prove model
- Lender benefit: More likely to succeed in long term
- **Often granted for Series A/B companies**

### 3. Tiered Covenants

**Propose increasing covenants** tied to milestones:
- Year 1: $[COVENANT_Y1] (loose)
- Year 2: $[COVENANT_Y2] (moderate)
- Year 3: $[COVENANT_Y3] (tight)
- Rationale: Give space early, tighten as company matures

### 4. Cure Period Requests

**Push for longer cure periods:**
- Standard: [CURE_DAYS] days to fix breach
- Your request: [LONGER_CURE_DAYS] days (+ 30 days for discussion)
- Rationale: Need time to adjust operations, get board approval for solutions

### 5. Covenant Relief Triggers

**Build in automatic relief** when certain milestones hit:
- When raise Series B → All covenants become 25% less restrictive
- When hit revenue target → Burn rate covenant relaxed
- When EBITDA positive → Financial covenants eliminated

## Warranty and Representation Risks

### Representations You're Making

When you sign venture debt agreement, you're saying:

**Financial representations:**
- "All financial statements are accurate and complete"
  - **Risk**: If later discover accounting error, misstatement, lender can claim breach
  - **Protection**: Audit financial statements before signing

**Operational representations:**
- "Company is in compliance with all laws"
  - **Risk**: Discover later that IP not properly assigned, contracts problematic
  - **Protection**: Review all major contracts and IP with lawyer before signing

**Disclosure representations:**
- "All material information has been disclosed"
  - **Risk**: Any omitted fact (major customer leaving, key employee departing) is breach
  - **Protection**: Comprehensive disclosure memo listing all risks/issues known

**Litigation representations:**
- "No pending or threatened litigation"
  - **Risk**: Any lawsuit/dispute is breach, even small one
  - **Protection**: Disclose all known disputes, even minor ones

### What Can Trigger Breach

- Accounting restatement (even small)
- Discovery of undisclosed liabilities
- Customer contracts don't match disclosure
- Key employee departure
- Major customer leaves
- Regulatory investigation
- IP dispute emerges
- Any material fact in documents is wrong

**Consequence:** Lender claims breach, demands repayment, accelerates loan, pursues guarantee.

## Negotiation Playbook: Venture Debt Terms

### 1. Get Multiple Term Sheets (3+ is ideal)

**Why it matters:**
- Lenders compete → Better terms for you
- Without competition, lender has leverage
- Each lender has different risk appetite

**Timing:**
- Approach 5-7 lenders simultaneously
- Ask for term sheets within [TERM_SHEET_DAYS] days
- Create competitive tension

**Sources:**
- [MAJOR_LENDERS] (if you fit their stage/model)
- Referrals from VCs or angels
- Specialty lenders in your industry
- Your existing bank relationships

### 2. Start with Interest Rate Negotiation

**Standard opener:**
- Lender offers: 12%
- You counter: "We're seeing 10% from other lenders; can you match?"
- Lender response: "For your profile, 11% is our best"
- **This is reasonable negotiation**

**Leverage points:**
- Strong revenue: Justifies lower rate
- Other term sheets: "We have 3 offers; looking for best all-in terms"
- Growth trajectory: "Revenue growing 20% MoM; reduces risk"
- Shorter duration: "Taking smaller loan; reduced risk"

### 3. Trade Interest for Covenants

**If lender won't lower interest:**
- Offer: "What if we accept 12% interest, but you relax revenue covenant?"
- Lender perspective: Higher interest compensates for higher risk
- **Often works better than asking for rate cut**

### 4. Warrant Percentage Negotiation

**Standard approach:**
- Lender proposes: 20% warrant coverage
- You counter: "That's 2% dilution; can we get to 10%?"
- Lender: "We need 15% for risk profile"
- **Often splits to 12-15% for negotiated deal**

**Leverage points:**
- Strong growth: "Revenue growing 30% MoM; warrants should be 10% or less"
- Traction: "Already generating $[REVENUE]/month; minimal risk"
- Other term sheets: Show competitor offering lower warrant %
- Tier down: "Start at 15%, but drops to 10% if exceed revenue targets"

### 5. Prepayment Penalty Negotiation

**Standard terms:**
- Year 1-2: 3%
- You counter: "Can you waive if Series B raised?"
- Lender: "Yes, 0% penalty on Series B proceeds"

**Or:**
- "Can we reduce penalty to 2% in Year 2?"
- "After [MONTHS], reduce penalty by 50%?"

### 6. Document Package Negotiation

**Don't accept boilerplate terms on:**

- **Board reporting**: Don't agree to monthly board meetings; negotiate to quarterly
- **Audit rights**: Lender wants access to audit; negotiate to financials only, not operations audit
- **Material contracts**: Don't need to disclose every contract; only >$[THRESHOLD]
- **Officer certification**: Provide quarterly, not monthly

## When to Walk Away from Venture Debt

### Red Flags in Term Sheet

1. **Covenant covenants unrealistic for your business**
   - Example: Revenue covenant assumes 50% growth, your market grows 10% max
   - **Action**: Renegotiate or decline

2. **Personal guarantee without release provision**
   - Example: Guarantee never expires, even after loan fully repaid
   - **Action**: Negotiate release condition or walk

3. **Lender has veto rights over strategy**
   - Example: Can't hire, acquire, or pivot without approval
   - **Action**: Negotiate limited approval rights or decline

4. **Warrant coverage excessive for your stage**
   - Example: Series C company, 25% warrant coverage
   - **Action**: Counter at 8%; if lender won't budge, find different lender

5. **Prepayment penalties outrageous**
   - Example: 5% penalty in Year 2
   - **Action**: Negotiate down or decline

6. **Lender conduct concerning**
   - Example: Vague about terms, seems disorganized, doesn't respond to queries
   - **Action**: Walk; this will be 3-year relationship; you need responsive lender

### Financial Scenarios to Avoid Debt

**Don't take venture debt if:**

1. **You need cash to survive next 6 months**
   - Debt service (interest + principal) adds to burn rate
   - If cash runway 6 months, debt payments eat runway even faster
   - **Better option:** Equity or bridge financing

2. **Your burn rate exceeds revenue by 5x+**
   - Example: $5M burn, $1M revenue
   - You need [YEARS] to break even
   - Debt repayment requires profitability that may never come
   - **Better option:** Burn down, raise more equity, or pivot

3. **Revenue declining month-over-month**
   - Covenants assume growth; if shrinking, can't meet covenants
   - **Better option:** Fix the product/business first

4. **Highly uncertain funding path**
   - Debt assumes Series B in 18 months
   - If Series B unlikely, you're stuck with debt you can't repay
   - **Better option:** Wait until Series B path clear

## Founder Checklist: Before Signing Venture Debt

### Financial Readiness

- [ ] **Revenue**: Minimum $[MINIMUM_REVENUE_THRESHOLD]/month (ideally $[IDEAL_REVENUE_MINIMUM]+)
- [ ] **Runway**: 14+ months remaining (not 6 months)
- [ ] **Growth trajectory**: Consistent month-over-month growth (not flat or declining)
- [ ] **Unit economics**: Understand CAC, LTV, payback period within your model
- [ ] **Predictability**: Can forecast revenue ±15% for next 6 months
- [ ] **Cash flow**: Not negative cash flow from operations (interest coverage good)

### Business Readiness

- [ ] **Validated model**: Product-market fit proven; not experimenting
- [ ] **Repeatable sales**: Can replicate customer acquisition consistently
- [ ] **Scalable operations**: Can grow without proportional cost increase
- [ ] **Market opportunity**: TAM large enough for planned growth
- [ ] **Competition**: Sustainable competitive advantage (not in commoditized market)
- [ ] **Team strength**: Leadership team has relevant experience

### Strategic Readiness

- [ ] **Funding plan**: Clear path to Series B (if Series A company) or exit
- [ ] **Timeline**: Confident in 12-18 month path to next milestone
- [ ] **Board alignment**: Board supportive of venture debt (VCs often resist)
- [ ] **Use of proceeds**: Clear, specific plan (not just "runway")
- [ ] **Covenant capacity**: Confident you can meet all proposed covenants
- [ ] **Multiple options**: Have 3+ term sheets to compare

### Legal Readiness

- [ ] **Cap table audit**: Verified all shares, options, warrants correctly recorded
- [ ] **IP audit**: Confirmed all IP properly assigned to company
- [ ] **Contract review**: Reviewed all major customer/vendor contracts
- [ ] **Litigation audit**: Disclosed all known disputes (even small ones)
- [ ] **Compliance**: Verified tax filing, payroll compliance, regulatory approval
- [ ] **Lawyer hired**: Startup attorney reviewing all documents (not general counsel)

### Negotiation Readiness

- [ ] **Market knowledge**: Know what other similar companies received (terms, rates, coverage)
- [ ] **Multiple lenders**: Have term sheets from 3-5 different lenders
- [ ] **Leverage understanding**: Know your negotiating leverage (strong revenue = high leverage)
- [ ] **Trade-offs planned**: Know what you're willing to trade (higher interest for lower covenants, etc.)
- [ ] **Walk-away price**: Know at what point you decline (e.g., "Won't accept >15% warrants")
- [ ] **Timeline**: Don't start negotiation when need cash in 2 weeks (lose leverage)

### Documentation Readiness

- [ ] **Financial statements reviewed**: Latest 12 months prepared, reviewed/audited
- [ ] **Projections reasonable**: 24-month projections conservative, achievable
- [ ] **Covenant models**: Built 24-month model showing covenant compliance
- [ ] **Disclosure memo**: Comprehensive memo of all risks, issues, known facts
- [ ] **Customer contracts**: Top [X] customer contracts available for lender review
- [ ] **Cap table final**: Cap table final version with all shares/options/warrants reflected

## Post-Signing: Managing the Debt Relationship

### Monthly Obligations

- [ ] **Pay interest**: Due on [PAYMENT_DAY] every month (automatic recurring?)
- [ ] **Pay principal** (if amortizing): Monthly payment due [PAYMENT_DAY]
- [ ] **Track covenant**: Monitor vs. quarterly covenants monthly (don't wait for quarter-end)
- [ ] **Communicate**: Notify lender immediately if covenant breach likely

### Quarterly Obligations

- [ ] **Financial reporting**: Deliver statements within [REPORTING_DAYS] days
- [ ] **Board package**: Prepare board materials, include variance analysis
- [ ] **Covenant certification**: Officer certificate confirming compliance
- [ ] **Board meeting**: Include lender as observer (if required)
- [ ] **Management call**: Quarterly call with lender to discuss results

### Annual Obligations

- [ ] **Audited statements**: Deliver within [AUDIT_DAYS] days of year-end
- [ ] **Covenant calculation**: Annual true-up of financial covenants
- [ ] **Budget submission**: Next year budget to lender
- [ ] **Insurance proof**: Certificates of insurance current
- [ ] **Strategic review**: Annual meeting on business direction

### Ongoing Relationship Management

**Key principle:** Lender is now stakeholder in your business for 3-5 years.

**Best practices:**
- **Over-communicate**: Tell them good news AND bad news early
- **No surprises**: If covenant might be missed, notify 30 days in advance (not 5 days after quarter-end)
- **Respond quickly**: Answer questions promptly; don't make lender chase you
- **Build trust**: Lender will extend terms, negotiate covenant adjustments IF relationship good
- **Escalate properly**: Don't ambush lender with bad news at board meeting; call CFO/loan manager first

## Common Pitfalls Founders Make

### Pitfall 1: Over-Optimistic Covenants

**Mistake:** Accept covenants based on best-case revenue projections
- Think: "We'll definitely hit $1M/month"
- Reality: Growth slower, market headwinds, lose customer
- Result: Breach covenant in Month 4, default

**Fix:**
- Use 70-80% confidence revenue in covenants
- Request covenant holiday first 12 months
- Build 20-30% buffer into targets

### Pitfall 2: Ignoring Opportunity Cost

**Mistake:** Don't account for covenant restrictions in strategic planning
- Want to pivot product (covenant requires not changing business)
- Want to experiment with new market (burn rate covenant prevents investment)
- Can't hire as aggressively (covenant-driven burn rate limit)
- Result: Miss market opportunity because stuck in debt covenant

**Fix:**
- Model covenants into business plan
- Request covenant flexibility for planned experiments/pivots
- Negotiate tiered covenants that relax as you grow

### Pitfall 3: Not Understanding Personal Guarantee

**Mistake:** Sign guarantee without understanding personal risk
- "It's standard; everyone signs it"
- Don't think through house at risk, personal liability
- Result: Company fails; lender pursues personal guarantee; lose house

**Fix:**
- Understand exact personal assets at risk
- Negotiate cap or release provision
- Consider guarantee insurance (if available)
- Discuss with spouse/family (if married; could affect community property)

### Pitfall 4: Inadequate Covenant Buffer

**Mistake:** Structure covenants too tight to actual operations
- Revenue covenant = expected revenue (no margin for error)
- Burn covenant = budgeted burn (no room for unexpected spending)
- Result: Normal business variation triggers covenant breach

**Fix:**
- Request covenant 15-25% below expected level
- Request covenant holiday first 12 months
- Request cure period after quarter-end (not before)

### Pitfall 5: Financing Trap

**Mistake:** Use venture debt to extend runway; don't improve unit economics
- Raise $500K debt
- Use to extend runway 6 months
- Don't improve revenue or reduce burn
- Result: 6 months later, out of cash again; now have debt service too

**Fix:**
- Venture debt for growth INVESTMENT, not just runway
- Use proceeds for customer acquisition that pays back in <1 year
- Use for product development that increases revenue
- Not for "running the business"

### Pitfall 6: Missing Prepayment Penalty Opportunity

**Mistake:** Pay off debt early without understanding penalty
- Raise Series B; want to pay off debt
- Don't realize 3% prepayment penalty
- Lose $30K unnecessarily
- Result: Could have negotiated waiver if asked upfront

**Fix:**
- Understand prepayment penalty timeline
- Negotiate exception for Series B proceeds upfront
- Model different payoff scenarios before signing

## Key Takeaways for Founders

1. **Venture debt is non-dilutive only if you can meet covenants**
   - If forced to breach covenants, dilution from default/warrant exercise can be severe

2. **Personal guarantee is real; understand your risk**
   - Could lose personal assets
   - Survives bankruptcy
   - Don't sign lightly

3. **Covenants are real constraints**
   - Not just suggestions
   - Restrict hiring, strategy pivots, spending
   - Build into business planning

4. **Warrants are hidden dilution**
   - 20% warrant coverage = ~2% dilution
   - Compounds with future equity rounds
   - Carefully negotiate percentage

5. **Negotiate while you have leverage**
   - Strong traction = high leverage
   - Declining traction = low leverage
   - Start negotiation from position of strength

6. **Venture debt best for profitable/near-profitable companies**
   - Capital-efficient financing
   - Improves returns for equity investors
   - Not for companies needing to burn lots to grow

7. **Treat lender as stakeholder, not adversary**
   - 3-5 year relationship
   - Lender benefits from your success
   - Over-communicate; build trust
