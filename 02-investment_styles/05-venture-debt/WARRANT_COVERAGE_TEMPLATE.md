# Warrant Coverage & Warrant Agreement Template

## What is Warrant Coverage?

Warrant coverage is an equity component of venture debt that gives the lender the right to purchase equity in the company at a fixed price. It's the lender's "upside" participation in the company's growth.

**Key purpose**: Compensate lender for taking high-risk unsecured debt position with equity participation upside.

---

## Why Lenders Require Warrants

### 1. Risk Compensation
- **High default rate**: 30-40% of venture-backed companies fail
- **Unsecured position**: Most venture debt unsecured; low recovery in bankruptcy
- **Long payoff**: Lender's return limited to interest (10-15%) over 3-5 years
- **Equity upside**: Warrants provide potential for 5-10x returns on successful exits

**Lender economics:**
- Interest income: 10-15% annually for 3-5 years = 30-75% total return on interest
- Warrant upside: Could be 500%+ if company successful
- Combined: Makes risk/reward more attractive

### 2. Incentive Alignment
- **Lender wants company success**: Warrant value increases with company valuation
- **No warrant dilution if company fails**: Only valuable if company succeeds
- **Shared upside**: Lender benefits from company growth like equity investors

### 3. Interest Rate Compensation
- **Higher warrant coverage** = Lower interest rate (you choose: interest vs. equity)
- **Lower warrant coverage** = Higher interest rate
- **Typical tradeoff**:
  - 15% interest + 10% warrants, vs.
  - 12% interest + 20% warrants, vs.
  - 10% interest + 30% warrants

## Warrant Coverage Terms

### Typical Coverage Percentages

| Company Stage | Warrant Coverage | Rationale |
|---------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Early Series A, pre-revenue** | 25-30% | High risk |
| **Series A, emerging revenue** | 20-25% | Moderate risk |
| **Series B, strong revenue** | 10-15% | Lower risk |
| **Series C+, profitability path** | 5-10% | Low risk |
| **Growth stage, proven unit economics** | 5% | Minimal risk |

### Calculation Examples

**Example 1: $1M Loan with 20% Coverage**
- Loan amount: $1,000,000
- Warrant coverage: 20% of loan = $200,000 of equity value
- Current company valuation: $10M
- Warrant coverage as % of cap table: 2% dilution
- Expected warrant shares: $200,000 ÷ price per share

**Example 2: Series B Pricing**
- $1M venture debt loan
- Company last valued at $10M (Series A)
- Expected Series B valuation: $30M
- 20% warrant coverage
- Exercise price: $30M valuation (Series B forward price)
- Warrant value: Represents stake at future (lower) price
- Benefit to lender: If Series B actually prices at $30M, warrants at-the-money

### Negotiating Coverage Percentage

**Founders want**: Lower percentage (less dilution)
**Lenders want**: Higher percentage (more upside)

**Negotiation leverage:**
- **Founder leverage**: Strong revenue traction, proven unit economics, multiple lender offers
- **Lender leverage**: High risk profile, negative revenue, weak team, limited financing alternatives

**Common negotiation points:**

1. **Stage/Traction-based reduction**
   - Founder offer: "Reduce from 20% to 15% because we have $[REVENUE]/month and clear path to profitability"
   - Outcome: Typically accepted for strong revenue companies

2. **Milestone-based warrants**
   - Founder offer: "Give us lower warrant coverage now (10%), increases to 15% if miss revenue covenant"
   - Outcome: Lender gains higher upside if company underperforms

3. **Covenant performance bonuses**
   - Founder offer: "Base 15%, but reduce to 10% if exceed revenue targets by 20%"
   - Outcome: Aligns lender/founder incentives

4. **Funding round relief**
   - Founder offer: "Start at 20%, reduces to 10% if we raise Series B"
   - Outcome: Common in market

5. **Other fee tradeoffs**
   - Founder offer: "Accept 20% warrants in exchange for lower origination fee (1% vs 2%)"
   - Outcome: Lender gets more equity but lower upfront cash

## 2. VESTING SCHEDULE

### 2.1 Vesting Determination

Warrants vest according to:

**Option 1: Immediate Full Vesting**
- 100% of warrants vest upon issuance
- Fully exercisable immediately

**Option 2: Time-Based Vesting**
- 25% vest upon issuance
- 25% vest on each anniversary (25% per year, 4-year vesting)
- Vesting occurs automatically; no action required

**Option 3: Milestone-Based Vesting**
- 50% upon first draw of Loan
- 25% upon achievement of $[REVENUE_MILESTONE] revenue
- 25% upon [OTHER_MILESTONE]

### 2.2 Acceleration Events

Unvested warrants automatically accelerate (100% vesting immediately) upon:

1. **Acquisition/Change of Control**: If Company acquired for consideration > $[THRESHOLD]
2. **Debt Acceleration**: If Loan accelerated due to default
3. **IPO**: Upon initial public offering
4. **Forced Liquidation**: Upon liquidation or bankruptcy

### 2.3 Forfeiture

If Warrant Holder no longer employed by/affiliated with Company (if applicable), vesting of unvested warrants ceases, and company may repurchase at nominal value.

*Note: Not applicable for lender warrant holders; included for reference only.*

## 3. EXERCISE RIGHTS AND MECHANICS

### 3.1 Right to Exercise

During the Exercise Period, Holder may exercise warrants in whole or in part at any time by:

**(a) Delivery of Exercise Notice**
- Written notice to Company specifying:
  - Number of shares to purchase
  - Exercise method (cash exercise vs. cashless)
  - Requested share certificate details

**(b) Payment of Exercise Price**
- Full exercise price due upon exercise
- OR: Cashless exercise (see Section 3.2)

**(c) Delivery of Warrant Certificate**
- Original warrant certificate or certified copy
- Lost certificate replacement available upon affidavit

### 3.2 Cashless Exercise

Holder may elect "cashless exercise" if:
- No effective registration statement covering shares, OR
- SEC rule 144 holding period not satisfied

**Cashless Exercise Formula:**
```
Shares received = (Fair Market Value - Exercise Price) / Fair Market Value × Warrant Shares
```

**Fair Market Value determination:**
- If publicly traded: Closing price on exercise date
- If recent funding round: Price per share from round
- If no recent pricing: Independent 409A valuation or Board determination
- If sale pending: Agreed purchase price

**Example cashless exercise:**
- Warrant shares: 100,000
- Exercise price: $1.00
- Fair market value: $4.00
- Calculation: ($4.00 - $1.00) / $4.00 × 100,000 = 75,000 shares
- Holder receives 75,000 shares; [75,000 × $1.00 = $75,000] of value "paid" via forgone appreciation

### 3.3 Timing of Issuance

Company shall issue share certificate(s) within [SHARE_ISSUANCE_DAYS] days of exercise and payment.

### 3.4 Fractional Shares

If exercise results in fractional share:
- Company may round down and issue whole shares only, OR
- Company issues check for fractional share value at FMV

### 3.5 Conditions to Exercise

Company may require Holder to:
- Represent ownership qualification under Rule 506(d) (no bad actor)
- Sign purchase agreement with customary reps
- Obtain necessary consents for share issuance
- Comply with securities law restrictions

**Company may NOT**:
- Condition exercise on redemption of other securities
- Require additional consideration beyond exercise price
- Impose conditions that materially delay exercise

### 3.6 Redemption by Company

Company **CANNOT** redeem, call, or force exercise of warrants except:
- Upon IPO, at Holder's election, or automatically cancel if not in-the-money
- With Board approval if fair value established through 409A

## 4. ANTI-DILUTION PROTECTION

### 4.1 Weighted Average Anti-Dilution

If Company issues shares at price per share less than Exercise Price, the Exercise Price shall be adjusted using weighted average formula:

```
New Exercise Price = Old Exercise Price ×
    (A + B) / (A + C)

Where:
A = Fully diluted shares outstanding before new issuance
B = Proceeds from new issuance ÷ New price per share
C = Number of shares issued in new issuance
```

### 4.2 Broad-Based Weighted Average

"Fully diluted" includes all:
- Outstanding Common Stock
- Outstanding Preferred Stock (converted to Common equivalent)
- Outstanding options and warrants (assuming full conversion)
- Convertible debt (assumed converted)

### 4.3 Narrow-Based Weighted Average (Alternative)

"Fully diluted" includes only:
- Outstanding Common Stock
- Outstanding options and warrants
(Excludes Preferred Stock and convertible debt)

*Note: Founder-favorable; lender would typically negotiate broad-based.*

### 4.4 Exceptions to Anti-Dilution

Following issuances **DO NOT** trigger anti-dilution adjustment:

1. **Stock Splits/Dividends**: Pro-rata splits or stock dividends
2. **Employee Options**: Grants under approved employee stock option plan up to [OPTION_RESERVE]%
3. **Acquisitions**: Stock issued in acquisition of another company (if Board approved)
4. **Equipment Leases**: Equipment-based financing
5. **Strategic Investments**: Stock issued for strategic partnerships
6. **Founder Equity**: Initial founder equity issuances

### 4.5 Example Anti-Dilution Adjustment

**Scenario:**
- Warrants for 100,000 shares at $1.00 exercise price
- Company has 1,000,000 fully diluted shares outstanding
- New Series B round at $0.75/share for $2,000,000 (2.67M new shares)

**Calculation:**
- A = 1,000,000
- B = $2,000,000 ÷ $0.75 = 2,666,667
- C = 2,666,667
- New Price = $1.00 × (1,000,000 + 2,666,667) / (1,000,000 + 2,666,667) = $0.75

**Result**: Exercise price reduced from $1.00 to $0.75, improving warrant holder's position

## 5. RIGHTS AS SHAREHOLDER

### 5.1 Voting Rights

Upon exercise and issuance, shares shall have same voting rights as Common Stock, including:
- Right to vote on Board elections
- Right to vote on company sale, merger, liquidation
- Right to vote on authorization of new share classes
- One vote per share

### 5.2 Dividend Rights

Holders of shares acquired by warrant exercise have same dividend rights as other Common Stock holders.

If Company declares dividends (cash or stock), warrant holder shares in pro-rata basis.

### 5.3 Liquidation Preference

Shares acquired by warrant exercise rank **EQUAL** to Common Stock in liquidation:
- Junior to all Preferred Stock holders
- Equal to other Common Stock holders

### 5.4 Anti-Dilution on Dividends

If Company declares stock dividend, number of warrant shares and exercise price adjust accordingly:
- Warrant shares increase pro-rata
- Exercise price decreases pro-rata
- Maintains Holder's economic interest

## 6. TRANSFER RESTRICTIONS AND REGISTRATION RIGHTS

### 6.1 Warrant Transfer Restrictions

Holder may transfer warrants only:
- To affiliates with written notice to Company
- To other investors with Company consent (not unreasonably withheld)
- Upon death, to estate or beneficiary with notice

Company may not unreasonably withhold consent for transfer to accredited investors or qualified institutional investors.

### 6.2 Transferee Obligations

Any transferee assumes all obligations and rights under this Agreement.

Transferee must:
- Acknowledge receipt of Warrant Agreement
- Agree to be bound by all terms
- Provide contact information for notices

### 6.3 Registration Rights (if Warrant Exercised)

If warrants exercised and shares issued, Holder has following registration rights:

**(a) Demand Registration**
- If after [DEMAND_REGISTRATION_DAYS] days of exercise
- Holder may require Company to register shares on Form S-1/S-3
- Holder entitled to [DEMAND_REGISTRATION_COUNT] demand registrations
- Company responsible for registration costs (not underwriting fees)

**(b) Piggyback Registration**
- Holder may include shares in any Company registration statement (IPO, equity financing)
- Company responsible for reasonable costs
- Underwriter may apply standard cutbacks if offering undersubscribed

**(c) S-3 Registration**
- If Company files S-3, Holder has right to include shares
- Available after [S3_AVAILABILITY_DAYS] days from exercise

**(d) Right to Convert**
- Before registration effective, Holder may convert to restricted shares (for tax purposes)

### 6.4 Lock-Up Restrictions

Upon Company IPO:
- Holder shares subject to standard IPO lock-up period [LOCKUP_MONTHS] months
- Holder required to execute lock-up agreement with Company and underwriters
- Lock-up released [LOCKUP_MONTHS] months after IPO

## 8. ADJUSTMENTS TO EXERCISE PRICE AND SHARES

### 8.1 Stock Splits

If Company declares stock split (e.g., 2:1 split):
- Number of warrant shares multiplied by split ratio
- Exercise price divided by split ratio
- Holder's economic position unchanged

**Example:**
- Before: 100,000 shares at $1.00
- 2:1 stock split
- After: 200,000 shares at $0.50
- Same total value: 200,000 × $0.50 = $100,000

### 8.2 Stock Dividends

If Company declares stock dividend:
- Warrant shares increase by dividend percentage
- Exercise price reduced pro-rata
- Similar to stock split economically

### 8.3 Recapitalizations

If Company recapitalizes (new share class, conversion, etc.):
- Warrant shares and exercise price adjusted to maintain economic equivalence
- Board determines adjustment methodology (with Holder input)

### 8.4 Combination of Outstanding Shares

If Company combines/consolidates shares (e.g., 2:1 reverse split):
- Warrant shares divided by combination ratio
- Exercise price multiplied by combination ratio
- Economically equivalent adjustment

## 9. TERMINATION AND EXPIRATION

### 9.1 Expiration Date

All unexercised warrants automatically **EXPIRE** on [EXPIRATION_DATE].

- After expiration, warrants have NO VALUE
- Holder has NO RIGHT to purchase shares
- Expired warrants cannot be exercised under any circumstances

### 9.2 Early Termination Events

Warrants terminate (without automatic exercise) upon:

1. **Failure to Achieve Liquidity**: If no liquidity event (IPO, acquisition, financing round) within [TERMINAL_EVENT_YEARS] years, warrants terminate worthless

2. **Bankruptcy**: Warrants converted to equity automatically OR terminate per bankruptcy court

3. **Cancellation**: Holder voluntarily cancels warrant via written notice

### 9.3 Termination on Acquisition

If Company acquired before warrant exercise:

**Option 1: Automatic Conversion**
- Warrants automatically convert to common stock immediately before acquisition
- Holder becomes shareholder in acquired company OR receives cash equivalent
- Exercise price becomes merger consideration

**Option 2: Assumed by Acquirer**
- Acquirer assumes warrant obligations
- Adjusted for any stock consideration issued in merger

**Option 3: Cash Settlement**
- Company pays Holder amount equal to (Acquisition Price - Exercise Price) × Warrant Shares
- Due at acquisition closing

**Most Common Approach:**
- Warrants automatically exercised (cashless) immediately before acquisition
- Acquisition price determines value

**Example:**
- Company acquired for $50M
- Warrants for 100,000 shares at $1.00 exercise price
- Just before acquisition, warrants exercised on cashless basis
- Exercise profit: 100,000 × ($50M/fully diluted shares - $1.00)
- Holder receives shares, then gets acquisition proceeds pro-rata

## 10. COMPANY OBLIGATIONS

### 10.1 Authorized Shares

Company shall maintain authorized but unissued shares sufficient to:
- Issue all warrant shares upon exercise
- Maintain buffer of additional shares (20% minimum)
- Accommodate warrant exercises without stock split

If authorized shares insufficient:
- Company shall amend certificate of incorporation immediately
- Hold shareholder vote if required
- All costs paid by Company (not Holder)

### 10.2 Financial Reporting

Company shall:
- Maintain books and records showing capitalization
- Provide annual update of fully-diluted capitalization
- Report warrant exercises and related transactions
- Provide cap table 10 days before any financing round

### 10.3 Securities Law Compliance

Company responsible for:
- Compliance with securities laws in warrant issuance and shares
- SEC rule compliance
- State blue-sky law compliance
- Private placement restrictions
- Legends on share certificate (if not registered)

### 10.4 No Adverse Actions

Company shall NOT:
- Redeem, call, or force exercise of warrants (except per terms)
- Take action to impair warrant holder rights
- Reclassify warrant shares without warrant holder approval
- Materially modify stock rights without warrant holder consent

### 10.5 Notices of Actions

Company shall provide notice within [NOTICE_DAYS] days of:
- Stock splits, dividends, recapitalizations
- Stock sales, financings, acquisitions
- Warrant exercises
- Changes in stock rights or preferences

## 11. REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES

### 11.1 Company Representations

Company represents and warrants:

**(a) Authority**
- Company authorized to issue warrants
- Warrant issuance does not violate law, certificates, or contracts
- All necessary approvals obtained

**(b) Capitalization**
- Cap table provided is accurate and complete
- No other warrants, options, or conversion rights omitted
- Fully diluted shares calculated correctly

**(c) No Conflicts**
- Warrant issuance does not violate any agreement
- No other party has conflicting rights to these shares
- No rights of first refusal or pre-emptive rights triggered

**(d) Valid Issuance**
- Company issuing shares from authorized reserves
- Shares will be validly issued, fully paid, non-assessable
- No legend required if demand registration obtained (or standard legend only)

### 11.2 Warrant Holder Representations

Holder represents:

**(a) Investment Qualification**
- Holder is accredited investor (if applicable)
- Holder capable of bearing risk of loss
- Holder reviewing at own expense

**(b) Investment Intent**
- Purchasing for own account
- Long-term investment intent
- Not for immediate resale

**(c) No Bad Actor**
- Holder not subject to "bad actor" disqualifications under Rule 506(d)
- No felony convictions, fraud, business sanctions

## WARRANT COVERAGE QUICK REFERENCE

| Factor | Impact on Coverage |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Early stage (pre-revenue)** | Higher coverage (25-30%) |
| **Revenue traction** | Lower coverage (10-15%) |
| **Multiple lender offers** | Lower coverage (founder leverage) |
| **High-risk profile** | Higher coverage (lender leverage) |
| **Series B+ company** | Lower coverage (5-10%) |
| **Strong unit economics** | Lower coverage (lower risk) |
| **Negative unit economics** | Higher coverage (higher risk) |
| **Founder track record** | Slightly lower coverage |
| **Market risk** | Higher coverage |
| **Proven business model** | Lower coverage |
