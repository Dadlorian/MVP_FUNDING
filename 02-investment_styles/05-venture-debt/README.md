# Venture Debt Templates & Guide

## What is Venture Debt?

Venture debt is **non-dilutive financing** provided by specialized lenders to early-stage and growth-stage companies. Unlike equity financing, it doesn't require giving up ownership or control. It's a loan that bridges cash needs between funding rounds.

### Key Characteristics:
- **Non-dilutive**: No equity or voting rights required
- **Structured as a loan**: Principal + interest repayment
- **Equity kickers**: Lenders often receive warrants (options to purchase equity)
- **Covenants**: Restrictive terms that protect lender interests
- **Shorter maturity**: Typically 3-5 years
- **Higher interest rates**: 10-15% annually (vs. 2-5% traditional loans)

---

## When to Use Venture Debt

### Ideal Scenarios:
- **Between fundraising rounds**: Bridge from Series A to Series B
- **Revenue-generating companies**: Have predictable cash flow
- **Expensive customer acquisition**: Need working capital
- **Operating company needs**: Inventory, equipment, payroll
- **Growth acceleration**: Speed up market expansion without dilution
- **Founder preservation**: Retain more equity for long-term ownership

### NOT Recommended:
- Early-stage pre-revenue companies
- Companies with negative or declining revenue
- Situations requiring immediate dramatic cash infusion (use equity)
- When covenant restrictions would harm business operations

## Key Venture Debt Terms

### 1. Interest Rate (10-15% annual)
- **Market range**: 8-16% depending on risk profile
- **Compounding**: Usually monthly
- **Early repayment**: Often has prepayment penalty (1-3% of principal)
- **Pricing factors**: Stage, revenue traction, team strength, market risks

### 2. Warrants (Equity Kickers)
- **What they are**: Options to purchase equity at fixed price
- **Coverage**: 10-30% of loan amount (e.g., $1M loan = warrants for 10-30% equity)
- **Pricing**: Usually at valuation of current or next funding round
- **Dilution impact**: Can significantly dilute founders if exercised

### 3. Covenants (Restrictions)
- **Financial covenants**: Minimum revenue, maximum burn rate, cash balance requirements
- **Operational covenants**: Board reporting, no asset sales without approval, no new debt
- **Negative covenants**: Can't acquire other companies, can't pay dividends, can't change business
- **Breach consequences**: Default triggers, acceleration of repayment

### 4. Maturity (3-5 years typical)
- **Repayment schedule**: Monthly, quarterly, or bullet payment at maturity
- **Amortization**: May defer principal for first 12-24 months (interest-only period)
- **Balloon payment**: Common to have 30-50% of principal due at maturity
- **Extension options**: Often have provisions for extension if performing

### 5. Collateral
- **Unsecured**: No specific collateral pledged (common for high-growth startups)
- **Secured**: Personal guarantees from founders, warrant coverage as security
- **Subordinated**: Secondary to other debt obligations

## FOUNDER RISKS

### 1. Debt Covenants (The Straightjacket)
**What founders lose:**
- **Operational flexibility**: Can't make hiring decisions, market pivots without lender approval
- **Cash control**: Minimum cash balance requirements may prevent necessary spending
- **Strategic decisions**: M&A, partnerships, or product pivots may breach covenants
- **Reporting burden**: Quarterly financial reporting and board reporting obligations

**Real impact:**
- Revenue covenant of $[MINIMUM_REVENUE_COVENANT] forces you to focus on specific metrics rather than long-term value
- Burn rate covenant means you can't invest in new product development if it increases burn
- Must get lender approval for hiring, acquisition, or strategic pivots

### 2. Personal Guarantees
**What's at risk:**
- **Personal liability**: Lender can pursue founders' personal assets if company defaults
- **Home and assets**: Can be pledged as collateral
- **No bankruptcy protection**: Even if company declares bankruptcy, personal guarantee remains
- **Impossible to escape**: Can't be released until loan fully repaid

**Real impact:**
- Founders personally liable for $[LOAN_AMOUNT] even if company is dissolved
- Lender can pursue founders' personal assets and bank accounts
- Affects founders' credit scores and personal credit availability

### 3. Default Consequences
**Triggers:**
- Missed interest or principal payment (even 1 day late)
- Breach of any covenant (missing revenue target, exceeding burn rate)
- Change in control without lender consent
- Insolvency or bankruptcy

**What happens:**
- **Acceleration**: Entire outstanding balance becomes immediately due
- **Enforcement**: Lender can seize assets, pursue personal guarantees
- **Interest increases**: Default interest rate often 5-10% higher
- **Forced sale**: Lender may force company sale at unfavorable terms
- **Equity dilution**: Forced exercise of warrants at unfavorable prices

**Worst case scenario:**
- Company forced into fire sale to repay debt
- Personal guarantees pursued against founders
- Founders liable for deficiency (amount still owed after asset sale)

### 4. Equity Kickers (Warrant Dilution)
**The hidden cost:**
- **Dilution of ownership**: Warrants dilute founder equity significantly
- **Pricing risk**: Warrants exercise at valuation from current/next round (could be much lower)
- **Double dilution**: Already diluted by funding round, then diluted more by warrant exercise
- **Control loss**: Dilution may give lender board seat or veto rights

**Real impact:**
- $1M loan with 20% warrant coverage = ~20% of future equity given to lender
- If next round at $10M valuation, warrants worth $2M
- Combined with founder dilution from equity round, could drop founder ownership to <30%

## INVESTOR (LENDER) RISKS

### 1. Default Without Collateral
**The problem:**
- **Unsecured claims**: Many venture debt deals unsecured
- **Low recovery**: In bankruptcy, venture debt is subordinated to secured debt
- **Personal guarantees weak**: Founders often lack sufficient personal assets
- **Asset depletion**: Startup assets deplete rapidly in failure scenario

**Real impact:**
- Lender may recover only 10-30% of outstanding balance
- Personal guarantees often uncollectible
- Subordinated to bank debt, landlord claims, employee wages

### 2. Subordination to Future Debt
**The hierarchy:**
- **Senior secured debt** (bank loans, equipment financing): Paid first
- **Venture debt** (unsecured or second position): Paid second
- **Equity**: Paid last (often gets nothing)

**Real impact:**
- If company needs additional financing, venture debt is subordinated
- New lenders have priority claim on assets
- Venture debt can become worthless if company takes larger secured loan

### 3. Startup Failure
**Outcome in business failure:**
- **Low recovery**: Often $0 recovery in startup failure
- **Time to recovery**: Takes years to recover anything in bankruptcy
- **Warrant dilution**: Warrants become worthless
- **Portfolio risk**: Need 7-8 successful exits to compensate for losses

**Real impact:**
- 30-40% of venture-backed companies fail
- Venture debt is last in line of claims
- Warrants worthless if company fails
- Lender needs very successful exits to maintain profitability

## PAIN POINTS & NEGOTIATION AREAS

### 1. Covenant Negotiations
**Sticking points:**
- **Revenue covenants** (minimum $[MINIMUM_REVENUE_COVENANT]/quarter)
  - *Founder argument*: Unpredictable, may need to pivot, kills flexibility
  - *Lender counter*: Need assurance of repayment capability
  - **Solution**: Exclude temporary revenue dips (if recovering), have covenant holiday

- **Burn rate caps** (max $[MAXIMUM_BURN_RATE]/month)
  - *Founder argument*: Prevents necessary growth investment
  - *Lender counter*: If burn increases, repayment ability decreases
  - **Solution**: Tiered covenants that relax as you raise more equity

- **Cash balance minimums** (maintain $[MINIMUM_CASH_BALANCE] at all times)
  - *Founder argument*: Restricts operations, can't deploy cash effectively
  - *Lender counter*: Need assurance cash is available for repayment
  - **Solution**: Lower requirement, or exclude operating cash reserves

### 2. Warrant Coverage Negotiations
**Key variables:**
- **Coverage percentage**: Negotiating from 20% down to 10-15%
- **Pricing mechanism**: Use current valuation vs. next round valuation
- **Exercise terms**: Acceleration of vesting on funding round, automatic exercise on exit

**Negotiation tactics:**
- *Founder offer*: Lower percentage if longer maturity or lower interest rate
- *Lender offer*: Increase coverage if lower revenue covenants or more flexibility
- *Compromise*: Tiered warrants - higher coverage if miss covenants, lower if exceed

### 3. Prepayment Penalties
**Founder desire:** Repay debt early to eliminate covenant restrictions
**Lender concern:** Loses interest income stream

**Typical terms:**
- **Years 1-2**: 3% prepayment penalty
- **Years 3-4**: 2% prepayment penalty
- **Year 5+**: 1% prepayment penalty
- **Acceleration events**: No penalty on IPO, but may have on acquisition

**Negotiation approach:**
- Request waiver if refinance with larger lender
- Request reduced penalty after performing for 12 months
- Request acceleration event to trigger lower/no prepayment

### 4. Personal Guarantees
**Founder position:** Wants to minimize personal exposure
**Lender position:** Wants personal recourse if company fails

**Negotiation points:**
- *Capped guarantee*: Limit personal guarantee to % of loan (e.g., 50% instead of 100%)
- *Joint guarantee*: Split guarantee among multiple founders instead of joint
- *Release provisions*: Guarantee released when loan paid to certain amount (e.g., 75% repaid)
- *Size threshold*: No personal guarantee if raising Series B+ from top-tier VCs

## Venture Debt vs. Traditional Bank Loans

| Factor | Venture Debt | Traditional Loan |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Interest Rate** | 10-15% | 4-8% |
| **Collateral Required** | Minimal/warrants | Significant (real estate, assets) |
| **Covenants** | Restrictive (revenue, burn) | Conservative (financial metrics) |
| **Eligibility** | Revenue-generating startups | Established businesses with assets |
| **Maturity** | 3-5 years | 5-10 years |
| **Equity Kickers** | Warrants standard | None |
| **Flexibility** | Somewhat restrictive | Very restrictive |
| **Speed to close** | 4-8 weeks | 3-6 months |
| **Lender type** | Specialized funds (Silicon Valley Bank, Horizon, Gold Hill) | Banks and credit unions |
| **Default recovery** | Poor (unsecured) | Better (secured by assets) |
| **Best for** | High-growth startups with revenue | Established businesses, real estate |

## Next Steps for Founders

### Before Pursuing Venture Debt:
1. **Revenue traction**: Minimum $[MINIMUM_REVENUE_THRESHOLD]/month recommended
2. **Cash runway**: At least 12-18 months of runway remaining
3. **Fundraising timeline**: Clear path to next funding round (typically 12-18 months)
4. **Covenant capacity**: Can realistically meet proposed covenants for 12+ months

### Due Diligence:
1. Get 3-5 term sheets from different lenders
2. Have lawyer review all documents (especially covenants and guarantees)
3. Model covenant compliance over 24 months (conservative projections)
4. Understand full cost: interest + warrants + covenant restrictions
5. Get founder personal guarantee insurance (if available)

### Negotiation Strategy:
1. **Lead with metrics**: Strong traction gives leverage
2. **Get multiple offers**: Competition among lenders
3. **Trade-offs**: Higher interest for lower covenants, higher covenants for lower warrants
4. **Professional representation**: Use startup-savvy lawyers (not general counsel)

## Key Takeaway for Founders

Venture debt is a **powerful tool for non-dilutive growth**, but it's not free. The true cost includes:
- **Interest payments** (10-15% annually)
- **Warrant dilution** (10-30% equity given away)
- **Covenant restrictions** (operational flexibility lost)
- **Personal exposure** (personal guarantees)
- **Default risk** (severe consequences)

**Use it strategically**: Most effective when you have:
1. Clear path to profitability or next funding round
2. Predictable revenue and manageable burn rate
3. Ability to confidently meet covenants
4. Specific use of proceeds (not just runway extension)
