## Federal SBIR/STTR Programs (11 Agencies)

### 1. National Science Foundation (NSF) SBIR/STTR
**Website:** https://sbir.nih.gov
**Contact:** [info@sbir.nih.gov]
**Focus:** Innovation across all sectors (deep tech, AI, biotech, climate, etc.)
**Phase I:** $150,000-$225,000 (6 months)
**Phase II:** $500,000-$750,000 (2 years)
**Phase III:** Non-SBIR funded (commercialization)
**Key Features:**
- Highest overall success rate (15-20%)
- Emphasis on commercialization from Phase I
- Fast review timeline (4-6 months Phase I)
- Open solicitations (ongoing)
**Solicitation Topics:** Listed on sbir.nih.gov - covers AI/ML, biotech, climate, manufacturing, etc.

### 2. Department of Energy (DOE) SBIR/STTR

## Application Timeline & Strategy

### Recommended Multi-Grant Strategy

#### Month 1-2: Research & Planning
- [ ] Identify 5-10 grant opportunities matching your profile
- [ ] Assess eligibility for each program
- [ ] Research sample applications and success stories
- [ ] Estimate time/resource investment for each

#### Month 3-4: Pipeline Development
- [ ] Start SBIR application (Phase I if first-time)
- [ ] Submit 1-2 corporate grant applications
- [ ] Research state matching programs
- [ ] Identify competition opportunities

#### Month 5-6: Parallel Submission
- [ ] Submit SBIR application
- [ ] Complete corporate grant applications
- [ ] Enter 1-2 pitch competitions
- [ ] Apply for state/local programs

#### Month 7-12: Review & Follow-up
- [ ] Monitor decision timelines
- [ ] Begin Phase II planning (if Phase I progress)
- [ ] Apply for additional rounds
- [ ] Document lessons learned

### Success Rate Optimization

**Diversify Across Types:**
- 1-2 SBIR applications (10-20% success rate)
- 2-3 corporate grants (20-30% success rate)
- 1-2 competitions (5-10% success rate)
- 1-2 foundation grants (15-25% success rate)

**Expected Outcomes:**
- 50% of applications should receive funding
- Typical success in 1-2 of 4-5 applications
- Expected funding: $100K-$500K over 12 months
