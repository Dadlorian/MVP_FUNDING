## 2. SUBORDINATION - PAYMENT PRIORITY

### 2.1 Subordination of Convertible Note
The Noteholder subordinates the Convertible Note Debt to the Senior Debt. This means:

**Payment Priority Hierarchy:**
```
TIER 1 (Paid First):    Employees (wages)
TIER 2 (Paid Second):   Certain government claims (taxes)
TIER 3 (Paid Third):    Senior Debt (bank loan)
TIER 4 (Paid Fourth):   This Convertible Note (SUBORDINATE)
TIER 5 (Paid Last):     Equity shareholders
```

### 2.2 Payment Restrictions

**The Company shall not make any payments on the Convertible Note unless:**

(a) **No Default:** The Company is not in default under Senior Debt (and no default has occurred and is continuing);

(b) **No Acceleration:** Senior Debt has not been accelerated;

(c) **Cash Flow Test:** The Company maintains minimum cash balance of $[PLACEHOLDER: Amount]] after making such payment;

(d) **Senior Creditor Consent:** Senior Creditor has not objected in writing within [PLACEHOLDER: 10]] days of receiving payment notice.

**Example of Restricted Payment:**
```
Company cash flow: $50,000/month
Senior Debt payment: $40,000/month
Convertible Note payment: $10,000/month

Payment must be made in order:
1. First: Senior Debt $40,000 (mandatory)
2. Second: Operating expenses (mandatory)
3. Third: Convertible Note $10,000 (if cash remains)
4. Fourth: Equity dividends (prohibited while subordinated)

If only $45,000 available:
- Senior Creditor gets $40,000
- Operating expenses paid from remaining $5,000
- Convertible Note gets $0 (insufficient cash)
```

### 2.3 No Prepayment of Convertible Note
The Company shall not prepay, refinance, or refinance the Convertible Note without:
- (a) Senior Debt fully paid, OR
- (b) Senior Creditor written consent

This prevents the Company from paying early investors at expense of senior lenders.

## 3. IMPACT ON CONVERTIBLE NOTE CONVERSION

### 3.1 Conversion Not Affected
Subordination does NOT impact the Convertible Note's conversion rights. Upon a Qualifying Financing:

(a) **Automatic Conversion:** The Convertible Note converts to equity per its terms;

(b) **No Senior Debt Approval:** Senior Debt conversion does not require Senior Creditor approval;

(c) **Equity Treatment:** Upon conversion, Noteholder becomes Common Stockholder with standard equity rights.

### 3.2 Senior Debt After Conversion
After Convertible Note converts to equity:

(a) **Debt Eliminated:** The Convertible Note debt is eliminated (converted to equity);

(b) **Senior Debt Remains:** Senior Debt continues as Company obligation;

(c) **Equity Holder Status:** Former Noteholder is now equity holder with rights junior to Senior Debt.

### 3.3 Mandatory Conversion Scenario

**If Series A Financing Occurs:**
```
Pre-Conversion:
  Senior Debt: $500,000 (bank loan)
  Convertible Note: $300,000

Series A Financing at $10M valuation:
  Note converts to equity (eliminates Note as debt)
  Investor becomes Common Stockholder

Post-Conversion:
  Senior Debt: $500,000 (still owed to bank)
  Investor equity: 5% (approximate ownership)
  Senior Debt still junior only to employees/taxes
  Investor has equity rights but subordinated to future preferred stock
```

## 4. BANKRUPTCY AND LIQUIDATION

### 4.1 Bankruptcy Waterfall
If Company enters bankruptcy or liquidation, assets shall be distributed in order:

```
Priority 1:  Administrative expenses and costs
Priority 2:  Employee wages (up to statutory limits)
Priority 3:  Certain taxes and government claims
Priority 4:  SENIOR DEBT - paid in full before...
Priority 5:  Convertible Note - paid in full before...
Priority 6:  Other unsecured debt (vendors, etc.)
Priority 7:  Equity shareholders - get whatever remains
```

### 4.2 Liquidation Example

**Scenario: Company Liquidates**
```
Liquidation Assets: $600,000

Waterfall:
  Employee claims (wages):      ($150,000)
  Bankruptcy administration:    ($50,000)
  ────────────────────────────
  Available for creditors:      $400,000

  Senior Debt ($500,000):       Paid $400,000 (shortfall: $100,000)
  Convertible Note ($300,000):  Paid $0 (no funds remain)
  Equity holders:               Paid $0 (no funds remain)

Noteholder Recovery: $0 out of $300,000 invested (0% recovery)
Senior Creditor Recovery: $400,000 out of $500,000 (80% recovery)
```

### 4.3 Bankruptcy Court Orders
If bankruptcy court orders otherwise, those court orders control. This Agreement does not override bankruptcy law.

## 5. NOTEHOLDER'S ACKNOWLEDGMENT

### 5.1 Risks of Subordination
The Noteholder acknowledges understanding the following risks:

**Risk 1: Junior Priority**
- In bankruptcy/liquidation, Noteholder is paid AFTER Senior Creditor
- If insufficient assets, Noteholder may recover $0

**Risk 2: Payment Blockage**
- Noteholder cannot receive principal or interest payments if Senior Debt in default
- Can create extended periods with no cash received

**Risk 3: Senior Creditor Decisions**
- Senior Creditor can accelerate Senior Debt, triggering payment freeze
- Noteholder has limited control over Senior Creditor actions

**Risk 4: Refinancing Risk**
- Senior Debt may be refinanced in ways adverse to Noteholder
- Noteholder cannot prepay Note without Senior Creditor consent

**Risk 5: Conversion Uncertainty**
- If Company cannot obtain Series A due to Senior Debt burden, Note may never convert
- Maturity date may arrive without conversion pathway

### 5.2 Noteholder Consent
By executing this Agreement, Noteholder consents to:

- Subordination of the Convertible Note to Senior Debt
- Payment restrictions described in Section 2.2
- Priority of Senior Debt in bankruptcy/liquidation
- Terms and conditions hereof

(b) **Notify Noteholder:** Promptly notify Noteholder of:
    - Any default or potential default under Senior Debt
    - Any acceleration of Senior Debt
    - Any modification to Senior Debt terms
    - Any intent to refinance or accelerate Senior Debt

(c) **Preserve Collateral:** Maintain and preserve any collateral securing Senior Debt;

(d) **Comply with Covenants:** Comply with all covenants in Senior Debt documents;

(e) **Not Amend Without Consent:** Not amend Convertible Note in a way that affects Senior Debt priority without Senior Creditor consent.

### 7.2 Company Shall Not

The Company shall not:

(a) **Create Other Senior Debt:** Issue additional Senior Debt without consent of existing Senior Creditor and Noteholder;

(b) **Subordinate Senior Debt:** Agree to subordinate Senior Debt to any other creditor without Noteholder consent;

(c) **Reduce Collateral:** Dispose of or encumber collateral securing Senior Debt;

(d) **Misrepresent:** Misrepresent Senior Debt status to any party.

## 14. SPECIFIC SCENARIOS

### 14.1 What Happens If Senior Debt is Paid Off

**Scenario: Bank Loan is Repaid**

```
Current Status:
  Senior Debt (bank): $500,000
  Convertible Note: $300,000

Company Decision: Use operations cash to pay off bank loan

Sequence:
  1. Company repays bank $500,000 (Senior Debt eliminated)
  2. Subordination Agreement automatically terminates
  3. Convertible Note is now senior to all other debt
  4. Noteholder can receive payments without restriction
  5. Company can refinance or extend Convertible Note without bank approval

Key Point: Once Senior Debt gone, Noteholder's subordination ends
```

### 14.2 What Happens If Series A Occurs

**Scenario: Company Raises Series A**

```
Current Status:
  Senior Debt: $500,000
  Convertible Note: $300,000 (~18 months, interest accrued = $320K)

Series A Financing: Raises $5M at $15M post-money valuation

Closing:
  1. Series A closes (investor puts in $5M)
  2. Convertible Note automatically converts to equity
  3. Note Principal + Interest ($320K) / Conversion Price = Shares issued
  4. Noteholder becomes Common Shareholder
  5. Series A investors buy Preferred Stock (senior to Common)

Capital Structure After Series A:
  Senior Debt: $500,000 (still owed to bank, still senior)
  Series A Preferred: $5M (now controls company)
  Common Stock: includes former Noteholder

Waterfall After Series A:
  Tier 1: Employee wages
  Tier 2: Bank debt ($500K)
  Tier 3: Series A Preferred dividend/liquidation pref
  Tier 4: Common Stock (including former Noteholder)

Key Point: Subordination to bank remains even after conversion
           Because Noteholder becomes Common (equity)
           Which is junior to Preferred (equity)
```

### 14.3 What Happens If Default on Senior Debt

**Scenario: Company Defaults on Bank Loan**

```
Situation: Company misses bank loan payment

Sequence:
  1. Bank notices payment default
  2. Bank sends notice to Company (per loan docs)
  3. Company must notify Noteholder (per Subordination Agreement Section 7.1)
  4. If not cured within grace period (typically 10-30 days per loan docs)
     Bank can declare default and accelerate loan

Effect on Convertible Note:
  1. Company cannot make payments on Convertible Note (Section 2.2)
  2. Interest accrues on Note but not paid to Noteholder
  3. Note reaches maturity with high accrued interest and unpaid principal
  4. If Company cannot raise Series A, maturity default occurs
  5. Noteholder now subordinate to bank with no repayment options

Example Timeline:
  Month 1: Bank loan default
  Months 1-12: Bank and Company negotiate/workout
  Month 13: Company forced sale by bank or bankruptcy
  Noteholder receives: Proceeds after bank paid in full

Risk: High risk if Senior Debt in trouble
```

### 14.4 What Happens in Series A with Valuation Cap

**Scenario: Subordination Interacts with Conversion Cap**

```
Convertible Note Terms:
  Principal: $300,000
  Interest: 5% annually
  Cap: $5,000,000
  Discount: 20%
  Maturity: 3 years

After 2.5 years:
  Accrued Interest: $75,000
  Total to convert: $375,000

Series A Financing:
  Valuation: $20,000,000
  Price per share (non-discounted): $20

Conversion Mechanics:
  Cap price = $5M / 1,000,000 shares = $5/share
  Discount price = $20 × (1 - 0.20) = $16/share
  Uses lower: $5/share

  Shares issued = $375,000 / $5 = 75,000 shares

Effect of Subordination:
  - Subordination does NOT affect conversion price calculation
  - Conversion still occurs per Note terms
  - Investor converts to equity successfully
  - BUT: Series A investors may require additional subordination
         of the converted equity (Common Stock) to new Preferred Stock

Key Point: Subordination helps with conversion by improving Series A
           likelihood (bank debt reduced when Note converts to equity)
```

## NOTES FOR USERS

**When is this document needed?**
- Company has bank debt AND convertible note
- Want to clarify that bank debt has priority
- Bank requires subordination agreement as condition of loan
- Noteholder wants clarity on their priority position

**Key Business Points:**
1. Bank debt is paid first (most cases)
2. Noteholder cannot get paid until bank debt paid
3. Conversion unaffected (Note still converts at maturity/Series A)
4. Protects bank's position
5. Establishes clear waterfall if company fails

**Typical Negotiation:**
- Bank requires this (non-negotiable)
- Noteholder negotiates: clear payment rights, notice rights, and conversion rights
- Company wants: simple language, clear metrics for "default"

**Common Problems:**
1. Ambiguous definition of "Senior Debt" - causes disputes
2. Too restrictive payment tests - Company can't function
3. Vague notice provisions - unclear when Noteholder informed
4. No conversion clarity - unclear if subordination affects equity conversion

**Best Practices:**
- Define "Senior Debt" precisely (amount, terms, lender)
- Set clear payment tests (e.g., "if $X cash remains after operations")
- Specify notice requirements (email, mail, phone)
- Clarify that subordination does NOT affect conversion to equity
- Include specific scenarios (what if Series A, what if default)
- Have bank and Noteholder sign same document (clarity)
