# Convertible Notes vs SAFEs: Comprehensive Comparison

## Executive Summary

When raising seed capital, founders must choose between **Convertible Notes** and **SAFEs (Simple Agreements for Future Equity)**. These are fundamentally different instruments with distinct legal, financial, and strategic implications.

| Aspect | Convertible Note | SAFE |
|--------|------------------|------|
| **Instrument Type** | Debt | Contractual right (not debt) |
| **Maturity Date** | Yes (2-5 years) | No maturity |
| **Interest Rate** | Yes (3-8% typical) | No interest |
| **Repayment Obligation** | Yes - legal debt | No - only converts |
| **Balance Sheet Impact** | Liability | Off-balance sheet |
| **Investor Protection** | Higher (debt seniority) | Lower (equity risk only) |
| **Governance Rights** | Possible (information, covenants) | Limited |
| **Valuation Cap** | Yes | Yes |
| **Conversion Discount** | Yes | Yes |
| **Preferred Stock** | Converts to common | Can convert to preferred |
| **Legal Complexity** | Higher | Lower |
| **Founder Friendly** | Less friendly | More founder-friendly |

---

## Detailed Comparison

### 1. DEBT STATUS: The Fundamental Difference

#### Convertible Note = DEBT

A convertible note is a **promissory note** - a legally binding debt obligation.

```
Legal Structure:
├─ Company borrows money from investor
├─ Creates liability on balance sheet
├─ Company OWES this money
├─ Must repay principal + interest if not converted
└─ Investor is creditor (not shareholder)

Accounting Treatment:
  Balance Sheet Classification: LIABILITY
  Debt-to-Equity Ratio: INCREASES (worsens)
  Financial Covenants: May trigger existing loan covenants
```

**Founder Perspective:**
- Shows up as "Convertible Note Payable" on liabilities
- Impacts debt-to-equity ratio negatively
- Reduces borrowing capacity with banks
- Must be disclosed to lenders/investors

**Investor Perspective:**
- You are technically a creditor
- Have claim on assets in bankruptcy (before equity holders)
- Company must honor repayment obligation
- Creates leverage for negotiation

---

#### SAFE = CONTRACTUAL RIGHT (Not Debt)

A SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity) is a **contractual agreement to convert capital into equity in the future**. It is NOT a debt instrument.

```
Legal Structure:
├─ Investor provides capital
├─ Company records as cash (not liability)
├─ Company receives cash; SAFE describes future equity rights
├─ No repayment obligation
├─ Investor is not creditor
└─ Agreement only converts to equity at trigger event

Accounting Treatment:
  Balance Sheet Classification: EQUITY (or memorandum)
  Debt-to-Equity Ratio: UNAFFECTED or IMPROVES
  Financial Covenants: Does NOT trigger existing debt covenants
```

**Founder Perspective:**
- No liability on balance sheet
- Cleaner financial statements
- Does not impact debt ratios
- Can't be "called" for repayment

**Investor Perspective:**
- You are not a creditor
- Have no claim in bankruptcy vs debt holders
- Company can never "owe" you money
- Pure equity-like instrument (pre-Series A)

---

### 2. MATURITY DATE: The Time Pressure Point

#### Convertible Note Has Maturity Date

A convertible note specifies a maturity date (typically 2-5 years) by which time:
- Note must convert to equity, OR
- Company must repay principal + interest

```
Timeline Example:
Year 0: Raise $500K convertible note, maturity Year 3
Year 1-2: Company grows
Year 3.0: MATURITY DATE ARRIVES

Company must choose:
  A) Repay $500K+ (plus interest) - immediate cash burden
  B) Convert to equity - automatic if Series A happened
  C) Extend note - requires renegotiation, often unfavorable to founder
  D) Default - breach of contract, legal liability

Reality: Most convertible notes either:
  - Convert to Series A equity (expected path)
  - Get extended with increased interest (founder stressed)
  - Never convert (company fails or M&A at bad terms)
```

**Pain Point: "Forced Funding"**
```
Scenario: Maturity arrives but Series A not closed

Year 3.0: $500K note matures
Company cash on hand: $200K
Burn rate: $50K/month
Company needs funds but has no Series A

Founder options:
  1) Repay $500K with $200K cash (IMPOSSIBLE)
  2) Take bridge financing (adds more debt)
  3) Sell company to repay (forced M&A)
  4) Extend note (requires investor negotiation)
  5) Default (lawsuit, credit damage, loss of trust)

This is a real crisis point that founders face
```

#### SAFE Has NO Maturity Date

SAFEs never mature. They exist in a perpetual "waiting" state until a conversion trigger occurs.

```
Timeline Example:
Year 0: Raise $500K SAFE (no maturity)
Year 1-2: Company grows
Year 3.0: Still no maturity date pressure
Year 4.0: Still convertible, no deadline
Year 5.0: Can eventually convert when ready

No time pressure - SAFE sits on cap table until:
  - Series A or later priced round (converts)
  - M&A (SAFE holders get pro-rata proceeds)
  - Liquidation (last to receive anything)
  - Mutual agreement (rare)
```

**Advantage for Founders:**
- No forced deadline
- Can focus on business growth
- No pressure to take unfavorable Series A just to convert notes
- Time to build genuine traction

**Disadvantage for Investors:**
- Indefinite wait for equity
- Company might stay private forever (never converts)
- Returns locked up indefinitely
- No mechanism to force conversion

### 3. INTEREST ACCRUAL: The Dilution Multiplier

#### Convertible Note Interest

Convertible notes accrue interest at a specified rate (typically 3-8% per annum).

```
Mechanics:
Interest accumulates on principal whether or not company is profitable

Example: $500K @ 8% simple interest over 3 years

Timeline:
Year 1: Principal $500K + Interest $40K = $540K accrued
Year 2: Principal $500K + Interest $80K = $580K accrued
Year 3: Principal $500K + Interest $120K = $620K accrued

Conversion Result:
At Series A, investor converts $620K (not $500K)
This dilutes founder equity by 24% vs immediate conversion
```

**Compound Interest Risk:**
```
Some notes use compound interest (less common):

$500K @ 8% compound over 3 years:
Year 1: $540K ($500K × 1.08)
Year 2: $583K ($540K × 1.08)
Year 3: $630K ($583K × 1.08)

Worst case: 26% total dilution vs founder's 24%
```

**Impact on Founder:**
- More equity dilution
- Incentivizes faster Series A (before more interest accrues)
- Interest keeps accruing even if company unprofitable
- Creates urgency: every month delay costs more equity

**Impact on Investor:**
- Protects downside if company doesn't convert
- Rewards patience (extra shares from interest)
- Makes notes attractive for risk/reward

---

#### SAFE Has NO Interest

SAFEs never accrue interest. You receive no payment for waiting.

```
Mechanics:
$500K SAFE raised in Year 0

Year 1: $500K → converts based on Year 1 price
Year 2: $500K → converts based on Year 2 price
Year 3: $500K → converts based on Year 3 price

No accrual, no interest, no time premium
You waited 3 years but get exactly what you invested
```

**Impact on Founder:**
- Equity dilution is stable (doesn't increase with time)
- No incentive pressure to rush Series A
- Can build business at natural pace
- Lower total equity given away

**Impact on Investor:**
- No interest return if things don't convert
- Pure equity play - only profit if company succeeds
- Waiting provides no compensation
- Must believe in upside, not time value

---

### 4. REPAYMENT OBLIGATION: The Forced Payment Risk

#### Convertible Note: Mandatory Repayment

If maturity date arrives and note hasn't converted, the company MUST repay.

```
Legal Status: BINDING DEBT OBLIGATION
If company can't repay:
  - Breach of contract
  - Investor can sue
  - Can trigger bankruptcy proceedings
  - Can demand forced sale/M&A
  - Creates lien on company assets

Example Crisis:
Company raises $1M in convertible notes, maturity Year 3
By Year 3: Company has only $300K cash
Notes come due: Must repay $1.1M (principal + interest)

Founder faces:
  ✗ Bankruptcy
  ✗ Personal guarantee liability (if signed)
  ✗ Investor lawsuits
  ✗ Forced asset sales
  ✗ Forced M&A at unfavorable terms
```

**This is a real risk many founders underestimate**

#### SAFE: No Repayment Obligation

SAFEs can never require repayment. There is no obligation to pay back cash.

```
Legal Status: CONVERTIBLE AGREEMENT (NOT DEBT)
If company can't repay (doesn't apply):
  - No breach of contract
  - No lawsuits
  - No bankruptcy trigger
  - No forced sale
  - SAFE simply sits on cap table

What happens instead:
  - SAFE converts at some future trigger
  - Or stays unconverted if no trigger occurs
  - Or converts in M&A/liquidation scenario
```

**Founder Advantage:**
- Zero repayment pressure
- Can raise pure capital without debt burden
- Can't go bankrupt from SAFE obligations
- Maximum financial flexibility

**Investor Disadvantage:**
- Cannot force repayment
- Stuck as equity-like instrument
- No debt claim in bankruptcy
- Only exits through company success

### 5. BALANCE SHEET IMPACT: Financial Statement Effects

#### Convertible Note Impact

Convertible notes appear as **Liabilities** on the balance sheet.

```
Balance Sheet - With Convertible Notes:

ASSETS:
  Cash: $500K (from note proceeds)

LIABILITIES:
  Convertible Note Payable: $500K ← Shows up here
  (Accounts Payable, Accrued Interest, etc.)

EQUITY:
  Common Stock
  Retained Earnings/(Deficit)

Financial Ratio Impact:
Debt-to-Equity Ratio WORSENS:
  Before Note: $100K debt / $400K equity = 0.25x
  After Note: $600K debt / $400K equity = 1.5x ← Worse!
```

**Consequences:**
1. **Bank Lending**: May violate covenants if you have other debt
2. **Credit Rating**: Improves if you appear riskier
3. **Valuation**: Investors discount enterprise value (more liability)
4. **Auditor Issues**: Auditors scrutinize classification
5. **Tax Implications**: Possible tax complications on conversion

---

#### SAFE Impact

SAFEs typically appear as a **memorandum item** or in equity section (depending on accounting choice).

```
Balance Sheet - With SAFE:

ASSETS:
  Cash: $500K (from SAFE proceeds)

LIABILITIES:
  (No liability - SAFE is not debt) ← Key difference

EQUITY:
  Common Stock
  Retained Earnings
  Convertible SAFEs (memorandum) ← Listed here or separately

Financial Ratio Impact:
Debt-to-Equity Ratio UNAFFECTED:
  Before SAFE: $100K debt / $400K equity = 0.25x
  After SAFE: $100K debt / $400K equity = 0.25x ← No change!
```

**Consequences:**
1. **Bank Lending**: Doesn't worsen debt covenants
2. **Credit Rating**: No negative impact
3. **Valuation**: No enterprise value impact (off balance sheet)
4. **Audit Clean**: Simpler accounting treatment
5. **Tax Neutral**: No tax complications on conversion

**This is a major advantage for founders with existing debt**

### 6. GOVERNANCE & CONTROL RIGHTS

#### Convertible Note Rights

Convertible notes often include governance provisions that give investors control rights.

```
Common Governance Provisions:

1. Information Rights:
   ✓ Quarterly financial statements
   ✓ Annual audited financials
   ✓ Board observation rights
   ✓ Investor updates/calls

2. Protective Provisions:
   ✗ Cannot incur additional debt > $X
   ✗ Cannot declare dividends
   ✗ Cannot change capital structure
   ✗ Cannot liquidate/sell company
   ✗ Cannot change key executive comp
   ✗ Cannot enter related-party transactions

3. Control Mechanics:
   - Require noteholder approval for major decisions
   - Can slow founder decision-making
   - Creates founder-investor friction

Example Restriction:
  "Company shall not incur indebtedness exceeding $250K
   without written consent of Noteholders owning >50% of principal"
```

**Impact on Founder:**
- Must get investor permission for major moves
- Can't pivot strategy quickly
- Quarterly reporting burden (admin time)
- Investor can block decisions

**Impact on Investor:**
- Participation in important decisions
- Early warning system for trouble
- Ability to defend investment

#### SAFE Rights

SAFEs typically include MINIMAL governance provisions.

```
Typical SAFE Rights:

1. Information Rights:
   ✓ Minimal (no quarterly reporting required)
   ✓ Optional: annual financial statements
   ✓ No board observation rights
   ✓ No investor calls

2. Protective Provisions:
   ✗ None typically
   ✗ Company can do whatever it wants
   ✗ No restrictions on founder actions
   ✗ No approval requirements

3. Control Mechanics:
   - Founder has 100% control
   - SAFE is purely passive
   - Investor has limited visibility

Typical SAFE Language:
  "SAFE holder has no voting rights, no board seat, and no
   approval rights over company decisions prior to conversion."
```

**Impact on Founder:**
- Maximum operational freedom
- No investor approval needed
- Can pivot, hire, spend as needed
- Complete founder autonomy

**Impact on Investor:**
- Limited visibility
- No control over decisions
- Can't stop bad founder choices
- Pure passive investment

### 7. CONVERSION MECHANICS

Both convertible notes and SAFEs have similar conversion triggers, but different mechanics.

#### Conversion Triggers (Similar for Both)

```
Typical Conversion Events:

1. Qualified Financing (Most Common)
   - Series A priced equity round occurs
   - Investor leads with professional VC
   - Automatic conversion to Series A preferred stock

2. Liquidity Event
   - Company acquisition/M&A
   - IPO (rare)
   - SAFE/Note holders get pro-rata proceeds

3. Maturity (Convertible Note Only)
   - Note reaches maturity date
   - Converts to equity or repayment triggered
   - SAFE has no maturity

4. Mutual Agreement
   - Founder and investor agree to convert anytime
   - Can happen at any time (rare)
```

#### Conversion Price Calculation (Key Difference)

**Convertible Note:**
```
Conversion mechanics typically:
  - Apply discount to Series A price (15-30% typical)
  - Cap valuation (limits conversion price)
  - Whichever is MORE FAVORABLE to investor is used

Example:
Series A at $10M valuation, $10/share price
Note has 20% discount and $5M cap

Calculation:
  Option 1 (Discount): $10 × (1 - 0.20) = $8/share
  Option 2 (Cap): Use $5M valuation = $?/share

If $5M cap gives price < $8/share, use cap (investor better off)
If discount gives price < cap-derived price, use discount
```

**SAFE:**
```
Conversion mechanics similar but:
  - Standard Y Combinator form uses caps + discounts
  - Post-money SAFE converts to preferred stock directly
  - Discount typically 20% (not negotiated much)
  - Valuation cap standard (typically $X based on seed round)

SAFE Conversion is simpler:
  Standard 20% discount applied uniformly
  Valuation cap applies uniformly
  Less negotiation around mechanics
```

### 8. INVESTOR PROTECTION LEVELS

#### Convertible Note Protection

Convertible notes offer MORE investor protection through multiple mechanisms:

```
Protection Layers:

1. Debt Status
   ✓ Creditor position (even if junior)
   ✓ Claim on assets in liquidation
   ✓ Seniority over equity holders
   ✓ Can force legal action

2. Interest Accrual
   ✓ Returns even if not converted
   ✓ Builds equity stake automatically
   ✓ Compensates for waiting

3. Conversion Advantages
   ✓ Discount on Series A price
   ✓ Valuation cap protection
   ✓ MFN clause (if better terms offered)

4. Governance Rights
   ✓ Information visibility
   ✓ Protective provisions (limits bad decisions)
   ✓ Approval rights on key actions

Total Protection: MULTI-LAYERED
```

**Example:**
```
$100K convertible note investment provides:
  + Interest (3% = $3K/year return if repaid)
  + Discount (20% better price than Series A)
  + Cap protection (max valuation applies)
  + Information rights (know what's happening)
  + Protective covenants (no bad dilution)
  + Potential approval rights (influence decisions)
```

#### SAFE Protection

SAFEs offer LESS investor protection - basically only discount + cap:

```
Protection Layers:

1. Contractual Right
   ✗ Not a creditor (no seniority)
   ✗ No claim on assets in liquidation
   ✗ Equity holders get paid first (bad for you)
   ✗ Cannot force legal action for repayment

2. No Interest Accrual
   ✗ Zero return if not converted
   ✗ Time value of money not compensated
   ✗ Waiting provides no benefit

3. Conversion Advantages
   ✓ Discount on Series A price (20% typical)
   ✓ Valuation cap (if included in terms)
   ✗ Limited negotiation room

4. Governance Rights
   ✗ NO information rights typically
   ✗ NO protective provisions
   ✗ NO approval rights
   ✗ Completely passive

Total Protection: MINIMAL
```

**Example:**
```
$100K SAFE investment provides:
  + Discount (20% better price than Series A)
  + Cap protection (max valuation applies)
  - No interest return
  - No information rights
  - No protective covenants
  - No influence over decisions
```

**This is why SAFEs are more founder-friendly**

### 9. VALUATION CAP: How It Works

Both convertible notes and SAFEs use valuation caps, but they work the same way:

#### Valuation Cap Purpose

```
A valuation cap LIMITS how high the conversion valuation can go.

Scenario: You invest in early seed stage with $5M cap

Outcome 1 - Company doesn't grow well:
  Series A valuation: $8M
  Your cap applies: You convert at $5M price
  Benefit: You get more shares (5/8 = 62.5% of normal shares)

Outcome 2 - Company grows really well:
  Series A valuation: $20M
  Your cap applies: You convert at $5M price (not $20M)
  Benefit: You get 4x more shares than non-capped investor
  Result: 5/20 = 25% of shares vs your 5/5 = 100% of seed round
```

#### Cap Setting: Critical Decision

```
Too Low Cap (e.g., $2M on $1M invested):
  ✓ Founder: More investor shares (more dilution)
  ✗ Investor: Unrealistic - company already doing well
  Result: Investor unfairly protected, founder over-diluted

Reasonable Cap (e.g., $5-10M on $500K invested):
  ✓ Founder: Sustainable dilution
  ✓ Investor: Meaningful protection if company grows
  ✓ Investor: Understands company could grow beyond cap
  Result: Balanced risk/reward

High Cap (e.g., $30M on $500K invested):
  ✗ Founder: Cap provides almost no protection
  ✗ Investor: Why have cap at all?
  Result: Cap is meaningless
```

**Market Standards:**
```
Seed Round Cap: 3-5x the investment amount
  Example: Invest $500K, cap at $2-2.5M

Series A SAFE Cap: 5-8x the seed round
  Example: $500K seed with $2.5M cap, new SAFE at $5-8M

Cap Negotiation:
  Founder wants: Higher cap (less dilution)
  Investor wants: Lower cap (more protection)
  Market typically: 4-5x investment
```

### 10. TAX IMPLICATIONS

#### Convertible Note Tax Treatment

```
For Founders:
  - Interest accrual is tax-deductible expense (good!)
  - Conversion triggers no immediate tax (good)
  - Repayment is not taxable event (good)

For Investors:
  - Interest received is ordinary income (bad - taxed higher)
  - Discount on conversion is not taxable (good)
  - Investment loss if company fails is capital loss (mediocre)

Accounting:
  - Interest expense flows through P&L
  - Liability grows on balance sheet
  - More accounting complexity
```

#### SAFE Tax Treatment

```
For Founders:
  - No interest expense (no tax benefit, but no burden)
  - Conversion triggers no immediate tax (good)
  - Cleaner accounting treatment

For Investors:
  - No interest income (no tax burden - bad if other option)
  - Discount on conversion is not taxable (good)
  - Investment loss if company fails is capital loss (mediocre)

Accounting:
  - Simpler accounting
  - No P&L impact pre-conversion
  - Cleaner financial statements
```

**Tax Complexity Winner: SAFE**

## Decision Framework: Which Should You Use?

### For FOUNDERS

#### Use Convertible Notes if:

```
✓ You have existing angel/investor relationships who expect notes
✓ You're raising $500K+ (justifies legal complexity)
✓ You're planning Series A in 18-24 months (clear path to conversion)
✓ Investors want conversion discount protection
✓ You have other debt (and can absorb another liability)
✓ You want faster closing (standardized templates)
```

#### Use SAFEs if:

```
✓ This is your first outside capital (angels expecting simplicity)
✓ You're in Y Combinator or similar (SAFEs are standard)
✓ You genuinely don't know Series A timeline
✓ You want simple, founder-friendly docs
✓ You want off-balance-sheet treatment
✓ You don't want maturity date pressure
✓ Investors are comfortable with minimal governance
```

#### Avoid Convertible Notes if:

```
✗ You have bank debt with debt covenants (might violate them)
✗ You already have multiple convertible notes (cap table complexity)
✗ You're uncertain about Series A timeline (maturity pressure)
✗ You prefer maximum operational freedom
✗ You want cleaner financial statements
```

---

### For INVESTORS

#### Use Convertible Notes (as investor) if:

```
✓ You want multiple protections (debt + discount + cap)
✓ Company has clear Series A path in 18-24 months
✓ You want information rights visibility
✓ You want protective provisions (governance)
✓ You want interest return (debt component)
✓ Company might not achieve Series A (repayment protection)
✓ You want to lead/influence major decisions
```

#### Use SAFEs (as investor) if:

```
✓ You're angel investor making small check ($25K-$50K)
✓ You trust founder to make good decisions (passive)
✓ You're comfortable with equity-only upside
✓ You don't need governance/visibility
✓ You're okay with no interest component
✓ You can wait indefinitely for conversion
✓ You prefer simplicity over protection
```

#### Avoid SAFEs as Investor if:

```
✗ You need downside protection (debt seniority)
✗ You want information/governance rights
✗ You need interest return if not converted
✗ You want to influence decisions
✗ You need clear repayment scenario
```

## Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario 1: First Seed Round ($250K)

**Situation:**
- Founder raising first $250K
- Mix of angels and family
- No institutional investors
- Uncertain Series A timeline

**Recommendation: SAFE**

```
Why SAFE:
  ✓ Simpler (angels don't expect complex terms)
  ✓ Faster closing (less legal back-and-forth)
  ✓ Founder-friendly (no maturity pressure)
  ✓ No balance sheet liability (cleaner)
  ✓ Investors happy with passive position
  ✗ Notes would be overkill for this size/stage

Implementation:
  1. Use standard Y Combinator SAFE template
  2. Set valuation cap at ~$1.5M (4-5x the raise)
  3. 20% discount (standard)
  4. Minimal legal costs (~$500-1K)
  5. Close in 2-3 weeks
```

**Alternative: Multiple SAFEs**
If raising from 5 different angels, doing individual SAFEs is simpler than managing 5 convertible note agreements.

---

### Scenario 2: Second Seed + Series A Planning ($500K)

**Situation:**
- Founder already raised $300K in SAFEs
- Now raising $500K more
- Series A planned in 6-9 months
- Working with institutional seed investors

**Recommendation: Convertible Notes**

```
Why Convertible Notes:
  ✓ Different investor expectations (more structured)
  ✓ Clearer conversion mechanics (defined Series A path)
  ✓ Mix of SAFEs + Notes is manageable
  ✗ But add more complexity to cap table

Alternative: More SAFEs
  ✓ Consistent with existing investors
  ✓ Simpler cap table (all SAFEs)
  ✗ Series A will have complex cap table (20+ SAFEs)
  ✗ New investors may not understand SAFE mechanics

Better Path:
  - Use SAFEs for first round
  - Use Convertible Notes for second round (if needed)
  - Minimize SAFEs by second round
```

---

### Scenario 3: Founder with VCs + Clear Timeline ($1M+)

**Situation:**
- Founder raising $1M+
- VCs leading round (not just angels)
- Clear Series A path in 18-24 months
- Founder wants investor input

**Recommendation: Convertible Notes**

```
Why Convertible Notes:
  ✓ VCs expect structured terms (standard format)
  ✓ Can negotiate specific caps, discounts
  ✓ Investor can get board observation/approval rights
  ✓ Clear Series A path (maturity not a risk)
  ✗ More legal complexity
  ✗ Higher legal costs ($2K-5K)

Why Not SAFEs:
  ✗ VCs prefer more structure than SAFEs provide
  ✗ Lack of governance/information rights unacceptable
  ✗ VCs want debt status for downside protection

Implementation:
  1. Use professional legal counsel
  2. Negotiate: cap ($4-6M), discount (15-20%), interest (3-5%)
  3. Include: information rights, pro-rata rights, protective provisions
  4. Set maturity: 3-4 years (Series A expected before then)
  5. Close in 4-6 weeks (VCs move slower)
```

---

### Scenario 4: Founder Who Already Borrowed From Bank

**Situation:**
- Founder has $200K bank debt with debt covenants
- Covenant: Cannot exceed 2.0x debt-to-equity ratio
- Current D/E ratio: 1.5x
- Wants to raise $300K seed capital

**Recommendation: SAFE (NOT Convertible Note)**

```
Why SAFE:
  ✓ Does NOT add to debt liabilities
  ✓ Does NOT worsen debt-to-equity ratio
  ✓ Will NOT violate bank covenants
  ✓ Bank won't require SAFE holder approval

Why NOT Convertible Note:
  ✗ Adds $300K debt to balance sheet
  ✗ New D/E ratio: ($200K + $300K) / $400K = 1.25x
  ✗ Still under 2.0x (technically okay)
  ✗ But moves wrong direction, reduces future borrowing
  ✗ Bank may get nervous about notes

Math:
  Current: $200K debt / $400K equity = 0.5x
  With SAFE: $200K debt / $400K equity = 0.5x ← SAME
  With Note: $500K debt / $400K equity = 1.25x ← WORSE
```

**This is a practical consideration many founders miss**

---

### Scenario 5: Founder in Uncertain Situation

**Situation:**
- Pre-product stage (early MVP)
- Founder has been working 1 year, still validating market
- Raising $100K from angels
- No clear Series A timeline (could be 1 year, could be 3 years)
- Founder wants maximum flexibility

**Recommendation: SAFE**

```
Why SAFE:
  ✓ No maturity date pressure (don't know Series A timeline)
  ✓ Can focus on product/market fit without deadline
  ✓ If Series A takes 3 years (expected for MVP stage), SAFE doesn't pressure
  ✓ Founder-friendly (maximum freedom)

Why NOT Convertible Note:
  ✗ 2-3 year maturity would be tight
  ✗ If Series A delayed, maturity date becomes stressful
  ✗ Interest accrual incentivizes rushing Series A
  ✗ More restrictive covenants/information rights
  ✗ Investors expect Series A which isn't guaranteed

Logic:
  Pre-product founder: Unknown timeline = SAFE
  Product + traction: Clear timeline = Convertible Note
  Strong traction: Series A now = Priced Equity Round
```

## Hybrid Approaches

### Mix of SAFEs + Convertible Notes

Some founders use both instruments:

```
Strategy: Segmented Investor Types

Investor Type 1: Angels ($10-50K each)
  ├─ Instrument: SAFE
  ├─ Reasoning: Simple, hands-off
  └─ Outcome: Easy closes, minimal friction

Investor Type 2: Seed Investors ($100K+)
  ├─ Instrument: Convertible Note
  ├─ Reasoning: Want structure/governance
  └─ Outcome: More organized, institutional-ready

Result:
  - Angels happy with simple SAFEs
  - Seed investors happy with structured notes
  - Cap table has mix but manageable
  - Series A investors understand both types
```

**When This Works:**
- Raising in stages (first round angels, second round seed funds)
- Different investor expectations in same round
- Intentionally segmenting by investor sophistication

**When This Fails:**
- Too many different instruments confuse Series A process
- Mixed terms create cap table complexity
- Series A investors want consistency

## Negotiation Tips

### For Founders Negotiating Convertible Notes

```
Terms to Push On (for founder benefit):

1. MATURITY DATE
   Founder Ask: 4-5 years (longer is better)
   Investor Typical: 2-3 years
   Market: 3-4 years is standard
   Tip: Longer maturity = less pressure on Series A

2. INTEREST RATE
   Founder Ask: 3-4% (lower is better)
   Investor Typical: 6-8%
   Market: 5-7% is standard
   Tip: Negotiate for simple (not compound) interest

3. VALUATION CAP
   Founder Ask: Higher is better (5-8x your raise)
   Investor Typical: 3-5x
   Market: 4-5x is standard
   Tip: Show comparable company caps to justify yours

4. APPROVAL RIGHTS
   Founder Ask: Information only (no approval needed)
   Investor Typical: Approval on major decisions
   Market: Protective provisions for specific items only
   Tip: Limit approvals to truly major decisions (Series A, M&A, debt > $X)

5. NUMBER OF NOTES
   Founder Ask: Fewer notes (2-3 investors)
   Investor Typical: Everyone gets individual note
   Market: Pooled notes preferred (one agreement)
   Tip: Suggest pro-rata shares in single note (simpler cap table)
```

### For Founders Negotiating SAFEs

```
SAFEs have less negotiation (mostly binary accept/reject):

1. VALUATION CAP
   Founder Ask: Higher is better
   Market Standard: 4-5x investment
   Example: Invest $500K, cap at $2-2.5M

2. DISCOUNT
   Founder Ask: Lower is better (20% is standard)
   Market Standard: 20% (rarely negotiated)
   Note: Discount built into almost all SAFEs

3. PRO-RATA RIGHTS
   Ask For: Include in SAFE terms
   Benefit: Can participate in Series A to maintain ownership %
   Typical: 1x pro-rata on amount invested

4. MFN CLAUSE
   Ask For: Most Favored Nation
   Benefit: If you get better cap/discount elsewhere, investor gets it too
   Typical: Automatic upgrade to best terms
```

## Conversion Scenarios & Examples

### Example 1: Smooth Conversion (Best Case)

```
Timeline:
Year 0: Raise $500K convertible note
  - Valuation cap: $5M
  - Discount: 20%
  - Maturity: 3 years

Year 2: Series A occurs
  - Series A valuation: $10M
  - Series A price per share: Determined by math

Conversion:
  Note: $500K + 2 years interest ($80K) = $580K

  Conversion calculation:
    Using discount: $10M valuation × 0.80 = $8/share (roughly)
    Using cap: $5M valuation = $5M conversion cap

  Compare:
    If cap is more favorable to investor:
      Convert at $5M price
      Investor converts $580K into shares at $5M basis
      Share count: $580K ÷ (price per share at $5M)

Outcome:
  ✓ Series A happened on time
  ✓ Note converted smoothly
  ✓ Founder not stressed about repayment
  ✓ Investor happy with conversion
  ✓ Both sides want Series B now
```

---

### Example 2: Series A Delayed (Pain Point)

```
Timeline:
Year 0: Raise $500K convertible note
  - Valuation cap: $5M
  - Discount: 20%
  - Maturity: 3 years

Year 3.0: MATURITY DATE - Series A not closed
  - Series A discussions ongoing but not complete
  - Company has $200K cash remaining
  - Note with interest now due: $580K

Problem:
  Founder can't repay $580K (only has $200K cash)
  Investor can't force repayment (no cash)
  Both need solution

Solutions (in order of likelihood):

1. EXTEND THE NOTE (Most Common)
   Negotiated outcome:
     - Maturity extended 12 months
     - Interest increases to 8% (incentivizes quicker closure)
     - Founder gets warrant coverage (15% of conversion amount)

   Result:
     - Founder gets 12-month lifeline
     - Investor compensated for extension
     - Both motivated to close Series A

2. CONVERT TO EQUITY AT AGREED PRICE
   Negotiated outcome:
     - Both agree to convert now at specific price
     - Price based on recent valuation progress
     - Investor gets equity instead of waiting

   Result:
     - Immediate conversion
     - Debt removed from balance sheet
     - Clear equity position established

3. BRIDGE FINANCING
   Outcome:
     - Take bridge loan to pay off note
     - Push maturity/conversion to Series A

   Result:
     - Moves problem forward
     - Adds more debt (not ideal)

4. FORCED M&A / ACQUISITION
   Outcome:
     - Company acquired before Series A
     - Acquisition proceeds used to repay notes
     - Founders may get unfavorable terms

   Result:
     - Investor gets repaid
     - Founder gets less from acquisition
     - Notes forced the sale
```

### Example 3: Company Fails (Worst Case)

```
Timeline:
Year 0: Raise $500K convertible note
Year 1: Company runs out of cash and fails
Year 2: Company in liquidation

Assets in liquidation: $150K cash
Liabilities:
  - Payroll & taxes owed: $50K
  - Vendor payables: $40K
  - Convertible note: $500K

Liquidation Waterfall:
1. Taxes & payroll: $50K → remaining $100K
2. Vendor payables: $40K → remaining $60K
3. Convertible note: Entitled to $60K of $500K owed
   Recovery: 12% of investment
4. Equity holders: $0

Investor Result:
  Invested: $500K
  Recovered: $60K
  Loss: $440K (88% loss)

This is why early-stage investing is risky
```

### Example 4: SAFE vs Note in Same Scenario

```
Same company, same timeline - but with SAFE instead of note:

Year 0: Raise $500K SAFE
Year 1: Company fails
Year 2: Company in liquidation

Assets: $150K
Liabilities:
  - Payroll & taxes owed: $50K
  - Vendor payables: $40K
  - No convertible note ← Different!

Liquidation Waterfall:
1. Taxes & payroll: $50K → remaining $100K
2. Vendor payables: $40K → remaining $60K
3. Equity holders (including SAFE holders): $0
   (Note: SAFE holders entitled to equity only, no debt claim)

Investor Result with SAFE:
  Invested: $500K
  Recovered: $0 (no debt claim)
  Loss: $500K (100% loss)

Key Difference:
  With Note: Creditor status = 12% recovery
  With SAFE: Equity status = 0% recovery

This is the TRADE-OFF:
  Notes offer more investor protection in failure (debt seniority)
  SAFEs offer founders more flexibility but investor worse protection
```

## Decision Checklist

### For Founders:

Before choosing convertible notes:
```
□ Do I have clear Series A path within 2-3 years?
□ Can I service debt obligations if Series A is delayed?
□ Am I okay with interest accruing and diluting future equity?
□ Can I accept investor approval rights on major decisions?
□ Do my investors expect structured terms (not simple SAFEs)?
□ Do I have bank debt that can absorb another liability?

If all NO → Use SAFE instead
If mostly YES → Convertible note is appropriate
```

### For Investors:

Before investing in convertible note:
```
□ Do I want multiple protections (not just equity upside)?
□ Am I comfortable with founder approval rights restrictions?
□ Do I understand the conversion mechanics fully?
□ Can I verify the valuation cap is reasonable?
□ Am I okay with subordination to senior debt?
□ Do I want information/governance rights?

If all YES → Convertible note is appropriate
If mostly NO → SAFE might be better fit
```

## Key Takeaways

1. **Convertible notes are DEBT** - appears on balance sheet, has repayment obligation, accrues interest
2. **SAFEs are EQUITY RIGHTS** - off-balance sheet, never repaid, no interest
3. **Maturity date is real** - convertible notes create forced conversion/repayment timeline
4. **Interest accrual dilutes founders** - accelerates equity dilution if converted, creates burden if repaid
5. **Both use caps/discounts** - investor protection mechanisms are similar
6. **Governance differs significantly** - notes come with control provisions, SAFEs don't
7. **Choose based on timeline** - clear Series A path = notes; uncertain path = SAFEs
8. **Balance sheet matters** - if other debt exists, SAFEs are safer for founders
9. **Investor expectations drive choice** - angels prefer SAFEs, VCs prefer notes
10. **Legal complexity has cost** - notes cost more ($2-5K), SAFEs cost less ($500-1K)
