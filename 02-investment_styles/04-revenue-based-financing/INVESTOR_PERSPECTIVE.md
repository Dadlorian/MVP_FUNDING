## RBF as an Investment Class

### How RBF Fits in Your Portfolio

**RBF vs Other Alternatives:**

| Factor | RBF | Equity | Debt | Hybrid |
|--------|-----|--------|------|--------|
| **Expected Return** | 25-40% IRR | 30-80% IRR | 8-12% IRR | 20-30% IRR |
| **Time Horizon** | 24-48 months | 5-10 years | 12-36 months | 24-60 months |
| **Downside Risk** | Moderate (no loss if default, but extended timeline) | High (total loss possible) | Low (senior claim) | Moderate-Low |
| **Upside Caps** | Yes (2x-3x cap) | Unlimited | No (interest only) | Partial (warrant coverage) |
| **Due Diligence** | Medium (financial review) | Heavy (full DD) | Light (credit analysis) | Medium-Heavy |
| **Decision Speed** | 4-8 weeks | 3-6 months | 2-4 weeks | 4-12 weeks |
| **Operational Load** | Medium (monthly reporting) | High (board seat) | Low (quarterly reports) | Medium (reporting + some governance) |
| **Company Control** | Minimal (reporting obligations) | Significant (board representation) | None (senior creditor rights) | Low-Moderate |

### RBF Returns Profile

**Expected Return Scenarios:**

```
Best Case (Fast Growth):
├─ Investment: $250,000 at 5% revenue share, 2.0x cap
├─ Payback: 18-20 months
├─ Total Return: $500,000
├─ Annualized IRR: 50%+
└─ Time to Redeployment: 20 months

Base Case (Expected):
├─ Investment: $250,000 at 4% revenue share, 2.0x cap
├─ Payback: 24-28 months
├─ Total Return: $500,000
├─ Annualized IRR: 35-40%
└─ Time to Redeployment: 26 months

Downside Case (Slow Growth):
├─ Investment: $250,000 at 4% revenue share, 2.0x cap
├─ Payback: 40-50 months
├─ Total Return: $500,000
├─ Annualized IRR: 15-20%
└─ Time to Redeployment: 45 months
```

### Where RBF Fits in Fund Strategy

**For Venture Capitalists:**
- Use RBF as **follow-on** investment in portfolio companies with revenue
- Helps founders avoid excessive dilution in down rounds
- Can be **co-investment** with equity (founder gets both options)
- Typical allocation: 5-15% of follow-on fund

**For Angel Investors:**
- Primary investment in profitable businesses (non-VC track)
- Lower risk than pure equity, higher return than debt
- Suits "boring but profitable" companies (not unicorn hunts)
- Typical allocation: 10-20% of angel portfolio

**For Family Offices:**
- Alternative yield investment (vs traditional bonds at 3-5% yields)
- 25-40% IRR much better than fixed income
- Lower operational burden than equity (no board seats)
- Diversification benefit: uncorrelated to public markets

**For Impact Investors:**
- Fund profitable social enterprises
- RBF doesn't require VC returns (unicorn exit)
- Works for businesses with positive social impact + revenue model
- Example: sustainable products, education tech, healthcare

```

Vs Equity:
├─ RBF: Defined returns (no surprise massive gains if unicorn)
├─ Equity: Unlimited upside (but unlimited downside and long wait)
└─ Choose RBF if you want: predictable returns, shorter timelines
```

### Scoring Framework

**Rate each company 1-5:**

```
5 = Excellent (ideal company)
4 = Good (meets requirements, some concerns)
3 = Acceptable (meets minimum, notable risks)
2 = Weak (below target, significant concerns)
1 = Poor (doesn't meet criteria, pass)

INVESTMENT DECISION:
├─ Score 4-5: INVEST (high confidence)
├─ Score 3-4: INVESTIGATE FURTHER (conditional)
├─ Score 3: RISK/REWARD FAVORABLE (negotiate better terms)
├─ Score 2: PASS unless terms heavily discounted
└─ Score <2: PASS (not right for us)
```

### Deal Size Considerations

**How Much to Invest:**

```
Early-Stage (Pre-PMF, <$50K MRR):
├─ Investment: $25K-$100K max
├─ Type: Bet on founder more than business
├─ Risk: Very high

Growth-Stage ($50K-$200K MRR):
├─ Investment: $100K-$300K typical
├─ Type: Proven revenue, clear growth path
├─ Risk: Moderate

Scale-Stage ($200K+ MRR):
├─ Investment: $250K-$500K+
├─ Type: Stable business, predictable revenue
├─ Risk: Low-Moderate

Rules of Thumb:
├─ Don't invest more than 3-5 years of operating costs
├─ Size should accelerate growth, not fund survival
├─ Larger investment = higher revenue share % required
├─ Consider follow-on rounds for winners
```

### Quick Pass / Deep Dive Decision

**When to Pass Immediately:**

```
✗ Revenue declining (down month-over-month)
✗ One customer >50% of revenue (concentration risk)
✗ Founder unable to explain financials
✗ Negative cash flow and burning runway
✗ Unproven business model (pre-revenue)
✗ Founder relationship seems adversarial
✗ Financial records are disorganized/questionable
✗ Market is clearly shrinking or saturated
```

**When to Deep Dive:**

```
✓ Revenue growing 15%+ month-over-month
✓ Multiple revenue streams (no >30% single customer)
✓ Founder is domain expert with prior success
✓ Unit economics clearly work (positive contribution margin)
✓ Gross margins healthy (50%+)
✓ Clear path to higher revenue (pipeline visible)
✓ Financial records professional and auditable
✓ Founder seems coachable and reasonable
```

## Due Diligence Playbook

### Phase 1: Initial Screening (Days 1-3)

**Goal:** Determine if company fits basic criteria

**Activities:**

```
Day 1 - Information Gathering:
├─ Initial call with founder (30 minutes)
├─ Ask about:
│  ├─ Current revenue and growth rate
│  ├─ Revenue streams and customer base
│  ├─ Why they're raising and how much
│  ├─ Timeline (when do they need capital?)
│  └─ Any previous investors or advisors
├─ Send follow-up email with document request list
└─ Time: 1 hour total

Days 2-3 - Document Review:
├─ Founder sends: 12 months bank statements, P&L, customer list
├─ You review:
│  ├─ Revenue trend (growing, flat, declining?)
│  ├─ Profitability (positive or negative margin?)
│  ├─ Customer concentration (are numbers accurate?)
│  └─ Obvious red flags (missing months, irregular deposits?)
├─ Call if questions arise
└─ Time: 2-3 hours total
```

**Pass/Continue Decision:**
```
IF company shows:
✓ Revenue $50K+/month
✓ Growing 10%+ month-over-month
✓ Reasonable profitability (or path to it)
✓ No obvious red flags
├─ CONTINUE to Phase 2

IF company has concerns:
├─ Revenue <$50K/month
├─ Revenue declining or flat
├─ Founder can't explain numbers
└─ PASS (not right fit)
```

### Phase 2: Detailed Financial Analysis (Days 4-10)

**Goal:** Verify revenue numbers and project payback

**Activities:**

```
Days 4-6 - Financial Verification:
├─ Request: Last 24 months of bank statements
├─ Request: Detailed revenue breakdown (by product, customer, etc.)
├─ Request: Customer list with monthly spend
├─ Request: Any contracts with customers (top 10)
├─ Request: Tax returns (personal and business)
├─ Request: Cap table and equity structure

Verification Process:
├─ Bank deposits = Reported revenue? (should match closely)
├─ Revenue pattern = Claimed growth rate? (check month-by-month)
├─ Customer list = Revenue adds up? (spot check top 10 customers)
├─ Gross margin reasonable for industry?
├─ Are there unusual large transactions to explain?

Red Flags if:
├─ Bank deposits don't match reported revenue (>5% variance)
├─ Revenue pattern inconsistent (claiming 20% growth but flat month-to-month)
├─ Customer list doesn't add up to reported revenue
├─ Major transactions unexplained
└─ Founder becomes defensive about requests

Days 7-10 - Projection Analysis:
├─ Build payback model based on actual revenue numbers
├─ Model conservative, base, and upside scenarios
├─ Calculate expected IRR under each scenario
├─ Identify key assumptions (growth rate, churn, etc.)
├─ Run sensitivity analysis (what if growth is half?)
└─ Stress test (what if revenue declines 30%?)
```

**Continue/Pass Decision:**
```
IF verification shows:
✓ Numbers match (bank deposits align with revenue)
✓ Growth trajectory realistic (can be explained)
✓ Payback timeline 24-36 months in base case
✓ IRR 25%+ even in conservative case
├─ CONTINUE to Phase 3

IF verification shows concerns:
├─ Numbers don't add up (discrepancies >5%)
├─ Growth cannot be sustained
├─ Payback timeline >48 months even in good case
├─ IRR <15% even in base case
└─ PASS or NEGOTIATE BETTER TERMS
```

### Phase 3: Business & Team Assessment (Days 11-18)

**Goal:** Evaluate business model, market, and founder capability

**Activities:**

```
Days 11-13 - Business Model Deep Dive:
├─ Understand product/service deeply
├─ Customer acquisition process (how do they get customers?)
├─ Customer retention (why do they stay? what's churn?)
├─ Revenue model (how do they make money?)
├─ Pricing strategy (why at this price point?)
├─ Competition (who else competes? how different?)

Questions to Ask:
├─ "What's your customer acquisition cost?"
├─ "What's your customer lifetime value?"
├─ "What's your monthly churn rate?"
├─ "Why aren't you more differentiated from competitors?"
├─ "What would cause you to lose 30% of customers?"
├─ "What's your biggest operational bottleneck?"
├─ "How dependent is revenue on specific customer?"
└─ "What could kill this business in next 24 months?"

Days 14-16 - Founder Assessment:
├─ Meet founder in person if possible (video call if remote)
├─ Evaluate:
│  ├─ Communication clarity (can they articulate vision?)
│  ├─ Financial knowledge (do they understand metrics?)
│  ├─ Market knowledge (deep expertise or surface level?)
│  ├─ Execution capability (track record of shipping)
│  ├─ Team leadership (how do employees describe them?)
│  └─ Coachability (open to feedback? defensive?)
├─ Reference calls:
│  ├─ Call previous investors (if any)
│  ├─ Call employees (ask about leadership)
│  ├─ Call customers (ask about product, support)
│  └─ Call advisors (ask about judgment, capability)

Days 17-18 - Market Assessment:
├─ Size (Total Addressable Market $1B+?)
├─ Growth (Is market growing? Declining? Saturated?)
├─ Trends (Where is market headed?)
├─ Competitiveness (How intense is competition?)
├─ Regulation (Any regulatory headwinds?)
└─ Technology shifts (Any disruption threats?)
```

**Continue/Pass Decision:**
```
IF assessment shows:
✓ Business model is sound and repeatable
✓ Founder is capable and has relevant experience
✓ Market is large and growing
✓ Limited competitive threats to business model
✓ References are strong and positive
├─ CONTINUE to Phase 4 (Offer)

IF assessment shows concerns:
├─ Business model is unclear or doesn't scale
├─ Founder lacks relevant experience
├─ Market is shrinking or over-saturated
├─ Major competitive threats
├─ Weak references from customers/employees
└─ PASS or MAKE VERY CONSERVATIVE OFFER
```

### Phase 4: Legal & Compliance (Days 19-22)

**Goal:** Ensure clean legal structure and compliance

**Activities:**

```
Legal Review:
├─ Certificate of incorporation (valid in good standing?)
├─ Capitalization table (clean? any unusual provisions?)
├─ Cap table modeling (dilution from future funding?)
├─ IP ownership (is all IP owned by company?)
├─ Employee agreements (any non-competes? equity cliffs?)
├─ Customer contracts (long-term lock-in? Price locks?)
├─ Liability concerns (any pending litigation?)

Compliance:
├─ Tax returns filed (current? All years?)
├─ Payroll taxes paid (no liens? Current?)
├─ Employee benefits (401k? Health insurance?)
├─ Insurance (D&O? General liability?)
├─ Regulatory compliance (licenses, permits?)
├─ Securities law (any prior unregistered offerings?)

Red Flags:
├─ Founder owns <75% equity (too diluted already)
├─ Major IP not owned by company
├─ Pending litigation
├─ Tax liens or back taxes owed
├─ Missing prior year tax returns
├─ Unregistered securities offerings
└─ Claims of regulatory uncertainty

Questions for Founder's Lawyer:
├─ "Is the company in good standing in all jurisdictions?"
├─ "Are there any pending or threatened claims?"
├─ "Is IP properly assigned to the company?"
├─ "Are there any lien holders with claims on revenue?"
└─ "Are there any issues with securities law compliance?"
```

### Phase 5: Term Negotiation & Offer (Days 23-30)

**Goal:** Create term sheet with appropriate risk-adjusted returns

**Activities:**

```
Days 23-25 - Return Modeling:
├─ Estimate appropriate revenue share based on:
│  ├─ Growth rate (faster growth = lower rate)
│  ├─ Revenue stability (stable = lower rate)
│  ├─ Competitive interest (other investors want in = lower rate)
│  ├─ Founder experience (seasoned = lower rate)
│  └─ Risk factors (concentration, market, team)
├─ Model different scenarios (conservative, base, upside)
├─ Calculate IRR under each scenario
├─ Identify minimal acceptable return (25% IRR?)
└─ Determine deal structure and size

Pricing Examples:
├─ Fast growth (20%+ MoM), low risk → 3% revenue share
├─ Moderate growth (12-15% MoM) → 4% revenue share
├─ Slower growth (5-10% MoM) → 5-6% revenue share
├─ Volatile/risky business → 6-8% revenue share

Days 26-30 - Negotiation:
├─ Present term sheet to founder
├─ Discuss expectations:
│  ├─ "Here's what we're offering..."
│  ├─ "Here's why (based on growth, risk, etc.)"
│  └─ "What's your view on these terms?"
├─ Negotiate key terms:
│  ├─ Revenue share percentage
│  ├─ Cap multiple
│  ├─ Term length
│  ├─ Reporting requirements
│  └─ Any protective provisions
├─ Reach agreement on terms
└─ Move to documentation phase
```

## Negotiating Terms

### Your Levers as an Investor

**Tools You Control:**

```
1. Revenue Share %:
   ├─ Higher % = Faster payback, lower founder comfort
   ├─ Lower % = Slower payback, founder more comfortable
   ├─ Adjust based on risk assessment
   └─ Typical range: 3%-8%

2. Cap Multiple:
   ├─ Lower cap (1.5x) = You're done faster
   ├─ Higher cap (2.5x) = Founder more comfortable
   ├─ Adjust based on return expectations
   └─ Typical range: 1.3x-3.0x

3. Term Length:
   ├─ Shorter term (24 months) = Faster certainty
   ├─ Longer term (48+ months) = Founder more comfortable
   ├─ Perpetual = Until cap reached (best for you if growth slows)
   └─ Choose based on business predictability

4. Protective Provisions:
   ├─ What can founder do without your consent?
   ├─ Can they raise other RBF?
   ├─ Can they incur other debt?
   ├─ Reporting frequency and detail
   └─ Audit rights

5. Special Provisions:
   ├─ Warrant coverage (equity upside sweetener)
   ├─ Equity conversion option (convert at future trigger)
   ├─ Acceleration clauses (higher rate if not on track)
   └─ Minimum payments (floor on monthly payment)
```

### Sample Negotiation Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Founder Thinks Terms Are Too High

```
FOUNDER SAYS: "Your 5% revenue share is too high. I've seen other deals at 3%."

YOUR RESPONSE 1 (Defend):
"Our rate reflects the risk. Your company is [early/has concentration risk/
unproven growth]. Here's why 5% is justified:
├─ Your customer concentration is higher than comparable deals
├─ Growth rate is moderate (12% vs 20% for similar investments)
├─ Market has some headwinds
└─ All this = we need 5% to hit 25% IRR minimum

If you can address these risks, we could discuss 4%."

YOUR RESPONSE 2 (Creative):
"Let's structure it differently. How about:
├─ 4% for months 1-12 (you're newer, we're building trust)
├─ 3% for months 13+ (you've proven execution)
└─ Same cap multiple (2.0x = $500K total), just better for you early"

YOUR RESPONSE 3 (Compromise):
"Can't do 3%, but:
├─ I'll do 4.5% revenue share, 2.5x cap
├─ That's equivalent return to 5% at 2.0x cap
├─ Slightly worse for us, slightly better for you
└─ Can you accept 4.5%?"

WHAT WORKS:
✓ Show your math (don't just say "5% is market")
✓ Acknowledge their concern (they want better terms)
✓ Offer creative solutions (tiered rates, higher cap, etc.)
✓ Find middle ground if possible
✓ Have walk-away point (minimum 25% IRR required)
```

#### Scenario 2: You Want Better Protection

```
YOU WANT: Founder can't raise other RBF without approval

FOUNDER SAYS: "That's too restrictive. I may need multiple funding sources."

YOUR NEGOTIATION:
"I understand you might need more capital. Here's what concerns me:
├─ If you stack 3 RBF sources at 5% each, that's 15% of revenue
├─ Your growth slows, everyone is angry
├─ You have problems, I lose returns

Let's compromise:
├─ Option A: You can raise 1 more RBF (total 10% max), requires my consent
├─ Option B: You can raise unlimited RBF, but total revenue share <10%
├─ Option C: If you raise RBF from others, my rate decreases proportionally

Pick one that works for you."

FOUNDER RESPONSE:
"I like Option B. Can I add: if total RBF >10%, you and other lenders coordinate?"

YOUR RESPONSE:
"Yes. We'll add a cross-default clause so we all stay coordinated."

RESULT:
✓ You get protection (capped total RBF burden)
✓ Founder gets flexibility (can raise if needed)
✓ Clear governance (cross-default coordination)
```

### Your Negotiation Playbook

**Position 1: You Have Alternatives**
```
"We're evaluating [3-4] similar companies right now. We prefer your business,
but if we can't reach terms, we'll fund one of the others. What would it take
to make this work?"

This works because:
├─ Shows you're not desperate
├─ Founder knows if they don't move, you'll fund competitor
└─ Creates urgency to negotiate
```

**Position 2: Show Comparable Data**
```
"I've done [5-10] RBF investments. Here's the median terms:
├─ 4% revenue share (range: 3-5%)
├─ 2.0x cap (range: 1.5x-2.5x)
├─ 36-month average payback (range: 24-48 months)

Your company is [stronger/weaker] than median, so [X] terms are justified."

This works because:
├─ Reference market data (not just your opinion)
├─ Shows you have track record
├─ Founder can't claim "other investors do better"
└─ Adds credibility to your ask
```

**Position 3: Value Their Execution**
```
"Your execution is excellent. Your growth rate is top quartile for RBF companies.
Because of that, I'm willing to do:
├─ 3.5% revenue share (vs our typical 4%)
├─ 2.0x cap
├─ Perpetual (vs typical 36 months)

This rewards your great execution while protecting our downside."

This works because:
├─ Flatters founder (recognizes their excellence)
├─ Gives them real value (lower rate)
├─ Protects you (perpetual vs fixed term)
└─ Shows you believe in them
```

**Position 4: Risk Adjustment**
```
"Here's how I'm pricing risk:
├─ If 1 customer >30% of revenue: Add 1% to rate
├─ If growth <10% MoM: Add 1% to rate
├─ If founder new to company building: Add 1% to rate

Your company has [issues], so base 4% + [X]% adjustments = [5]% total.
Fix the issues and we can reduce the rate."

This works because:
├─ Transparent methodology (founder understands reasoning)
├─ Clear path to better terms (fix issues = lower rate)
├─ Hard to argue (you're being logical, not arbitrary)
```

### Monitoring Your Investments

**Monthly Activities:**

```
By 5th of month:
├─ [ ] Receive revenue report from [X companies]
├─ [ ] Verify payment received to your account
├─ [ ] Calculate % of cap reached (are we on track?)
├─ [ ] Flag any unusual items (missing reports, late payments)
└─ [ ] Time: 2-3 hours total

Monthly Spreadsheet to Maintain:

Company | Investment | Rate | Cap | Cumulative | Months | IRR | Status
--------|-----------|------|-----|-----------|--------|-----|--------
Company A | $250K | 4% | $500K | $120K | 12 | 38% | On Track
Company B | $200K | 5% | $400K | $240K | 10 | 52% | Early Payoff?
Company C | $300K | 4% | $600K | $80K | 8 | 15% | SLOWING?
```

**Quarterly Activities:**

```
Each Quarter (Every 3 months):
```

### Downside Protection

**Terms That Protect You:**

```
1. Revenue Share %:
   ├─ Higher % protects you if growth slows
   ├─ Lower % works if growth accelerates
   └─ Match % to growth risk

2. Cap Multiple:
   ├─ Lower cap (1.5x-1.8x) = Faster payout, less downside
   ├─ Higher cap (2.5x-3.0x) = Longer exposure, more risk
   └─ Use lower cap if high uncertainty

3. Term Length:
   ├─ Fixed term = Limits your exposure (you know when it ends)
   ├─ Perpetual = Longer timeline if slowdown occurs
   └─ Perpetual better if high growth confidence

4. Protective Covenants:
   ├─ Revenue share % increases if growth slows (acceleration clause)
   ├─ Founder can't raise competing RBF (prevents stacking)
   ├─ Founder can't sell major customer (prevents loss)
   ├─ Mandatory reporting (transparency)
   └─ Audit rights (verification)

5. Equity Sweetener:
   ├─ 0.5%-2% warrant coverage (upside if company succeeds wildly)
   ├─ Equity conversion option (convert to equity at trigger)
   ├─ This caps your downside (at most lose revenue share)
   └─ Provides upside option (if company becomes unicorn)

6. Seniority Clause:
   ├─ RBF ranked senior to equity (paid before equity in liquidation)
   ├─ RBF ranked pari passu with equity (same priority)
   ├─ Negotiate higher share if junior to other debt
```

## Return Analysis & IRR

### IRR Calculation Framework

**How IRR Works:**

```
Simple Definition:
IRR is the annual rate of return on your investment,
accounting for timing of cash flows.

Example:
├─ You invest $100K today (year 0)
├─ You receive $50K in year 1
├─ You receive $60K in year 2
├─ Total received: $110K (profit of $10K)
├─ IRR ≈ 4.88% (not 10%, because you got money early)

vs.

RBF Example:
├─ You invest $250K in month 0
├─ You receive $6K/month starting month 2
├─ You reach $500K total return in month 24
├─ IRR ≈ 35% annualized
└─ This assumes you redeploy returned $6K monthly into new investments
```

### IRR Modeling for RBF

**Quick IRR Calculator:**

```
For Deal Structure:
├─ Investment: $250,000
├─ Monthly Payment: ~$10,000 (4% of $250K growing revenue)
├─ Expected Payback: 24 months
├─ Total Return at Cap: $500,000

Simplified IRR Calculation:
├─ Profit: $500K - $250K = $250K
├─ Annual Profit Rate: $250K / 2 years / $250K = 50% annual return
├─ But cash comes monthly, not yearly, so reduce slightly
├─ Expected IRR: ~35-40% annualized
```

**Accurate IRR Calculation (Using Financial Calculator or Excel):**

```
Excel Function: =IRR(Cash Flow Array)

Example:
Month 0: -$250,000 (you invest)
Month 1: $0
Month 2: $6,000 (first payment received)
Month 3: $6,500 (slight growth)
Month 4: $7,000
... continues ...
Month 24: $15,000 (final payment, total = $500K)

=IRR([-250000, 0, 6000, 6500, 7000, ..., 15000])
= 35.2% annualized return

This accounts for:
├─ Timing of cash flows (monthly vs lump sum)
├─ Capital tied up for 24 months
├─ Reinvestment of returned capital
└─ Actual growth trajectory
```

```
├─ You lose: $380K unreceived, $130K cash loss

Why This Happens:
├─ Founder couldn't execute business plan
├─ Market conditions changed
├─ Product didn't maintain traction
├─ Competition killed the business
└─ Unforeseen events (pandemic, etc.)

Your Recovery Options:

1. Company Assets:
   ├─ If RBF is senior to equity, you may have claim on assets
   ├─ Example: Company has $50K in equipment, can be liquidated
   ├─ Recover: $50K (better than nothing)
   └─ But typically minimal (startups have few assets)

2. Personal Guarantees (if any):
   ├─ Some RBF agreements include founder personal guarantee
   ├─ Could pursue founder personally
   ├─ Risk: Founder also has no money (startup failure scenario)
   └─ Not practical for most RBF deals

3. Bankruptcy Claims:
   ├─ If company files formal bankruptcy, you become creditor
   ├─ Rank senior to equity (if agreed)
   ├─ Recover $0-50K from liquidation proceeds
   └─ Expensive legal process, likely not worth it

REALITY:
├─ Accept that you lose $130K
├─ This is the downside of RBF (not a secured loan)
├─ Offset by wins in other portfolio companies
├─ Example: If 1 of 5 fails ($130K loss) and others average 2.2x return:
│  └─ Total return: 4×$280K - $130K = $1.12M - $130K = $992K
│  └─ $1.25M invested → $992K return = 1.33x (after one failure)

HOW TO MINIMIZE:
├─ Better due diligence upfront
├─ Pass on high-risk founders early
├─ Monitor regularly and intervene early if slowing
├─ Diversify across 10+ companies (one failure is manageable)
└─ Accept some failures as cost of venture investing
```

- [ ] Do I have a clear investment thesis?

- [ ] Term length matches business predictability?

- [ ] Do I have audit/verification rights?
