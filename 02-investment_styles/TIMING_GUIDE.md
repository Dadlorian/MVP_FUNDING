# Investment Styles Timing Guide: Complete Timeline for All Funding Vehicles

## Quick Reference: Time to Close by Vehicle Type

| Investment Vehicle | Typical Timeline | Fastest Possible | Slowest Case | Best Timing to Start |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SAFE** | 1-2 weeks | 3-5 days | 3 weeks | Any time (no fixed deadlines) |
| **Convertible Note** | 2-4 weeks | 10 days | 6 weeks | Q3-Q4 preferred |
| **Angel Round** | 4-8 weeks | 2-3 weeks | 12+ weeks | Q1-Q2 preferred |
| **Seed Round** | 2-3 months | 6-8 weeks | 6+ months | Q1 or Q3 preferred |
| **Series A** | 3-6 months | 8-10 weeks | 9+ months | Q1-Q2 preferred |
| **Venture Debt** | 4-8 weeks | 2-3 weeks | 12 weeks | Anytime (no seasonality) |
| **Revenue-Based Financing (RBF)** | 2-6 weeks | 2-7 days | 8 weeks | 3 months before peak season |
| **Equity Crowdfunding** | 2-4 months | 1.5-2 months | 6+ months | Q2-Q3 preferred |
| **Grants** | 3-12 months | 8-12 weeks | 18+ months | Varies by grant cycle |

## I. SAFE Agreements: Fast Track Option (1-2 Weeks)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Initial Conversation (1-3 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Investor expresses interest in SAFE round
  - Brief founder call (15-30 minutes) to confirm fit
  - High-level discussion of terms (valuation cap, discount rate, MFN clause, pro-rata rights)
  - Investor reviews existing pitch deck

- **Founder Time:** 2-3 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare 2-3 sentence investment thesis summary
  - Have investor deck ready for sharing
  - Confirm basic terms preferences

#### Phase 2: Term Sheet & SAFE Documentation (3-5 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Investor legal counsel (or standard template) prepares SAFE document
  - Terms finalized (valuation cap, discount, MFN, pro-rata)
  - Investor reviews SAFE document
  - Founder reviews SAFE document

- **Founder Time:** 4-6 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Review SAFE terms carefully (especially valuation cap)
  - Request changes if needed (most investors accept 1-2 revisions)
  - Prepare to negotiate if discount rate differs from expectation

#### Phase 3: Execution & Closing (1-2 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Digital signature of SAFE agreement (typically DocuSign or Hellosign)
  - Fund transfer (wire, ACH, or check)
  - Shareholder registry update
  - Investor receives SAFE document copies

- **Founder Time:** 1-2 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Verify wire instructions are correct
  - Confirm receipt of funds
  - Update cap table immediately
  - Send thank-you note and investor update

### Fastest Path to Close (3-5 days)
- **Investor type:** Repeat investor from your network, experienced angels
- **Keys:**
  - Use Y Combinator standard SAFE template (most investors accept)
  - Minimal negotiation (1 call to finalize terms)
  - Digital execution only (no legal review delays)
- **Tools:** DocuSign, Carta for cap table management

### Why SAFEs Close Fast
- **No valuation needed:** Delays requiring 409A or complex modeling eliminated
- **Standardized documents:** Market-standard templates reduce legal friction
- **Minimal due diligence:** Investors assess fit quickly
- **Simple terms:** Usually 3-4 key variables (cap, discount, MFN, pro-rata)

### Common Delays & Mitigation

| Delay | Typical Addition | How to Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Investor legal review | +3-5 days | Use standard Y Combinator template; offer it first |
| Valuation cap negotiation | +2-4 days | Pre-discuss valuation cap in initial call |
| Multiple closing delays | +5-10 days | Execute with one investor first; others often follow |
| MFN clause disputes | +2-3 days | Clearly define MFN terms upfront |

### Parallel vs. Sequential SAFEs

**Recommended Strategy: Parallel (3-5 SAFEs simultaneously)**
- Close first SAFE after 1-2 weeks
- Other investors often accelerate once first SAFE is signed
- Reduce total timeline from 10 weeks to 4-6 weeks for multiple SAFEs
- Set "anchor investor" to close first, others follow

## II. Convertible Notes: Moderate Timeline (2-4 Weeks)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Interest & Term Sheet (3-5 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Investor indicates interest in convertible note
  - Founder & investor discuss basic terms:
    - Investment amount
    - Interest rate (typically 3-8%)
    - Maturity date (typically 24-36 months)
    - Conversion cap or discount rate
    - Conversion trigger (Series A, IPO, etc.)
  - Investor reviews pitch deck and financials
  - Investor conducts light diligence

- **Founder Time:** 4-6 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare cap table summary
  - Have financial projections ready
  - Define clear conversion trigger
  - Document your current valuation estimate

#### Phase 2: Term Sheet Negotiation (3-7 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Term sheet drafted (investor's counsel or standard template)
  - Terms discussed and finalized:
    - Interest rate negotiation (key variable)
    - Conversion cap pricing (typically 20-30% discount to Series A)
    - Maturity mechanics (cash repayment? auto-convert?)
    - Board observer rights (if applicable)
  - Multiple rounds of revision typically needed

- **Founder Time:** 6-10 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Negotiate interest rate (push for 3-5%, accept up to 8%)
  - Agree on conversion cap vs. discount rate trade-off
  - Clarify what happens at maturity (critical for growth trajectory)
  - Have board meeting to approve terms

#### Phase 3: Due Diligence (5-10 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Investor conducts diligence:
    - Financial review (P&L, runway calculations)
    - Legal review (IP ownership, contract review, liability assessment)
    - Customer reference checks (if Series A trajectory)
    - Technology assessment (if applicable)
  - Any questions or concerns raised
  - Founder provides additional documentation as needed

- **Founder Time:** 8-12 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare data room (organized folder with legal docs)
  - IP assignment agreements ready
  - Customer/user reference list prepared
  - Address any investor questions within 24 hours

#### Phase 4: Legal Documentation (5-7 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Final note document drafted by investor counsel
  - Founder counsel reviews (if hiring external counsel)
  - Terms reconciled
  - Execution ready
  - Board resolutions prepared

- **Founder Time:** 4-6 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Review note document line-by-line
  - Prepare shareholder authorization resolution
  - Confirm board meeting date for final approval
  - Ensure signer authority (CEO or board-authorized)

#### Phase 5: Closing (1-2 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Digital signature execution
  - Fund transfer
  - Closing document delivery
  - Cap table update

- **Founder Time:** 1-2 hours

### Fastest Path (10 days)
- **Investor type:** Experienced angels, early-stage VCs familiar with notes
- **Keys:**
  - Use standard Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) convertible note template
  - Pre-align on valuation cap (no negotiation needed)
  - Minimal due diligence (reference calls optional)
  - Lawyer reviews template, doesn't modify (saves 5-7 days)

### Slowest Scenario (6 weeks)
- **Investor type:** Corporate investor, international investor, or extremely cautious angel
- **Reasons:**
  - Extended due diligence (international legal review, corporate approval)
  - Multiple term negotiations (cap, rate, trigger)
  - Document revisions back-and-forth (5+ iterations)
  - Investor legal counsel unfamiliar with startup formats

### Typical Delays & Solutions

| Issue | Delay | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Interest rate negotiation | +3-5 days | Market rate is 3-8%; come with ask and ceiling |
| Valuation cap pricing | +2-4 days | Use comparable Series A prices or previous rounds |
| Conversion trigger ambiguity | +2-3 days | Define: "Series A" = minimum $500K at $XM cap |
| Multiple note documents | +5-10 days | Use same template for all investors; batch close |
| Investor legal review delays | +3-7 days | Provide SVB template; most accept with minimal changes |

### When to Use Parallel vs. Sequential Notes

**Parallel Strategy (3-5 investors, 4-6 weeks):**
- Close first note with 1-2 investors
- Announce to other interested investors: "First note closing Thursday"
- Others often close same week to avoid losing opportunity
- Reduces total time from 12+ weeks to 4-6 weeks

**Sequential Strategy (1 investor at a time):**
- Only use if you absolutely cannot manage multiple processes
- Time: 12-20 weeks for multiple notes
- Better for: Extremely resource-constrained founders

### Optimal Timing for Convertible Notes

**Best Months to Launch:**
- **September-October:** Before Series A season (Q1), show momentum
- **March-May:** Bridge rounds in advance of Q3 Series A closes
- **Avoid:** November-December (investors in holiday mode), January (diligence backlog)

**Recommended Prep Timeline:**
- 8 weeks before: Start investor outreach
- 6 weeks before: Schedule initial investor meetings
- 4 weeks before: Have pitch deck perfected
- 2 weeks before: Begin term sheet discussions
- Week 1-2: Execution phase

## III. Angel Round: Early Growth Stage (4-8 Weeks)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Angel Sourcing & Initial Meetings (2-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Identify angel investors (warm introductions preferred)
  - Schedule 30-minute coffee/call meetings with 15-30 potential angels
  - Pitch brief (5-10 minutes) on why this person
  - Investor indicates interest level (yes/maybe/no)
  - Typical conversion: 5-10 yes's from 25 asks

- **Founder Time:** 30-40 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Create target angel list (segment by industry, network, check size)
  - Prepare 1-page angel memo (problem, solution, team, ask)
  - Have 30-second elevator pitch perfected
  - Set up meeting calendar blocks (10-15 meetings/week)
  - Document follow-ups needed

#### Phase 2: Deeper Engagement & Due Diligence (2-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - 1-hour meetings with serious angels
  - Present full pitch deck (20 slides)
  - Discuss investment terms:
    - Equity % or SAFE/note preference
    - Valuation range
    - Timeline expectations
  - Angels conduct informal reference checks
  - Customer visits or demo sessions (if requested)
  - Angel may introduce other potential investors

- **Founder Time:** 30-40 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare 20-slide pitch deck (with detailed financials slide)
  - Customer reference list ready
  - Demo environment stable and rehearsed
  - Prepare 3-5 customer success stories
  - Cap table and dilution analysis ready

#### Phase 3: Terms Negotiation (1-2 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Angel asks detailed questions:
    - Use of funds
    - Financial projections
    - Competitive landscape
    - Team capability
    - Exit timeline expectations
  - Negotiate terms:
    - Equity stake (typically 0.5%-2% per angel)
    - SAFE vs. equity decision
    - Valuation discussion
    - Investor rights (board seat, information rights, pro-rata)
  - Multiple angels close simultaneously or sequentially

- **Founder Time:** 20-25 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare detailed cap table with dilution scenarios
  - Financial model with 3-year projections
  - Use of funds breakdown (monthly)
  - Board meeting to approve terms
  - Investor update template prepared

#### Phase 4: Legal Documentation (1-2 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Legal structure chosen: SAFE, convertible note, or equity (SAFEs most common for angels)
  - Documents drafted
  - Investor counsel reviews (if they hire counsel)
  - Final document versions prepared
  - Shareholder agreements finalized

- **Founder Time:** 8-12 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Use SAFE or standard note template
  - Prepare shareholder registry
  - Authorize board to execute documents
  - Have notary/execution plan ready
  - Prepare cap table update schedule

#### Phase 5: Closing (1 week)
- **Activities:**
  - All angels execute documents in batches
  - Funds transfer (wire, check, crypto)
  - Cap table updated in Carta or similar
  - Angel investor onboarding (information rights, updates schedule)

- **Founder Time:** 5-10 hours

### Fastest Path (3-4 weeks)
- **Conditions:**
  - You have warm angel network (50+ people who know you)
  - Use SAFE agreements exclusively (no term negotiation)
  - Angels decide quickly (experienced investors)
  - Minimal due diligence (relationship-based trust)
  - Sequential closing (first angel closes, others follow rapidly)

- **Example Timeline:**
  - Week 1: 20 investor meetings scheduled
  - Week 2: 8 angels say "yes, on SAFEs"
  - Week 3: All SAFEs executed
  - Week 4: Funds received

### Slowest Path (12+ weeks)
- **Conditions:**
  - Cold outreach required (no warm network)
  - Angels want detailed due diligence
  - Equity structure (not SAFEs) with complex terms
  - Multiple rounds of negotiation
  - Angels require board seat and governance rights

### Parallel vs. Sequential Angel Strategies

**Parallel (Recommended for Angels): Multiple Simultaneous Investors**
- Target 8-12 angels simultaneously
- Close first 2-3, announce "we're raising X and already at Y%"
- Momentum drives others to close faster
- Total timeline: 4-6 weeks
- Psychological advantage: FOMO drives faster decisions

**Sequential (Not Recommended):**
- Close one angel, move to next
- Total timeline: 12-20 weeks
- Risk: Each succeeding angel demands better terms than previous

### Common Angel Round Delays

| Delay Factor | Typical Addition | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Finding warm intros | +2-3 weeks | Start outreach 6 weeks prior; create target list immediately |
| Angels wanting equity (not SAFEs) | +2-3 weeks | Offer SAFEs first; only negotiate equity if critical investor |
| Due diligence requests | +1-2 weeks | Have data room ready; customer references pre-arranged |
| Board seat/governance negotiation | +1-2 weeks | Agree upfront (most angels don't require seats) |
| Angels demanding 2x changes | +1-2 weeks | Use standard terms; accept max 1 revision per investor |

### Optimal Timing for Angel Rounds

**Best Windows:**
- **January-February:** New year capital planning, tax-deductible investments
- **June-July:** Mid-year funding decision, pre-Series A prep
- **September-October:** Post-summer proof of traction, fall momentum
- **Avoid:** July (summer vacations), November-December (holidays)

### Founder Commitment by Phase

| Phase | Hours/Week | Percentage of Time |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing & meetings | 30-40 hrs | 75-100% (other activities paused) |
| Due diligence & pitch refinement | 20-30 hrs | 50-75% |
| Terms negotiation | 15-20 hrs | 40-50% |
| Legal & documentation | 10-15 hrs | 25-40% |
| Closing | 5-10 hrs | 10-25% |

## IV. Seed Round: Institutional Capital (2-3 Months)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Investor Sourcing & Initial Meetings (3-4 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Identify 50-100 seed-stage VCs using Crunchbase, PitchBook
  - Create warm introduction strategy
  - Schedule 20-minute initial pitches with seed funds
  - Typical conversion: 10-15 fund meetings from 50 asks
  - Goal: 3-5 interested lead investors

- **Founder Time:** 40-60 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Create target VC list (sorted by focus area, check size, pace)
  - Research 30-second pitch for each firm
  - Prepare one-pager (product, problem, team, traction, ask)
  - Document each meeting outcome
  - Schedule follow-up meetings with interested VCs

#### Phase 2: Deep Pitch Cycle & Due Diligence (2-4 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Multiple pitches to 3-5 seed funds (30-40 total meeting hours)
  - Full 20-slide deck presentations
  - Partner meetings (not just associates)
  - VCs conduct detailed diligence:
    - Customer reference calls (3-5 calls)
    - Technical diligence (if applicable)
    - Financial analysis (cap table, burn rate, runway)
    - Team background checks
    - Competitive landscape analysis
  - Multiple follow-up questions and meetings

- **Founder Time:** 50-80 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare detailed pitch deck (25-30 slides)
  - Customer reference list with contact info
  - Financial model with 5-year projections
  - Competitive matrix analysis
  - Team bios and background materials
  - Shareholder agreement templates ready
  - Pitch rehearsal (internal + with advisors)

#### Phase 3: Term Sheet Negotiation (1-2 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Lead VC prepares term sheet
  - Key terms negotiated:
    - Valuation (pre- and post-money)
    - Investment amount
    - Founder/employee equity (dilution)
    - Board composition (lead VC gets board seat typically)
    - Investor rights (pro-rata, information rights, approval rights)
    - Liquidation preferences (typically 1x non-participating preferred)
    - Anti-dilution clause (broad-based weighted average typical)
  - Negotiation typically 2-4 rounds
  - Follow-on investors often accept lead's terms

- **Founder Time:** 30-40 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare board meeting to approve terms
  - Have term sheet counsel review (if hiring attorney)
  - Negotiate valuation confidently (compare to similar companies)
  - Prepare responses to expected investor questions
  - Document cap table impact
  - Plan for follow-on investor strategy

#### Phase 4: Due Diligence Deep Dive (2-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Extended diligence:
    - Legal review (IP, contracts, litigation, liability)
    - Customer contract analysis
    - Employee agreements check
    - Compliance review (data protection, regulations)
    - Technology assessment (code review, architecture)
    - Revenue model validation
  - Background checks on team
  - Reference calls (customers, prior employers)
  - Advisor calls from investor network
  - Tax analysis (QSBS eligibility, structure verification)

- **Founder Time:** 30-50 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Organize data room (digital folder with all documents)
  - Prepare to answer detailed technical questions
  - Customer revenue/retention documentation
  - Employee roster with equity grants
  - IP assignment agreements
  - Insurance policies
  - Lease/real estate documentation
  - Banking arrangements documentation

#### Phase 5: Legal Documentation & Stock Issuance (2-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Stock purchase agreement drafted by investor counsel
  - Disclosure schedules prepared (exceptions to representations)
  - Exhibits prepared (cap table, customer list, financial projections)
  - Investor counsel and founder counsel negotiate terms
  - Equity documents finalized:
    - Stock option plan (if not existing)
    - 83(b) elections filed
    - Board resolutions prepared
    - Amendment to articles of incorporation
  - Closing statement prepared

- **Founder Time:** 20-30 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Review legal documents thoroughly (hire counsel if budget allows)
  - Prepare board meeting documentation
  - Ensure all reps and warranties can be satisfied
  - Negotiate indemnification terms
  - Plan for 83(b) election filing (must file within 30 days)
  - Confirm use of proceeds plan

#### Phase 6: Closing (1 week)
- **Activities:**
  - Final due diligence complete
  - Closing checklist verified
  - Wire instructions confirmed
  - Documents executed
  - Funds deposited
  - Cap table updated with new stock grants

- **Founder Time:** 5-10 hours

### Typical Seed Round Timeline Breakdown

| Activity | Weeks | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing & initial meetings | 3-4 | 3-4 |
| Deep pitch cycle & VC diligence | 2-4 | 5-8 |
| Term sheet negotiation | 1-2 | 6-10 |
| Legal due diligence | 2-3 | 8-13 |
| Documentation & closing | 2-3 | 10-16 |
| **Total** | **12-16 weeks** | **2-4 months** |

### Fastest Path (6-8 weeks)
- **Conditions:**
  - You have strong VC relationships (partner personally knows you)
  - Minimal due diligence needed (clean company, proven market, stellar team)
  - Term sheet accepted immediately (no negotiation)
  - Use accelerator-style standard docs
  - Parallel investor meetings (multiple VCs interested simultaneously)

- **Example:**
  - Week 1-2: 5 VC meetings (lead wants to move forward)
  - Week 3: Term sheet received
  - Week 4: Term sheet signed
  - Week 5-6: Legal diligence (parallel)
  - Week 7: Documentation complete
  - Week 8: Closing

### Slowest Path (6+ months)
- **Reasons:**
  - Need to find lead investor first (4+ weeks)
  - VCs want to see 1-2 quarters of traction (add 12-16 weeks)
  - Diligence concerns require resolutions
  - Multiple rounds of negotiation on terms
  - Follow-on investor approval delays

### Founder Time Commitment by Phase

| Phase | Hours/Week | Founder Availability |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing (weeks 1-4) | 40-60 hrs | 100% (core focus) |
| Pitch cycle (weeks 5-8) | 50-70 hrs | 100% (core focus) |
| Negotiation (weeks 9-10) | 30-40 hrs | 75% |
| Diligence (weeks 11-13) | 30-50 hrs | 75% |
| Documentation (weeks 14-16) | 20-30 hrs | 50% |

**Total Time Commitment:** 250-350 hours over 4 months

## V. Series A: Major Growth Round (3-6 Months)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Investor Sourcing (3-5 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Research 100-200 Series A funds using Crunchbase, PitchBook
  - Leverage warm intros from:
    - Seed investors
    - Board members/advisors
    - Other entrepreneurs in network
    - Service providers (lawyers, accountants)
  - Schedule initial 20-minute "coffee chats"
  - Target: 20-30 conversations leading to 5-7 partner meetings

- **Founder Time:** 60-80 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Create VC prospect database (fund name, partner names, focus, check size)
  - Prepare custom 30-second pitches per fund
  - Research each partner's portfolio (personalize each outreach)
  - Prepare one-pager with product demo link
  - Document every conversation in CRM

#### Phase 2: Partner Pitch Cycle (4-6 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Present to 5-7 Series A funds
  - Detailed pitches to partners (30-45 minutes each)
  - Multiple meetings with same firm (associate → partner → managing partner)
  - Partners bring questions from their network (advisors, other investors)
  - Demo sessions with full team
  - Site visits (if location relevant)
  - Total meeting time: 30-40 hours of founder pitch time

- **Founder Time:** 80-120 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare 30-slide Series A pitch deck
  - Customer case studies (3-5 detailed stories)
  - Product walkthrough presentation
  - Financial model (3-5 year projections)
  - Competitive landscape deck
  - Market size analysis (TAM, SAM, SOM)
  - Team backgrounds and bios
  - Traction metrics dashboard
  - Investor update template

#### Phase 3: Diligence Begins (parallel, 4-6 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - VCs assign diligence team
  - Diligence questions submitted (50-100 questions)
  - Deep financial analysis:
    - Revenue model deep dive
    - Unit economics analysis
    - Burn rate and runway
    - Cap table analysis with dilution scenarios
  - Reference calls (10-15 customer calls)
  - Technology assessment (code review, scalability analysis)
  - Legal review (contracts, IP, litigation, regulatory)

- **Founder Time:** 80-100 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Create detailed data room (organized virtual folder)
  - Prepare to answer 100+ diligence questions
  - Customer reference list with metrics
  - Detailed use of funds breakdown
  - Competitor comparison analysis
  - Market validation documentation
  - Employee cap table with equity grants
  - All contracts and agreements

#### Phase 4: Term Sheet & Negotiation (1-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Lead investor prepares term sheet
  - Key terms for negotiation:
    - Valuation ($X million pre-money and post-money)
    - Check size (typically $500K-$3M+ for Series A)
    - Equity stake for investors
    - Founder equity impact (new option pool? grants?)
    - Board composition (1-2 investor directors + founder)
    - Governance rights (approval rights, protective provisions)
    - Anti-dilution clause (broad-based weighted average typical)
    - Liquidation preference (1x non-participating preferred)
    - Voting rights and preference structure
  - Negotiation takes 2-4 weeks (multiple rounds)
  - Board meeting to approve terms

- **Founder Time:** 40-60 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Hire legal counsel (or have investor's lawyer explain)
  - Prepare cap table impact analysis
  - Valuation comparison with similar companies
  - Negotiation strategy (what's critical vs. negotiable)
  - Board meeting materials
  - Approval documentation from other shareholders
  - Plan for follow-on investors (will they accept lead terms?)

#### Phase 5: Final Due Diligence (2-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Extended legal review by investor counsel
  - Final customer reference calls
  - Team background verification
  - Insurance documentation review
  - Material contracts review
  - Tax structure analysis
  - Regulatory compliance check (if industry-specific)
  - Final financial audit/review

- **Founder Time:** 30-50 hours

#### Phase 6: Stock Purchase Agreement & Documentation (2-3 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Comprehensive stock purchase agreement drafted
  - Disclosure schedules (exceptions to reps and warranties)
  - Exhibits (cap table, customers, employees, contracts)
  - Registration rights agreement (if applicable)
  - Stockholders' agreement (voting agreements)
  - Employee matters agreement
  - Investor rights agreement
  - Amendment and restatement of articles
  - Officer certificates and board resolutions

- **Founder Time:** 30-40 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Review all legal documents with counsel
  - Negotiate specific reps and warranties
  - Ensure management representations are accurate
  - Prepare closing statement
  - Coordinate with all shareholder signatories
  - File SEC filings (Form D if required)

#### Phase 7: Closing (1 week)
- **Activities:**
  - Final due diligence condition checklist
  - Last representations verified
  - Closing documents signed
  - Wire transfer received
  - Cap table updated
  - New directors onboarded
  - Press release issued (optional)

- **Founder Time:** 5-10 hours

|---|---|---|
| Sourcing | 3-5 weeks | 3-5 |
| Partner pitches | 4-6 weeks | 7-11 |
| Diligence (parallel) | 4-6 weeks | 11-17 |

### Fastest Path (8-10 weeks)
- **Conditions:**

  - Minimal negotiation (accept standard terms)
  - Clean company (no complex legal issues)

### Slowest Path (9+ months)
- **Reasons:**
  - Need to build traction first (waiting for metrics)
  - Need to establish warm VCs relationships (no quick access)
  - Extensive due diligence (new market, complex technology)
  - Multiple rounds of negotiation
  - Legal complexity (international operations, regulatory issues)

## VI. Venture Debt: Capital Bridge (4-8 Weeks)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Sourcing & Initial Outreach (1-2 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Identify venture debt providers:
    - Specialized VD firms (Gold Hill, Silicon Valley Bank, Horizon)
    - Banks (Silicon Valley Bank, Western Alliance)
    - Non-bank lenders (Clearco, Lighter Capital)
  - Schedule 30-minute calls with 5-10 providers
  - Brief discussion of:
    - Amount needed
    - Use of proceeds
    - Runway implications
    - Series A timeline (critical for VD)
  - Provider indicates interest level

- **Founder Time:** 10-15 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Prepare 1-page venture debt summary (amount, use, timeline)
  - Have current cap table ready
  - Prepare financial model (12-month projection)
  - Create list of potential lenders (sorted by speed and terms)

#### Phase 2: Term Sheet Discussion (1-2 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Detailed conversations with 2-3 most interested lenders
  - Discuss detailed terms:
    - Principal amount
    - Interest rate (8-15% typical)
    - Warrant coverage (10-25% of principal typical)
    - Maturity date (24-36 months typical)
    - Prepayment terms
    - Covenants (revenue milestones, minimum cash balance)
    - Monthly vs. quarterly interest payments
  - Term sheet issued (usually quick, 3-5 days)
  - Evaluation of best offer

- **Founder Time:** 10-15 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Create comparison spreadsheet (rate, warrants, covenants)
  - Prepare board presentation
  - Negotiate critical terms (warrant coverage, covenants)
  - Confirm Series A timeline (VD lenders want Series A visibility)

#### Phase 3: Due Diligence (2-4 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Lender conducts financial due diligence:
    - Revenue model validation
    - Unit economics
    - Burn rate and runway
    - Cap table review
    - Prior debt obligations
  - May require Series A commitments (not contracts, just strong signals)
  - Lender references your Series A investors
  - May want revenue/metrics verification

- **Founder Time:** 10-15 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Provide detailed financials (monthly P&Ls, cash projections)
  - Written confirmation of Series A timeline (email from lead investor ideal)
  - Revenue documentation (contracts, customer agreements)
  - Answer financial questions (lenders may ask 20-30 questions)

#### Phase 4: Term Sheet & Loan Agreement (1-2 weeks)
- **Activities:**
  - Final term sheet signed
  - Loan agreement drafted by lender counsel
  - Warrant agreement documentation
  - Representations and warranties
  - Covenant documentation (monthly reporting requirements)
  - Board resolutions approving debt

- **Founder Time:** 5-10 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Review loan agreement (relatively standard across VD providers)
  - Board meeting to approve venture debt
  - Prepare for monthly reporting requirements (you'll need to provide)
  - Understand covenant implications

#### Phase 5: Closing (1 week)
- **Activities:**
  - Final documents executed
  - Wire transfer of funds
  - Warrants issued and registered
  - Cap table updated
  - Lender dashboard access provided

- **Founder Time:** 2-5 hours

### Venture Debt Timeline Summary

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Sourcing & initial calls | 1-2 weeks |
| Term sheet discussion | 1-2 weeks |
| Due diligence | 2-4 weeks |
| Loan agreement | 1-2 weeks |
| Closing | 1 week |
| **Total** | **4-8 weeks** |

### Fastest Path (2-3 weeks)
- **Conditions:**
  - Existing Series A investor relationship (lender calls them to confirm)
  - Minimal due diligence (strong financials, clear business model)
  - Standard terms (accept rates and warrants without negotiation)
  - Digital execution only

### Why Venture Debt is Faster than Equity
- **No valuation needed:** Doesn't affect cap table valuation
- **Minimal due diligence:** Lender cares about repayment ability, not market opportunity
- **Standard documents:** Loan agreements are relatively standardized
- **Quick underwriting:** Financial analysis simpler than venture capital

### Common Delays in Venture Debt Rounds

| Delay | Typical Addition | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Series A timeline unclear | +2-3 weeks | Get written confirmation from Series A lead investor |
| Financial data missing | +1-2 weeks | Have 12-month financials ready before outreach |
| Covenant terms dispute | +1 week | Understand what "revenue milestone" means upfront |
| Multiple debt facilities | +1-2 weeks | Don't take VD from multiple sources (creates conflicts) |

### Optimal Timing for Venture Debt

**Best Timing:** 6-9 months BEFORE Series A close
- Gives you 12+ months of runway before Series A required
- Shows Series A investors you're capital efficient
- Avoids desperation/low valuation dynamics

**Avoid:** Within 3 months of Series A closing (reduces Series A valuation)

## VII. Revenue-Based Financing (RBF): Quick Capital (2-6 Weeks)

### Timeline Breakdown

#### Phase 1: Initial Application (1-3 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Choose RBF platform/provider:
    - Marketplace (Pipe: connect once, see 50+ offers)
    - Direct (Clearco, Lighter Capital, Capchase)
  - Submit application (10-20 minutes typically)
  - Connect financial data sources:
    - Stripe, Shopify, PayPal (e-commerce)
    - QuickBooks, Xero (accounting)
    - Bank accounts (cash flow)
  - Application submitted and received

- **Founder Time:** 1-2 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Gather business login credentials (Stripe, accounting software)
  - Prepare basic business description
  - Decide: want marketplace comparison or direct with single lender?

#### Phase 2: Initial Underwriting & Assessment (2-7 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Automated underwriting review
  - System analyzes:
    - Transaction history (minimum 3 months)
    - Revenue trends (growth, seasonality)
    - Business model viability
    - Churn rate (if SaaS)
  - Pre-qualification decision issued
  - Preliminary offer terms provided

- **Founder Time:** 0.5-1 hour
- **Action Items:**
  - Monitor email for pre-qualification decision
  - Prepare for possible follow-up questions
  - Evaluate pre-qual offer (if interested, move forward)

#### Phase 3: Detailed Underwriting (3-10 business days)
- **Activities:**
  - Deeper financial analysis
  - Questions about business model, strategy, use of funds
  - Customer verification (some providers call customers)
  - Risk assessment
  - Offer finalized and presented

- **Founder Time:** 2-5 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Answer underwriting questions (provide within 24 hours)
  - Prepare financials documentation if requested
  - Customer contact list ready
  - Use of funds explanation prepared

#### Phase 4: Offer Review & Decision (1-5 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Receive formal offer:
    - Principal amount
    - Repayment cap (1.3x-1.5x)
    - Monthly repayment percentage
    - Contract terms
  - Review terms carefully
  - Negotiate if needed (rates/terms may be flexible)
  - Accept offer

- **Founder Time:** 2-3 hours
- **Action Items:**
  - Compare multiple offers (if using marketplace)
  - Understand repayment timeline implications
  - Ask lender to clarify any terms
  - Accept offer digitally

#### Phase 5: Closing & Fund Disbursement (2-7 days)
- **Activities:**
  - Final agreement executed (DocuSign)
  - Bank account verification
  - Funds transferred (typically ACH or wire)
  - Repayment schedule established
  - Dashboard access provided

- **Founder Time:** 0.5-1 hour
- **Action Items:**
  - Execute final agreement

## X. Parallel vs. Sequential Fundraising Strategies

### When to Run Parallel Fundraising (RECOMMENDED)

**Best Use Cases:**

1. **SAFE → Convertible Note → Series A (Sequential)**
   - **Timeline:** SAFE (weeks 1-2) → Close → Convertible Note (weeks 3-8) → Close → Series A (weeks 9-20)
   - **Why:** Each round provides market validation for next
   - **Total time:** 20 weeks (vs. 30+ if sequential with same investor base)

2. **Multiple SAFEs (Parallel)**
   - **Timeline:** Months 1-2 (close 5-10 SAFEs)
   - **Why:** Momentum accelerates subsequent closes
   - **Total time:** 6-8 weeks for all closes (vs. 15-20+ sequentially)

3. **Multiple Angels (Parallel)**
   - **Timeline:** Weeks 1-6 (target 8-12 angels, close batches)
   - **Why:** FOMO effect drives faster decisions
   - **Total time:** 4-6 weeks total (vs. 15-20+ sequentially)

4. **Series A + Venture Debt (Parallel)**
   - **Timeline:** Start VD process 6-9 months before Series A close
   - **Why:** VD adds buffer, doesn't affect Series A valuation
   - **Total time:** Reduces Series A timeline by removing urgency

### When to Run Sequential Fundraising

**Use Cases:**

1. **Very early stage (pre-product)**
   - Sequential makes sense: need validation before approaching VCs
   - Timeline: Build product → Angel round → Seed → Series A

2. **Founder is sole operator (resource constraint)**
   - Can't manage multiple processes simultaneously
   - Focus on one round completely, then next

3. **Major company pivot/changes**
   - New product requires new narrative
   - Finish implementation, then fundraise

### Gantt Chart: Parallel Fundraising Timeline

```
PARALLEL FUNDRAISING (RECOMMENDED)
================================

Month 1
  Week 1-2: SAFE Investor 1-3 (close simultaneously)

Month 2
  Week 5-6: Convertible Note Investors 1-2 (while SAFEs closing)
  Week 7-8: Convert Notes close

Month 3-4
  Week 9-14: Series A sourcing (while notes close)
  Week 15-16: Partner pitches

Month 5-6
  Week 17-20: Term sheet negotiation + diligence
  Week 21-24: Documentation
  Week 25: Closing

Total: 6 months (vs. 9-12 months sequential)

SEQUENTIAL FUNDRAISING (NOT RECOMMENDED)
=======================================

Month 1-2: SAFE Round (complete all activities)
Month 3-4: Convertible Note Round (start after SAFEs close)
Month 5-6: Series A Sourcing & Pitches
Month 7-10: Series A Diligence & Documentation

Total: 10 months (vs. 6 months parallel)
```

- Seed & Series A (post-Q1 proof of traction)

- RBF for e-commerce: Final application window

**Best For:**
- Series A (major fundraising month, Q3 results visible)
- Seed rounds (strong funders executing H2 plans)

**Why:** Q3 results strong, Series A investors actively deploying, Fall conferences

**Activities:**
- Launch Series A (largest fundraising month)

**Best For:**
- Series A diligence/closing (deals sourced in Sep-Oct)

| Missing financial documentation | +3-7 days | Have organized data room ready (Google Drive with sections) |

| IP ownership unclear | +5-10 days | Have IP assignment agreements with all founders signed |
| Customer reference unavailable | +3-5 days | Pre-confirm all references before giving list; prep them |
| Legal/compliance questions | +1-2 weeks | Have lawyer review corporate structure before fundraising |

### Legal & Documentation Delays

| Delay | Typical Addition | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Cap table errors | +2-5 days | Audit cap table quarterly; use Carta or cap table software |
| Signer authority issues | +1-2 days | Confirm CEO signing authority in bylaws/board resolutions |
| Missing board resolutions | +1-2 days | Have legal template ready; board meets monthly for approvals |
| Investor counsel disagreements | +3-7 days | Use standard templates (Y Combinator, SVB) that most accept |
| Missing exhibits/schedules | +2-3 days | Create document checklist week before anticipated closing |

### Investor Decision Delays

| Delay | Typical Addition | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Partner hasn't decided yet | +1-3 weeks | Ask about decision timeline upfront; get commitment to timeline |
| Investment committee meeting delayed | +1-2 weeks | Understand VC's approval process and decision calendar |
| Due diligence questions unanswered | +2-4 days | Respond to ALL inquiries within 4 hours, not 24 |
| Valuation negotiation stalling | +2-4 days | Come with valuation data (comparables, SAM/TAM analysis) |
| Investor's fund not yet closed | +2-8 weeks | Ask about fund status in initial conversation; avoid unfunded VCs |

## XVI. Crisis Timeline: Accelerated Fundraising

### When You Need Capital FAST (< 8 weeks)

**Recommended Vehicles (in order of speed):**

1. **RBF (2-7 days):** Best if you have revenue
2. **SAFE (1-2 weeks):** Quick, no valuation
3. **Convertible Note (2-4 weeks):** Slightly slower but still fast
4. **Venture Debt (4-8 weeks):** If you can articulate Series A timeline
5. **Angels (4-6 weeks):** If you have network

**Avoid During Crisis:**
- Series A (too slow, takes 3-6 months)
- Equity rounds (complex legal, slow)
- Crowdfunding (requires months of PR)
- Grants (typically 3-12 months)

### Crisis Timeline Execution

**Week 1-2: Assess & Plan**
- [ ] Calculate exact runway (weeks remaining?)
- [ ] Determine minimum capital needed
- [ ] Identify fastest funding sources
- [ ] Activate advisor/investor network
- [ ] Prepare compact pitch (2 slides: problem + ask)

**Week 3-4: Launch Capital Raise**
- [ ] Apply for RBF (if you have revenue)
- [ ] Approach 15-20 angels with warm intros
- [ ] Contact VCs about convertible note bridge
- [ ] Explore venture debt options
- [ ] Priority: Speed over terms

**Week 5-6: Close First Sources**
- [ ] Close first RBF/SAFE/note
- [ ] Get 3-4 angels signed on
- [ ] Announce to network ("raising $X")
- [ ] Momentum attracts others quickly

**Week 7-8: Finalize & Deploy**
- [ ] Receive capital in bank
- [ ] Adjust burn rate based on capital
- [ ] Create new runway plan
- [ ] Plan next fundraising round (don't repeat crisis)

## Final Recommendations

### Best Practice Timeline by Stage

**Pre-Product**
- Timeline: 6-12 months
- Best Funding: Friends & family, angels
- Amount: $25K-$250K
- Process: Informal conversations, handshake deals

**Post-Launch, Pre-PMF (0-6 months)**
- Timeline: 2-4 months fundraising
- Best Funding: Angels, early seed
- Amount: $100K-$500K
- Process: SAFE agreements, informal pitch decks

**Proven Traction (6-18 months post-launch)**
- Timeline: 3-4 months fundraising
- Best Funding: Seed round or Series A
- Amount: $500K-$3M (Seed) / $1M-$10M+ (Series A)
- Process: Institutional investors, formal pitch decks, due diligence

**Growth Phase (18+ months post-launch)**
- Timeline: 4-6 months fundraising
- Best Funding: Series B, Series C, corporate rounds
- Amount: $5M-$50M+
- Process: Formal process, extensive diligence, multiple investors

### Success Metrics for Each Phase

Track these metrics during fundraising to improve timeline:

1. **Meeting conversion rate:** Target 20-30% of meetings → interested investors
2. **Interest to term sheet:** Target 30-50% of interested investors → term sheet
3. **Term sheet to close:** Target 80-90% of term sheets → actual funding
4. **Overall close rate:** 5-10% of initial investor conversations → capital

If conversion rates are lower, you likely need:
- Better product/traction
- Clearer pitch/story
- More targeted investor list
- Better warm introductions

## Conclusion

Fundraising timelines vary dramatically by vehicle type and stage:

**Fastest:** RBF (2-7 days) and SAFEs (1-2 weeks)
**Medium:** Convertible notes (2-4 weeks), angel rounds (4-8 weeks), venture debt (4-8 weeks)
**Slowest:** Series A (3-6 months), seed rounds (2-3 months), grants (3-12 months)

**Key Principles:**
1. Plan 6 months ahead of your capital needs
2. Run parallel fundraising when possible (not sequential)
3. Optimize for the fastest option that fits your situation
4. Prepare materials early (doesn't cost extra time)
5. Track metrics and adjust strategy mid-process
6. Avoid crisis fundraising through better planning

Use the templates and checklists in this guide to ensure you're ready and can execute within these timelines.
