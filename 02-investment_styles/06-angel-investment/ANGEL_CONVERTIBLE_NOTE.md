## 2. Conversion Upon Equity Financing

### 2.1 Automatic Conversion Trigger

**This Note will automatically convert to Preferred Stock upon:**

Occurrence of "Equity Financing," defined as:

A bona fide transaction in which Company issues shares of Preferred Stock for a minimum purchase of $[MINIMUM_INVESTMENT] in new capital.

Typical minimum: $250K-$1M

*If Series A is smaller than threshold, Note does NOT auto-convert. Investor chooses.*

### 2.2 Conversion Mechanics

**Upon Conversion:**

The principal amount plus accrued interest (unless forgiven) converts to Preferred Stock.

**Conversion Price Formula:**

```
Conversion Price = Series A Price × (1 - Conversion Discount)

OR

Conversion Price = Series A Price CAPPED at Valuation Cap

Whichever is LOWER (better for investor)
```

**Conversion Example:**

```
Investor: $100K principal + $5K accrued interest = $105K total
Series A price: $2.00/share
Conversion Discount: 20%
Valuation Cap: $5,000,000

Calculation:
Option A: $2.00 × (1 - 0.20) = $1.60/share
Option B: $5M cap / [total shares outstanding] = estimated $1.50/share
WINNER: $1.50/share

Shares received: $105,000 / $1.50 = 70,000 shares
```

### 2.3 Conversion Terms

**Conversion Discount Rate:** [DISCOUNT_PERCENTAGE]%

Typical range: 20-30%

*Discount compensates investor for risk taken early.*

**Valuation Cap:** $[VALUATION_CAP]

*Caps maximum price investor converts at, protecting against up rounds.*

**Definition of Valuation Cap:**
The maximum implied pre-money valuation at which conversion occurs, regardless of actual Series A price.

## 3. Investor Rights & Protections

### 3.1 Creditor Rights (As Noteholder)

**Until Conversion, Investor Has:**
- [ ] Claim as creditor (ahead of equity holders in bankruptcy)
- [ ] Interest payments (accrued but not paid until maturity)
- [ ] Right to information (financials, business updates)
- [ ] Right to participate in conversion on stated terms

### 3.2 Upon Conversion to Equity

**After Conversion, Investor Becomes:**
- Preferred shareholder
- Subject to all rights/obligations of Series A investors
- Loses creditor status
- Gains pro-rata participation rights (if negotiated)

### 3.3 Pro-Rata Rights (Optional)

**Does Investor Have Pro-Rata Rights After Conversion?** [YES / NO]

If YES:
- In future rounds, investor can invest to maintain ownership %
- Applies to Series B, C, etc.
- Must invest on same terms as new investors
- Optional (investor chooses to participate)

### 3.4 Information Rights

**Prior to Conversion:**

Investor receives:
- [ ] Monthly financial updates (P&L, metrics)
- [ ] Quarterly management reports
- [ ] Annual audit (if applicable)
- [ ] Access to data room
- [ ] Notice of major business events

**After Conversion:**

Investor becomes preferred shareholder, entitled to:
- Standard preferred stock information rights
- Board updates (if applicable)
- Same info as other preferred investors

## 4. Default & Maturity

### 4.1 Maturity Date

**Note Matures:** [MATURITY_DATE]

If Series A has NOT occurred by maturity, the following happens:

**Option A: Automatic Conversion at Cap**
- Note automatically converts to equity at Valuation Cap
- Company must issue preferred shares
- Interest accrual stops

**Option B: Note Becomes Payable**
- Company owes full principal + accrued interest in cash
- Investor can demand payment
- If unpaid, constitutes default
- (Most common - forces Series A or restructuring)

**Option C: Automatic Extension**
- Note extends [X] years automatically
- New maturity date: [DATE]
- Interest continues to accrue
- Conversion terms unchanged

*Most notes use Option B (creates urgency for Series A).*

### 4.2 Default Events

**Note is in Default if Company:**
1. Fails to deliver Series A conversion by Maturity Date, OR
2. Fails to pay principal + accrued interest by [X] days after maturity, OR
3. Becomes insolvent or files bankruptcy, OR
4. Dissolves or liquidates without converting note

**Upon Default:**

Investor can:
- Demand full payment of principal + accrued interest
- Foreclose on company assets (unusual)
- Join with other creditors in bankruptcy
- Negotiate restructuring/extension

## 5. Special Investor Protections

### 5.1 Most Favored Nation (MFN) Rights

**Does Investor Have MFN?** [YES / NO]

If YES:
- If company issues another convertible note with better terms within [X months], this note gets same terms
- "Better" typically means: lower conversion price OR higher discount rate OR lower interest rate
- Automatic adjustment (no negotiation needed)

**Example:**
"If Company issues another convertible note at 25% discount and this note was at 20%, Investor's note automatically adjusts to 25%."

### 5.2 Anti-Dilution Protection

**Does Investor Have Anti-Dilution?** [YES / NO]

If YES, upon down round:

**Weighted Average:**
- Conversion price adjusts downward
- Math-heavy, protects against down rounds
- "Narrow" vs. "Broad" (broad is more investor-friendly)

**Full Ratchet:**
- Investor gets full benefit of any lower pricing
- Harsh on company
- Rare in angel notes

### 5.3 Demand Registration Rights (For Future Exit)

**Rare for angel convertible notes, but if included:**

Investor can demand company register shares for public sale (if/when IPO occurs).

*Usually only in institutional investor notes.*

## 9. Tax Treatment

**IMPORTANT: This is not tax advice. Consult a tax professional.**

### 9.1 For Investor (Typical)

**At Investment:**
- No immediate tax event
- No income recognized

**Upon Conversion:**
- Investor's basis = principal + accrued interest
- Preferred stock acquired at "conversion price"
- Potential tax deferral (Section 1202, etc.)
- Future dividend/gains taxable

**On Exit (Sale or IPO):**
- Capital gains tax on profit above basis
- Long-term if held 12+ months
- Potential Section 1202 exclusion (if qualified)

### 9.2 For Company

**Interest Deduction:**
- Company can deduct interest on note
- Reduces taxable income
- Consult accountant on deductibility

**Upon Conversion:**
- No expense (conversion is balance sheet event)
- No gain/loss recognized
- Preferred shares issued at conversion price

## 13. Conversion Mechanics Walkthrough

### 13.1 Complete Example

**Initial Note:**
- Principal: $100,000
- Interest Rate: 5% annual
- Valuation Cap: $5,000,000
- Conversion Discount: 20%
- Maturity Date: [DATE - 2 years out]

**Timeline:**

**Year 1 (No activity):**
- Accrued interest: $5,000
- Note balance: $105,000
- Status: Outstanding, accruing interest

**Year 2 (Series A occurs):**
- Company raises $2M Series A
- Valuation: $8M pre-money, $10M post-money
- Series A price: $2.00/share

**Conversion Calculation:**

Step 1: Accrued interest
- Year 1: $5,000
- Year 2 (through Series A): $5,250 (simple interest)
- Total accrued: $10,250
- New total: $110,250

Step 2: Calculate conversion price
- Series A price: $2.00/share
- Discount: 20%
- Discounted: $2.00 × 0.80 = $1.60/share
- Valuation cap: $5M / [outstanding shares] = $1.25/share
- Effective price: $1.25/share (lower = better)

Step 3: Calculate shares
- Conversion amount: $110,250
- Conversion price: $1.25/share
- Shares received: $110,250 / $1.25 = **88,200 shares**

**Result:**
- Investor owns 88,200 shares of Series A Preferred
- Interest was "capitalized" into equity (not paid in cash)
- Investor now has same rights as other Series A investors
- Original note no longer exists

## 17. FAQ - Convertible Notes for Angels

**Q: How is this different from a SAFE?**
A: Note has interest + maturity date (debt). SAFE has neither. Note is more protective for investor.

**Q: What if company is in trouble at maturity?**
A: Investor can demand repayment OR negotiate extension OR convert at cap. Investor has leverage.

**Q: Is interest actually paid?**
A: Usually not. Interest accrues and converts to equity. Only paid if company doesn't raise Series A or goes public.

**Q: What if there's a down round?**
A: Investor converts at Valuation Cap (protected). No additional shares issued for down round.

**Q: Can I negotiate better terms?**
A: Yes. Interest rate, cap, discount, and maturity date are all negotiable. MFN and pro-rata are common requests.

## 18. When to Use Convertible Notes

**Use a Convertible Note When:**
- You expect Series A within 18-24 months (✓)
- You want investor protection from down rounds (✓)
- Timing of equity is uncertain (✓)
- You want faster negotiations than SAFE (✓)
- You want interest accrual for investor advantage (✓)

**Don't Use a Convertible Note When:**
- You don't expect Series A for 3+ years (use SAFE)
- You want simplest possible document (use SAFE)
- You want no debt on balance sheet (use SAFE)
- Your company has no path to Series A (use Priced Equity instead)
