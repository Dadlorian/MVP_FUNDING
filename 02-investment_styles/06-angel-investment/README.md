# Angel Investment: Complete Guide

## Overview

Angel investment is capital from high-net-worth individuals (typically $25K-$500K per investor) in early-stage companies. Unlike institutional investors, angels are flexible, fast, and often provide mentorship alongside capital.

---

## What Is Angel Investment?

### The Basics
- **Amount per investor**: $25K-$500K (often smaller)
- **Timeline**: 2-12 weeks from commitment to capital
- **Equity given**: 3-10% per angel (highly variable)
- **Typical structure**: SAFE, Convertible Note, Priced Equity, or Simple Agreement
- **Decision makers**: Individual investors (not committees)
- **Follow-on capital**: NOT guaranteed

### Angel vs. Institutional Rounds

| Aspect | Angel Round | Seed VC | Series A |
|--------|-------------|---------|----------|
| **Check size** | $25K-$500K | $250K-$2M | $500K-$5M+ |
| **Decision time** | 2-12 weeks | 4-12 weeks | 8-16 weeks |
| **Due diligence** | Light (informal) | Medium | Heavy |
| **Legal docs** | Simple (SAFE/Note) | Medium complexity | Complex (TS) |
| **Follow-on** | Unlikely | Often | Expected |
| **Investor type** | Individual (wealthy) | Fund (manager) | Fund (manager) |
| **Board seat** | No | Possible | Yes |
| **Advising** | Often | Sometimes | Yes |

## Why Raise from Angels?

### Advantages for Founders
- **Speed**: Fast decisions (no committee)
- **Flexibility**: Creative structures possible
- **Relationships**: Mentorship + capital + networks
- **No follow-on requirement**: Free to raise elsewhere
- **Founder-friendly**: Generally less aggressive than VCs
- **Lower dilution on SAFE/Notes**: Convertibles = less ownership given upfront

### When Angels Make Sense
- Pre-product or early product
- $500K-$2M total needed
- Building networks is a priority
- You want speed + flexibility
- You're not pursuing venture (>$10M raise)

## Common Angel Deal Structures

### 1. SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
**Best for:** First-time founders, non-dilutive initial capital
- No equity upfront
- Converts at next priced round
- $100K typical minimum per SAFE

**Pros:**
- Simple 3-page document
- Fast to negotiate
- Founder-friendly
- Non-dilutive at raise

**Cons:**
- Doesn't give investor rights
- Can stack into large obligations
- No equity incentive

### 2. Convertible Note
**Best for:** When timing of Series A is uncertain
- Like a loan but converts to equity
- Interest accrues (3-8%)
- Discount (20-30%) on conversion
- Maturity date (typically 2-3 years)

**Pros:**
- Investor gets downside protection (loan)
- Founder gets time before equity sale
- Clear terms
- Tax-efficient for investor

**Cons:**
- Creates debt on balance sheet
- Interest accrues over time
- Maturity risk

### 3. Priced Equity Round
**Best for:** Investors wanting clear ownership
- Immediate equity stake
- Standard Series Seed or Series A-1
- Clear valuation set upfront
- Full investor rights

**Pros:**
- Investor has clear ownership
- Standard investor protections
- Professional appearance
- Less ambiguity

**Cons:**
- Most dilutive upfront
- Complex legal docs
- Longer negotiation
- Caps table complexity

### 4. Angel Syndicate Structure
**Best for:** Coordinating multiple angels
- Lead investor negotiates
- Other angels follow on same terms
- Shared due diligence
- 1-2 decision makers instead of 10

**Pros:**
- Cleaner cap table
- Faster coordinated close
- Less founder time on repetition
- Professional appearance

**Cons:**
- Gives lead investor power
- Less negotiation flexibility
- Requires lead investor confidence

## Founder Risks & Pain Points

### 1. Too Many Small Investors
**The Problem:**
- 30+ investors each with small stakes
- Each investor feels they should have influence
- Cap table becomes unwieldy
- Admin burden for annual updates
- SAFE/Note stack becomes confusing

**Solutions:**
- Set investor minimums ($25K-$50K)
- Use syndicates to consolidate
- Consider rolling your SPV for small investors
- Create simple investor dashboard

### 2. Informal Terms Becoming Issues
**The Problem:**
- "Handshake deals" on valuations/terms
- Investor expectations not documented
- Board seat promises made casually
- Advisor commitments unclear
- Follow-on disputes later

**Solutions:**
- Use written term sheets even for angels
- Document all terms upfront
- Be explicit about what you're NOT offering
- Use ANGEL_TERM_SHEET.md template
- Get legal review ($2-5K)

### 3. Conflicting Advice from Investors
**The Problem:**
- Angel 1 says go up-market
- Angel 2 says focus on SMBs
- Angel 3 says pivot to B2B2C
- You're pulled in multiple directions
- Morale hits when you can't follow everyone's advice

**Solutions:**
- Create an advisor council (select 3-5 key investors)
- Have clear founder decision rights
- Monthly founder updates set expectations
- Be explicit: "Taking this path because..."
- Know when to ignore advice

### 4. Insufficient Follow-On Capital
**The Problem:**
- Angels won't follow Series A
- Have to find all new investors
- Early angels feel betrayed
- Your best supporters don't benefit

**Solutions:**
- Set reserves for existing investor participation
- Include pro-rata rights in docs
- Build ambassador angels who will follow
- Give better terms to angels who commit to follow

## Investor Risks & Pain Points

### 1. High Failure Rate
**The Reality:**
- ~90% of funded startups fail
- Angel portfolio companies fail 50-80% of the time
- Even "good" angel syndicates see 60%+ failure
- Most angels lose money

**How investors manage:**
- Portfolio approach (10+ companies)
- Expect only 1-2 to return capital
- Build in 10-15% annual expected return requirement
- Follow successful founders

### 2. Lack of Follow-On Capital
**The Problem:**
- Early investors have no way to participate in later rounds
- Their ownership gets diluted
- They're stuck with small percentage
- No exit opportunity

**Solutions investors want:**
- Pro-rata rights (right to follow at same terms)
- Board seat or information rights
- Anti-dilution protections
- Clear path to liquidity

### 3. Limited Investor Rights
**The Problem:**
- On a SAFE, investor has NO rights
- No information about financials
- No say in company direction
- No liquidation preference
- Can't force founder to do anything

**Solutions investors negotiate:**
- Information rights (monthly updates, financials)
- Pro-rata participation rights
- Board observations
- Conversion discounts
- Anti-dilution provisions

## Pain Points for Both Founders & Investors

### 1. Coordinating Multiple Angels
**The Challenge:**
- Different investors have different terms
- Each wants slightly different package
- Closing is like herding cats
- Last-minute demands from investor #7

**Solutions:**
- Use lead investor + SPV model
- Have one standard SAFE/Note template
- Limit negotiation points (valuation cap, discount only)
- Clear communication on non-negotiables

### 2. Pro-Rata Rights Complexity
**The Challenge:**
- 10 angels each want pro-rata
- You're raising Series A
- Each angel wants to invest $50K more
- How much allocation to existing vs. new investors?

**Solutions:**
- Document pro-rata rights clearly upfront
- Specify percentage (e.g., 50% for existing)
- Use waterfall model (seed investors first, then new)
- Communicate early on reserve expectations

### 3. Information Rights & Admin
**The Challenge:**
- Investors want monthly updates
- 20 different email addresses
- Nobody gets the same template
- Inconsistent disclosure

**Solutions:**
- Monthly investor email template
- Dashboard or investor portal (Carta)
- Quarterly deep-dives (optional)
- Annual comprehensive report
- Clear what data is confidential

### 4. Valuation Conversation Complexity
**The Challenge:**
- Investor 1 wants $2M cap
- Investor 2 wants $3M cap
- Last investor wants $1.5M cap
- You're only $100K revenue

**Solutions:**
- Lead investor sets cap (others accept or pass)
- Use benchmarks (peers, comps)
- Be transparent on cap logic
- Accept that different investors see different risk

### 5. Founder Focus & Distraction
**The Challenge:**
- Raising is 80/20: time = founder distraction
- Each investor wants meetings/calls
- Board relations require time
- Less time building product

**Solutions:**
- Set clear communication cadence
- "Monthly email, quarterly video call, annual in-person"
- Use async updates where possible
- Remind investors: "You invested for product vision, let me build it"

## The Angel Fundraising Timeline

### Weeks 1-2: Preparation
- [ ] Set fundraising target and cap (e.g., "$500K at $5M cap")
- [ ] Identify 50-100 potential angels
- [ ] Create warm intro list (through advisors, mentors)
- [ ] Draft pitch deck (1-2 pages)
- [ ] Prepare financials/model
- [ ] Choose structure (SAFE vs. Note)

### Weeks 3-6: Outreach
- [ ] Send 20-30 warm intro requests
- [ ] Conduct 10-15 intro calls
- [ ] Follow up with interested angels
- [ ] Schedule 5-10 pitches
- [ ] Get feedback and refine

### Weeks 7-10: Pitch & Close
- [ ] Run 10-15 investor meetings
- [ ] Address questions and concerns
- [ ] Send term sheets to interested investors
- [ ] Get 3-5 commitments
- [ ] Negotiate terms with stragglers

### Weeks 11-12: Legal & Close
- [ ] Legal docs drafted (use template)
- [ ] Send for investor review
- [ ] Get counteroffers/feedback
- [ ] Final signatures
- [ ] Wire transfers
- [ ] Update cap table

## Red Flags to Avoid

### Founder Red Flags
❌ Raising from too many small investors
❌ Not documenting terms
❌ Making board seat promises to everyone
❌ Accepting 50+ investor terms
❌ Not having conversation about follow-on
❌ Taking money from investors who misunderstand your vision

### Investor Red Flags
❌ Founder won't share financials/data
❌ Weak/changing product market fit narrative
❌ Team has high turnover already
❌ No clear go-to-market
❌ Founder defensive to feedback
❌ Valuation completely out of market

## Quick Decision Tree

**Investor asking what structure:**

```
"Do you have revenue or Series A timeline?"
  ↓
  YES: Use Convertible Note
       (investor gets interest + discount)
  NO: Use SAFE
      (simpler, faster, founder-friendly)

"Do you want investor rights now?"
  ↓
  YES: Priced Equity
       (clear ownership, more complex)
  NO: SAFE or Note
      (simpler, flexibility)

"Do you have a lead investor?"
  ↓
  YES: SAFE or Note on lead's terms
       (others follow on same deal)
  NO: Will need to negotiate individually
      (or find lead investor to simplify)
```

## Common Questions

### Q: What valuation cap should I use?
**A:** Typically 1.5-3x your annual revenue. Examples:
- $0 revenue: $2-4M cap
- $100K revenue: $3-8M cap
- $500K revenue: $5-15M cap

Go 1.5x if you have traction, 3x if early. Get investor feedback on 2-3 options, don't negotiate every dollar.

### Q: Should I use SAFE or Convertible Note?
**A:**
- **SAFE**: If you want to move fast and don't have Series A timeline (12+ months away)
- **Note**: If you think Series A is likely within 18-24 months (investor gets interest/discount)

### Q: How many investors is too many?
**A:**
- Ideal: 3-5 lead investors
- Reasonable: 5-15 total investors
- Challenging: 15+ investors (cap table management)
- Messy: 30+ investors (nightmare)

### Q: Can I have different terms for different angels?
**A:** Technically yes, but try not to. Standard terms (same cap, same discount) make everything simpler. If you have multiple caps, document and communicate clearly.

### Q: What about board seats?
**A:** For most angels, NO. Save board seats for major investors ($250K+) or strategic advisors. Instead offer information rights and investor calls.

### Q: Should I give equity to advisors vs. angels?
**A:** Different purposes:
- **Advisor**: Option grants (0.25-1% vest over 4 years)
- **Angel investor**: Equity on SAFE/Note/Priced round

Don't mix them; be clear on each person's role.

## Final Thoughts

Angel investing is the bridge between friends/family capital and institutional funding. Done right, it's:
- **Fast** (weeks, not months)
- **Flexible** (creative structures)
- **Valuable** (capital + mentorship + networks)

The key is clarity: clear terms upfront, clear communication ongoing, and clear expectations about follow-on capital and investor roles.
