# Angel Investing from the Investor's Perspective

**Your guide to investing $25K-$500K in early-stage startups and actually making money (sometimes)**

---

## Why Invest as an Angel?

### Financial Returns
| Scenario | Investment | Exit (5 years) | Return |
|----------|-----------|----------------|--------|
| **Home run** | $100K at $3M | Acquired for $100M | $3.3M (33x) |
| **Good outcome** | $100K at $3M | Acquired for $30M | $1M (10x) |
| **Okay outcome** | $100K at $3M | Acquired for $10M | $333K (3.3x) |
| **Breakeven** | $100K at $3M | Acquired for $3M | $100K (1x) |
| **Loss** | $100K at $3M | Company fails | $0 (-100%) |

**Reality:** In a portfolio of 10 angel investments:
- 6-7 will fail completely (you lose all capital)
- 2-3 will return 1-3x
- 1 might return 5x+
- Average return: 20-30% annually (if you're good at picking)

### Non-Financial Returns
- **Learning:** Direct access to how successful founders operate
- **Network:** Connections to other investors, entrepreneurs, potential business partners
- **Mentorship:** Personal satisfaction from helping founders build something
- **Optionality:** Sometimes an investment leads to business opportunity for you
- **Impact:** If you care about mission (climate, health, etc.), direct impact on meaningful companies

## Angel Investor Checklist: Start to Finish

### Phase 1: Building Your Angel Portfolio (Before You Invest)

#### Setting Your Parameters
- [ ] **Capital allocation:** How much total can you invest in angels? ($250K? $500K? $1M?)
- [ ] **Check size:** Individual investment size preference ($10K-$100K range?)
  - Typical: $25K-$100K per investment
  - Larger bets = fewer companies, higher impact
  - Smaller bets = more companies, better diversification
- [ ] **Concentration:** How many companies to invest in?
  - Safe: 10-15 companies across $250K (diversification)
  - Aggressive: 5-7 companies across $500K (concentrated bets)
  - Rule of thumb: Invest in 10x+ companies to get 1 home run
- [ ] **Portfolio expected return:** What's your target?
  - Conservative: 15-20% annual
  - Moderate: 25-30% annual
  - Aggressive: 35%+ annual
- [ ] **Time horizon:** When do you want exits?
  - Early: 3-5 years
  - Standard: 5-7 years
  - Patient: 7-10 years

#### Your Investment Thesis
- [ ] What types of companies excite you?
  - Stage: Pre-product? Early product? Early revenue?
  - Industry: B2B SaaS? Healthcare? Climate? Consumer?
  - Geography: Local? National? International?
  - Founder type: First-time founders? Repeat entrepreneurs?

- [ ] What you're looking for:
  - Problem they're solving (is it real?)
  - Market size (is it big enough?)
  - Founder experience (can they execute?)
  - Unique advantage (moat or network?)
  - Realistic path to $50M+ (can it be big?)

#### Building Your Network
- [ ] Where will you find deals?
  - AngelList (online platform)
  - Local accelerators (Y Combinator, 500 Startups, etc.)
  - University programs (MIT, Stanford, etc.)
  - Personal network (friends of friends)
  - Secondary investors (other angels who recommend)
  - Industry conferences (where founders pitch)

### Phase 2: Deal Sourcing & Pipeline (Months 1-12)

#### Finding Deals
- [ ] **Target:** 100+ interesting founders / year
- [ ] **Qualify:** 20-30% worthy of deeper conversation
- [ ] **Deep dive:** 10-15 get serious evaluation
- [ ] **Invest:** 1-2 per year (in a focused portfolio)

#### Evaluating Opportunities
- [ ] **Founder quality** (40% of decision)
  - Track record (serial entrepreneurs better than first-time?)
  - Resilience (how do they handle adversity?)
  - Coachability (will they listen to advisors?)
  - Domain expertise (do they understand the market?)
  - Integrity (do they say what they mean?)

- [ ] **Problem/market** (35% of decision)
  - Real pain point (not "nice-to-have"?)
  - Big enough market ($100M+?)
  - Growing market (tailwinds vs. headwinds?)
  - Competition (manageable?)
  - Realistic GTM (how will they acquire customers?)

- [ ] **Product/traction** (20% of decision)
  - Stage-appropriate (product vs. pre-product?)
  - Early validation (customers, waitlist, NPS?)
  - Technical feasibility (can they build this?)
  - Scalability (does it work at 10x usage?)

- [ ] **Terms/fit** (5% of decision)
  - Valuation reasonable (or absurdly high?)
  - Equity terms make sense (SAFE/Note/priced?)
  - Timeline realistic (Series A 18-24 months out?)
  - Your investment role (advisor? board observer? silent?)

#### Red Flags
❌ Founder has never had a real job
❌ Founder dismisses all feedback
❌ No clear path to revenue
❌ Market is too small or contracting
❌ Valuation completely disconnected from reality
❌ Founder is evasive about competition
❌ Technical cofounder doesn't exist
❌ Using your capital to pay themselves a salary before PMF
❌ Founder is distracted (another business or job)
❌ No clear metrics of traction

### Phase 3: Due Diligence & Decision (Weeks 1-4)

#### Investment Decision Process

**Step 1: Gut Check (Days 1-2)**
- Do I believe in this founder?
- Do I understand the problem/market?
- Do I want to be involved?
- Is this the type of company I invest in?

If NO to any → PASS. Move on.

**Step 2: Market Research (Days 3-5)**
- [ ] Market size research
  - TAM (Total Addressable Market): Is it real?
  - Growth rate: Is market growing?
  - Competition: Who else is playing?
  - Trends: Are there tailwinds?

- [ ] Founder research
  - LinkedIn: What's their background?
  - References: Call 2-3 other investors in them
  - References: Call 2-3 people who worked with them
  - Social: Do they live the mission or just say it?

- [ ] Customer research (if possible)
  - Are there real customers?
  - What's the NPS/feedback?
  - How much are customers using it?
  - Would customers stay if competitors emerged?

**Step 3: Technical Due Diligence (Days 6-7)**
- [ ] Product evaluation
  - Does it work as described?
  - Is the tech sound?
  - Scalability concerns?
  - Security/compliance issues?

- [ ] Founder technical capability
  - If they claim technical ability, can they prove it?
  - Is there a real technical cofounder?
  - Are they solving the hard problems?

**Step 4: Financial Sense Check (Day 8)**
- [ ] Unit economics (if any revenue)
  - What does each customer cost to acquire?
  - What revenue do they generate?
  - Is it positive or massive bleed?
  - Path to profitability real?

- [ ] Burn rate & runway
  - How much capital do they need?
  - When will they raise Series A?
  - Do they have runway buffer?
  - Overspending or appropriate?

**Step 5: Investment Decision (Day 9-10)**
- [ ] Final questions answered?
- [ ] Confidence level: 1-10?
  - 8-10: YES, invest
  - 5-7: MAYBE, but not excited
  - 1-4: NO, pass
- [ ] Can you write a check with conviction?
- [ ] Are the terms acceptable?

#### Questions to Ask in Due Diligence

**On Business:**
- "Walk me through your customer acquisition path."
- "Who is your primary competitor, and how are you different?"
- "What happens to your business if [biggest risk] occurs?"
- "When do you expect to be profitable?"
- "What's your Unit Economics?" (Cost to acquire / Revenue per customer)

**On Founder:**
- "Tell me about your last failure. What did you learn?"
- "Who on the team is most critical? What if they leave?"
- "What piece of feedback have you heard most? How are you addressing it?"
- "When have you disagreed with an advisor? How did you handle it?"

**On Market:**
- "How do you know there's a real problem to solve?"
- "Who has confirmed they'd pay for this solution?"
- "How big is the opportunity if you win?"
- "How will customers hear about you?"

### Phase 4: Terms & Commitment (Weeks 5-6)

#### Understanding What You're Investing In

**If Investing in SAFE:**
```
You give: $[AMOUNT]
You get: Conversion rights to future Series A
You own: Nothing now. Converts when Series A happens.
Downside: If acquired pre-Series A for < $[CAP], you get treated as common (lose money)
Upside: If Series A at $[HIGH_VALUATION], your conversion cap protects you
```

**If Investing in Convertible Note:**
```
You give: $[AMOUNT]
You get: Debt instrument + conversion rights
Interest: Accrues at [X]% per year (you don't get paid cash, converts to equity)
Maturity: If no Series A by [DATE], company owes you principal + interest
Upside: Interest accrual gives you more equity when converts
Downside: If company fails, you're creditor but still likely lose money
```

**If Investing in Priced Equity:**
```
You give: $[AMOUNT]
You get: [X]% equity in company immediately
You own: Real shares from day 1
Upside: Get board seat, rights, governance
Downside: Most dilution. You own 5-10% that gets diluted to 2-3% by Series A
Rights: Full preferred stock rights (liquidation preference, etc.)
```

#### Term Sheet Evaluation

Key terms to review:

| Term | What It Means | Red Flag |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **Valuation Cap** | Max price you convert at | Cap way too high (3x+ revenue) |
| **Discount** | Discount to Series A price | Discount <15% (you're not protected) |
| **Pro-Rata** | Can you follow in Series A | No pro-rata (you're abandoned) |
| **Interest Rate** (if Note) | Annual % that accrues | >8% (suggests company is risky) |
| **Maturity** (if Note) | When note is due | No maturity (no urgency) |
| **Liquidation Pref** | Your priority in sale | Full ratchet (too harsh on founder) |
| **Board/Info Rights** | What information you get | No updates provided (red flag) |

#### Negotiation Approach

**What to negotiate:**
- Valuation cap (you can ask for lower)
- Discount rate (you can ask for higher)
- Pro-rata rights (ask for yes)
- Information rights (ask for monthly updates)

**What NOT to negotiate:**
- Board seat (angels don't get board seats)
- Veto rights (no voting)
- Liquidation preference (too complex for angel round)
- Follow-on commitment (your choice, not obligation)

**If founder won't budge on terms:**
- Decide: Take it or pass
- Don't negotiate for months
- If you're not excited about valuation, PASS
- There are more deals

### Phase 5: Investment Execution (Weeks 7-8)

#### Finalizing Legal Documents
- [ ] **Review documents:**
  - SAFE or Note reviewed by your lawyer (optional but recommended)
  - Understand every term
  - Confirm valuation cap / discount / interest rate
  - Confirm information rights

- [ ] **Confirm company details:**
  - Legal name, state of incorporation
  - Capitalization table (make sure you understand it)
  - Option pool allocated
  - No existing investor conflicts

- [ ] **Get legal review:**
  - Your lawyer: $500-$2,000 (just document review)
  - Founder pays for shared template

#### Conditions & Confirmations
- [ ] **Before you wire funds:**
  - Signed legal documents (SAFE or Note)
  - Cap table accurate and certified
  - Company confirms no material adverse change
  - Your legal questions answered

- [ ] **Wire funds:**
  - Bank wire to company's account
  - Keep confirmation
  - Notify founder (and lead investor if syndicated)

#### Post-Investment Admin
- [ ] **Update your records:**
  - Add to your portfolio tracking
  - Document date, amount, terms
  - Set calendar reminder for Series A follow-up decision (18 months)

- [ ] **Receive confirmations:**
  - Executed SAFE/Note (both parties signed)
  - Updated cap table from company
  - Investor information request (tax ID, address)

### Phase 6: Portfolio Management (Year 1+)

#### Active Investing
- [ ] **Monthly:** Receive investor updates
  - Product/business metrics
  - Team updates
  - Challenges faced
  - Upcoming milestones

- [ ] **Quarterly:** Optional call with founder
  - Q&A on updates
  - Discuss challenges
  - Offer help if requested
  - Assess company health

- [ ] **Annually:** Review investment performance
  - Are founders executing on plan?
  - Are metrics moving in right direction?
  - Major pivots or challenges?
  - Are you still happy with investment?

#### Advisor Role (Optional)
- [ ] **If you have relevant expertise:**
  - Offer specific help (introductions, advice, expertise)
  - Offer [2-5 hours/month] of time
  - Be specific: "I can help with sales/product/fundraising"
  - Don't offer generic "let me know if you need help"

- [ ] **If you don't have expertise:**
  - Let founder reach out if they want something
  - Make introductions to people who can help
  - Celebrate wins, support through challenges

#### Follow-On Investment Decisions

**18 months in (approaching Series A):**

- [ ] **Evaluate company progress:**
  - Did they hit milestones?
  - Are metrics strong?
  - Is founder still capable?
  - Market still promising?

- [ ] **Decide on Series A:**
  - Option A: Participate (good company, want to follow)
  - Option B: Pass (not excited, but don't want out)
  - Option C: Sell stake (found better opportunity)
  - Option D: Full exit (something went wrong)

- [ ] **If participating in Series A:**
  - Use pro-rata rights (if you have them)
  - Invest $[X] more at Series A terms
  - Your ownership gets diluted but you maintain stake
  - You now share board seat with other VC investors

## What You're Really Getting for Your $100K

### At Investment (Year 0)
```
You: Pay $100K
Company: Issues SAFE worth $[VALUATION_CAP] in future
Company value: Let's say $5M
Your implied ownership: ~2% (at conversion)
Actual ownership now: 0% (SAFE is convertible, not equity)
```

### At Series A (Year 1.5-2)
```
Company raises: $2M Series A
Series A valuation: $10M pre-money, $12M post-money
Your SAFE converts: At cap of $5M → $1.25/share
Your share count: $100K / $1.25 = 80,000 shares
Post-Series A valuation: $12M
Your ownership: Now ~0.67%
Dilution: From 2% to 0.67% (normal for Series A)
```

### At Exit (Year 5+)
```
Company acquired for: $100M
Your equity: 0.67% × $100M = $670K
Your return: $670K / $100K = 6.7x
Time to return: 5 years
Annual return: ~43% per year
Rank: Good outcome (top 20% of angel outcomes)
```

## Build Your Angel Investor Mindset

### Key Principles

#### 1. Diversification is Essential
- Invest in 10+ companies to get 1 home run
- Don't put all eggs in one startup
- Different stages, industries, founders

#### 2. You Need Convictions
- You can't be right on every investment
- Pick 2-3 thesis areas and go deep
- Say no to deals outside your area
- Be willing to miss deals if terms don't fit

#### 3. Founder Quality > Everything
- Can hire, teach, pivot
- But can't change founder DNA
- Invest in people you believe in
- Even if business pivots

#### 4. Be a Helper, Not a Board Member
- You're advising, not governing
- Offer introductions + expertise
- Let founder make decisions
- Celebrate wins, support through challenges

#### 5. Plan for Most Companies to Fail
- 60-80% of your portfolio will fail
- Don't take it personally
- Learn from each failure
- Focus on the companies that survive

#### 6. Exits Take 5-7 Years Minimum
- Don't expect money back in 2 years
- Be patient with founder
- Keep checking in but don't micromanage
- Most valuereturns happen in years 4-7

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Pitfall 1: Following the Herd
❌ Everyone else is investing, so you do too
❌ FOMO (fear of missing out)
✅ Instead: Have conviction in YOUR thesis
✅ Invest in companies YOU believe in

### Pitfall 2: Negotiating Forever
❌ Spend months negotiating terms
❌ Try to get slightly better discount
✅ Instead: Decide quickly (take it or pass)
✅ Good founders won't wait forever

### Pitfall 3: Over-Advising
❌ Give founder constant feedback
❌ Try to steer company direction
❌ Expect founder to implement your ideas
✅ Instead: Give advice only when asked
✅ Let founder make decisions

### Pitfall 4: Not Diversifying
❌ Put $200K into 1 company
❌ Expect massive return
✅ Instead: Spread $200K across 5-8 companies
✅ Expect 1-2 to return capital

### Pitfall 5: Checking Out After Investment
❌ Wire funds, never hear from founder again
❌ Founder delivers no updates
❌ You miss Series A opportunity
✅ Instead: Establish clear update cadence
✅ Check in quarterly at minimum

### Pitfall 6: Bad Reference Checks
❌ Call founder's best friend (obviously biased)
❌ Don't ask hard questions
✅ Instead: Call previous investors
✅ Call people who worked WITH founder (employees, cofounders)
✅ Ask: "Would you invest again?" (honest answer)

## What to Expect Over Time

### Year 1
- Company executing on plan (hopefully)
- Monthly updates showing progress
- Possible pivot (normal)
- Key hires happening
- Burn rate becoming clearer

### Year 2
- Company approaching Series A
- Metrics becoming clearer
- Some traction (revenue or users)
- Competitive dynamics emerging
- Series A starting to look possible (or not)

### Years 3-5
- Series A closed (or company died)
- Company scaling
- Multiple rounds of funding
- Some portfolio companies have exited
- Some portfolio companies failing

### Years 5-10
- Some exits (acquisitions, IPO)
- Returns coming back to you
- Some total losses
- Portfolio performance becoming clear

## Red Flag Checklist: When to Walk Away

If you see ANY of these, consider passing:

- [ ] Founder is evasive about competition
- [ ] Cap table is messy or undisclosed
- [ ] Valuation is way outside market norms
- [ ] Founder dismisses all feedback/advice
- [ ] No clear customer traction (pre-product is okay, but what's the plan?)
- [ ] Founder is distracted (another job/business)
- [ ] Technical cofounder doesn't exist (for software)
- [ ] Founder has track record of failure (without learning)
- [ ] Market seems contracting, not growing
- [ ] Founder can't articulate the problem clearly
- [ ] Terms include full ratchet anti-dilution (harsh on founder)
- [ ] Founder wants board seat for angel (oversteps)
- [ ] Company is bootstrapping but asking you to fund salaries
- [ ] Founder's story keeps changing

## Your Angel Investor Playbook Summary

1. **Know yourself:** What check size? What industries? What return expectations?
2. **Source deals:** Build network, attend pitches, ask for referrals
3. **Evaluate quickly:** 80/20 rule - 20% of evaluation determines 80% of success
4. **Due diligence:** Focus on founder + market + problem
5. **Make decision:** Take it or pass. Don't waffle.
6. **Invest:** Get legal docs, wire funds, close quickly
7. **Stay involved:** Monthly updates, quarterly check-ins, annual reviews
8. **Be helpful:** Intros, advice, endorsement (when earned)
9. **Diversify:** 10+ companies to expect 1 home run
10. **Be patient:** 5-7 year time horizon minimum
