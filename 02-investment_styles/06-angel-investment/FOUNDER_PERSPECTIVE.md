# Angel Fundraising from the Founder's Perspective

**Your guide to raising $250K-$2M from individual angels without losing your mind or your equity**

---

## Why You Should Raise from Angels (Strategic Reasons)

### 1. Speed & Flexibility
- Decisions in **weeks**, not months
- Less due diligence (no committee)
- Creative structures possible (SAFE, Note, equity, hybrid)
- Can start before product is perfect

### 2. Mentorship + Capital
- Access to successful founder's network
- Operational advice (not just capital)
- Introductions to customers/partners
- Accountability (in a good way)

### 3. Lower Dilution (If You Structure Right)
- SAFE: Non-dilutive at raise, converts later
- Convertible Note: Same + protects investor with interest
- Priced equity: Dilutive now, but you negotiate valuation
- vs. Series A: 25-40% dilution inevitable

### 4. Control Remains with Founder
- Most angels don't take board seats
- Information rights (not governance rights)
- You set the vision, they advise
- vs. VC: Board seat = someone can fire you

### 5. Perfect for Stage of Growth
| Stage | Why Angels Work |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Pre-product** | Fast capital, no revenue needed |
| **Early product** | Traction story, not yet hockey-stick |
| **Post-product, pre-revenue** | Product exists, customers pending |
| **Early revenue** | Proof of concept, need growth capital |
| **$500K-$2M revenue** | Not big enough for Series A, angels perfect |

## The Founder's Master Checklist

### Phase 1: Preparation (Weeks 1-2)

#### Strategic Decisions
- [ ] **Fundraising target:** How much capital do you need? ($250K, $500K, $1M?)
- [ ] **Valuation cap:** What's a reasonable value for your company?
  - Pre-product: $2-4M
  - Early product: $3-8M
  - Early revenue ($100K): $4-12M
  - Early revenue ($500K+): $5-20M+
- [ ] **Dilution tolerance:** How much equity will you give away?
  - SAFE/Notes: No dilution now, ~15-20% later in Series A
  - Priced equity: 5-10% per round is reasonable
- [ ] **Timeline:** When do you actually need this capital?
  - ASAP: Push harder, offer better terms
  - In 6 months: More flexibility on terms

#### Document Preparation
- [ ] Draft pitch (1-2 pages, not deck yet)
  - Problem: What's the pain?
  - Solution: How do you solve it?
  - Market: How big is opportunity?
  - Traction: What's your proof?
  - Ask: How much + use of funds

- [ ] Financial model (simple)
  - Monthly P&L projection (12-24 months)
  - Use of funds breakdown
  - Key assumptions (growth rates, churn, etc.)
  - Runway calculation (months of capital)

- [ ] One-pager (leave behind)
  - Company name, vision, founders
  - Problem + solution
  - Traction to date
  - Use of funds
  - How to reach you

- [ ] Cap table (spreadsheet)
  - Current shares/founders
  - Current stock options
  - Pro forma after this round
  - Shows you're organized

#### Investor List
- [ ] Identify 100+ potential angels
  - Warm introductions (best)
  - Network friends (referrals)
  - LinkedIn search (cold, but okay)
  - AngelList platform

- [ ] Qualify by:
  - Relevant experience (industry/skill)
  - Access to networks
  - Check size: $25K-$500K sweet spot
  - Geographic preference

- [ ] Priority list: Top 30 you'd actually want

### Phase 2: Outreach (Weeks 3-6)

#### Warm Intros
- [ ] Create outreach email template
  - Short (5 sentences max)
  - Specific to investor
  - What you're raising
  - One or two reasons why them
  - Link to one-pager or video

- [ ] Ask for intros through:
  - Current advisors
  - Early customers
  - Other investors you know
  - Mentors (accelerator, Y Combinator, etc.)
  - Friends who know wealthy people

- [ ] Track: Who said yes, who ignored, follow-up dates

#### Investor Conversations
- [ ] Phone/video call format:
  - 5 min intro (you and problem)
  - 5 min deep dive (solution + traction)
  - 5 min Q&A (investor questions)
  - End with: "Are you interested?" or "Any intro ideas?"

- [ ] Listen for:
  - Real interest vs. "let me know when you're bigger"
  - Objections (you can fix or accept)
  - Follow-on investment signals ("I'll follow in Series A")
  - Advisor potential beyond capital

### Phase 3: Pitch & Terms (Weeks 7-10)

#### Investor Meetings
- [ ] Prepare materials:
  - Pitch deck (10-15 slides) only if requested
  - Live demo (if product exists)
  - Customer stories (if you have customers)
  - Financials/model (if requested)

- [ ] What to expect:
  - Deep questions on market size
  - Questions on competition
  - Why now? Why you? Why will you succeed?
  - References from customers or advisors
  - Asks for materials to review

- [ ] Red flags from investor:
  - Asks about board seat (pre-Series A)
  - Wants exclusivity (walk away)
  - Wants to manage your money
  - Wants to hire their friend as CEO
  - Seems focused on tech, not market

#### Term Sheet Negotiation
- [ ] YOU set the initial terms (template in ANGEL_TERM_SHEET.md)
  - This is YOUR offer, not negotiation yet
  - Be reasonable (don't lowball)
  - Show you've thought it through

- [ ] Send to interested investors
  - Valuation cap
  - Discount (if Note)
  - Type of security (SAFE vs. Note)
  - Pro-rata rights (yes/no)
  - Any investor governance

- [ ] Expect pushback on:
  - Valuation cap (they'll want lower)
  - Discount (they'll want higher)
  - Pro-rata (they'll ask for it)
  - Board seat (stand firm: no for angels)
  - Follow-on commitment (non-binding intent only)

- [ ] Negotiation approach:
  - Lead investor negotiates once, others follow on same terms
  - Only negotiate with lead, not all investors
  - Non-negotiable: Don't negotiate with every investor
  - Negotiable: Valuation cap, discount, interest rate
  - Decision: Investor takes it or passes

### Phase 4: Legal & Close (Weeks 11-12)

#### Legal Docs
- [ ] Choose structure:
  - SAFE: Fastest, simplest (most angels)
  - Convertible Note: More investor protection, better for Series A timeline
  - Priced equity: Full documentation, slowest

- [ ] Get legal review:
  - Your lawyer: $2-5K (SAFE), $5-10K (Note or priced)
  - Use template (cheaper than custom)
  - Have investor's lawyer review (they pay)

- [ ] Investor conditions:
  - No material change to business
  - Cap table accurate
  - Key person insurance (some investors ask)
  - No existing investor conflicts

#### Closing
- [ ] Execute agreements:
  - Each investor gets separate SAFE or Note
  - All have identical terms
  - Add closing conditions
  - Get signatures

- [ ] Receive funds:
  - Wire instructions to company
  - Confirm receipt
  - Update cap table immediately

- [ ] Post-close:
  - Thank you email/call to each investor
  - Send first update within 2 weeks
  - Set up monthly update cadence
  - Create investor data room
  - Track commitments for follow-on

## Key Decisions: Breaking Down Your Choices

### Decision 1: SAFE vs. Convertible Note vs. Priced Equity

#### SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
**Choose SAFE if:**
- You want simplest possible documents
- You don't expect Series A for 12+ months
- You want non-dilutive capital now
- You want founder-friendly terms

**Choose SAFE over Note if:**
- Speed/simplicity > investor protection
- You'll likely need to raise again before priced round

**Drawbacks:**
- Investor gets no rights (no governance)
- If acquired before Series A, investor may lose money
- Investor gets no interest on capital

**Typical terms:**
- Valuation Cap: 1.5-3x your annual revenue (or similar company)
- Discount: 20-30%
- Pro-rata: Yes (investors can follow in Series A)
- Maturity: None (stays until Series A)

#### Convertible Note
**Choose Note if:**
- Series A is expected within 18-24 months
- You want investor protection (for faster close)
- Interest accrual incentivizes conversion
- You want maturity date (creates urgency)

**Choose Note over SAFE if:**
- Investor pushes for debt protection
- You have clear Series A timeline
- You value investor happiness over simplicity

**Drawbacks:**
- Creates debt on balance sheet
- More complex (longer docs)
- Interest accrual can get messy
- Maturity date risk (what if no Series A?)

**Typical terms:**
- Principal: $[AMOUNT]
- Interest Rate: 5-8%
- Maturity: 2-3 years
- Conversion Discount: 20-30%
- Valuation Cap: Same as SAFE

#### Priced Equity (Series Seed or Series A-1)
**Choose Priced if:**
- You have product + meaningful traction
- Clear market validation
- Investors want clear ownership upfront
- You're already raising institutional ($500K+)

**Choose Priced if you DON'T want:**
- Ambiguity in conversion terms
- Future negotiations
- Investor who doesn't understand equity
- Multiple tranches of financing

**Drawbacks:**
- Most dilutive immediately
- Complex legal docs (30-50 pages)
- Slow negotiation (preference stack, etc.)
- Investor gets full rights (board seat potential)

**Typical terms:**
- Valuation: $5-20M (depending on traction)
- Investor equity: 5-15%
- Liquidation preference: Non-participating preferred
- Board seat: Usually yes
- Information rights: Yes (full transparency)

### Decision 2: Single Lead vs. Coordinated Individuals

#### Lead Investor Model
**How it works:**
- One lead investor negotiates terms
- Other angels follow on identical terms
- Lead represents investor interests
- Cleaner cap table, less founder time

**Pros:**
- ONE negotiation, not 10
- Cleaner cap table (fewer investors)
- Coordinated follow-on in Series A
- Lead filters investor requests

**Cons:**
- Lead investor gets power/leverage
- Other investors feel constrained
- If lead backs out, syndicate collapses
- Lead wants compensation (carry, fee)

**When to use:**
- Raising >$250K from multiple angels
- Want organized investor group
- Need strong lead who investors trust

#### Individual Investors
**How it works:**
- Each investor negotiates with you
- You can negotiate different terms
- More founder flexibility
- Investors not coordinated

**Pros:**
- You have more control
- Can customize terms per investor
- Flexibility if an investor wants different structure
- No single point of failure

**Cons:**
- Multiple negotiations
- Cap table becomes unwieldy
- Investor doesn't feel special
- Follow-on becomes messy (30 different conversations)

**When to use:**
- Raising <$250K
- Want maximumflexibility
- Have investors at very different levels
- Don't have strong lead investor

### Decision 3: Investor Governance Rights

#### What to Offer (And Defend)
| Right | Your Position | Why |
|-------|---------------|-----|
| **Information rights** | Always offer | Low burden, makes investors happy |
| **Pro-rata rights** | Usually offer | Good faith, reasonable ask |
| **Board seat** | Rarely offer | Angels ≠ board members |
| **Voting rights** | Never offer | You control company |
| **Veto rights** | Never offer | You make decisions |
| **Follow-on obligation** | Never offer | Make it optional |
| **Advisor role** | Case-by-case | Only if you want advice |

**Lines to Use:**
- "We're giving information rights, but we need founder control."
- "Board seat is only for Series A investors or $500K+ commitments."
- "Pro-rata is optional; you can participate or pass."
- "We'd love your advice, but not as governance requirement."

## Common Challenges You'll Face

### Challenge 1: Investor Indecision
**"Let me think about it" means PASS**

- Investors who are genuinely interested will decide in 1-2 weeks
- "Let me get back to you" after 2 weeks = they passed, just haven't said it
- Move on to next investor

**What to do:**
- After 2 weeks of no decision: "Let me know by [DATE]. If we don't hear, I'm assuming you're passing."
- Don't let investors tie you up for months
- Have pipeline of 10x your target (expect 80% pass rate)

### Challenge 2: Dilution Creep
**You give 2% SAFE to investor 1, then 1.5% to investor 2, etc.**

**Solutions:**
- Pre-announce dilution budget: "We're allocating 15% to investors this round"
- Use lead investor model (everyone same terms)
- Cap per investor: "Max 2% per investor"
- Set firm cap and stick to it

### Challenge 3: Board Bloat
**Every investor wants a board seat or observation right**

**Your answer:**
- "We have [X] board seats. Series A investor(s) will get board seats. Angels get information rights + pro-rata."
- If investor pushes: "We appreciate your interest. We're looking for founder control at this stage."
- Observation rights (attend but don't vote) = compromise

### Challenge 4: Advice Overload
**Ten investors, ten different opinions on product strategy**

**Solutions:**
- Create advisory council: Select 3-5 investors you actually want advice from
- Monthly email: "Here's what we're doing and why. Questions welcome but we're executing this."
- Be professional but firm: "Appreciate the perspective. We're going in this direction because [X]."
- Remember: YOU'RE the CEO. You make decisions.

### Challenge 5: Follow-On Capital Expectations
**Investors think giving money now means you'll ask them for Series A**

**Set expectations upfront:**
- "We're raising from angels now. Series A will be institutional investors for scale. We'll absolutely offer you pro-rata rights, but no promises."
- If investor expects Series A follow-on: Either give them confidence you'll be ready for Series A, or they should pass
- Some angels will be offended when you raise Series A from VCs (deal with it)

## When Things Go Wrong

### Scenario 1: You Need More Time
**What if you won't be ready for Series A in 18 months?**

Option A: Raise a smaller first check
- Raise $250K now, $250K in 12 months
- Keeps you lean, extends runway
- Investors still on same terms

Option B: Extend maturity on Notes
- If you issued Convertible Notes, extend maturity
- Keep interest accruing (investor gets something)
- Set realistic Series A target (24 months out)

Option C: Convert early
- If you're doing well, convert SAFE early to equity
- Raise Series A on strong terms
- Investors benefit from upside

### Scenario 2: Company Pivots
**Investors invested in Product A, you're pivoting to Product B**

- Notify investors immediately
- Explain reasoning (customer feedback, market opportunity, etc.)
- Reassure on use of capital (still spending on right things)
- Acknowledge risk (pivots fail sometimes)

Investors signed up for your vision + team. If you're credible, they'll trust the pivot.

### Scenario 3: Key Person Leaves
**Your lead engineer quits.**

- Notify investors quickly (transparency builds trust)
- Explain replacement plan
- Don't panic (it happens, investors know this)
- Show founder resilience

### Scenario 4: Missed Metrics
**You promised 1,000 users by month 4, you have 200.**

- Explain what changed (market, product fit, timeline)
- Show what you learned
- Show new metrics that matter
- Be honest (don't spin it)

Investors respect founders who course-correct. They hate founders who lie.

## What Investors Will Ask You

### Revenue Questions
- "Why do you think you'll hit $X revenue?"
- "What's your unit economics?" (What does each customer cost you vs. make you?)
- "What's churn?" (How many customers leave each month?)
- "What's your CAC?" (Customer Acquisition Cost)

### Team Questions
- "Tell me about your co-founders."
- "Why are you qualified to do this?"
- "What's your competitive advantage?"
- "What will you do if you're wrong?"

### Market Questions
- "How big is your market?"
- "Who's your competition?"
- "Why will customers choose you?"
- "What happens if a big company copies you?"

### Risk Questions
- "What's your biggest risk?"
- "What could kill this company?"
- "What if [competitor] launches first?"
- "What if your key assumptions are wrong?"

**Best answer strategy:**
- Answer directly (don't evade)
- Show you've thought about it deeply
- Admit what you don't know
- Show framework for how you'll figure it out

## Final Founder Mindset

### Remember These Things

1. **You have leverage.** Investors WANT to invest in good founders. They're looking for deals, not the other way around.

2. **Most investors will pass.** 80% of people you pitch will say no. That's normal. You only need a few yeses.

3. **Speed is your weapon.** Make decisions fast. Angels move quickly; don't slow-roll them.

4. **Your terms are fair.** If you offer SAFE on reasonable cap, investors should take it. If they don't like it, they pass. Don't negotiate endlessly.

5. **You're not selling equity.** You're inviting people to believe in your vision. Frame it that way.

6. **Board control is non-negotiable.** You can offer information rights, pro-rata, advisor role. But the company remains yours to run.

7. **Relationships matter more than capital.** A $50K investor who makes 3 introductions is worth more than a $500K investor who ghosts you.

8. **Transparency builds trust.** Tell investors what's happening. Good news and bad. Transparency > spinning good stories.
