## INTRODUCTION

### Why SAFE Negotiation Matters

SAFEs appear simple—just 2-3 pages—but contain critical terms that dramatically affect founder dilution and investor returns. Most negotiation happens around:

1. **Valuation Cap** - Biggest value driver
2. **Discount Rate** - Reward for early investment
3. **MFN Clause** - Protect against future dilution
4. **Pro-Rata Rights** - Control future dilution
5. **Information Rights** - Track company progress
6. **Conversion Triggers** - When SAFE converts to equity

### Negotiation Philosophy

**For Founders:** Minimize dilution, keep terms founder-friendly, maintain flexibility

**For Investors:** Maximize downside protection, maintain control, ensure clear conversion path

**For Both:** Balance interests to create win-win terms that allow capital to close quickly

## KEY NEGOTIABLE TERMS

### 1. VALUATION CAP

**What It Is:**
Maximum valuation used for conversion calculation, setting a floor on investor ownership.

#### Founder Perspective on Cap Negotiation

**Founder Goal:** Higher cap = less investor dilution

| Cap Amount | Founder Impact | Investor Impact |
|-----------|---|---|
| **$2M cap** | Founder heavily diluted (~5-8% per $100k) | Investor gets strong protection |
| **$5M cap** | Moderate dilution (~2-3% per $100k) | Investor has reasonable protection |
| **$10M cap** | Light dilution (~1-1.5% per $100k) | Investor has minimal protection |
| **No cap** | No dilution from cap | No protection in up rounds |

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Starting Position (Optimistic):
"We'd prefer no valuation cap to keep things simple and founder-friendly."

Realistic Position (Prepared):
"Based on comparable rounds, we're comfortable with $8-10M cap reflecting
our current traction and market opportunity."

Fallback Position (Necessary):
"If a cap is required for investor comfort, $6M reflects our Series A
expectations and gives reasonable protection."

Worst Case (Don't Go Here):
"Okay, fine, $2M cap..." (likely not acceptable)
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **Research Comparables:** Know what similar companies raised at what caps
2. **Justify the Cap:** "We expect Series A at $20M valuation based on [metrics]"
3. **Use Multiple Investors:** "If you need a lower cap, we may explore other investors"
4. **Offer Trade-Off:** "We'll accept $4M cap if you waive pro-rata rights"
5. **Document Rationale:** Show why cap is reasonable given traction, market, team

#### Investor Perspective on Cap Negotiation

**Investor Goal:** Lower cap = more downside protection

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position (Aggressive):
"We'd like a $2-3M cap to protect our downside in this early stage."

Realistic Position (Prepared):
"Industry standard for seed is 3-5x our investment in post-money cap.
Our $100k investment suggests $3-5M cap range."

Fallback Position (Compromise):
"$6M cap with pro-rata rights and MFN clause gives us reasonable protection."

Walk-Away Position:
"Above $8M cap with no other protections is not acceptable for this stage."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Use Benchmarks:** "For this stage, typical caps are $3-6M"
2. **Justify Based on Risk:** "At pre-product stage, we need strong protection"
3. **Combine Protections:** "Lower cap + MFN + pro-rata = acceptable terms"
4. **Evaluate Context:** Higher cap acceptable if: strong team, clear market, existing traction
5. **Get Multiple Signals:** Ask about other investors' cap expectations

#### Cap Negotiation Scenarios

**Scenario A: Founder Has Multiple Investors, Different Caps**

```
Problem: Investor 1 gets $5M cap, Investor 2 demands $3M cap due to MFN
Solution:
1. Negotiate all caps together (batch close)
2. Use highest acceptable cap ($4M) for all investors
3. Offer all investors MFN to same cap
4. Include time limit on MFN (e.g., 12 months)
```

**Scenario B: Cap Way Too High**

```
Investor Position: "You're asking for $20M cap on a pre-product startup"
Founder Response Options:
- Option 1: Accept this is excessive, propose $8-10M
- Option 2: Explain traction: "We have [500k users / $50k MRR / partnerships]"
- Option 3: Counter: "How about $15M cap + no discount?"
```

**Scenario C: Cap Way Too Low**

```
Investor Position: "$1M cap protects our seed investment"
Founder Response Options:
- Option 1: "That implies 10%+ dilution per $100k, unsustainable for multiple rounds"
- Option 2: "Okay, but no MFN or pro-rata to offset the dilution"
- Option 3: "Let's use $2M cap as middle ground"
```

### 2. DISCOUNT RATE

**What It Is:**
Percentage discount on Series A price, rewarding early investors for taking risk.

#### Founder Perspective on Discount Negotiation

**Founder Goal:** Lower discount = less investor advantage

| Discount | Founder View | Investor View |
|----------|---|---|
| **0% (No discount)** | Founder prefers, but less attractive to investors | Poor investor protection |
| **10-15%** | Reasonable for later-stage SAFEs | Minimal advantage, needs other protections |
| **20-25%** | Standard for seed SAFEs | Good early investor reward |
| **30-35%** | High for seed, aggressive | Strong early investor reward |

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We'd prefer no discount to keep all investors equal at conversion."

Realistic Position:
"For early seed, 15-20% discount rewards early risk while remaining fair."

Fallback Position:
"We can offer 25% discount if combined with either: (a) no cap, or (b) limited MFN."

Walk-Away Position:
"Above 30% discount is excessive and unsustainable across multiple rounds."
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **Time-Bind Discount:** "30% discount only for investments before [date]"
2. **Stage-Adjust:** "Pre-MVP gets 25% discount; post-MVP gets 15% discount"
3. **Combine EITHER Cap OR Discount:** "You get 30% discount with NO cap" (founder-friendly)
4. **Monitor Cumulative Impact:** If 5 SAFEs at 25% discount, that's significant dilution
5. **Use Discount as Negotiation Chip:** Trade higher discount for waiving other protections

#### Investor Perspective on Discount Negotiation

**Investor Goal:** Higher discount = better Series A conversion

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"For early-stage risk, we expect 30-35% discount."

Realistic Position:
"Seed stage typically gets 20-25% discount based on risk timing."

Fallback Position:
"If discount is lower (15%), we need a lower cap ($3-4M) for balance."

Walk-Away Position:
"Below 10% discount with no cap is not sufficient protection."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Justify by Stage:** "Pre-product needs 30%+ discount; post-product needs 15%"
2. **Link to Dilution:** "If cap is high, discount needs to be higher"
3. **Combine with Cap:** "We prefer low cap ($3M) over high discount (30%)"
4. **Use Series A Pricing:** "Based on current metrics, Series A likely $3-4/share; we want $2.25-3/share via discount"
5. **Don't Stack:** Avoid 30% discount + $2M cap (unsustainable)

#### Discount Rate Negotiation Scenarios

**Scenario A: Founder Says "No Discount"**

```
Investor Response Options:
1. "Then we need a $2M cap to protect our downside"
2. "Industry standard is 15-20% discount; no discount means no SAFE investment"
3. "Let's split difference: 10% discount + $4M cap"
```

**Scenario B: Investor Demands 35%+ Discount**

```
Founder Response Options:
1. "35% discount across multiple rounds = excessive founder dilution"
2. "That works if we combine with no valuation cap"
3. "For that discount, I need to approve all future SAFEs to manage dilution"
4. Alternative: "How about 20% discount + $3M cap instead?"
```

**Scenario C: Cap vs. Discount Trade-Off**

```
Founder Offer: Choose A or B, not both
A: No cap + 15% discount
B: $5M cap + 25% discount
C: $3M cap + 30% discount

Investor Evaluates Each:
- Option A: Unlimited upside cap if Series A > expected, but only 15% early reward
- Option B: Good balance of cap protection and early investor discount
- Option C: Strong protection with higher discount, but higher cap means less dilution

Note: This framing helps both sides see trade-offs clearly
```

### 3. VALUATION CAP METHOD: POST-MONEY vs. PRE-MONEY

**What They Are:**

| Type | Definition | Investor Impact |
|------|---|---|
| **Post-Money Cap** | Valuation AFTER SAFE investment included | Simpler math; "I know my exact ownership%" |
| **Pre-Money Cap** | Valuation BEFORE SAFE investment | Slightly more favorable to investor; more complex |

#### Founder Perspective on Cap Method

**Founder Goal:** Use method that minimizes investor ownership

```
Comparison Example:
Company: $1M pre-SAFE valuation
Investment: $250,000
Series A: $4M pre-money

POST-MONEY CAP ($1.5M):
- Investor's ownership = $250k / $1.5M = 16.7%
- Founder heavily diluted

PRE-MONEY CAP ($1.5M):
- Investor's ownership slightly lower (more complex math)
- Less investor dilution, but more confusing

Founder Strategy: Use POST-MONEY (industry standard, easier to understand)
Both investors and founders prefer post-money for simplicity.
```

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Default Position:
"We use post-money caps—industry standard for SAFEs, simpler for everyone."

Response to Pre-Money Request:
"We can use pre-money if that's your preference, but post-money is more standard.
Either way, the cap amount ($X) matters most."

Note: Don't die on this hill. Both work; post-money is just clearer.
```

#### Investor Perspective on Cap Method

**Investor Goal:** Use method that maximizes investor protection

```
Most investors prefer POST-MONEY because:
1. Simpler to calculate
2. Clear ownership percentage
3. Industry standard
4. Easier to compare with other SAFEs

Use PRE-MONEY only if:
- You're a sophisticated investor comfortable with complex math
- Pre-money cap significantly favors investor
- Company insists on pre-money
```

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Default Position:
"We prefer post-money for clarity, but are comfortable either way."

If Company Insists on Pre-Money:
"Okay, then we'll adjust the cap amount to equivalent protection."
(Calculate equivalent pre-money cap: roughly 60-70% of post-money cap)

Example:
Post-money cap: $5M
Pre-money equivalent: ~$3.3M
(Due to dilution formula)
```

### 4. MOST FAVORED NATION (MFN) CLAUSE

**What It Is:**
Investor automatically gets terms as good as any investor in subsequent SAFEs within a time period.

#### Founder Perspective on MFN

**Founder Goal:** Avoid or limit MFN to manage cap table

**MFN Concerns for Founders:**

```
Scenario: MFN Clause Problem

Month 0: SAFE #1
- Investor A: $100k at $5M cap, 20% discount, WITH MFN clause (broad)

Month 3: SAFE #2
- You negotiate with Investor B: $100k at $4M cap, 25% discount
- Better terms to close the round quickly

Month 4: MFN Trigger
- Investor A's MFN clause activates
- Investor A automatically gets $4M cap and 25% discount
- You now have dilution from both SAFEs with best terms

Result: More dilution than intended, harder to manage cap table
```

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We'd prefer no MFN clauses to maintain flexibility in pricing subsequent SAFEs."

Realistic Position:
"We'll include MFN clause but ONLY if:
1. Limited to [12 months] or next $X of investment
2. Only covers 'material' term improvements (not minor changes)
3. Investor acknowledges they're responsible for monitoring our cap table updates"

Fallback Position:
"Okay, full MFN clause, but we reserve right to issue SAFEs with different terms
(e.g., different discount tiers for different timing or company milestones)."

Compromise Option:
"Single-recipient MFN: If you're our lead investor, you get MFN.
Otherwise, you get notified if better terms are issued, but no automatic rights."
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **Time-Limit MFN:** "MFN applies only for [12 months] from signing"
2. **Amount-Limit MFN:** "MFN applies only to first $X of subsequent investment"
3. **Carve-Out Exceptions:** "MFN doesn't apply to [employee options / strategic investors / debt]"
4. **Material-Only MFN:** "Only material improvements (>5%) trigger MFN"
5. **Batch Closing:** Close multiple SAFEs at same terms (no MFN disputes)

#### Investor Perspective on MFN

**Investor Goal:** Protect against future dilution via better terms to others

**MFN Value for Investors:**

```
Scenario: Why MFN Matters

You invest $100k at $5M cap, 20% discount, without MFN

Three months later:
- Company issues SAFE at $3M cap, 30% discount (much better terms)
- Your MFN clause would trigger, but you don't have one
- You're now left with inferior terms
- Your ownership % is much lower than it should be

MFN protects against this scenario.
```

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We want a broad MFN clause covering all material term improvements for 24 months."

Realistic Position:
"We need MFN protection for [12 months] covering cap and discount improvements.
Notice of new SAFEs required within [10 days]."

Fallback Position:
"Okay, MFN limited to [12 months] and [material] changes only, but we need
quarterly notice of any new SAFEs issued."

Walk-Away Position:
"If no MFN clause, cap must be significantly lower ($2-3M) to compensate."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Request Notice Requirement:** "Company must notify me of new SAFEs within 10 days"
2. **Define 'Material':** "Material = >5% improvement in cap or >5% discount change"
3. **Scope Clearly:** "MFN applies to SAFEs and convertible notes, not debt or stock options"
4. **Time-Limit Explicitly:** "MFN applies to [12 months] from signing, then expires"
5. **Include Cure Right:** "If better terms issued, I can match them or get full refund"

#### MFN Negotiation Scenarios

**Scenario A: Founder Refuses MFN Entirely**

```
Investor Counter Offers:
1. "Then we need a 25% discount + $3M cap to compensate for lack of protection"
2. "Okay, no MFN, but we're leading investor and want pro-rata rights + board seat"
3. "No MFN is a deal-breaker for us; this is a standard investor protection"
```

**Scenario B: Investor Wants Perpetual MFN**

```
Founder Response:
"Perpetual MFN creates indefinite liability and management overhead.
We can offer [12-month] MFN, after which terms reset for each round."

Investor Counter:
"Okay, 18-month MFN, but only for the first $X of additional investment"
```

**Scenario C: Broad vs. Narrow MFN Scope**

```
Broad MFN (Investor Wants):
"Any improvement in any term triggers MFN benefit"
Result: Hard for founder to issue any future SAFE without cascading to past investors

Narrow MFN (Founder Prefers):
"Only material improvements (cap AND discount both lower) trigger MFN"
Result: Founder can fine-tune terms more precisely

Compromise:
"MFN triggers if: (1) cap drops >$500k, OR (2) discount increases >10%"
Both parties define "material" upfront
```

### 5. PRO-RATA RIGHTS

**What They Are:**
Right to maintain ownership % by investing proportionally in future rounds.

#### Founder Perspective on Pro-Rata Rights

**Founder Goal:** Avoid or limit pro-rata rights to maintain flexibility

**Pro-Rata Concerns:**

```
Scenario: Pro-Rata Rights Burden

You raise SAFE from 10 investors, each gets pro-rata rights.

Series A: $10M round
- You want to take only $5M (to minimize dilution)
- 10 investors with pro-rata rights = must allow each to participate
- To maintain 10 investors' ownership, you need to raise closer to $15-20M
- This forces larger Series A than planned

Impact: More dilution than intended, larger round than comfortable
```

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We'd prefer to keep Series A allocation flexible by not including pro-rata rights."

Realistic Position:
"We'll include pro-rata rights for lead/major investors (>$X), but not all investors.
Limits pro-rata to those who want ongoing involvement."

Fallback Position:
"Pro-rata rights allowed, but capped at [X%] of future rounds to maintain flexibility."

Compromise Option:
"Investors get pro-rata rights only after SAFE converts to Preferred Stock,
not in interim funding rounds."
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **Lead Investor Only:** "Pro-rata rights for lead investor (round facilitator)"
2. **Threshold:** "Pro-rata rights only if investor commits >$X initially"
3. **Cap Pro-Rata:** "Pro-rata participation capped at 30% of future rounds"
4. **Time-Limit:** "Pro-rata rights expire after [3 years] or [Series C], whichever first"
5. **Notice Requirement:** "Pro-rata participation by notice within [15 days]"

#### Investor Perspective on Pro-Rata Rights

**Investor Goal:** Maintain ownership % through future rounds

**Pro-Rata Value:**

```
Scenario: Why Pro-Rata Matters

You own 3% post-Series A from your $100k SAFE conversion.
Company raises Series B: $20M
Without pro-rata rights: Your 3% dilutes to 2.25% (auto dilution)
With pro-rata rights: You can invest $600k in Series B, maintain 3%

Pro-rata lets you follow growth and maintain influence.
```

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We want standard pro-rata participation rights in all future equity rounds."

Realistic Position:
"We want pro-rata rights in Series A and B, with right to negotiate removal after."

Fallback Position:
"Okay, pro-rata limited to Series A conversion and next 2 rounds,
but we can decline to participate if we choose."

Bare Minimum:
"If no pro-rata, we need much lower cap to compensate for lack of control."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Negotiate Scope:** "Pro-rata rights in Series A and B, not C+"
2. **Conditional on Good Investor Behavior:** Only for investors who participated in previous rounds
3. **Request Information Rights First:** "Pro-rata is only valuable if I know about future rounds early"
4. **Link to Lead Investor Status:** "Pro-rata applies if I'm lead of SAFE round"
5. **Build in Time Limits:** "Pro-rata expires [3 years] from SAFE signing or at IPO"

### 6. INFORMATION RIGHTS

**What They Are:**
Investor's right to receive company financial and operational updates.

#### Founder Perspective on Information Rights

**Founder Goal:** Minimize reporting burden while maintaining investor confidence

**Information Rights Concerns:**

```
Scenario: Information Rights Burden

You have 15 SAFE investors, each with information rights.
- Monthly financial statements
- Quarterly business updates
- Annual audit

Impact: 15 different formats, 15 email threads, 15 "clarification" calls
Time burden: 5-10 hours/month of admin work
```

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We'll provide annual audited statements within [120 days] of year-end."

Realistic Position:
"We'll provide quarterly unaudited statements within [45 days], annual audited within [120 days],
and key metrics monthly via dashboard."

Fallback Position:
"We'll provide standardized reporting: quarterly P&L, monthly metrics snapshot, annual audit.
All investors receive identical reports."

Accommodate Major Investors:
"Lead investor gets monthly calls + quarterly deep-dive; other investors get group quarterly updates."
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **Standardize Format:** "All investors get identical quarterly reports"
2. **Use Dashboard:** "Real-time metrics available via Carta/Pulley/dashboard"
3. **Group Updates:** "Quarterly group investor calls instead of individual calls"
4. **Limited Detail:** "Summary P&L only, not detailed P&L by department"
5. **Build in Exceptions:** "Reporting suspended if due diligence process underway"

#### Investor Perspective on Information Rights

**Investor Goal:** Track company progress and foresee Series A timing

**Information Rights Value:**

```
You invested $100k; you need to know:
- Is the company on track to Series A?
- Are metrics growing as expected?
- Are there problems brewing?
- When should Series A happen?

Information rights answer these questions.
```

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We want monthly financial statements, quarterly board updates, and annual audit."

Realistic Position:
"Monthly P&L, quarterly comprehensive business update, annual audit.
Timely enough to flag issues early."

Fallback Position:
"Quarterly financial statements within 45 days, annual audit,
and right to call quarterly investor meeting."

Bare Minimum:
"At minimum: quarterly P&L and annual audit. Can't support company blind."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Request Key Metrics:** "Monthly MRR, user growth, burn rate, runway"
2. **Require Timeliness:** "Statements within 45 days, not 90+ days"
3. **Request Audit:** "Annual audited statements (not review or compilation)"
4. **Inspection Rights:** "Right to audit books/records if concerns arise"
5. **Material Event Notice:** "Notify within 10 days if material negative change occurs"

### 7. CONVERSION TRIGGER TIMELINE

**What It Is:**
When and under what conditions SAFE converts to Preferred Stock.

#### Founder Perspective on Conversion Trigger

**Founder Goal:** Maximize flexibility on Series A timing

**Conversion Trigger Concerns:**

```
Scenario: Series A Timing Pressure

SAFE signed with automatic conversion trigger: "Qualifying Equity Financing"
Definition: Sale of Preferred Stock with minimum $500k proceeds

Problem: What if you're not ready for Series A?
- SAFE investors expect conversion soon
- But market timing bad, or you're building wrong product
- Investors will pressure: "When is Series A?"
- You may be forced into premature Series A

Solution: Build flexibility into conversion definition
```

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We define Qualifying Equity Financing as Series A of $2M+, subject to company approval."

Realistic Position:
"Qualifying Equity Financing is Series A of $X+ that company elects to pursue.
Minimum proceeds threshold ensures meaningful financing, not trivial seed rounds."

Fallback Position:
"Qualifying Financing has $X minimum threshold and [24-month] deadline.
If no Qualifying Financing by Month 24, SAFE converts to common stock."

Compromise:
"If no Series A by Month 20, investor gets option to:
(a) SAFE converts to common stock at [X]% ownership, OR
(b) Company repays investment + 5% annual interest"
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **Require Minimum Proceeds:** "Qualifying must be $1M+, not $100k"
2. **Define Series A Characteristics:** "Preferred Stock must have [liquidation preference / board seat / etc.]"
3. **Build in Optionality:** "Company retains right to accept/reject SAFE conversion"
4. **Time-Limit SAFE:** "SAFE expires Month [24]; after that, converts to common or repaid"
5. **Investor Approval:** "Series A pricing subject to [lead investor] approval"

#### Investor Perspective on Conversion Trigger

**Investor Goal:** Clear path to conversion, not indefinite waiting

**Conversion Trigger Value:**

```
You invested for equity, not cash:
- SAFE needs clear conversion trigger
- Otherwise capital trapped indefinitely
- Need to know Series A timeline
```

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We need automatic conversion on Series A > $1M, with 18-month Series A target."

Realistic Position:
"Automatic conversion when company raises Series A (>$500k preferred stock).
Series A expected within [18-24 months]."

Fallback Position:
"If no Series A by Month 24: (a) SAFE converts to common stock at [X]% ownership,
OR (b) Company repays with 5% annual interest."

Bare Minimum:
"Must have conversion trigger (either Series A or 24-month expiration)."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Define "Qualifying Financing":** "Series A with board seat, liquidation preference, etc."
2. **Set Minimum Proceeds:** "Only >$500k-$1M qualifies (eliminates small financings)"
3. **Build in Backstop:** "If no Qualifying Financing by Month 24, mandatory conversion"
4. **Request Repayment Option:** "If no Series A by Month 24, return $X + interest"
5. **Get Timeline Commitment:** "Founder commits to Series A push in [months 18-24]"

### 8. LIQUIDATION PREFERENCES AND CHANGE OF CONTROL

**What It Is:**
How SAFE holders get paid if company is sold or liquidated before conversion.

#### Founder Perspective on Liquidation Terms

**Founder Goal:** Minimize preference amounts, keep SAFE holders junior to debt

**Liquidation Concerns:**

```
Scenario: SAFE Preferences Eat Exit Value

You raise $1M in SAFEs with liquidation preferences.
Company gets acquisition offer for $5M.

High Preference Scenario:
- SAFE holders each get investment back: $1M
- Founders/employees get remaining $4M
- Fair enough, founders still benefit

Very Preferred Scenario:
- SAFE holders each get 1.5x multiple: $1.5M
- This reduces founder exit dramatically
```

**Founder Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"SAFE holders treated as unsecured creditors, subordinate to all debt."

Realistic Position:
"SAFE holders receive investment amount back OR pro-rata share of proceeds,
whichever is greater. No preference multiple."

Fallback Position:
"SAFE holders receive 1x preference (investment amount back) in change of control.
Participate pro-rata in anything left over."

Minimum Acceptable:
"Liquidation preference limited to investment amount (no 1.5x/2x multiple)."
```

**Founder Pro Tips:**

1. **1x Only:** "SAFE holders get 1x (investment back), not 2x or higher"
2. **No Participating Preference:** "Either get preference OR pro-rata, not both"
3. **Subordinate to Debt:** "Secured debt, employee options, founder loans all senior"
4. **Pro-Rata Sharing:** "After getting investment back, pro-rata with equity holders"
5. **Change of Control Definition:** "Includes only >90% asset sale or >50% control change"

#### Investor Perspective on Liquidation Terms

**Investor Goal:** Protect downside in acquisition/liquidation

**Liquidation Value:**

```
You invested $100k, hoping for 5x return.
But if company acquired for $2M (down round from expectations):
- Without preference: Your $100k might only be worth $50k
- With 1x preference: You get $100k back, keeps downside floor
```

**Investor Negotiation Approach:**

```
Opening Position:
"We want 1x participating preference (investment back + pro-rata of remainder)."

Realistic Position:
"1x non-participating preference (investment back OR pro-rata, whichever greater)."

Fallback Position:
"1x preference (investment back), no pro-rata participation."

Bare Minimum:
"At least 1x preference to protect downside in acquisition."
```

**Investor Pro Tips:**

1. **Push for 1x Non-Participating:** "Standard investor protection"
2. **Avoid Super-Senior Preference:** "Don't accept 2x+ unless warranted by risk"
3. **Understand Priority:** "Secured debt seniors you; get comfortable with debt levels"
4. **Define Change of Control:** "Includes M&A, asset sale, control change"
5. **Ensure Clarity:** "Get written definition of change of control to avoid disputes"

## COMMON NEGOTIATION PATTERNS

### Pattern 1: The "Industry Standard" Anchor

**How It Works:**

Investor says: "Industry standard for seed SAFEs is $3M cap and 20% discount"
Founder responds: "Based on comparables, we're seeing $6-8M caps"

**Resolution Approach:**

1. **Both sides research:** What are actual comps?
   - Similar company stage, market, product maturity
   - When were those SAFEs signed? (earlier = lower caps)
   - What was company valuation post-Series A?

2. **Acknowledge valid reference points**
   - "You're right that $3M is on the conservative end"
   - "We're comfortable with $5M based on [our metrics]"

3. **Find mutual middle ground**
   - "How about $4.5M cap + MFN clause + pro-rata rights?"
   - This gives investor protection if cap seems high

### Pattern 2: The "Dilution Waterfall" Discussion

**How It Works:**

Founder realizes: "If 5 investors each get 20% discount + $3M cap, I'm heavily diluted"

**Resolution Approach:**

1. **Model the impact**
   ```
   Show investors: "Here's the dilution math"
   SAFE 1: $100k at $3M cap = 3.3%
   SAFE 2: $100k at $3M cap = 3.3%
   SAFE 3: $100k at $3M cap = 3.3%
   SAFE 4: $100k at $3M cap = 3.3%
   SAFE 5: $100k at $3M cap = 3.3%
   Total: 16.5% dilution before Series A
   ```

2. **Propose tiered terms**
   - Early investors (first $250k): $3M cap
   - Later investors (second $250k): $4M cap
   - Final investors (third $250k): $5M cap
   - Spreads dilution, still fair to early investors

3. **Cap total SAFE raise**
   - "We're raising maximum $500k in SAFEs before Series A"
   - Limits dilution damage
   - Gives later investors certainty

### Pattern 3: The "Series A Expectations" Conversation

**How It Works:**

Investor asks: "When do you expect Series A?"
Founder says: "About 18 months"
Investor thinks: At $10M valuation, based on current metrics

**Resolution Approach:**

1. **Discuss Series A assumptions**
   ```
   FOUNDER: "We expect Series A in 18 months at $15M post-money valuation"
   INVESTOR: "That seems optimistic. I'd assume $8-10M more likely"

   This affects cap/discount negotiation:
   - If Series A likely $8-10M, cap should be $3-5M for investor protection
   - If Series A likely $15M, cap can be $6-8M
   ```

2. **Agree on publicly stated timeline**
   - "Series A expected in [18-24 months]"
   - Both parties know what "on track" looks like
   - If delayed beyond 24 months, investors know something's wrong

3. **Build this into SAFE terms**
   - Conversion trigger: Series A expected by Month 24
   - Backstop provision: If no Series A by Month 24, convert to common or repay

### Pattern 4: The "Trade-Offs" Framework

**How It Works:**

Instead of haggling, present clear trade-offs:

```
OPTION A: Lower Cap, No Discount
- Cap: $3M (strong investor protection)
- Discount: 0% (equal treatment at Series A)
- MFN: Yes
- Pro-Rata: Limited

OPTION B: Higher Cap, Higher Discount
- Cap: $6M (weaker protection)
- Discount: 25% (reward early risk)
- MFN: No
- Pro-Rata: Limited

OPTION C: Medium Cap, Medium Terms (Balanced)
- Cap: $4.5M
- Discount: 15%
- MFN: Yes (12-month limit)
- Pro-Rata: Yes (limited scope)
```

**How This Helps:**

- Investor chooses based on risk tolerance, not endless haggling
- Founder shows discipline: "Here are my options"
- Faster closure: investor sees choices clearly

### Pattern 5: The "Lead Investor" Premium

**How It Works:**

First investor gets better terms, funds partially, helps recruit others.

```
Lead Investor Gets:
- Board seat or board observation
- Pro-rata rights in Series A
- MFN clause
- Information rights
- Potentially lower cap/higher discount

Other Investors Get:
- Standard terms
- No pro-rata (or limited)
- Maybe no MFN
- Same information rights
```

**Why This Works:**

- Lead investor has incentive to help company succeed
- Other investors comfortable knowing someone is watching the company
- Founder has one key stakeholder to manage
- Easier cap table management

## RED FLAGS AND WALK-AWAY SCENARIOS

### Red Flags for Founders

**Investor Red Flags:**

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Response |
|----------|---|---|
| **Demands excessive cap** ($1-2M on $500k raise) | Unreasonable investor protection | Politely decline; this investor won't close cleanly |
| **Demands all special rights** (cap + discount + pro-rata + board) | Treating SAFE like preferred stock | Clarify SAFE is not equity; special rights come at Series A |
| **Wants to negotiate every detail** | Will be problematic throughout | Ask: "What 2-3 things matter most to you?" and focus there |
| **Wants controlling stock at Series A** | Not investor-SAFE dynamic | This needs preferred stock, not SAFE; pass on this investor |
| **Demands unlimited dilution controls** | Wants veto power over future funding | Explain you need funding flexibility; maybe not right partner |
| **Doesn't understand SAFE mechanics** | Will blame you for "unfair" conversion | Spend time educating, or reconsider relationship |
| **Wants personal guarantees** | SAFE is unsecured investment | Absolutely decline |

**Document Red Flags:**

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Response |
|----------|---|---|
| **Non-standard SAFE language** | Introduces unintended liability | Stick with Y Combinator standard form, minimal modifications |
| **Vague definitions** ("material business change", "adequate capitalization") | Creates future disputes | Require specific, objective definitions |
| **Personal liability clauses** | Exposes founder personally | Never accept; SAFE is corporate instrument |
| **Conversion triggers that are unrealistic** ("Automatic conversion if product launches") | Will never be met, creating disputes | Stick with standard triggers: Series A or time expiration |
| **Indemnification of investor** ("Company indemnifies investor for all losses") | Founder bears all investor risk | Minimal or mutual indemnification only |

**Relationship Red Flags:**

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Response |
|----------|---|---|
| **Investor is passive but demanding** | Won't help but will second-guess decisions | Seek engaged co-investors |
| **Investor has conflicts of interest** | May prioritize their other investments over yours | Full transparency; maybe decline investment |
| **Investor has track record of disputes** | Will litigate SAFE terms | Ask references; look for patterns |
| **Investor doesn't trust you** | Will demand excessive controls before Series A | Clarify: Series A investors will do deep diligence; SAFE based on trust |

### Red Flags for Investors

**Company Red Flags:**

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Response |
|----------|---|---|
| **Founder evasive about Series A timing** | Suggests no clear path; capital may be stuck | Require: "Series A expected by Month X or SAFE converts" |
| **Multiple SAFEs with conflicting terms** | Cap table is a mess; conversion nightmare | Request detailed cap table; understand term stack |
| **No clear product-market fit** | Company may need to pivot; SAFE may never convert | Require: clear product metrics and user traction |
| **Founder has history of failed startups** | Pattern of abandonment risk | Require: lower cap, more information rights |
| **Founder doesn't understand SAFE mechanics** | Will mismanage conversion, create disputes | Educate, or reconsider; bad sign if founder doesn't understand own instrument |
| **Company is pre-product with no resources** | SAFE capital may be wasted | Understand timeline; accept high risk |
| **Multiple SAFEs but no Series A progress** | Capital accumulating, no clear goal | Require: Series A push timeline, or backstop conversion |

**Document Red Flags:**

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Response |
|----------|---|---|
| **Non-standard SAFE language heavily favoring founder** | Terms heavily skewed | Don't accept; use standard Y Combinator form |
| **Vague conversion triggers** | When/how SAFE converts unclear | Require objective, clear conversion definitions |
| **Extremely high cap** ($15M+ on pre-product company) | Suggests Series A expectations are unrealistic | Probe: what metrics justify this cap? |
| **No information rights** | Can't track company progress | Information rights are minimum investor protection |
| **No MFN clause** | You could be stuck with worst terms | Request MFN for at minimum 12-month period |
| **No liquidation preference** | All downside, no protection | 1x preference is standard minimum |

**Relationship Red Flags:**

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Response |
|----------|---|---|
| **Founder is hiding cap table details** | Total dilution may be worse than stated | Request full cap table before signing |
| **Founder wants no information rights** | Will disappear after taking your money | Information rights are table stakes |
| **Founder is defensive about terms** | Suggests problematic cap table or unrealistic expectations | Probe why; consider walking |
| **Founder misrepresents metrics** | Shows lack of integrity | Verify all claims independently; decline investment |
| **Founder dismisses investor concerns** | Won't collaborate well through Series A | Look for founder who respects investor questions |

### Walk-Away Scenarios for Founders

**When to decline investor:**

1. **Investor demands 2x+ liquidation preference on SAFE**
   - This is excessive for unsecured investment
   - Signals investor expects company to fail
   - Wrong risk-return fit for SAFE

2. **Investor demands veto rights over future fundraising**
   - Gives investor too much control
   - Blocks your ability to raise capital
   - Not appropriate for SAFE holder

3. **Investor wants meaningful equity position pre-Series A**
   - If they want equity now, they should invest in preferred stock round
   - SAFE is for investors accepting converted-at-Series-A outcome

4. **Investor demands board seat immediately**
   - Board seats come post-Series A with preferred stock
   - SAFE holders don't get governance pre-conversion

5. **Terms are so unfavorable, Series A will be extremely difficult**
   - Example: 10 SAFEs at $2M cap each = 20%+ dilution pre-Series A
   - Makes Series A pricing impossible
   - Walk away from later SAFEs if early ones already committed

### Walk-Away Scenarios for Investors

**When to decline investment:**

1. **Company has no credible Series A path**
   - No clear product-market fit
   - No traction metrics
   - Founder has no Series A experience
   - Your capital may be stuck indefinitely

2. **Cap table is hopelessly complex**
   - Multiple SAFEs with conflicting terms
   - Founder doesn't understand total dilution
   - Series A negotiation will be nightmarish

3. **Terms are extremely founder-friendly**
   - $15M+ cap on pre-product company
   - No valuation cap (just discount)
   - No information rights
   - No MFN clause
   - Suggests founder has reality-distortion field

4. **Founder is evasive or dishonest**
   - Won't share full cap table
   - Misrepresents metrics
   - Avoids questions about Series A
   - Pattern of broken commitments
   - This will be painful when issues arise

5. **You can't afford to wait 3+ years**
   - SAFE may not convert for years
   - If you need earlier liquidity, SAFE is wrong instrument
   - Better to wait and invest in Series A

## NEGOTIATION CHECKLISTS

### Founder's SAFE Negotiation Checklist

**Before Negotiating:**
- [ ] Model total SAFE dilution across all investors
- [ ] Document justification for valuation cap (comps, metrics)
- [ ] Understand Series A expectations and timeline
- [ ] Prepare "tiered terms" option (different caps for different rounds)
- [ ] Have template SAFE terms in mind

**During Negotiation:**
- [ ] Listen for investor's key concerns (cap? discount? timeline?)
- [ ] Explain why your cap is reasonable (don't just assert)
- [ ] Offer trade-offs, not haggling (cap OR discount, not both worse)
- [ ] Introduce lead investor incentives if applicable
- [ ] Confirm investor understands SAFE is pre-equity, not governance

**Terms to Finalize:**
- [ ] Valuation cap (amount and post/pre-money)
- [ ] Discount rate (if any)
- [ ] MFN clause (time-limited and scope-defined)
- [ ] Pro-rata rights (scope and time limits)
- [ ] Information rights (reporting schedule)
- [ ] Conversion trigger (Series A definition, minimum proceeds)
- [ ] Liquidation preference (if any)
- [ ] Conversion expiration (24 months or Series A, whichever first)

**Before Signing:**
- [ ] Calculate total dilution impact across all SAFEs
- [ ] Verify investor is accredited (if needed)
- [ ] Confirm company has authority to issue SAFE
- [ ] Have lawyer review (even if using Y Combinator template)
- [ ] Set expectations on Series A timeline

### Investor's SAFE Negotiation Checklist

**Before Negotiating:**
- [ ] Research comparable SAFEs (stage, market, cap, discount)
- [ ] Model your expected ownership % at Series A (multiple scenarios)
- [ ] Understand company's Series A timeline and expectations
- [ ] Review cap table: total SAFEs already issued, terms
- [ ] Identify your 2-3 key term priorities

**During Negotiation:**
- [ ] Listen for founder's concerns (dilution, flexibility)
- [ ] Explain why cap is important (protects you in up rounds)
- [ ] Offer trade-offs that help both parties (higher cap + MFN)
- [ ] Probe on Series A: timing, expected valuation, next milestones
- [ ] Ask about other SAFEs: terms, investors, total amount

**Terms to Finalize:**
- [ ] Valuation cap (amount and methodology)
- [ ] Discount rate (if any)
- [ ] MFN clause (scope and duration)
- [ ] Pro-rata rights (important for follow-on)
- [ ] Information rights (what, when, to whom)
- [ ] Board observation (if you're lead investor)
- [ ] Conversion trigger clarity (when does conversion happen?)
- [ ] Liquidation preference (investment protection)
- [ ] Conversion expiration (what if no Series A?)

**Before Signing:**
- [ ] Model: What will my ownership % be if Series A is at various valuations?
- [ ] Verify company is incorporated, good standing
- [ ] Get full cap table including all SAFEs and options
- [ ] Confirm you're accredited (if needed)
- [ ] Understand company's burn rate and runway

### Joint Negotiation Checklist

**To Streamline Negotiation:**
- [ ] Both parties confirm use of Y Combinator standard SAFE template
- [ ] Both parties understand SAFE basics (no equity yet, converts at Series A)
- [ ] Both parties agree on 2-3 key term priorities
- [ ] Both parties commit to resolving within [X days]

**Key Conversation Topics:**
- [ ] Company: "Here's why we expect Series A at $X valuation"
- [ ] Investor: "Here's what we see for comparable companies"
- [ ] Both: "Let's agree on cap range ($3-5M) and find exact number"
- [ ] Both: "Which protective terms matter most? (cap, discount, MFN, pro-rata)"
- [ ] Both: "What happens if Series A doesn't happen by Month 24?"

**To Close Efficiently:**
- [ ] Narrow to 3-5 key terms (not all 20 possible terms)
- [ ] Use "trade-offs" framework: Option A / Option B / Option C
- [ ] Set deadline: "We'll decide between options by [date]"
- [ ] Get lawyer involved (on one side or both) only after terms agreed
- [ ] Plan signing and funding date

## SAMPLE LANGUAGE

### 1. Balanced Valuation Cap

**Investor-Friendly (Lower Cap):**
```
Valuation Cap = $3,000,000 (post-money)
Investment Amount = $100,000
Investor Ownership at Conversion = $100,000 / $3,000,000 = 3.33%
(Good protection for investor in up rounds)
```

**Founder-Friendly (Higher Cap):**
```
Valuation Cap = $8,000,000 (post-money)
Investment Amount = $100,000
Investor Ownership at Conversion = $100,000 / $8,000,000 = 1.25%
(Light dilution for founder)
```

**Balanced Compromise:**
```
Valuation Cap = $5,000,000 (post-money)
Investment Amount = $100,000
Investor Ownership at Conversion = $100,000 / $5,000,000 = 2.0%
(Reasonable protection; moderate dilution)
```

### 2. Tiered SAFE Terms (Managing Multiple Investors)

```
SAFE Round: Total Target = $500k

Tier 1: First $200k raised
- Valuation Cap: $3,000,000 (post-money)
- Discount: 20%
- MFN: Yes (12 months)
- Pro-Rata: Yes

Tier 2: Next $200k raised
- Valuation Cap: $4,000,000 (post-money)
- Discount: 15%
- MFN: Yes (12 months)
- Pro-Rata: Limited

Tier 3: Final $100k raised
- Valuation Cap: $5,000,000 (post-money)
- Discount: 10%
- MFN: No
- Pro-Rata: No

Rationale: Early investors get better terms (reward early risk)
Later investors accept lighter terms (less risk, more momentum)
```

### 3. Clear MFN Language

```
MOST FAVORED NATION CLAUSE:

1. Scope: MFN applies to any SAFE or convertible instrument issued
   within twelve (12) months from this SAFE's date.

2. Coverage: "Material Improvements" include:
   - Decrease in valuation cap by more than $250,000
   - Increase in discount rate by more than 5 percentage points
   - Addition of pro-rata rights
   - Reduction in MFN time period is NOT considered material

3. Trigger: If Company issues SAFE with material improvements,
   Investor automatically receives such improved terms.
   No renegotiation required.

4. Notice: Company shall notify Investor of any new SAFE within
   ten (10) business days of execution.

5. Expiration: MFN clause expires on [date], regardless of whether
   Series A has closed.

Example:
- Your SAFE: $5M cap, 20% discount
- New SAFE issued: $4M cap, 25% discount
- Your MFN triggers: You get $4M cap, 25% discount automatically
```

### 4. Clear Conversion Trigger

```
CONVERSION TRIGGER - STANDARD:

This SAFE converts to Series [Letter] Preferred Stock automatically
upon the earlier of:

(a) Qualifying Equity Financing
    Definition: Company's bona fide sale of Series [Letter] or later
    series of Preferred Stock, resulting in gross proceeds of at least
    $500,000 and following the Company's issuance of equity with
    liquidation preferences, board representation rights, and
    anti-dilution protection.

(b) Change of Control
    Definition: Sale of Company, merger with another entity, sale of
    >90% of assets, or change in voting control of board or shareholders
    such that pre-transaction shareholders hold <50% of voting control.

(c) Conversion Expiration
    If no Qualifying Equity Financing by month 24 from SAFE date,
    SAFE converts automatically to Common Stock at $[valuation]/share.
    [OR: Company repays Investment + 5% annual interest]
```

### 5. Balanced Pro-Rata Rights

```
PRO-RATA PARTICIPATION RIGHTS:

1. Scope: Pro-rata rights apply to Series A Equity Financing only.
   (Not to Series B, C, or later rounds.)

2. Participation Right: Upon Series A, Investor may invest up to
   Investor's ownership percentage × total Series A raise amount.

3. Example:
   - Your SAFE converts to 3% ownership post-Series A
   - Series A raise: $10,000,000
   - Your pro-rata right: Can invest up to $300,000 to maintain 3%

4. Timing: Company gives Investor [15 days] notice before Series A
   closes; Investor must commit within [10 days] of notice.

5. Expiration: Pro-rata rights expire [3 years] from SAFE date,
   or upon Series C closing, whichever is earlier.

6. Discretionary: Investor may decline to participate pro-rata.
   Ownership will dilute normally if Investor declines.
```

### 6. Information Rights Language

```
INFORMATION AND REPORTING RIGHTS:

1. Quarterly Financial Statements
   - Company provides unaudited P&L and balance sheet
   - Within 45 calendar days of quarter end
   - Delivered to all SAFE holders via shared link or email

2. Monthly Key Metrics (Optional)
   - Monthly revenue / MRR (if applicable)
   - User growth or other key metric
   - Burn rate and runway months
   - Delivered by 10th of following month via shared dashboard

3. Annual Audited Statements
   - Annual audited financial statements
   - Within 120 days of fiscal year end
   - CPA-audited (not reviewed or compiled)

4. Material Event Notice
   - Company notifies Investor within 10 days of:
     - Key employee departure
     - Major customer loss
     - Significant litigation
     - Product launch or material pivot
     - Any event that materially affects valuation

5. Board Observation (If Applicable)
   - [Lead Investor] shall have right to observe all board meetings
   - Receives board materials 5 days before each meeting
   - Can attend all meetings but has no voting rights

6. Inspection Rights
   - Investor has right to review Company's books and records
   - Upon request (not more than quarterly) to assess financial condition
   - During normal business hours at Company's offices
```

## CONCLUSION

**Negotiating a SAFE requires:**

1. **Clear communication** of each party's priorities
2. **Understanding of mechanics** (cap, discount, conversion math)
3. **Good faith** desire to structure fair terms
4. **Flexibility** on low-priority items in exchange for high-priority terms
5. **Speed** (SAFEs are meant to close quickly, not be negotiated for months)

**Best outcome:** Both sides understand the terms, feel protected, and can move forward to building the company and executing the Series A.

**Key principle:** Don't let the tail wag the dog. Spend 80% of time on 20% of terms (cap, discount, MFN, conversion trigger). Don't haggle over boilerplate.
