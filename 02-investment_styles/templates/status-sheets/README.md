# Investment Status Tracking Templates

This directory contains practical templates for tracking every stage of the investment process, from initial fundraising through post-closing operations. Use these templates to organize, monitor, and manage your investment rounds effectively.

---

## Overview of Templates

### Investment Lifecycle Timeline

```
PLANNING → PITCHING → NEGOTIATING → CLOSING → OPERATING
   ↓         ↓           ↓            ↓         ↓
   1         2,3         4            5,6       7
```

| Stage | Templates | Timeline |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Fundraising Planning** | #1 (Fundraising Status Tracker) | Pre-launch to funding completion |
| **Investor Outreach** | #2 (Investor Pipeline Tracker) | Ongoing during fundraising |
| **Due Diligence** | #5 (Due Diligence Checklist) | 2-8 weeks during active discussions |
| **Term Negotiation** | #4 (Term Sheet Comparison) | 1-4 weeks before closing |
| **Equity Management** | #3 (Cap Table Template) | Throughout and after each round |
| **Closing Execution** | #6 (Closing Checklist) | Final 2-4 weeks |
| **Post-Investment** | #7 (Post-Closing Tracker) | Immediately after closing & ongoing |

## Template Guide

### 1. FUNDRAISING_STATUS_TRACKER.md
**Purpose:** High-level view of your entire funding round

**When to Use:**
- At the start of each fundraising round
- Update weekly or bi-weekly during active fundraising
- Track progress toward funding goal
- Communicate with board and key advisors

**Key Sections:**
- **Round Overview** - Target vs. actual raised, commitment progress
- **Funding Status by Category** - VC, Angel, Strategic investors
- **Key Milestones** - Major dates and completions (pitch deck finalized, term sheet signed)
- **Monthly Progress Snapshot** - Burn rate, runway, new commitments
- **Risk Tracking** - Identify and monitor fundraising blockers
- **Action Items** - Task list for fundraising activities

**Best Practices:**
- Update every Friday with week's progress
- Use color-coded status indicators (🟢 On Track, 🟡 At Risk, 🔴 Blocked)
- Share monthly snapshot with board
- Identify risks early to pivot if needed
- Track both capital raised and investor commitments (not same thing)

**Example Use Case:**
*"We're raising $5M Series A. After 6 weeks, we've got $2.8M committed from 4 investors, we're in final negotiations with 2 more for $2.5M, and we have 8 active conversations. We're on track but need to accelerate one conversation to hit our close date."*

### 2. INVESTOR_PIPELINE_TRACKER.md
**Purpose:** Detailed tracking of each investor conversation and relationship

**When to Use:**
- Add new investors as you meet them
- Update after every investor interaction
- Weekly review to identify next steps
- Maintain throughout entire fundraising process and beyond

**Key Sections:**
- **Pipeline Summary** - Count of investors by stage (leads, pitches, DD, term sheets)
- **Active Investor Conversations** - Detailed list with contact, stage, timeline
- **Conversation Log** - Detailed record of each interaction per investor
- **Investment Criteria Match** - Does this investor fit your round?
- **Recent Rejections** - Learn from passes, plan rereach timing
- **Upcoming Pitch Schedule** - Calendar of scheduled pitches
- **Relationship Building Activities** - Coffee meetings, intros, demos
- **Pipeline Health Metrics** - Conversion rates, deal cycle length

**Best Practices:**
- Log every conversation within 24 hours (while details are fresh)
- Track action items and next steps for each investor
- Note specific interests and concerns for each investor
- Maintain investor research notes (thesis, recent deals, red flags)
- Update probability of close after each interaction
- Ideal pipeline multiplier: 3-4x your target raise (e.g., $15-20M pipeline for $5M raise)

**Example Use Case:**
*"We pitched ABC Ventures last Thursday. They loved our unit economics but are concerned about market size. Next step: send them competitive analysis by Friday, then they want to meet with our CTO. Probability: HIGH. Expected close timeline: 6 weeks if due diligence goes smoothly."*

### 3. CAP_TABLE_TEMPLATE.md
**Purpose:** Official record of company ownership, equity, and shareholder information

**When to Use:**
- Prepare before each funding round
- Update immediately after investor closes
- Maintain as official document for legal/tax purposes
- Share with investors as required by term sheet
- Reference before making equity grants (especially option pool)

**Key Sections:**
- **Summary** - Total shares, dilution, valuations, total invested
- **Main Capitalization Table** - All shareholders and ownership percentages
- **Detailed Investor Holdings** - By round with full term details
- **Employee Option Pool** - Vesting, grants, available pool
- **Preferred Stock Classes** - Different share classes and their rights
- **Key Terms Summary** - Important governance thresholds
- **Ownership Timeline** - How ownership has evolved through rounds
- **Founder Equity Vesting** - Individual founder vesting status
- **Documents & Signatures** - Legal document status tracking

**Critical Notes:**
- This is a LEGAL document—ensure accuracy with your counsel
- Update immediately after any equity event
- Distinguish between "current" shares and "fully diluted" (includes options, warrants, convertible notes)
- Track anti-dilution provisions carefully
- Founder vesting cliff typically 1-year, vesting over 4 years

**Example Use Case:**
*"Pre-Series A, we had 10M shares (founders 80%, ESOP 15%, advisors 5%). Post-$5M Series A at $10/share: We issued 500K shares. New breakdown: founders 74%, ESOP 14%, advisors 4%, Series A investor 8%. Fully diluted includes 200K RSUs granted, so Series A owns 7.5% FD."*

### 4. TERM_SHEET_COMPARISON.md
**Purpose:** Compare multiple term sheets side-by-side to make an informed decision

**When to Use:**
- Have at least 2 competing term sheets (ideally 3+)
- Use when deciding between investors
- Reference during negotiations to set targets
- Document decision rationale for board/advisors

**Key Sections:**
- **Quick Decision View** - Compare key metrics at a glance
- **Core Economics** - Valuation, price per share, dilution
- **Liquidation Preferences** - How payouts work in exit scenarios
- **Board & Governance** - Board seats, voting, information rights
- **Control & Protective Provisions** - Drag-along, tag-along, approval requirements
- **Conditions & Closing Requirements** - Any contingencies
- **Investor Profile** - Track record, value-add, references
- **Fees & Expenses** - Who pays what
- **Key Differences** - Advantages and red flags for each
- **Scoring Summary** - Weighted evaluation framework
- **Negotiation Priorities** - What to push back on

**Best Practices:**
- Don't just optimize for valuation—consider value-add and control
- "Better" valuation with bad terms can be worse than lower valuation with founder-friendly terms
- Consider liquidation preference impact (1x non-participating = founders keep more in exit)
- Check anti-dilution type (weighted average usually best for founders)
- Validate investor references with their portfolio companies

**Example Use Case:**
*"Investor A: $25M pre, 20% of company, but wants 2x participating preference + full ratchet AD. Investor B: $22M pre, 18%, 1x non-participating, weighted average AD. Investor A is higher valuation but gives up more upside. Investor B better for us. Negotiation: push for $23M pre as middle ground."*

### 5. DUE_DILIGENCE_CHECKLIST.md
**Purpose:** Founder's checklist to prepare for investor due diligence

**When to Use:**
- Create 4-8 weeks before expected closing (when you have warm leads)
- Assign ownership of each section
- Work through systematically—don't cram at the last minute
- Update continuously as documents are gathered

**Key Sections:**
- **Corporate & Legal Documents** - Formation, bylaws, cap table, stock options
- **Founder & Key Team** - Agreements, IP assignment, background checks
- **Intellectual Property** - Patents, trademarks, tech stack, open source audit
- **Financial Records** - P&L, tax returns, projections, metrics
- **Contracts & Revenue** - Customer agreements, partner deals, terms of service
- **Operational & Compliance** - HR, insurance, regulatory filings, data protection
- **Product & Market** - Roadmap, market research, competitive analysis
- **Virtual Data Room Setup** - Secure document sharing with investors
- **Investor Questions** - Prep 1-2 minute answers to common questions
- **Final Checklist** - Pre-DD signoff items

**Critical Timeline:**
- **Corporate/Legal Documents:** 4-8 weeks before close
- **Financial Records:** 2-4 weeks before close
- **Contracts & IP:** 4-8 weeks before close
- **Compliance/Insurance:** 2-4 weeks before close
- **Data Room & Q&A Prep:** 1-2 weeks before close

**Best Practices:**
- Assign an owner to each section (typically CFO/CEO/ops person)
- Don't wait for investor to request items—be proactive
- Fix problems BEFORE due diligence (clean up cap table, update IP assignments, etc.)
- Virtual data room = investor confidence (shows you're organized)
- Prepare answers to tough questions in advance (burn rate, revenue concentration, etc.)

**Example Use Case:**
*"Lead investor is starting DD in 3 weeks. We assigned each section owner and discovered: (1) One founder never formally assigned IP to company—fix with lawyer ASAP, (2) Missing some option agreements—recreate from emails, (3) No SOC2 certification—get started now. We'll have data room ready 2 weeks before expected close, giving them time without rushing us."*

### 6. CLOSING_CHECKLIST.md
**Purpose:** Step-by-step checklist for the final 2-4 weeks before closing

**When to Use:**
- Start once you have accepted a term sheet
- Execute in parallel with legal document preparation
- Critical items must happen before wires arrive
- Reference on closing day to ensure nothing is missed

**Key Sections:**
- **Legal Document Preparation** - Stock purchase agreement, investor rights agreement, board resolutions
- **Corporate Actions** - Board approval, cap table updates, stock certificates
- **Financial & Banking** - Wire instructions, bank account setup, fund reconciliation
- **Capitalization & Securities Law** - Form D filing, Rule 506 compliance, accredited investor verification
- **Tax & Accounting** - Section 83(b) elections, Form D preparation
- **Documentation & Records** - Closing binder, executed documents, backup copies
- **Communication & Notifications** - Investor coordination, team announcements, press release
- **Compliance & Regulatory** - Business licenses, regulatory filings, state amendments
- **Vendor Coordination** - ESOP administrator, accountant, investor portal setup
- **Final Legal Review** - Sign-off by counsel before closing
- **Closing Day Logistics** - Document assembly, notarization, wire coordination
- **Post-Closing** - Fund verification, document delivery, thank yous

**Critical Dates:**
- **T-30 days:** Begin legal document drafting with counsel
- **T-14 days:** All drafts complete, ready for investor review/negotiation
- **T-7 days:** Documents finalized and signature-ready
- **T-2 days:** Final review, printouts, notary scheduled
- **T-Day:** Execute documents, coordinate wire transfer
- **T+1 day:** Verify funds received, record cap table update

**Best Practices:**
- Work CLOSELY with legal counsel—they're your guide
- Get wire instructions in WRITING from investor (fraud prevention)
- Have multiple authorized signers to avoid delays
- Confirm closing date 24 hours in advance with all parties
- Prepare thank you communications before you're in closing chaos
- The lawyer is your teammate, not an obstacle—communicate frequently

**Example Use Case:**
*"We accepted our Series A term sheet on a Monday. By Wednesday, counsel has first draft of all docs. By Friday, we review and send to investor. Negotiations on valuation adjustments happen over the weekend. By Wednesday of the next week, all docs are final. We schedule closing for Friday. On Thursday, we assemble docs, confirm wire instructions again, schedule notary. Friday morning: sign documents, Friday afternoon: funds arrive, funds verified, cap table updated. Done!"*

### 7. POST_CLOSING_TRACKER.md
**Purpose:** Manage all items that must happen immediately and months after closing

**When to Use:**
- Immediately after funds are wired and documents are signed
- Update daily/weekly for first month
- Then ongoing for regular investor management
- Reference for quarterly/annual requirements

**Key Sections:**
- **Immediate Post-Closing (24-48 hours)** - Fund verification, announcements, documentation
- **Legal & Corporate Filing (Within 1 week)** - Form D, cap table, stock certificates, board minutes
- **Accounting & Tax Compliance (1-2 weeks)** - Balance sheet entry, 409A valuation, tax memo
- **Board & Governance Setup (1-2 weeks)** - Investor director, board portal, information rights
- **Financial & Banking Setup (1-2 weeks)** - Budget, signatory authority, monthly close process
- **Cap Table & Equity Plan Updates (2-4 weeks)** - Final cap table, employee communications
- **Investor Onboarding (1-2 weeks)** - Investor contact, portal access, KYC information
- **Employee Communications (1-2 weeks)** - Equity details, growth plan, team morale
- **Insurance & Risk Management (2-4 weeks)** - D&O coverage, insurance updates
- **Compliance & Legal (2-4 weeks)** - Website updates, regulatory notifications, contract review
- **90-Day Review** - First investor alignment check
- **Ongoing Investor Relationship** - Monthly, quarterly, annual rhythms
- **Document Archive** - Secure long-term storage

**Critical Deadlines:**
- **Form D Filing:** Within 15 days of close
- **Section 83(b) Elections:** Within 30 days if founders have vesting shares
- **Board Meeting:** Within 2 weeks
- **Cap Table Updated:** Within 1 week
- **Investor Portal:** Within 2 weeks
- **First Board Meeting:** Within 1 month

**Best Practices:**
- Don't disappear after closing—you have MORE work to do, not less
- Monthly investor updates keep relationship strong
- Board meetings aren't punishment—they're your stakeholder alignment
- 90-day review is critical inflection point—address issues early
- Ongoing communication prevents surprises

**Example Use Case:**
*"We closed on Friday. Monday: announced to team, confirmed wire received, sent thank yous to investor and advisors. Tuesday: counsel filed Form D. Wednesday: cap table updated in our system and shared with board. Thursday: investor director access to board portal set up. We schedule first board meeting for 3 weeks out. Then we shift to implementation: spend money according to plan, track metrics, report monthly. First board meeting review progress. At 3-month mark, formal check-in on whether we're on track with milestones."*

## Workflow Integration Guide

### For Founders Raising Capital

**Phase 1: Pre-Launch (Months -2 to 0)**
- [ ] Use **#3 Cap Table** to understand current ownership before pitching
- [ ] Create **#1 Fundraising Status Tracker** to set goals and milestones
- [ ] Prepare **#5 Due Diligence Checklist** items in advance (don't wait for ask)

**Phase 2: Pitching (Months 0-3)**
- [ ] Start **#2 Investor Pipeline Tracker** with first 20-30 outreaches
- [ ] Log every investor conversation in **#2** (conversation logs are gold)
- [ ] Update **#1** weekly with pipeline progress and metrics
- [ ] Identify any DD items that need work now, before investor asks

**Phase 3: Active Negotiations (Months 1-3)**
- [ ] When you have 1-2 interested investors, they request DD
- [ ] Work through **#5 Due Diligence Checklist** sections systematically
- [ ] Set up virtual data room and upload organized documents
- [ ] Prepare answers to common investor questions section

**Phase 4: Term Sheets Received (Month 2-3)**
- [ ] Use **#4 Term Sheet Comparison** to evaluate offers
- [ ] Score weighted criteria if multiple offers
- [ ] Identify negotiation priorities
- [ ] Present recommendation to board

**Phase 5: Closing (Month 3)**
- [ ] Accept term sheet and begin legal process with counsel
- [ ] Use **#6 Closing Checklist** to track all items
- [ ] Coordinate document execution, wire transfers, regulatory filings
- [ ] Celebrate milestone!

**Phase 6: Post-Closing (Weeks 1-12 after close)**
- [ ] Use **#7 Post-Closing Tracker** for all items
- [ ] Execute critical items in first 2 weeks
- [ ] Set up board governance, information rights, monthly rhythms
- [ ] 90-day review at the 3-month mark

### For Ongoing Portfolio Management

**Monthly:**
- [ ] Update **#1 Fundraising Status Tracker** with KPIs
- [ ] Send metrics to investors per **#7 Post-Closing Tracker**
- [ ] Review **#7** outstanding items

**Quarterly:**
- [ ] Board meeting using cadence in **#7**
- [ ] Cap table review with **#3**
- [ ] Review **#7** 90-day items at first board meeting

**Annually:**
- [ ] Cap table cleanup—remove options not granted, update vesting
- [ ] **#3 Cap Table** full review and refresh
- [ ] Plan next fundraising round (restart with **#1**)

## Key Metrics to Track

### Fundraising Metrics (#1)
- **Capital Raised vs. Target:** % of goal achieved
- **Number of Commitments:** Investor count vs. target
- **Conversion Rate:** Pitches → Term sheets
- **Average Deal Cycle:** Days from first meeting to close
- **Burn Rate vs. Runway:** How long until you run out of money
- **Pipeline Multiplier:** Total active conversations ÷ target raise (should be 3-4x)

### Pipeline Metrics (#2)
- **Stage Distribution:** % of prospects in each stage
- **Pipeline Value by Stage:** $ opportunity at each stage
- **Win Rate:** % of pitches that advance
- **Rereach Timing:** When to follow up with passed investors
- **Upcoming Pitches:** This week's calendar

### Equity Metrics (#3)
- **Founder Ownership Post-Round:** Dilution from this round
- **Fully Diluted Ownership:** Including options, warrants, convertibles
- **Option Pool %:** Post-round pool size (typically 10-20% post-Series A)
- **Liquidation Preference:** 1x vs. 2x vs. 3x (impacts exit math)
- **Anti-Dilution Type:** Weighted avg. vs. full ratchet (impacts future raises)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Impact | Solution |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Not tracking investor conversations | Dropped threads, missed follow-ups | Use **#2** religiously—log same day |
| Waiting until DD request to gather documents | Scrambling, delays, shows disorganization | Prepare **#5** items 2 months early |
| Not comparing term sheets properly | Negotiate poorly, accept bad terms | Use **#4** scoring framework |
| Messy cap table | Investor red flag, hard to close | Use **#3**, update immediately after each event |
| Disappearing after closing | Investor relationship suffers, missed accountability | Use **#7**—it's a 12-month roadmap |
| Poor communication of metrics | Investors think you're hiding problems | Monthly updates per **#7** (good news AND bad) |
| Not preparing for DD questions | Looks unprepared, raises concerns | Prepare **#5** investor questions section |
| Missing regulatory deadlines | Legal/tax problems | **#6 & #7** have deadlines built in—follow them |

## Templates Best Practices Summary

### Consistency
- Use the same templates across all funding rounds
- Update templates immediately (don't batch updates)
- Share templates with advisors/board for accountability
- Version control important documents (cap table, term sheets)

### Communication
- Share **#1** status with board monthly
- Send **#2** pipeline summary to advisor when asking for intros
- Share **#4** comparison with board before choosing investor
- Send **#7** items checklist to all responsible parties

### Confidentiality
- Don't share **#2** (pipeline) with investors
- Don't share **#4** (comparison) with other investors
- Cap table (**#3**) is shared with investors/counsel but not public
- Keep **#6** & **#7** internal (between company and counsel)

### Automation
- Spreadsheet versions: Use Google Sheets for easy sharing
- Consider VC management tools (Carta, Pulley, Captable.io) for cap table
- Calendar integration for **#6** & **#7** deadlines
- Monthly reminder emails for **#7** investor reporting
