# Stage-Appropriate Investment Vehicles

## What Investment Types Fit Each Company Stage

---

## OVERVIEW BY STAGE

| **Stage** | **Company Age** | **Revenue** | **Team** | **Key Vehicles** | **Capital Range** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ideation/Pre-Seed** | 0-3 months | $0 | Co-founders | Bootstrapping, Grants, SAFEs | $0-100k |
| **Seed** | 3-12 months | $0-50k/mo | 2-5 people | SAFEs, Convertible Notes, Angel | $100k-500k |
| **Series A** | 12-24 months | $50k-200k/mo | 5-15 people | Priced Equity, Strategic VC | $1M-5M |
| **Series B** | 24-36 months | $200k-1M/mo | 15-50 people | Priced Equity, Venture Debt | $5M-20M |
| **Growth Stage (Series C+)** | 36+ months | $1M+/mo | 50+ people | Priced Equity, Venture Debt, M&A | $20M+ |

---

## STAGE 1: IDEATION / PRE-SEED (0-3 Months)

### Company Characteristics
- **Status:** Idea stage, no product yet
- **Revenue:** $0 (no revenue yet)
- **Team:** Founder(s) only
- **Traction:** Concept validation, perhaps MVP started
- **Capital Needed:** Minimal, to reach seed stage

### Capital Requirements
- **Total Capital:** $25k-$100k
- **Use Case:** Initial product development, market validation
- **Timeline:** 3-6 months to seed stage
- **Burn Rate:** Minimal (founder living on savings or part-time income)

### BEST VEHICLES FOR PRE-SEED

#### 1. Bootstrap / Self-Funding
**Fit: ★★★★★ (Excellent)**

**Why It Works:**
- Founder has full control
- No dilution
- No investor management overhead
- Forces efficient spending
- Tests founder's commitment

**How to Approach:**
- Use personal savings
- Part-time consulting to fund development
- Use free tools (GitHub, Figma free tier, etc.)
- Bartering for services
- Friends and family help (unpaid)

**Limitations:**
- Limited capital ($25k-$50k realistic)
- Founder may need income
- Slow progress
- High founder stress

**Timeline:** 3-6 months until seed-ready

---

#### 2. Grants
**Fit: ★★★★☆ (Very Good)**

**Why It Works:**
- Non-dilutive capital
- No repayment obligation
- Can fund early R&D
- Founder maintains control
- Validates concept

**Best Grant Sources at Pre-Seed:**
- SBIR Phase 1: $150k (federal)
- Angel grants: Various non-dilutive funds
- Startup competitions: Prize money
- University-based: SXSW, etc.
- Corporate grants: Microsoft, Google for founders

**Realistic Amounts:** $25k-$150k
**Timeline:** 2-4 months (shorter than Series A grants)
**Conditions:** Often require R&D focus, product development

**Application Tips:**
- Focus on innovation/R&D angle
- Show prototype or strong MVP
- Clear use of funds for development
- Research-based approach
- Team qualifications important

---

#### 3. Pre-Seed SAFEs / Angel Notes
**Fit: ★★★☆☆ (Good, but early)**

**Why It Works:**
- Quick capital
- Minimal documentation
- Can aggregate small checks
- Sets cap table baseline
- Rewards early believers

**How to Approach:**
- Target angel networks (local tech scenes)
- Leverage personal network
- Friends and family (informed)
- Early-stage funds with pre-seed focus
- Accelerators (Y Combinator, Techstars pre-seed)

**Realistic Amounts:** $50k-$250k
**Number of Investors:** 3-5 preferred
**Terms:** High caps ($2M-$3M), 20%+ discounts
**Timeline:** 4-8 weeks

**Challenges:**
- Small checks are inefficient to raise
- Investors harder to find at pre-seed
- Founder distraction from product building
- Multiple investors create cap table overhead
- Need compelling story to attract investors

---

### AVOID AT PRE-SEED

| Vehicle | Why Not |
|---|---|
| Priced Equity | Too early - no product, no traction |
| Series A from VCs | Won't fund pre-product |
| Venture Debt | No revenue to support repayment |
| Revenue-Based | No revenue yet |
| Strategic Corporate VC | Too early for strategic partnerships |
| Equity Crowdfunding | Premature, needs more traction |

## STAGE 2: SEED (3-12 Months)

### Company Characteristics
- **Status:** MVP/early product, initial market validation
- **Revenue:** $0-$50k/month (pre-revenue common)
- **Team:** 2-5 people
- **Traction:** Early users, product feedback, proof of concept
- **Capital Needed:** Moderate, to reach Series A

### Capital Requirements
- **Total Capital:** $250k-$1M
- **Use Case:** Product development, team building, initial marketing
- **Timeline:** 12-18 months to Series A
- **Burn Rate:** $10k-$30k/month typical

### BEST VEHICLES FOR SEED

#### 1. SAFEs (Primary Vehicle)
**Fit: ★★★★★ (Excellent)**

**Why It Works:**
- Quick capital deployment (days vs. weeks)
- Minimal legal overhead
- Investor-friendly terms (cap + discount)
- Standardized format (well understood)
- Sets expectations for Series A
- Perfect for multiple small investors

**Typical Terms:**
- Cap: $3M-$5M post-money
- Discount: 20% typical
- MFN clause: Recommended
- Pro-rata rights: Helpful if included

**How to Approach:**
- Use Y Combinator SAFE form (standardized)
- Target angel groups, micro-VCs, lead angels
- Typical investors: $25k-$100k checks
- Batch rounds: 3-5 SAFEs in phase, close within 4-6 weeks

**Realistic Amounts:** $300k-$750k
**Number of Investors:** 5-10 common
**Timeline:** 3-6 weeks to close

**Tips:**
- Model your dilution (SAFEs + Series A)
- Know your Series A target valuation
- Set caps relative to Series A expectations
- Communicate to investors about Series A timing
- Consolidate investors when possible

---

#### 2. Convertible Notes
**Fit: ★★★★☆ (Very Good)**

**Why It Works:**
- Slightly more structured than SAFEs
- Maturity date protection (important if no Series A)
- Interest accrual (6-8% common)
- Investor protection via maturity
- Easier for convertible-first investors

**Typical Terms:**
- Amount: $25k-$500k per note
- Interest: 6-8% annually
- Maturity: 24-36 months
- Conversion: Discount 15-25% typical

**How to Approach:**
- Use standardized documents (500 Startups, etc.)
- Target angel investors who prefer debt/maturity
- Easier for investors who want downside protection
- Quick closing (1-2 weeks typical)

**Realistic Amounts:** $300k-$750k
**Number of Investors:** 3-7 common
**Timeline:** 2-4 weeks to close

**Considerations:**
- Interest accrues (becomes liability)
- Must track maturity date (conversion or repayment)
- More complex than SAFEs
- Better for later seed (closer to Series A certainty)

**When to Use Over SAFEs:**
- Investors want maturity protection
- Company not sure about Series A timing
- Want interest income (benefits investors, costs founders)
- Investors more risk-averse

---

#### 3. Angel Investment (Direct Equity)
**Fit: ★★★☆☆ (Good, Limited)**

**Why It Works:**
- Large checks possible ($100k-$500k from single investor)
- Mentorship and network access
- Faster to close if one lead angel
- But: Complex cap table early

**How to Approach:**
- Target angel networks in your industry
- Leverage introductions from advisors/founders
- Angel groups (often invest together)
- Equity: 2-5% per angel typical

**Realistic Amounts:** $100k-$500k per investor
**Number of Investors:** 1-3 optimal
**Timeline:** 4-8 weeks to close

**Challenges:**
- Complex documentation early (50+ pages)
- Each investor wants different terms
- Board seats/governance overhead
- Fragmented investor base
- Harder to manage than SAFEs

**When to Use:**
- One lead angel who brings significant network
- Mentor relationship valuable
- Need strategic advice along with capital
- Can negotiate standard terms

---

### CONDITIONAL VEHICLES FOR SEED

#### Grants (Continued)
**Fit: ★★★☆☆ (Good if available)**

**Relevance:**
- SBIR Phase 2: $500k-$750k (if Phase 1 completed)
- Fast-growing tech grants: Various sources
- Industry-specific grants: Available in many sectors
- Non-dilutive, but restrictive

**Limitations:**
- Long approval timeline (3-6 months)
- Often limited to specific use cases (R&D)
- Reporting burden
- Not suitable as only source for seed stage

**Best Use:** Supplemental to SAFEs
- Use SAFEs for cash (3-4 months)
- Apply for grants in parallel
- Grants extend runway (6-12 additional months)

---

### AVOID AT SEED

| Vehicle | Why Not |
|---|---|
| Priced Equity | Too early - no product-market fit yet |
| Series A from VCs | Will demand traction first |
| Venture Debt | No revenue to support loans |
| Revenue-Based | No revenue yet |
| Strategic VC | No strategic value yet |
| Equity Crowdfunding | Premature visibility |
| Bootstrap only | Too slow for competitive market |

## STAGE 3: SERIES A (12-24 Months)

### Company Characteristics
- **Status:** Product-market fit emerging, clear go-to-market
- **Revenue:** $50k-$200k/month
- **Team:** 5-15 people
- **Traction:** Growing user base, repeatable growth, clear metrics
- **Capital Needed:** Larger round, to scale sales/product

### Capital Requirements
- **Total Capital:** $1M-$5M
- **Use Case:** Sales team, product expansion, market expansion
- **Timeline:** 18-36 months to Series B
- **Burn Rate:** $50k-$150k/month typical

### BEST VEHICLES FOR SERIES A

#### 1. Priced Equity Rounds (PRIMARY)
**Fit: ★★★★★ (Excellent)**

**Why It Works:**
- Industry standard for Series A
- Investors expect equity and governance
- Clear valuation (vs. ambiguity of SAFEs)
- Board seat and control framework
- Institutional capital
- Attracts quality VCs

**Typical Terms:**
- Amount: $1M-$5M
- Valuation: $5M-$15M post-money
- Lead Investor: Institutional VC
- Board Seats: Founder + 1-2 investors + observer
- Liquidation Preference: 1x non-participating
- Anti-Dilution: Weighted average
- Pro-Rata Rights: Standard

**How to Approach:**
1. **Preparation (2-3 months before)**
   - Clean up cap table (convert SAFEs before Series A)
   - Audit financials (GAAP ready)
   - Prepare data room (all documents)
   - Build investor pipeline (20-30 targets)
   - Create narrative (compelling story)

2. **Pitch Process (4-8 weeks)**
   - Pitch to VCs (2-3 meetings typical per firm)
   - Initial interest → term sheet
   - Term sheet negotiation (1-2 weeks)
   - Final board approval

3. **Due Diligence (2-4 weeks)**
   - Legal diligence (lawyers review docs)
   - Financial diligence (auditor review)
   - Reference checks
   - Customer calls (some investors do)

4. **Closing (1-2 weeks)**
   - Final docs signed
   - Board meeting for approval
   - Capital deposited
   - Cap table updated

**Timeline:** 12-16 weeks total (3-4 months typical)

**Cost:**
- Legal fees: $20k-$50k
- Accounting fees: $10k-$20k
- Misc: $5k-$10k
- Total: $35k-$80k

---

#### 2. Strategic Corporate VC / CVCs
**Fit: ★★★☆☆ (Good, if strategic)**

**Why It Works:**
- Large capital ($2M-$5M+)
- Strategic resources + capital
- Business development opportunities
- Partnership potential
- Can have non-monetary benefits

**Typical Terms:**
- Equity: 10-25% (often higher than pure VC)
- Valuation: Usually lower (strategic value)
- Board seat: Standard
- Control: Possible restrictions/veto rights
- Partnership: Often expected

**Challenges:**
- Strategic requirements (may constrain company)
- Slower decision-making (large company)
- Potential conflicts (investor = competitor?)
- Veto rights on partnerships
- May want licensing/technology access

**How to Approach:**
- Target corporate strategic partners
- Partnership value: Important as capital value
- Understand strategic fit
- Negotiate non-compete and veto restrictions
- Consider long-term relationship

**When Strategic VC Makes Sense:**
- Company can genuinely partner with CVC parent
- Access to partner's customers/distribution
- Technology transfer makes sense
- Partnership adds real value beyond capital

---

#### 3. SAFEs (Bridge Approach)
**Fit: ★★☆☆☆ (Limited, Bridge Only)**

**Why It Works:**
- If Series A delayed (closing takes longer)
- Bridge capital to close gap
- Bridges between term sheet and close
- Typically much smaller ($200k-$500k)

**Typical Use:**
- Term sheet signed, closing in 3+ months
- Need capital to operate in interim
- Investors agree to bridge SAFEs
- Convert on same terms as Series A

**Not Best For:**
- Don't use SAFEs instead of Series A
- VCs expect priced equity at Series A
- Using SAFEs past Series A signals problem
- Better to take venture debt if capital gap

---

### CONDITIONAL VEHICLES FOR SERIES A

#### Venture Debt (Supplemental)
**Fit: ★★★☆☆ (Good, supplemental only)**

**Purpose:**
- Supplement priced round
- Extend runway without equity dilution
- Typical: $500k-$2M alongside $3M equity

**Benefits:**
- Non-dilutive capital
- Extends runway 12-24 months
- No equity dilution
- No governance overhead

**How to Use:**
- Structure: Close equity first, debt second
- Timing: Same close or staggered
- Amount: 25-50% of equity round size
- Terms: 3-4 year maturity, 10-12% interest

**Typical Structure:**
```
Series A: $3M equity
Series A Debt: $1M venture debt
Total Capital: $4M
Equity Dilution: Same as $3M round
Additional Runway: 12-24 months from debt proceeds
```

---

### AVOID AT SERIES A

| Vehicle | Why Not |
|---|---|
| Purely Bootstrapping | Too capital-intensive to scale |
| Grants | VCs want to own equity at this stage |
| Convertible Notes | Outdated for Series A (use equity) |
| Revenue-Based | VCs expect ownership stake |
| Equity Crowdfunding | Dilutes future rounds, cap table mess |

## STAGE 4: SERIES B (24-36 Months)

### Company Characteristics
- **Status:** Clear traction, repeatable GTM, profitable unit economics
- **Revenue:** $200k-$1M/month
- **Team:** 15-50 people
- **Traction:** Clear growth trajectory, solid retention, path to profitability
- **Capital Needed:** Large round, for scaling infrastructure/team

### Capital Requirements
- **Total Capital:** $5M-$20M
- **Use Case:** Scale sales org, expand product, international expansion
- **Timeline:** 24-48 months to Series C or exit
- **Burn Rate:** $200k-$500k/month typical

### BEST VEHICLES FOR SERIES B

#### 1. Priced Equity (PRIMARY)
**Fit: ★★★★★ (Excellent)**

**Why It Works:**
- Standard for growth rounds
- Institutional investors only
- Substantial capital available
- Clear valuation
- Multiple lead investors possible

**Typical Terms:**
- Amount: $5M-$20M
- Valuation: $25M-$100M post-money
- Lead: Tier-1 or Tier-2 VC, often institutional
- Board Seats: Typically 2-3 investor seats, plus founder
- Preferences: 1x non-participating common in this stage
- Anti-Dilution: Weighted average increasingly negotiated away
- Pro-Rata Rights: Standard for larger investors

**Differences from Series A:**
- Larger capital amounts
- More sophisticated investors
- More complex governance
- Potential multiple lead investors
- Higher valuation expectations
- Longer due diligence (market size validation)

---

#### 2. Venture Debt (Supplemental)
**Fit: ★★★★☆ (Very Good, supplemental)**

**Why It Works:**
- Extend runway without equity dilution
- Revenue-based or fixed term options
- Institutional debt available at Series B stage
- Can be $5M-$10M+ for profitable companies
- Alongside equity round

**Typical Structure:**
```
Series B Equity: $8M
Series B Debt: $2M
Total Capital: $10M
Equity Dilution: Same as $8M round
Additional Runway: 12-18 months
Additional Repayment: $2M + 12% interest = $2.24M over 3 years
```

**Best Use:**
- Companies with clear revenue
- Predictable growth trajectory
- Want to extend runway and minimize dilution
- Need capital velocity (fast deployment)

---

### CONDITIONAL VEHICLES FOR SERIES B

#### Revenue-Based Financing
**Fit: ★★☆☆☆ (Limited, for specific cases)**

**Relevance:**
- If company profitable or near profitable
- Could use instead of some equity
- Depends on company's revenue growth trajectory

**Example:**
```
Company Situation:
- Revenue: $500k/month growing 10%/month
- Profitable: Already break-even
- Need Capital: $3M for growth

Options:
A) $5M equity at $50M valuation (10% dilution)
B) $3M equity + $2M revenue-based

Revenue-Based Structure:
- $2M capital
- 6% of monthly revenue until $3M returned
- Current payment: $30k/month (6% of $500k)
- Growing to $36k/month within 6 months
- Payback in ~100 months (8+ years)
```

**When It Makes Sense:**
- Profitable or near-profitable
- Want to minimize dilution
- Revenue growth will cover payments
- Don't need additional governance

---

### AVOID AT SERIES B

| Vehicle | Why Not |
|---|---|
| SAFEs | VCs will demand equity (not SAFEs) |
| Convertible Notes | Outdated at this stage |
| Angel Investment | Need institutional capital |
| Bootstrap | Cannot scale fast enough |
| Grants | VCs expect company to have funding |
| Equity Crowdfunding | Dilutes institutional cap table |

## STAGE 5: GROWTH STAGE / SERIES C+ (36+ Months)

### Company Characteristics
- **Status:** Clear market leader trajectory, strong profitability path
- **Revenue:** $1M+/month
- **Team:** 50+ people
- **Traction:** Market-leading position, strong retention, clear exit path
- **Capital Needed:** Large rounds for final scaling or exit positioning

### Capital Requirements
- **Total Capital:** $20M-$100M+ (C, D, E rounds possible)
- **Use Case:** Market dominance, geographic expansion, acquisitions
- **Timeline:** 12-36 months to exit
- **Burn Rate:** Often profitable or near-profitable

### BEST VEHICLES FOR GROWTH STAGE

#### 1. Priced Equity (PRIMARY)
**Fit: ★★★★★ (Excellent)**

**Why It Works:**
- Only option for companies at this scale
- Institutional capital only
- Multiple lead investors common
- Sophisticated term sheets

**Typical Terms (Series C):**
- Amount: $20M-$100M+
- Valuation: $100M-$500M+ post-money
- Lead: Tier-1 VCs only
- Multiple investor rounds: Series C, D, E common
- Governance: Complex with multiple board seats
- Preferences: Negotiated heavily (non-participating increasingly common)

---

#### 2. Venture Debt
**Fit: ★★★★☆ (Very Good, common)**

**Why It Works:**
- Substantial loans available ($10M-$50M+)
- Revenue-based for profitable companies
- Extends runway without equity dilution
- Often taken alongside Series C/D

**Typical Structure:**
```
Series C: $30M equity
Series C Debt: $10M venture debt
Total Capital: $40M
Equity Dilution: From $30M round
Debt Repayment: $10M + ~$12M interest over 5 years
```

**Why Popular at This Stage:**
- Companies nearly/fully profitable
- Don't need all capital immediately
- Debt cheaper than equity (interest vs. dilution)
- Lenders comfortable with large loan amounts

---

#### 3. Strategic M&A / Acquisition
**Fit: ★★★★★ (Excellent exit path)**

**Why It Works:**
- Many growth companies acquired before IPO
- Strategic buyer pays premium for market position
- Often 60-70% of funded companies exit this way
- Only 20-30% achieve IPO

**Typical Exit:**
- Acquirer: Larger company in same/adjacent market
- Valuation: $500M-$5B+
- Structure: Cash + stock + earnouts possible
- Timeline: Takes 3-6 months to negotiate

---

### CONDITIONAL VEHICLES FOR GROWTH STAGE

#### Strategic Corporate VC
**Fit: ★★★☆☆ (Limited, if strategic)**

**Relevance:**
- Sometimes strategic investor leads later rounds
- Only if strategic partnership value high
- Usually supplemental to VC rounds

---

### AVOID AT GROWTH STAGE

| Vehicle | Why Not |
|---|---|
| SAFEs | Way too late for SAFEs |
| Convertible Notes | Institutional capital expected |
| Angel Investment | Need institutional scale |
| Bootstrap | Cannot compete without capital |
| Grants | Below-scale funding |
| Revenue-Based | Usually too small at this stage |

## STAGE-SPECIFIC TIPS

### Pre-Seed Tips
- Focus on product, not fundraising
- Minimal capital needed ($25k-$100k realistic)
- Bootstrap as long as possible
- Build relationships with angels
- Get ready for seed round (18 months of capital)

### Seed Tips
- Multiple small investors (SAFEs) preferred
- Model Series A assumptions now
- Know your target Series A valuation
- Keep cap table manageable (5-10 investors)
- Build traction relentlessly

### Series A Tips
- Clean cap table (resolve SAFEs before closing)
- Prepare comprehensive data room
- Know your story and metrics cold
- Expect 12-16 week fundraising cycle
- Budget $35k-$80k for legal/admin costs

### Series B Tips
- Expect larger rounds and longer diligence
- Multiple lead investors common
- Governance becomes complex
- Plan for board management
- Consider venture debt to extend runway

### Growth Stage Tips
- Acquisitions becoming realistic exit
- Multiple rounds (C, D, E) possible
- Consider path to profitability
- Plan for eventual exit (M&A or IPO)
- IPO possible but expensive/complex
