## 1. DILUTION RISK MATRIX

### What is Dilution Risk?
The probability of significant ownership loss and the unpredictability of that loss.

| **Vehicle** | **Risk Level** | **Best Case** | **Typical Case** | **Worst Case** | **Mitigation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrap** | **None** | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | N/A (no investor) |
| **Revenue-Based** | **None** | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | Agreed upfront |
| **Venture Debt** | **None** | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | Secured debt |
| **Grants** | **None** | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | 0% dilution | Non-dilutive |
| **SAFEs** | **Low-Moderate** | 3% SAFE | 8-12% SAFE | 20-25% SAFE | Set valuation cap |
| **Convertible Notes** | **Low-Moderate** | 3% conv | 8-12% conv | 20-25% conv | Negotiate cap |
| **Angel Investment** | **Moderate** | 5% | 15-20% | 30-40% | Clear negotiation |
| **Equity Crowdfunding** | **Moderate-High** | 8% | 15-20% | 30%+ | Control round size |
| **Priced Equity** | **High** | 12% | 20-30% | 40-50% | Negotiate valuation |
| **Strategic VC** | **High** | 10% | 20-30% | 40%+ | Clear exit terms |

### Dilution Risk Deep Dive

**Multiple SAFE Scenario (High Risk):**
```
Round Timeline:
- Month 1: Angel SAFE #1 - $100k, $3M cap → 3.3% dilution
- Month 3: Angel SAFE #2 - $150k, $3M cap → 5% dilution
- Month 6: Institutional SAFE - $500k, $5M cap → 10% dilution
- Month 12: Series A at $10M valuation

Total SAFE Dilution: 18%+ BEFORE Series A
Series A Dilution: 15-20%
Post-Series A Founder Ownership: 45-50% (from 100%)

Risk: Cascading dilution erodes founder stake faster than expected
```

**Multiple Priced Equity Scenario (Very High Risk):**
```
Round Timeline:
- Year 1, Seed Round: $500k at $2M → 20% dilution
- Year 2, Series A: $3M at $8M → 25% dilution
- Year 3, Series B: $10M at $40M → 20% dilution

Cumulative Dilution: 20% → 15% → 12% ownership remaining
Founder Stake: 100% → 80% → 60% → 48%

Plus Option Pool (10-15%): Final ownership ~35-40%

Risk: Compounding dilution in each round
```

### Dilution Mitigation Strategies

**For SAFEs and Convertible Notes:**
- [ ] Negotiate valuation cap (lower = better protection)
- [ ] Track aggregate SAFE caps + planned Series A
- [ ] Model dilution in conservative Series A scenario
- [ ] Limit SAFE rounds to 2-3 maximum
- [ ] Plan option pool BEFORE first financing

**For Angel Investment:**
- [ ] Negotiate clear percentage upfront
- [ ] Ensure investor doesn't have MFN clause
- [ ] Set board seat limits (max 1-2 angels on board)
- [ ] Plan for pro-rata in Series A (control dilution)

**For Priced Equity:**
- [ ] Negotiate valuation carefully (biggest impact)
- [ ] Ensure option pool reserved from pre-money (not post)
- [ ] Control round size (smaller = less dilution)
- [ ] Negotiate anti-dilution rights (weighted average preferred)
- [ ] Plan for multiple rounds in advance

## 2. CONTROL RISK MATRIX

### What is Control Risk?
Loss of decision-making authority, governance rights, or strategic direction.

| **Vehicle** | **Risk Level** | **Pre-Convert** | **Post-Convert** | **Board Impact** | **Mitigation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrap** | **None** | 100% control | 100% control | 100% control | N/A |
| **Revenue-Based** | **None** | 100% | 100% | 100% | Metrics monitoring |
| **Venture Debt** | **Low** | 100% | 100% | 100% | Financial covenants |
| **Grants** | **Low** | 100% | 100% | Reporting only | Use requirements |
| **SAFEs** | **Low** | 100% | Diluted | No seat | Manage conversion |
| **Convertible Notes** | **Low** | 100% | Diluted | No seat | Plan for Series A |
| **Angel Investment** | **Moderate** | 100% | Diluted | Often 1 seat | Negotiate governance |
| **Equity Crowdfunding** | **High** | 100% | Diluted | No seat | Crowd voting |
| **Priced Equity** | **High** | 100% | Diluted | 1-2 seats likely | Board composition |
| **Strategic VC** | **Very High** | 100% | Diluted | 1+ seat | Strategic control |

### Control Risk Deep Dive

**Series A Board Composition (Common):**
```
Pre-Series A:
- Founder CEO: 1 seat
- Co-Founder: 1 seat (or founder owns both)
- Total: 2 seats (founder controlled)

Post-Series A:
- Founder CEO: 1 seat
- Series A Lead: 1 seat
- Series A Investor #2: Optional (if large round)
- Mutual board observer or rotating seat
- Total: 3-4 seats

Founder Control Change:
- Before: 2/2 control (100%)
- After: 1/3 or 1/4 (33% or 25% control)
- Veto rights: Only on major decisions (dilution, M&A)
- Day-to-day: Still controlled by founder

Risk: Loss of unilateral decision authority, must negotiate major decisions
```

**Strategic VC Control (Highest Risk):**
```
Typical Strategic VC Terms:
- Board seat: Strategic VC investor
- Information rights: Full access
- Tag-along rights: Can join acquisitions
- Co-sale rights: Can block outside partnerships
- Strategic veto: Approval on certain decisions (licensing, partnerships)

Founder Control Impact:
- Cannot pivot without investor approval
- Cannot license technology without investor consent
- Cannot partner with competitors
- Cannot exit without investor agreement
- Risk: Investor controls strategic direction, not founder

Example: Pharma Company Invests in Biotech Startup
- Investor: Controls R&D direction, testing protocol
- Founder: Must align with investor's therapeutic focus
- Partnership Terms: Any partnership needs investor approval
- Risk: Company becomes asset of larger pharma company
```

### Control Risk Mitigation

**For Angel Investment:**
- [ ] Negotiate board observer rights only (not seat)
- [ ] Set observer approval threshold (50%+ ownership for seat)
- [ ] Limit veto rights to specific decisions
- [ ] Require supermajority for major decisions (2/3 founders)

**For Priced Equity:**
- [ ] Negotiate for founder CEO seat + 1 other board seat
- [ ] Ensure founder veto on dilution (equity issuance)
- [ ] Define major decisions requiring investor vote
- [ ] Set board size limit (3-5 seats)
- [ ] Require supermajority for M&A/major pivots

**For Strategic VC:**
- [ ] Negotiate co-sale rights (not control) only
- [ ] Exclude operational decisions from veto
- [ ] Set explicit carve-outs (product direction, hiring)
- [ ] Plan exit date or founder buyback option
- [ ] Ensure founder remains CEO (not replaced)

## 3. FINANCIAL RISK MATRIX

### What is Financial Risk?
Personal financial exposure, repayment obligations, and personal guarantees.

| **Vehicle** | **Risk Level** | **Personal Liability** | **Repayment Terms** | **Personal Guarantee** | **Mitigation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrap** | **Moderate** | Very high | N/A | N/A | Use business cash |
| **Revenue-Based** | **Moderate-High** | Moderate | % of revenue | Rarely | Revenue tracking |
| **Venture Debt** | **High** | High | Monthly payments | Sometimes | Lender negotiations |
| **Grants** | **Low** | None | Non-repayable | None | Use requirements |
| **SAFEs** | **Low** | None | Converts to equity | None | Conversion terms |
| **Convertible Notes** | **Low** | None | Converts to equity | None | Conversion terms |
| **Angel Investment** | **Low** | None | Converts to equity | None | Conversion terms |
| **Equity Crowdfunding** | **Low** | None | Converts to equity | None | Crowdfunding terms |
| **Priced Equity** | **Low** | None | None (equity) | None | Dividend preference |
| **Strategic VC** | **Moderate** | Moderate | Often involved | Sometimes | Strategic alignment |

### Financial Risk Deep Dive

**Bootstrap Risk Scenario:**
```
Founder Personal Financial Exposure:
- Initial capital: $50k personal savings
- Year 1: Burn $20k/month = $240k needed
- Runway: Only 2.5 months with personal money
- Option 1: Add more personal capital ($200k from mortgage/credit)
- Option 2: Pause growth, slow-scale business

Personal Liability Issues:
- Using personal credit cards for business
- Personal bank loans secured against home
- Personal guarantee on business credit line
- Risk: If business fails, personal bankruptcy

Mitigation:
- Separate business bank account (only)
- No personal credit cards
- Only invest what you can afford to lose
- Seek outside capital before personal depletion
```

**Venture Debt Risk Scenario:**
```
Typical Venture Debt Terms:
- Loan Amount: $500k
- Term: 3 years
- Monthly Payment: ~$16k
- Personal Guarantee: Possibly required

Financial Risk Calculation:
- Monthly Runway Without Debt: $80k
- With Debt Obligation: $80k + $16k = $96k/month needed
- Increase in Monthly Burn: 20% higher
- Hit Date for Additional Capital: 3-4 months earlier

If No Revenue Growth:
- Company runs out of capital 3-4 months earlier
- Must raise Series A with less time
- Or default on venture debt (personal guarantee triggered)
- Risk: Personal liability for debt default

Mitigation:
- Only take venture debt if confident in revenue growth
- Model debt repayment + operational burn together
- Negotiate personal guarantee removal if possible
- Ensure Series A timeline allows repayment window
```

**Revenue-Based Financing Risk Scenario:**
```
Typical Revenue-Based Terms:
- Capital: $500k
- Repayment: 6% of monthly revenue until $750k returned
- No personal guarantee
- Caps: Total repayment capped at $750k (50% return)

Financial Stress Points:
- If monthly revenue: $50k → monthly payment ~$3k (6%)
- If monthly revenue: $100k → monthly payment ~$6k (6%)
- If monthly revenue: $200k → monthly payment ~$12k (6%)

Cash Flow Impact:
- 6% perpetual royalty feels small
- But in downturns, cash flow still required
- If revenue drops 50%, payment drops but still required
- Risk: Unpredictable cash obligation vs predictable debt

Mitigation:
- Only take if confident in revenue growth
- Understand cap (e.g., $750k vs $1M return)
- Model revenue scenarios and payment amounts
- Ensure positive unit economics before taking
```

## 4. COMPLEXITY RISK MATRIX

### What is Complexity Risk?
Administrative burden, legal/accounting complexity, and ongoing management overhead.

| **Vehicle** | **Risk Level** | **Legal Complexity** | **Admin Burden** | **Cap Table Impact** | **Ongoing Mgmt** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrap** | **Low** | Minimal | Low | None | Minimal |
| **Revenue-Based** | **Moderate** | Moderate | Moderate | None | Moderate |
| **Venture Debt** | **Moderate** | Moderate-High | High | None | Moderate |
| **Grants** | **High** | High | Very High | None | Very High |
| **SAFEs** | **Low** | Low | Low | Moderate | Low |
| **Convertible Notes** | **Low-Moderate** | Moderate | Low | Moderate | Low |
| **Angel Investment** | **Moderate** | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate |
| **Equity Crowdfunding** | **High** | High | Very High | Very High | High |
| **Priced Equity** | **Very High** | Very High | Very High | Very High | High |
| **Strategic VC** | **Very High** | Very High | Very High | Very High | Very High |

### Complexity Risk Deep Dive

**Grant Administration (High Complexity):**
```
SBIR Grant Complexity:
- Phase 1: $150k, 6 months
- Deliverables: Working prototype, business plan
- Reporting: Monthly progress reports, quarterly financials
- Compliance: SBIR compliance, foreign ownership restrictions
- Audit: Annual audit requirement, SBIR-specific format

Time Cost:
- Initial application: 40-80 hours
- Monthly reporting: 10-20 hours
- Quarterly compliance: 10-15 hours
- Annual audit support: 30-50 hours
- Total: 150-200 hours/year = 4-5 weeks of full-time work

Founder Burden:
- Must track funding separately from other capital
- Restricted use (R&D only, not operations)
- Foreign ownership limits (US companies only)
- Employee restrictions (must employ US citizens)
- Reporting templates required by government

Risk: Administrative burden offsets capital benefit
```

**Cap Table Fragmentation (Priced Equity Complexity):**
```
Post-Series A Complex Cap Table:
- Founder: 40% common
- Co-Founder: 15% common
- Advisors: 2% common (vested over 4 years)
- Employees: Option pool 10% (vesting over 4 years)
- Series A Lead: 20% Series A Preferred
- Series A Investor #2: 10% Series A Preferred
- Earlier SAFEs: 3% converted to Series A Preferred

Complications:
- Different share classes (common, preferred)
- Different vesting schedules (advisor, employee)
- MFN clauses triggering from earlier rounds
- Board composition (3-5 seats, 1 rotating)
- Liquidation preferences (1x non-participating for Series A)
- Anti-dilution rights (weighted average)
- Pro-rata rights for Series A investors
- Option pool tracking and vesting cliff

Management Burden:
- Monthly cap table updates
- Quarterly board meeting prep
- Annual equity audits
- Option grant approvals and paperwork
- Equity financing negotiations (Series B, C, etc.)

Risk: Cap table becomes increasingly complex, hard to manage
```

**Equity Crowdfunding Complexity (Very High):**
```
Equity Crowdfunding Platform (e.g., Reg A+):
- Many small shareholders: 500-5,000+ investors
- Regulatory reporting: SEC reporting requirements
- Annual reporting: Must file annual report
- Material events: Must disclose within specific timeframe
- Investor communications: Handle thousands of investors
- Share transfers: Must manage secondary market
- Dividend policy: If paying dividends, distribute to all

Founder Burden:
- Cannot go dark (must report annually to SEC)
- Investor relations: Handle dozens of investor inquiries
- Annual updates: Prepare audited financials for SEC
- Investor reporting portal: Manage online investor relations
- Liquidation events: Must disclose and manage exit

Risk: Ongoing reporting burden larger than private equity rounds
```

### Complexity Risk Mitigation

**For Grants:**
- [ ] Hire grant administrator or finance person ($30-60k/year)
- [ ] Use accounting software with grant tracking
- [ ] Monthly budget tracking for compliance
- [ ] Plan for audit costs ($5-15k/year)
- [ ] Only pursue grants worth > $250k

**For Priced Equity:**
- [ ] Use cap table software (Carta, Pulley)
- [ ] Quarterly board meetings (calendar now)
- [ ] Hire experienced CFO before Series A
- [ ] Document option pool and vesting upfront
- [ ] Update cap table monthly (not after-the-fact)

**For Equity Crowdfunding:**
- [ ] Use crowdfunding platform with investor portal
- [ ] Plan for annual audited financials
- [ ] Hire investor relations specialist
- [ ] Quarterly investor updates (standardized)
- [ ] Only pursue if open-ended capital strategy

## 5. TIME COMMITMENT RISK MATRIX

### What is Time Commitment Risk?
Founder time spent on financing vs. building product/company.

| **Vehicle** | **Initial Setup** | **Ongoing Management** | **Fundraising Rounds** | **Investor Relations** | **Total Annual** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrap** | 5-10 hrs | 5-10 hrs/month | None | None | 60-130 hrs/year |
| **Revenue-Based** | 20-30 hrs | 10 hrs/month | One-time | 5 hrs/month | 85-110 hrs/year |
| **Venture Debt** | 40-60 hrs | 10-15 hrs/month | One-time | 5 hrs/month | 115-150 hrs/year |
| **Grants** | 80-120 hrs | 30-40 hrs/month | Per grant | 10 hrs/month | 380-520 hrs/year |
| **SAFEs** | 10-20 hrs | 5 hrs/month | Per investor | 5 hrs/month | 70-110 hrs/year |
| **Convertible Notes** | 20-40 hrs | 10 hrs/month | Per investor | 5 hrs/month | 95-130 hrs/year |
| **Angel Investment** | 30-50 hrs | 15-20 hrs/month | Per investor | 10 hrs/month | 165-240 hrs/year |
| **Equity Crowdfunding** | 60-100 hrs | 40-50 hrs/month | One-time | 20 hrs/month | 540-700 hrs/year |
| **Priced Equity** | 100-150 hrs | 30-40 hrs/month | Per round | 20 hrs/month | 460-620 hrs/year |
| **Strategic VC** | 100-150 hrs | 40-60 hrs/month | Per round | 30 hrs/month | 640-900 hrs/year |

### Time Commitment Deep Dive

**Grant Administration (Highest Time):**
```
Year 1 SBIR Grant Timeline:
- Month 1: Complete Phase 1 application (80 hours)
- Months 2-3: Waiting period
- Months 4-6: Phase 1 execution (20 hrs/month = 60 hours)
- Month 6: Phase 1 final report (30 hours)
- Month 7: Phase 2 application (60 hours)
- Months 8-12: Phase 2 execution (20 hrs/month = 100 hours)

Year 1 Total: 330 hours = 8+ weeks full-time

Ongoing (Annual):
- Monthly progress reports: 10 hours/month = 120 hours
- Compliance audits: 20 hours = 20 hours
- Financial reconciliation: 10 hours/month = 120 hours
- Total Annual: 260 hours = 6+ weeks full-time

Founder Reality:
- Cannot focus on product development during execution
- Must hire operations person or CFO (cost: $50-80k/year)
- Or accept reduced product innovation during grant period
- Risk: Grant funding comes with time cost == product slowdown
```

**Strategic VC Partnership (High Time):**
```
Typical Strategic VC Involvement:
- Monthly board meeting: 4 hours/month
- Quarterly strategy sessions: 8 hours/quarter = 2 hours/month
- Ongoing advisor communication: 10 hours/month
- Partnership/licensing discussions: 5 hours/month
- Board prep (materials, financials): 10 hours/month
- Investor relations (updates, metrics): 5 hours/month

Monthly Total: 36 hours = ~1 week/month
Annual Total: 432 hours = 10+ weeks/year

Plus:
- Quarterly earnings prep: 20 hours/quarter = 80 hours
- Annual strategic planning: 40 hours
- Annual investor meetings: 20 hours
- Ad-hoc partnership negotiations: 30-50 hours

Total Realistic Annual: 600-750 hours = 15+ weeks/year

Founder Reality:
- Founder spends 15+ weeks/year on investor relations
- That's 30% of founder time on investor management
- Only 70% left for product, team, strategy
- Risk: Strategic priorities driven by investor, not founder
```

## RISK MITIGATION STRATEGIES BY FOUNDER PROFILE

### Conservative Founder (Risk Averse)

**Priority:** Minimize risk, maintain control, avoid personal liability

**Vehicle Selection:**
1. Bootstrap (best) - No external risk
2. Grants (conditional) - No dilution, but high complexity
3. Revenue-Based (if revenue exists)
4. SAFEs (if must fundraise) - Simple, low dilution
5. Avoid: Priced Equity, Strategic VC, Venture Debt

**Risk Mitigation Checklist:**
- [ ] Only raise capital you need (not maximum possible)
- [ ] Negotiate SAFEs with high caps, low discounts
- [ ] No personal guarantees on debt
- [ ] Keep board small (3 seats max, founder controls 2)
- [ ] No strategic veto rights to investors
- [ ] Plan Series A before taking multiple SAFEs
- [ ] Model all dilution scenarios in advance

---

### Growth Founder (Balanced Risk)

**Priority:** Optimize growth, accept some risk, maintain flexibility

**Vehicle Selection:**
1. Angel Investment (with good terms)
2. SAFEs or Convertible Notes (for bridging)
3. Priced Equity (once product-market fit)
4. Strategic VC (if aligned)
5. Avoid: Equity Crowdfunding (too complex)

**Risk Mitigation Checklist:**
- [ ] Negotiate clearly defined terms upfront
- [ ] Ensure angel investors fit company culture
- [ ] Set board composition expectations early
- [ ] Plan for investor liquidity/exit requirements
- [ ] Model dilution for multiple rounds
- [ ] Maintain founder veto on major decisions
- [ ] Regular communication with investors (quarterly)

---

### Aggressive Founder (Growth at All Costs)

**Priority:** Maximize capital, accept high risk, optimize for growth

**Vehicle Selection:**
1. Priced Equity (fastest capital)
2. Strategic VC (capital + resources)
3. Multiple funding sources (SAFE + Angel + Venture Debt)
4. Equity Crowdfunding (if fits strategy)
5. Venture Debt (for runway extension)

**Risk Mitigation Checklist:**
- [ ] Negotiate valuation carefully (biggest long-term impact)
- [ ] Ensure option pool sized appropriately (10-15%)
- [ ] Maintain founder CEO role (non-negotiable)
- [ ] Get investor commitment beyond capital (resources, network)
- [ ] Plan for Series B/C from day 1
- [ ] Keep strategic control on product direction
- [ ] Model exit scenarios from first investor
