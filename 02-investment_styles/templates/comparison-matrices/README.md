## KEY DECISION FRAMEWORKS

### For Founders: "What vehicle should I raise?"

**Step 1: Know Your Stage**
- Pre-Seed (0-3 months)? → Bootstrap, Grants, Pre-seed SAFEs
- Seed (3-12 months)? → SAFEs, Convertible Notes, Angels
- Series A (12-24 months)? → Priced Equity, Strategic VC
- Series B+ (24+ months)? → Priced Equity, Venture Debt

**Step 2: Identify Your Constraints**
- How much capital do you need? ($25k, $500k, $5M?)
- How quickly do you need it?
- Can you afford dilution? (what % is acceptable?)
- Do you want/need investor governance?
- How much complexity can you handle?

**Step 3: Compare Top 2-3 Vehicles**
Use INVESTMENT_VEHICLE_COMPARISON.md to compare:
- Dilution impact (how much you give up)
- Control implications (governance)
- Timeline to close (speed)
- Founder-friendliness (overall rating)

**Step 4: Assess Founder Risks**
Use FOUNDER_RISK_MATRIX.md for your top choices:
- Dilution risk (worst case scenario)
- Control risk (governance implications)
- Financial risk (repayment obligations)
- Complexity risk (admin burden)

**Step 5: Make Decision**
Choose the vehicle that:
- Fits your stage (from STAGE_APPROPRIATE_VEHICLES.md)
- Meets your capital needs
- Minimizes your key risks
- Can be closed in your timeline

---

### For Investors: "What investment should I make?"

**Step 1: Assess Company Stage**
Use STAGE_APPROPRIATE_VEHICLES.md:
- Is the vehicle appropriate for the stage?
- Is the company ready for this instrument?
- Are expectations aligned?

**Step 2: Review Investor Protections**
Use INVESTMENT_VEHICLE_COMPARISON.md:
- Investor protection level
- Information rights typical for vehicle
- Exit timeline clarity

**Step 3: Analyze Investor Risks**
Use INVESTOR_RISK_MATRIX.md:
- Return uncertainty (how unpredictable?)
- Liquidity risk (when can you exit?)
- Protection level (what rights do you get?)
- Information rights (what access do you have?)
- Exit timeline (how long will capital be tied up?)

**Step 4: Check Your Investor Profile**
From INVESTOR_RISK_MATRIX.md:
- Are you conservative (want downside protection)?
- Are you growth-focused (want upside + governance)?
- Are you portfolio-investor (want efficiency)?
- Does the vehicle fit your profile?

**Step 5: Make Decision**
Choose the investment that:
- Provides appropriate risk/return profile
- Fits your investor profile
- Has clear exit path
- Provides necessary protections/information rights

## COMMON COMPARISON SCENARIOS

### "SAFE vs. Convertible Note"

| Aspect | SAFE | Convertible Note |
|--------|------|---|
| **Speed** | Faster (3-7 days) | Slower (1-2 weeks) |
| **Complexity** | Simpler (2-3 pages) | More complex (10-15 pages) |
| **Cost** | Lower legal fees ($2-5k) | Higher legal fees ($5-10k) |
| **Maturity Protection** | None | Yes (maturity date) |
| **Interest** | None | Yes (6-8% typical) |
| **Debt vs. Equity** | Equity instrument | Debt instrument |
| **Best For** | Most seed rounds now | Investor wants maturity |
| **Founder Perspective** | 9/10 | 8/10 |
| **Investor Perspective** | 7/10 | 8/10 |

**Decision Rule:**
- **Use SAFE** if: Quick close, standard terms, multiple investors
- **Use Convertible Note** if: Investor wants maturity protection, wants interest income

---

### "Priced Equity vs. SAFE"

| Aspect | Priced Equity | SAFE |
|--------|---|---|
| **Valuation** | Explicit | Implicit (set at Series A) |
| **Ownership** | Determined now | Determined at conversion |
| **Governance** | Board seat, voting | None (until conversion) |
| **Complexity** | Very high (50+ pages) | Very low (2-3 pages) |
| **Timeline** | Long (4-8 weeks) | Quick (3-7 days) |
| **When to Use** | Product-market fit, ready for governance | Very early stage, high uncertainty |
| **Founder Perspective** | 5/10 | 9/10 |
| **Investor Perspective** | 10/10 | 7/10 |

**Decision Rule:**
- **Use SAFE** if: Pre-seed/seed stage, moving fast, uncertain timeline
- **Use Priced Equity** if: Product-market fit, multiple investors, ready for governance

---

### "Venture Debt vs. Equity Raise"

| Aspect | Venture Debt | Equity |
|--------|---|---|
| **Dilution** | None (0%) | Yes (10-20% typical) |
| **Returns** | Fixed (10-15% annual) | Unlimited upside |
| **Governance** | Covenants only | Board seat typical |
| **Timeline to Capital** | 2-3 weeks | 4-8 weeks (Series A) |
| **Repayment** | Required (monthly) | Never (unless exit fails) |
| **When to Use** | Extend runway, near-profitable | Need growth capital |
| **Risk (Founder)** | High (repayment obligation) | High (control loss) |
| **Risk (Investor)** | Low (senior position) | Moderate-High (equity) |

**Decision Rule:**
- **Use Venture Debt** if: Want to minimize dilution, have revenue, need runway extension
- **Use Equity** if: Need large capital, want investor resources/governance

## TERMINOLOGY

**Dilution:** Reduction in founder ownership percentage due to new capital investment.

**Valuation Cap:** Maximum valuation used for converting SAFEs/notes to equity (limits investor dilution in successful scenarios).

**Discount Rate:** Percentage off the Series A price (rewards early investors with cheaper entry).

**Board Seat:** Right to participate in company board decisions (board members typically vote on major decisions).

**Liquidation Preference:** What investors get paid first in a sale/acquisition/liquidation.

**Governance Rights:** Right to vote on major company decisions.

**Information Rights:** Right to receive financial statements and company updates.

**Pro-Rata Rights:** Right to maintain ownership percentage in future rounds.

**Control:** Decision-making authority (founders want to keep; investors want to gain).

**MFN (Most Favored Nation):** Right to get same terms as any better-term investor.

**Anti-Dilution:** Protection against founder ownership being reduced in future down-rounds.

**Cash Flow:** Monthly revenue minus monthly expenses.

**Runway:** Months of operating budget company can support with current cash.

**Burn Rate:** Monthly rate at which company spends cash.

**Unit Economics:** Revenue per customer minus cost to acquire/serve that customer.

**Exit:** Company sale, acquisition, or IPO (how investors get returns).
