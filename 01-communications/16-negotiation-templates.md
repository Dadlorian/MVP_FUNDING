# Negotiation Communication Templates

## Overview

This guide provides 30+ tactical negotiation templates across multiple categories: term sheet negotiations, salary negotiations, vendor negotiations, partnership terms, licensing agreements, counter-offers, compromise proposals, and deal closing communications. Each template includes tactical guidance for maximizing negotiation outcomes.

---

## Tactical Principles for All Negotiations

### Before Sending:
1. **Anchor High/Low**: Set initial terms favorable to your position
2. **Document Everything**: Keep all communications in writing
3. **Build Options**: Present multiple pathways to agreement
4. **Know Your BATNA**: Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement
5. **Create Urgency**: Natural deadlines increase negotiation momentum

### During Negotiation:
1. **Silence is Negotiation**: Don't fill pauses - let them respond
2. **Separate People from Problems**: Focus on issues, not personalities
3. **Explain Rationale**: "Why" matters more than "what"
4. **Validate Concerns**: Acknowledge their position before countering
5. **Track Movement**: Document all concessions offered and received

### After Reaching Agreement:
1. **Confirm in Writing**: Recap all terms immediately
2. **Build Goodwill**: Reference collaboration positively
3. **Plan Implementation**: Set clear next steps
4. **Preserve Relationship**: Leave door open for future deals

### Template 1.2: Counter to Investor's Term Sheet (Rejecting Unfavorable Terms)

**Tactical Guidance:**
- Never reject all terms - show willingness to negotiate
- Prioritize which terms matter most to you
- Provide economic rationale for your positions
- Offer alternative structures that achieve investor's goals

---

**Subject:** RE: Term Sheet - Proposed Modifications [Company Name]

Dear [Investor Name],

Thank you for your term sheet proposal. We're excited about the investment potential and have reviewed the terms carefully with our board and counsel.

We accept the majority of your proposed terms and appreciate the reasonable valuation. However, we've identified several areas where we'd like to propose modifications that we believe better reflect market standards and align our incentives long-term:

**Proposed Modifications:**

1. **Liquidation Preference:** We propose 1x non-participating preferred (vs. your 2x non-participating). This aligns with Series A market standards for our company stage and allows founder liquidity in reasonable outcomes.

   *Rationale:* Multiple-preference terms typically emerge in later-stage financings or distressed situations. Our comparable transactions show 1x is market.

2. **Board Composition:** We propose a 5-person board: 2 founder seats, 2 investor seats, 1 independent director selected jointly. Your proposal of 3 investor seats would give investors control outside of preferred stock rights.

   *Rationale:* This structure maintains founder operational authority while giving you meaningful governance participation.

3. **Protective Provisions:** We can agree to investor consent for major decisions (hiring C-suite, material acquisitions, debt >$[X]), but propose removing:
   - Annual budget variance >15% (too restrictive operationally)
   - Related-party transactions >$[X] (internal flexibility needed)

4. **Anti-Dilution:** We can agree to weighted-average anti-dilution rather than full ratchet, with your provision only applying to below-market rounds. This balances protection with incentivizing fair pricing in future rounds.

These modifications maintain investor downside protection while allowing us operational flexibility. I've attached a redline with proposed language and am available to discuss these points this week.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

### Template 1.4: Securing Better Anti-Dilution Terms

**Tactical Guidance:**
- Frame anti-dilution as "protecting both parties"
- Explain how weighted-average protects founders and investors
- Use data on failure rates to justify limits
- Offer restrictions on triggering terms

---

**Subject:** Proposed Anti-Dilution Framework [Company Name]

Dear [Investor Name],

As we finalize our term sheet, I'd like to address anti-dilution protection with a proposal that protects your downside while maintaining our ability to raise capital efficiently in future rounds.

**Our Proposal: Weighted-Average with Reasonable Thresholds**

We propose weighted-average anti-dilution (not full ratchet) with these qualifications:

1. **Does NOT trigger for:**
   - Financing rounds at or above the greater of: (a) previous valuation, or (b) fair market value
   - Stock splits, dividends, recapitalizations
   - Equity awards under option plans

2. **DOES trigger for:**
   - Financing rounds below [X]% of previous round valuation
   - Only applies if round is $[X]+ in size

3. **Weighted-Average Calculation:**
   - Uses standard formula: (Old valuation × Old shares + New investment) / (Old valuation + New price × New shares)
   - More precise than full ratchet, rewards pricing discipline

**Why This Matters:**

- **For you:** Protects against down-round from ~30% dilution (if we were forced to raise at 50% of current valuation)
- **For us:** Doesn't penalize us for market conditions outside our control while remaining incentive-aligned on future valuations
- **For the company:** Allows us to raise capital in future rounds without artificial dilution triggering

This is market standard for Series A and aligns with our comparable financings. I'm happy to discuss the specific percentage thresholds if you'd like different protection levels.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

### Template 1.5: Resolving Material Adverse Change (MAC) Clause Disagreement

**Tactical Guidance:**
- Define MAC narrowly to protect both parties
- Use carve-outs for industry-wide events
- Create process for good-faith dispute resolution
- Demonstrate you've thought about edge cases

---

**Subject:** Proposed MAC Clause Language [Company Name]

Dear [Investor Name],

As we near closing, I'd like to address the Material Adverse Change (MAC) clause language. I've reviewed your proposal and would like to suggest modifications that provide appropriate protections for both parties:

**Your Proposed Language Issues:**

Your proposal includes "material adverse effect on business, financial condition, or results of operations" without sufficient definition. This creates ambiguity and could lead to disputes post-closing. We recommend:

**Proposed MAC Definition:**

"Material Adverse Effect" means any event, condition, or circumstance that, individually or in the aggregate with other events, has a material adverse effect on the business, assets, or financial condition of the Company, **except for:**

- **Industry-wide events:** Changes in general economic conditions, market conditions, or regulatory environment affecting the industry generally
- **Foreseeable events:** Items disclosed in due diligence or included in projections
- **Company control:** Actions taken with investor consent or required by law
- **Competitive actions:** Actions by competitors or market participants

**Materiality Thresholds:**

We define "material" as:
- Revenue impact of >$[X] annual or >$[X] quarterly impact, OR
- Affects >20% of core business operations for >90 days, OR
- Loss of customer representing >10% of revenue (unless replacement found within 60 days)

**Dispute Resolution:**

If either party claims MAC, we follow this process:
1. Claiming party provides written notice within 10 days with specific facts
2. Other party may provide written response within 10 days
3. Senior executives meet to discuss resolution within 5 days
4. If unresolved, neutral industry expert makes binding determination

This approach protects you from catastrophic unexpected events while giving us certainty that routine business volatility doesn't trigger disputes.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

**Tactical Guidance:**
- Front-load cash, back-load equity (equity feels less concrete)
- Explain equity value conservatively with cliff language
- Create incentive through grant size based on performance
- Build in vesting milestones

---

**Offer Details:**

- **Annual Bonus:** Up to [X]% based on goals: customer acquisition, technical milestones, and team growth
- **Equity Grant:** [X] options ([X]% fully diluted post-raise)

**Equity Details - Important to Understand:**

I want to be transparent about equity since it represents significant long-term value:

- **Vesting Schedule:** 4-year vest with 1-year cliff (standard market terms)
  - You earn 25% after 1 year of employment
  - Remaining 75% vest monthly over next 3 years
  - If you leave before year 1, you forfeit all equity

- **Strike Price:** $[X]/share (fair market value today)

- **Current Equity Context:**
  - Company last valued at $[X]M in Series A (6 months ago)
  - At that valuation, your grant represents approximately $[X] in equity value
  - Conservative projections suggest 3-5x growth over next 4 years

- **Equity Upside Scenarios:**
  - Conservative: Exit at $[X]M, your equity worth ~$[X]
  - Base Case: Exit at $[X]M, your equity worth ~$[X]
  - Optimistic: Exit at $[X]M, your equity worth ~$[X]

**Benefits & Perks:**
- Comprehensive health insurance (medical, dental, vision)

**Start Date:** [Date] or mutually agreed upon

**Next Steps:**

**Tactical Guidance:**
- Frame flexibility as productivity/retention benefit

---

**Operational Commitments:**
- Present for [X]% of team meetings (particularly all-hands, planning sessions)

- In-office for [X-specific events] (launches, offsites, important client meetings)

**Why This Works:**

- **For company:** You get committed presence for important moments while maintaining team collaboration
- **For me:** Reduces commute time, enables better focus on deep work

### Template 3.3: Handling Unexpected Price Increases from Vendor

**Tactical Guidance:**
- Don't accept immediately - request justification
- Use increase as leverage to revisit entire contract
- Show willingness to explore alternatives
- Offer creative solutions (usage reduction, longer term)

---

**Subject:** RE: Price Increase Notification - Let's Discuss [Vendor Name]

Dear [Vendor Name],

I received your notification of a [X]% price increase effective [date], raising our monthly rate from $[X] to $[X]. I'd like to understand the rationale before accepting this change.

**Contextual Questions:**

1. **Market Context:** What's driving the increase? (Broader market, inflation, new features?)
2. **Our Account:** Is this across all customers or based on our usage/account expansion?
3. **Alternatives:** What's happening with competitor pricing? (I've seen some move down to capture market share)

**Our Concerns:**

- This increase of $[X]/year (on top of existing spend) significantly impacts our margins
- If I'm understanding correctly, we're 2+ years into the relationship at the original rate
- Market rates for comparable services haven't increased proportionally

**My Proposal:**

Rather than accept the full increase, let's use this as an opportunity to revisit the entire relationship:

**Option 1: Negotiated Increase**
- Accept [X]% increase (vs. [X]%) to acknowledge rising costs
- Agree to 2-year term at that rate with no further increases
- Both parties commit to the relationship

**Option 2: Usage Optimization**
- We identify areas to reduce our usage by [X]%
- This reduces our base cost, offsetting increases
- You maintain customer with confidence in churn reduction

**Option 3: Service Consolidation**
- Evaluate whether [module X] is still needed, potentially removing it
- Reduces our usage to $[X]/month
- Simplifies both our setup and your management

**Option 4: Transition Timeline**
- If we can't agree on pricing, we'd need [X] months to evaluate and migrate to an alternative

### Quick Reference: When to Use Each Template Set

**Term Sheet Negotiations (Sections 1.1-1.5)**
- Use when fundraising and exchanging ownership/governance terms
- Key focus: Anchor, document everything, propose flexible mechanisms
- Success metric: Signed agreement that reflects fair market terms

**Salary & Equity (Sections 2.1-2.6)**
- Use when hiring, negotiating compensation, or reviewing equity
- Key focus: Market data, transparency about equity value, creative structures
- Success metric: Signed offer that attracts talent and retains focus

**Vendor Negotiations (Sections 3.1-3.5)**
- Use when negotiating service provider rates, contracts, or exits
- Key focus: Market data, alternatives, creative cost structures
- Success metric: Better economics or improved terms without vendor relationship deterioration

**Partnership (Sections 4.1-4.5)**
- Use when forming strategic partnerships or managing underperformance
- Key focus: Mutual value, fair contribution, clear governance
- Success metric: Partnership that creates value exceeding what either party could alone

**Licensing (Sections 5.1-5.4)**
- Use when licensing or licensing out IP or technology
- Key focus: Defining scope clearly, incentive alignment, performance metrics
- Success metric: Revenue model that rewards commercialization efforts

**Counter-Offers (Sections 6.1-6.5)**
- Use when counterparty makes unreasonable offer or claims finality
- Key focus: Data-driven responses, understanding underlying needs, structured deadlock resolution
- Success metric: Movement toward acceptable terms through principled negotiation

**Compromise Proposals (Sections 7.1-7.4)**
- Use when stuck and need structured path forward
- Key focus: Package deals, creative solutions, contingent agreements
- Success metric: Agreement that feels fair to both parties

**Deal Closing (Sections 8.1-8.4)**
- Use when finalizing agreement and implementing
- Key focus: Clear confirmation, no last-minute surprises, smooth execution
- Success metric: Signed agreement that smoothly transitions to implementation

### Universal Negotiation Principles

1. **Lead with Data:** Use market information, comparable transactions, and objective metrics to justify positions
2. **Validate Before Countering:** Acknowledge the other party's position and concerns before proposing alternatives
3. **Create Options:** Rather than debating single position, propose multiple pathways to agreement
4. **Separate People from Problems:** Stay focused on solving the issue, not winning against them
5. **Document Everything:** Keep all major agreements in writing to prevent future disputes
6. **Know Your BATNA:** Understand your best alternative to a negotiated agreement and communicate it subtly
7. **Propose Process:** When stuck, propose a fair process (arbitration, split-the-difference, etc.) rather than continuing to debate positions
8. **Build Momentum:** Get early agreements on easy points to create forward momentum
9. **Trade Asymmetrically:** Give on what costs you little but they value highly; get concessions on what costs them little but you value highly
10. **Preserve Relationship:** Even tough negotiations should end with the other party willing to work with you again

### Tactical Toolkit

**When They Say "No":**
- Ask "What would it take to get to yes?" (reveals their real constraint)
- Propose alternative structures that achieve their goal differently
- Escalate to decision-maker if your contact can't authorize

**When You Say "No":**
- Explain the genuine constraint (budget, board approval, etc.)
- Propose what you CAN do instead
- Leave door open for future negotiation if circumstances change

**When Stuck:**
- Request pause to "gather more information" to reset
- Propose structured process (arbitration, split-the-difference, mediator)
- Make bold move showing willingness to walk if needed
- Identify what would move them (often different than stated position)

**When Negotiating Up:**
- Use data showing you're below market
- Propose interim increase with review date
- Tie to specific achievements if possible
- Reference external offers if applicable
