## PRESS RELEASE FORMAT GUIDE

### Key AP Style Guidelines:
- Use 12-point Times New Roman or similar serif font
- Single spacing with double spaces between paragraphs
- Line length: approximately 65-70 characters
- Place "###" or "-30-" at the end to indicate story conclusion
- Use standard "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE" or embargo dates
- Inverted pyramid structure: most important information first
- Active voice, strong verbs, concise language
- Full name on first mention, last name only thereafter
- Avoid superlatives; let facts speak for themselves

### Standard Boilerplate Components:
- Header with release date and availability
- Headline (preferably under 10 words)
- Subheading/summary line (optional)
- Location slug line
- Body paragraphs (5-10 paragraphs typically)
- About company boilerplate
- Contact information
- Social media handles (optional)

## PRESS RELEASE BEST PRACTICES

### Structure
1. **Dateline**: City, State – Date
2. **Headline**: Informative, active voice, under 10 words
3. **Opening paragraph**: Who, what, when, where, why, how
4. **Body**: Supporting details, quotes, context
5. **Boilerplate**: Company description
6. **Contact information**: Media, investor relations, social media

### Writing Guidelines
- Use active voice ("Company launches" not "Launch announced by")
- Include concrete facts and data
- Limit company jargon
- Quotes should sound natural
- Avoid marketing superlatives unless quoting
- Double-check all facts and figures
- Include specific dates and timeframes
- Provide context for industry outsiders

### Distribution Best Practices
- Include "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE" or embargo date
- Send at optimal times (Tuesday-Thursday, morning)
- Target relevant journalists and media outlets
- Follow up with key media
- Post on company website and social media
- Allow 30 minutes before public distribution
- Include all contact information
- Provide high-resolution images when appropriate

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Unsubstantiated claims or superlatives
- Unclear or convoluted sentences
- Too much internal company context
- Missing or incomplete contact information
- Factual errors or inconsistencies
- Exaggeration of product capabilities
- Focusing on company rather than customer benefit
- Excluding newsworthiness elements

   - Longer for complex announcements
