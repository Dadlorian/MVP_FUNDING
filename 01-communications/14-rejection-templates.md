# Rejection and Decline Templates

Professional templates for declining offers, partnerships, and opportunities while preserving relationships and leaving doors open for future collaboration.

---

## Declining Investment Offers

### Template 1: Polite Investment Decline (Early Stage)
Subject: Re: Investment Opportunity

Dear [Investor Name],

Thank you so much for considering an investment in [Company Name]. We are genuinely honored by your interest and confidence in our vision.

After careful consideration, we have decided to pursue funding that aligns more closely with our current growth stage and strategic direction. We believe this approach will serve both our company and our investors more effectively.

We would very much like to keep in touch as we develop. Your insights and perspective would be invaluable, and we may revisit this conversation at a more opportune time.

Thank you again for your time, diligence, and belief in what we're building.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

## Key Principles for Declining

1. **Be Prompt**: Respond to offers and requests within 5-7 business days to show respect for the person's time

2. **Be Clear**: Make your decision obvious immediately—don't create false hope or ambiguity

3. **Be Grateful**: Express genuine appreciation for the opportunity, effort, or thoughtfulness

4. **Be Specific**: Provide a real reason when possible, not vague platitudes

5. **Be Professional**: Maintain the tone and professionalism expected in business relationships

6. **Leave the Door Open**: Indicate whether and when you'd revisit the conversation (be honest about this)

7. **Offer Alternatives**: When appropriate, suggest an alternative solution or point them toward a better fit

8. **Personalize**: Avoid generic templates—add specific details about their proposal or offer

9. **Follow Through**: If you say you'll reconnect in six months, actually do it

10. **Don't Over-Explain**: One good reason is better than three weak reasons

## Customization Checklist

Before sending any decline:

- [ ] Have you responded within 5-7 business days?
- [ ] Is the recipient's name spelled correctly?
- [ ] Have you personalized the template with specific details?
- [ ] Have you been specific about the reason for decline?
- [ ] Have you expressed genuine appreciation?
- [ ] Have you been honest about whether/when you'd revisit?
- [ ] Have you offered an alternative when appropriate?
- [ ] Have you proofread for tone—is it warm, not cold?
- [ ] Would you be comfortable if this email were forwarded?
- [ ] Have you matched the formality level to the relationship?

## When Declining Requires a Call Instead

Consider a phone call or video meeting instead of email for:

- Long-term, significant relationships
- Complex explanations requiring back-and-forth
- Sensitive situations with emotional components
- When the ask is particularly substantial or personal
- When there's existing conflict or tension

Lead with: *"I wanted to give this the respect it deserves by talking through it directly. Do you have 15 minutes for a quick call?"*

---

## Following Up After a Decline

If you indicated you'd revisit a conversation, follow up proactively at the stated time:

> Hi [Name],
>
> I wanted to circle back on our conversation about [opportunity] from [timeframe]. As promised, I'm checking in to see if this is still relevant for you and if our situation has changed.
>
> [Option 1: "Unfortunately, we're still not in a position to move forward."]
> [Option 2: "Our situation has evolved, and we'd like to revisit this."]
>
> Either way, I appreciate you for being patient with us.

This demonstrates integrity and keeps important relationships warm.
