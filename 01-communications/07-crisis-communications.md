# Crisis Communications Templates: Master Playbook

## The Mission

Protect your company, customers, and stakeholders during crisis situations through clear, honest, and timely communications. The speed and quality of your initial response determine whether you control the narrative or lose it.

This playbook provides 25+ ready-to-adapt templates for eight crisis categories, with both internal (employee) and external (customer/public) versions. Use these as starting points—personalize ruthlessly, but move fast.

---

## Crisis Communications Framework

### The Golden Rules

**Rule 1: Speed > Perfection**
Silence is deadly. In a crisis, bad news + your early transparency beats bad news + 24 hours of radio silence. A good response now is better than a perfect response in 48 hours.

**Rule 2: One Spokesperson, One Message**
Designate ONE person (usually CEO or Chief Communications Officer) as the voice of the company. Everyone else refers media/public to that person. Conflicting messages multiply damage.

**Rule 3: Lead With Humanity**
Before data, timelines, and solutions: Acknowledge the impact on people. "We know this affected you. We're sorry." This prevents escalation and loss of trust.

**Rule 4: Full Transparency (Within Legal Constraints)**
Share what happened, what we're doing, what we don't yet know, and when you'll have more info. Mystery and evasion destroy trust faster than bad news.

**Rule 5: Action > Words**
Communicate what you're DOING, not just what you're SAYING. Concrete steps and progress metrics matter more than tone.

**Rule 6: Internal First, Then External**
Employees find out before customers find out on Twitter. Brief your team first, arm them with talking points, then go external.

## Crisis Communication Timeline

### Hour 1: Assess & Activate
- [ ] Confirm the crisis is real (not rumor)
- [ ] Brief executive leadership
- [ ] Activate crisis management team
- [ ] Inform legal/compliance counsel
- [ ] Identify spokesperson

### Hour 2-4: Assess Damage & Create Message
- [ ] Gather all available facts
- [ ] Determine impact scope (how many people? revenue impact? brand damage?)
- [ ] Draft initial internal message
- [ ] Prepare holding statement for external inquiries
- [ ] Notify key stakeholders (board, investors, key customers)

### Hour 4-8: Communicate Internally
- [ ] All-hands meeting or email brief (transparency + talking points)
- [ ] Department heads briefing
- [ ] Q&A document for employees
- [ ] Social media monitoring setup

### Hour 8-24: External Communication
- [ ] Public statement/blog post
- [ ] Email to affected customers
- [ ] Social media response
- [ ] Press inquiry response template
- [ ] Customer support training

### Day 2-7: Sustained Communication
- [ ] Daily updates (at 9am, 12pm, 6pm if crisis is active)
- [ ] Progress reports to stakeholders
- [ ] Transparency reports on investigation
- [ ] Customer support escalation management

### Week 2+: Recovery & Reputation Repair
- [ ] Post-mortem report (if appropriate to share)
- [ ] Compensation/correction plan
- [ ] Long-term prevention measures
- [ ] Stakeholder engagement calls
- [ ] Recovery narrative

## Section 1: Data Breach Notifications

**When to use:** Unauthorized access to customer data, employee records, or sensitive company information.

---

### 1. INTERNAL: Data Breach Initial Notification

**Timing:** Immediately upon discovery (or within 4 hours max)
**Recipient:** All employees
**Format:** Email or all-hands Slack message

Subject: **Urgent: Security Incident Notification - [Do Not Share Externally Yet]**

---

Team,

At [TIME] today, our security team identified a potential data breach affecting [SCOPE: e.g., "customer payment information" or "1,200 user accounts"]. I'm writing to brief you before this becomes public, so you're informed and can represent us accurately.

**What happened:**
A [DESCRIBE METHOD: e.g., "misconfigured database" / "unauthorized access to our systems"] resulted in potential exposure of [DATA TYPES: e.g., "names, emails, and encrypted passwords"]. We detected this through [DETECTION METHOD] and immediately [CONTAINMENT ACTION: e.g., "shut down the vulnerable system and isolated affected accounts"].

**What we know:**
- Access occurred between [DATE RANGE]
- We believe [X] customers/users are affected
- No evidence of misuse yet, but we're investigating
- We've [IMMEDIATE ACTIONS: e.g., "reset affected passwords" / "notified our cybersecurity team"]

**What we don't know yet:**
- Complete timeline of unauthorized access
- Whether any data was exfiltrated
- Full scope of impacted records

**What happens next:**
- [TODAY]: Forensic investigation launch (external security firm engaged)
- [24 HOURS]: Customer notification + free credit monitoring offer
- [48 HOURS]: Public statement with full details
- [72 HOURS]: Detailed transparency report on our blog

**Your role:**
- **Do NOT share this with anyone outside the company** until we go public (likely within 24 hours)
- Point any external inquiries to [SPOKESPERSON NAME] at [EMAIL]
- We'll have a Q&A document and talking points ready for you by [TIME]
- If you're contacted by media, say: "I'm not authorized to speak. Please contact [SPOKESPERSON] at [EMAIL]."

**My commitment to you:**
This is serious, and we're treating it that way. We will be fully transparent about what happened, why it happened, and how we're preventing it in the future. I'll update you again at [TIME] today.

Thank you for your understanding and discretion.

[SIGNATURE]

### 2. INTERNAL: Data Breach Detailed Briefing (24 Hours After Initial)

**Timing:** 24 hours post-discovery, after customer notification is live
**Recipient:** All employees
**Format:** Email + optional all-hands meeting

Subject: **Data Breach: Full Details + How We're Responding**

---

Team,

Yesterday we notified you of a data breach we discovered. I want to give you the full picture now that we've informed customers and begun our public response.

**The complete timeline:**
- [DATE]: Attacker gained unauthorized access via [METHOD]
- [DATE]: Attacker accessed [DATA TYPES] for [X] user accounts
- [DATE]: Our monitoring system alerted us to unusual activity
- [DATE TIME]: We confirmed the breach and began investigation
- [DATE TIME]: We notified law enforcement
- [DATE TIME]: We contacted all affected customers
- [NOW]: We're publishing full transparency report

**What customer data was exposed:**
Names, emails, phone numbers, and encrypted passwords (not plaintext). Payment card information was NOT accessed. Home addresses and account histories were NOT accessed.

**What we're doing to fix it:**
1. **Investigation**: [SECURITY FIRM] is conducting full forensic analysis (completion: [DATE])
2. **Technical remediation**: We've patched the vulnerability (description of fix) and added enhanced monitoring
3. **Customer protection**: All affected users have been notified, offered 2 years of free credit monitoring, and advised to reset their passwords
4. **Transparency**: We're publishing a detailed incident report on our blog today
5. **Prevention**: We're implementing [NEW SECURITY MEASURES] by [DATE]

**What this means for our business:**
- We're absorbing the cost of credit monitoring (~$[X])
- We've allocated [X] hours to remediation work
- Regulatory review will take 30-60 days
- Customer trust is damaged, but our transparent response helps recovery

**What we're NOT doing:**
- Hiding behind corporate speak
- Blaming the customer
- Downplaying the severity
- Dodging responsibility

**Your role as an employee:**
- You can share that we identified and disclosed the breach promptly
- You can share that we're offering credit monitoring and support to affected users
- You can share that we're conducting a full investigation and transparency report
- Do NOT speculate about the investigation timeline or additional impacts
- Do NOT engage in debate with critics on social media (we'll handle that)

**If customers contact you directly:**
Use this template:
"Thank you for reaching out. We know this is concerning. Here's what you need to know: [COPY FIRST 3 BULLETS FROM CUSTOMER EMAIL]. For detailed information, visit [LINK TO TRANSPARENCY PAGE]. If you have specific concerns, contact our support team at [EMAIL]."

**For leaders managing teams:**
- Check in with your team members, especially those who were directly affected
- Let them know we're covering the cost of credit monitoring and that it's optional (many won't use it)
- Reinforce that this is fixable and we're committed to earning back trust

**Our comeback plan:**
- Over the next 90 days, we'll demonstrate stronger security than before
- We'll publish quarterly transparency reports on our security improvements
- We'll engage third-party audits and share the results
- We'll contribute to industry conversation on preventing similar breaches

This is painful. It's also an opportunity to show our values: transparency, accountability, and relentless focus on customer protection.

Let's get through this together.

[SIGNATURE]

### 11. INTERNAL: PR Crisis - Employee Talking Points

**Timing:** Within 6 hours of decision to respond
**Recipient:** All employees
**Format:** Email with Q&A section

Subject: **PR Situation Update: Here's What You Need to Know**

---

Team,

You may see an article today from [PUBLICATION] that makes critical claims about [COMPANY NAME]. Here's what's happening and how to respond if asked.

**THE ARTICLE:**

[PUBLICATION] published "[TITLE]" making these claims:
- [CLAIM 1]
- [CLAIM 2]
- [CLAIM 3]

**OUR RESPONSE:**

We disagree with the article's framing. Here's our position:

"[COMPANY NAME] is committed to [CORE VALUE]. The article misrepresents [SPECIFIC CLAIM] by ignoring [CONTEXT / DATA]. The facts are: [FACT 1], [FACT 2], and [FACT 3]."

**IF SOMEONE ASKS YOU ABOUT IT:**

Use this framework:
1. Acknowledge the article exists: "Yes, I've seen it."
2. State your belief: "I don't think the article accurately represents our position."
3. Reference the official response: "Our CEO published a response with the actual facts. Here's the link: [LINK]"
4. Redirect to experts: "For more detail, [COMMS TEAM MEMBER] has done a great job explaining our position in this [BLOG POST / VIDEO / STATEMENT]."

**EMPLOYEE Q&A:**

**Q: Is this criticism valid?**
A: We believe [PUBLICATION] misunderstood [KEY ISSUE]. Our CEO's statement has the full context.

**Q: What does this mean for [MY DEPARTMENT]?**
A: No changes to your work. This is about external perception, not internal direction. We'll keep you updated.

**Q: Should I respond on social media?**
A: Please don't engage in arguments. If you want to share our response, you can retweet/share our official statement, but avoid extended debate.

## How to Use These Templates

### Customization Checklist

Before using any template:

- [ ] Replace all [BRACKETED PLACEHOLDERS] with your specific details
- [ ] Adjust tone to match your company culture (these lean professional/transparent)
- [ ] Legal review before external communication (especially data breaches, recalls, financial news)
- [ ] Get executive sign-off on major announcements (CEO/Board as appropriate)
- [ ] Test communications with small group before wide distribution
- [ ] Ensure timing coordination across internal/external/media channels
- [ ] Prepare Q&A for each announcement

### Crisis Communication Best Practices

1. **Speed over perfection**: A 90% response in 2 hours beats a 99% response in 24 hours
2. **One voice**: Designate ONE spokesperson; everyone refers media/public to them
3. **Lead with impact on people**: Customers/employees first, then business details
4. **Transparency within legal constraints**: Share what you know and what you don't know
5. **Regular updates**: In active crises, update hourly or every 2 hours
6. **Consistency**: Same message across all channels (email, social, press, status page)
7. **Accountability**: Own the mistake, don't deflect or blame
8. **Action orientation**: Communicate what you're DOING, not just saying
9. **Follow-up**: Each crisis deserves a post-mortem and public report on learnings

### Crisis Communication Timeline (Quick Reference)

**Hour 1-2:** Assess + Activate (internal briefing)
**Hour 2-4:** Gather facts + Create message
**Hour 4-8:** Notify affected parties (customers, employees, investors)
**Hour 8-24:** Public statement + Ongoing updates
**Day 2-7:** Sustained communication + Progress updates
**Week 2+:** Recovery + Prevention + Reputation repair

### Red Flags to Avoid

- Silence (worst possible response)
- Conflicting messages from different speakers
- Blaming the customer/user/employee
- Corporate jargon ("We're committed to best practices...")
- Minimizing impact ("Minor issue...")
- Dodging hard questions
- Making promises you can't keep
- Late response (every hour of silence = more reputation damage)

## Final Principles

**Principle 1: Transparency Beats Spin**
People can tell when you're hiding something. Honest, specific communication wins trust. Corporate speak loses it.

**Principle 2: Speed Builds Credibility**
Your first communication sets expectations. Fast + honest > slow + perfect.

**Principle 3: Accountability Matters**
Own the problem. Don't deflect, blame, or make excuses. People forgive accountability; they never forgive avoidance.

**Principle 4: Communication is Action**
Each update should answer: "What are we doing about this?" Not just "Here's what happened."

**Principle 5: Crises Are Opportunities**
How you handle crisis defines your company more than success ever could. This is where values become real.
